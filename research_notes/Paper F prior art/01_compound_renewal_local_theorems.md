## 1. Integro-local and local theorems for compound renewal and renewal-reward processes

**Question.** Is there a local limit theorem for S(t) = (sum of rewards up to time t), uniform in the bin, for heavy-tailed (infinite-variance) renewals and without a Cramér condition? A_N is such a functional: plus time up to slot N, with cycle (R, W) = (X_+ + X_−, X_+). One difference matters from the start. A_N includes the reward of the cycle that is unfinished at time N (the censored plus run). The standard compound renewal process Z(t) = Σ_{j ≤ ν(t)} ζ_j counts only completed cycles.

## 3. Local limit theorems for the renewal counting process at the stable scale

**Question.** Is there a known LLT for N(t) with infinite-variance interarrivals that, combined with a univariate LLT, already gives the bulk of Theorem A in the symmetric case at the center?

### 3.1 Searched

"local limit theorem renewal counting process stable infinite variance lattice P(N(t)=k)" (extended search), and "Kevei OR Alsmeyer local limit theorem number of renewals N(t) stable domain lattice". Hits checked: Alexander–Berger, EJP 21 (2016) (index 0, no moments); Kevei–Terhesiu, J. Theoret. Probab. 35 (2022), DOI 10.1007/s10959-021-01081-w (semistable LLT for sums and SRT for α ≤ 1); Caravenna–Doney 2019 (below). Gut's *Stopped Random Walks* and Bingham's papers were not opened.

### 3.2 Findings

- No source located states a lattice LLT for P(N(t) = k) at the stable scale. **GAP**: Gut (2009, 2nd ed.) and Feller vol. II were not opened. The statement follows in one line from P(N(t) = k) = Σ_j P(S_k = j) T(t − j + 1) and Gnedenko's LLT, so it is folklore-level in any case.
- **DERIVATION (why it is not enough).** A_N is not a function of a single counting process. Given the number m of completed plus runs, A_N = S^+_m + (censored part), and the same m controls the minus total N − A_N = S^−_{m or m±1} + (censored part). The law of A_N is a sum over m of products of two LLTs (the identity (O) in `Occupation theorem priority investigation/01_F_classical_source_closure.md` §2C). The N(t)-LLT for one sign gives the law of m but not the joint law with S^+_m. The bulk needs the joint (product) LLT for (S^+_m, S^−_m) summed over m, which is a two-dimensional renewal (Green function) statement. That is exactly what Berger 2019, Theorem 3.3, provides along the favorite direction (§4.3 below).
- **Symmetric case at the center.** Godrèche–Luck §7(iii) is formal (§2). The only rigorous local statement at the center that was located is Melbourne–Pène–Terhesiu 2026, Theorem 6.1, applied to the paired-run tower with fixed starting and ending tower levels (PRIOR NOTE `Local probability novelty and uniqueness/01_F_prior_art.md` §2; re-checked here, §4.1). That needs an extra interchange (summing over unboundedly many terminal levels) to give the unrestricted fresh-start atom. Berger 2019 Theorem 3.3 together with F's fresh identity gives the bulk window, including the center, rigorously (§4.3).

### 3.3 Verdict for question 3

**Does not cover as a known theorem.** No published N(t)-LLT plus univariate LLT argument for the occupation count was found. The center of the symmetric case is covered in a special case: MPT Theorem 6.1 with restricted endpoint levels. The bulk window follows from Berger's bivariate strong renewal theorem plus F's fresh identity (§4.3). The whole-row statement (a), (b) is not covered.

## 4. Melbourne–Pène–Terhesiu (2026), Melbourne–Terhesiu (2022), Doney (1991), and what they give for Theorem A

### 4.1 Melbourne, Pène, Terhesiu, arXiv:2610.15720v1 (11 October 2026)

**Level: TEXT** (arXiv HTML v1, read in two passes covering §§1–2, 6, 8 and App. B; PDF retrieval was not attempted again because the earlier note records its failure).

- Title: "Multivariate stable mixing local limit theorem for intermittent diffeomorphisms and flows". math.DS. Abstract (verbatim fragments): "We prove mixing local limit theorems for dynamical systems in the superdiffusive regime where there is convergence to a multivariate stable law (including the nonstandard domain of the central limit theorem). We apply our results to d-dimensional observables for intermittent maps and solenoids", together with intermittent semiflows and flows.
- Sections: 2 (abstract stable MLLT for flows, hypotheses (N1)–(N4)); 4–5 (verification for Gibbs–Markov semiflows and flows); **6 "Stable MLLT for Young tower maps": 6.1 ℤ^d-valued observables, 6.2 ℝ^d-valued, 6.3 verification**; 7 (nonuniformly expanding maps); **8 applications: 8.1 intermittent (Thaler) maps, 8.2 intermittent solenoids, 8.3 intermittent semiflows and flows**; App. B "Stable MLLT and LLD for Gibbs–Markov maps".
- **Theorem 1.2** (scalar intermittent map, α ∈ (1, 2]): a_n ∫ ζ_1 · ζ_2∘T^n · ξ(v_n − q a_n) dμ → g(q) ∫ζ_1 ∫ζ_2 ∫ξ dLeb, with ζ_i ∈ C_c((0,1)), ξ ∈ C_c(ℝ) and q fixed. These are test functions, not bins.
- **Theorem 2.2** (flows): lim a_t^d μ^τ(x ∈ A ∩ F_t^{−1}B : w_t(x) ∈ q a_t + K) = g(q) μ^τ(A) μ^τ(B) |K|, for a bounded K with null boundary, rectangles A, B ∈ 𝒜, and q fixed.
- **Theorem 6.1** (ℤ^d-valued observables on Young towers): the event is the single lattice point w_n − [n w̄] = [q a_n], with A, B single tower levels and q ∈ ℝ^d fixed. No uniformity in q is claimed. Hypothesis (L1) is a joint stable MLLT for Ψ = (W, R), uniform only on |z| ≤ L. **(L2) is an upper bound**: μ(Ψ̂^{(n)} = z) ≤ C (n/a_n^{d+1}) ℓ̃(|z|)/|z|^α for all n and z, a local large-deviation bound, not an asymptotic. (L3) is needed only when d ≥ α². (L4) controls the within-cycle coboundary H.
- Examples: Thaler maps, solenoids, intermittent flows. **No alternating renewal process, Lévy walk, renewal-reward process, Caravenna–Doney, Berger or Doney appears in the visible text.** Bibliography key [37] is the Gibbs–Markov LLD source; its authors were not resolved in the excerpt.
- **DERIVATION** (PRIOR NOTE `Local probability novelty and uniqueness/01_F_prior_art.md` §2, re-checked against the theorem statement above): the paired-run walk is a Young tower over a Bernoulli full-branch base, with cycle (R, W) = (X_+ + X_−, X_+), d = 1 < α². The span-one condition for each run law gives a full affine lattice for (W, R). Theorem 6.1 then gives P(A_N = pN + [q b_N], start level A, end level B) asymptotics at a fixed q for fixed tower levels A, B. The center constant agrees with F.

**What MPT gives for Theorem A.** A special case: pointwise in q, with restricted start and end levels, which by summing over finitely many levels gives a lower-order part of the bulk window. It does not give (i) the unrestricted terminal level (the censored run of arbitrary length), which needs lim_J limsup_N a(N) P(A_N = k, J_N > J) = 0 as an additional input; (ii) uniformity over |q| ≤ K, although this would follow by a standard argument once (i) holds; (iii) anything outside the bulk window; (iv) the uniform bound (b) over the whole row. (L2) is a cycle-level upper bound, not an occupation-count bound. (v) MPT's normalization is a single radial a_n per coordinate block, so the different-index moving profile (θ_{+,N}, θ_{−,N} with oscillating ratio) is not addressed as such. **Verdict: covers a special case (fixed bin at the stable scale, restricted endpoint levels, fresh paired start). Does not cover Theorem A as stated, nor Theorem B or C.**

Verbatim: "Multivariate local limit theorems are established for sums of random variables which are in the domain of attraction of a …" and "The results cover the lattice, non-lattice, and jointly lattice and non-lattice cases." Its content is known through two primary restatements:

- Berger EJP 2019, eq. (2.1), TEXT: "Let us start by stating the local limit theorem obtained by Griffin in [19] in our setting, and disentangled by Doney [11] (it is proven in dimension 2, but as stressed by Doney its proof is valid in any dimension): uniformly for x ∈ ℤ^d, a_n^{(1)} ⋯ a_n^{(d)} P(S_n = x) − g(x_n) → 0", with diagonal scalings that may differ by coordinate. [19] is P. S. Griffin, "Matrix normalized sums of independent identically distributed random vectors", Ann. Probab. 14(1) (1986) 224–246.
- Doney–Korshunov 2011 author preprint, Theorem 1 (PRIOR NOTE `Occupation theorem priority investigation/01_F_classical_source_closure.md` §1).

For F the two run laws are independent, so the bivariate LLT for (S^+_m, S^−_m) at a fixed m is just the product of two Gnedenko LLTs, and needs no Doney (PRIOR NOTE, formula (P)). The non-trivial step is summing over the random number of runs.

**Q. Berger, "Strong renewal theorems and local large deviations for multivariate random walks and renewals", Electron. J. Probab. 24 (2019), paper no. 46, 1–47, DOI 10.1214/19-EJP308; arXiv:1807.03575v2.** Level: TEXT (arXiv v2: §§1–4 statements read; proofs not read).

- Setting: ℤ^d-valued i.i.d. walk in the domain of a multivariate stable law with **diagonal, possibly different coordinate scalings** a_n^{(i)} and indices α_i ∈ (0,2]. Multivariate renewal processes are included.
- **Theorem 2.1** (Assumption 1.1 only): a local large-deviation upper bound for P(Ŝ_n = x) when |x_i| ≥ a_n^{(i)}, of the form C n φ_i(|x_i|)|x_i|^{−α_i}/(a_n^{(1)} ⋯ a_n^{(d)}) (up to the normal-case term). **Theorem 2.3** sharpens this under **Assumption 2.2, a local condition**; for d = 1 this is Doney's condition P(X = x) ≤ C L(x) x^{−(1+α)}.
- **Theorem 3.3 (case II, nonzero mean, α_{i0} > 1)**: if one of (i) a condition on Σ_i α_i^{−1} relative to 2, (ii) a condition on α_{i0} relative to the other indices, or (iii) Assumption 2.2 holds, then along the favorite direction, (x_i − b^{(i)}_{n_{i0}})/a^{(i)}_{n_{i0}} → t_i ∈ ℝ, the Green function satisfies G(x) ~ C_t a^{(i0)}_{n_{i0}}/(a^{(1)}_{n_{i0}} ⋯ a^{(d)}_{n_{i0}}), with C_t an integral of the limit density g along the mean direction (3.6). (The inequality signs in (i) and (ii) were lost in text extraction. The balanced-case comment says "if α > d/2 …", which reads (i) as Σ_i α_i^{−1} < 2. For d = 2 and α_i ∈ (1, 2) this always holds. **GAP**: confirm the sign on the PDF page.)
- **Theorems 4.1–4.3**: uniform bounds on G(x) away from the favorite direction, **all under Assumption 2.2**.

**DERIVATION: what Theorem 3.3 gives F.** Take the ℤ²-valued renewal walk with steps (X_+, X_−). Its Green function G(k, l) is the probability that, for a fresh plus start, the first k + l slots end exactly at the end of a minus run and contain k plus slots. The two coordinates are independent, each has positive mean, and the limit (Z_+, Z_−) is nondegenerate under the separate scalings b_{+}, b_{−}, whatever the indices, including the case α_+ ≠ α_−. Using coordinates (X_+ + X_−, X_+) instead would make the limit degenerate when α_+ < α_−, so the pair (X_+, X_−) is the right one. Under (i), which holds automatically for d = 2 and α_i ∈ (1,2), Theorem 3.3 gives G(k, N − k) asymptotics for (k − pN)/B_N → t, with no local condition. F's fresh identity writes P_N(k) as G convolved with the two summable censored-run kernels (one for the unfinished run and one for the run before it). Truncating the kernels at a fixed shift and using Theorem 3.3 on compact t-sets gives the bulk window, |k − pN| ≤ K B_N, pointwise in t and then uniformly on compacts by a subsequence argument, provided the kernel tails are controlled by a uniform bound on G near the favorite direction. Berger proves that bound under Assumption 2.2 (Theorems 4.1–4.2) but not under (RV) alone. The crude product-LLT error bound is not enough by itself when the scales separate (PRIOR NOTE, estimate (E)), so the bulk window under (RV) alone still needs F's own renewal-sum bounds, or a tightness argument for the kernels.

### 4.4 Verdict for question 4

- **Confirmed, with one refinement.** The bulk window (Theorem A(c), and A(a) restricted to |k − pN| ≤ K B_N) follows from known inputs plus F's fresh identity. The cleanest input is Berger 2019, Theorem 3.3, applied to the walk (X_+, X_−), which already handles different indices and scalings. The product LLT from Gnedenko/Stone, or the Doney/Griffin bivariate LLT, is enough only with an additional renewal-sum argument. The bivariate LLT plus the identity does not close the window by itself when b_{+,N}/b_{−,N} → 0 or ∞.
- **Confirmed.** Whole-row uniformity (Theorem A(a) on the full row, and A(b) sup_k B_N P_N(k) ≤ C over 0 ≤ k ≤ N under (RV) only) is in none of these papers. MPT has fixed q with restricted levels. Doney/Griffin are uniform in x but at fixed m, not at a random count with a censored run. Berger's off-direction bounds need Assumption 2.2, a local condition that F does not assume in Theorem A. Melbourne–Terhesiu and Caravenna–Doney give i.i.d. upper bounds only.
- **Far bins.** The statement that every bin outside the window is o(1/B_N) uniformly, including the k close to N where the censored run dominates, is not in any of them.

Its big-jump asymptotic G_N(k) has two terms: the completed long plus run, contributing (N − L_k) f_+(L_k)/μ_−, which needs a local (LR)-type input and a renewal count of the possible positions; and the censored final run, contributing T_+(L_k) κ_+(N − k), with the renewal boundary kernel κ. ### 5.2 Berger, EJP 24 (2019) paper 46

See §4.3. In summary: the multivariate local large-deviation upper bounds (Theorem 2.1 under (RV)-type Assumption 1.1; Theorem 2.3 under the local Assumption 2.2) and the Green-function asymptotics along the favorite direction (Theorems 3.1–3.4), with off-direction bounds under Assumption 2.2. **Why it does not give Theorem B.** The Green function G(k, N − k) is the occupation law restricted to the event that slot N ends a minus run. ## 8. Overall verdict on priority

Within the coverage stated above, Theorems A, B and C were not found in the literature as stated. The ingredients are classical or recent and must be credited: the i.i.d. stable LLT (Gnedenko, Stone), the i.i.d. local large-deviation upper bound (Caravenna–Doney 2019 Thm 1.1; Melbourne–Terhesiu 2022 Thm 1.1), the i.i.d. local one-big-jump asymptotic under a local tail condition (Doney 1989; Denisov–Dieker–Shneer 2008 Thm 9.1; Berger PTRF 2019 Thm 2.4), and the distributional stable occupation limit (Takács, Kesten 1962). Two recent results come close to the bulk of Theorem A. Berger's bivariate strong renewal theorem along the favorite direction (EJP 2019, Thm 3.3), applied to the walk (X_+, X_−) together with F's fresh identity, gives the bulk window with different indices and scales, but not the whole-row uniformity or the bound sup_k B_N P_N(k) ≤ C under (RV) alone, because Berger's off-direction bounds need a local condition. Melbourne–Pène–Terhesiu's Theorem 6.1 gives a single stable-scale bin with restricted start and end levels on a tower that contains the paired-run walk, but no unrestricted censored run, no uniformity and no far bins. So the new content of Theorem A is (a) and (b) on the whole row under (RV) and (SPAN) only, including the censored run and the moving profile for oscillating scale ratios; the bulk window alone should not be claimed as new. Theorem B has a formal physics antecedent for its two-term shape (Rebenshtok et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2018). Its rigorous lattice form is not anticipated: the double-limit ratio asymptotic, the censored-run term with the renewal boundary kernel, and the boundary layer P_N(N − j)/T_+(N) → κ(j), which PRE 2014 §VII explicitly sets aside. Theorem C answers the question Wang et al. 2018 (§VIII) leave for future work, and no source located gives sharp criteria on the first run for any of these approximations. Residual risk: the Takács 1957/1959 papers, Smith 1955, Nagaev 1957, Ch. 2 of Borovkov's 2022 book and Borovkov's 2018 TVP paper case (a) were not read.

### Sentences for the introduction

On Melbourne, Pène and Terhesiu:

> Melbourne, Pène and Terhesiu [MPT26, Theorem 6.1] prove a mixing local limit theorem at the stable scale for ℤ^d-valued observables on Young towers. The walk of this paper, with runs grouped in pairs, is such a tower, and their theorem gives the probability of a single bin k = pN + [q B_N] at fixed q when the first and last runs are restricted to fixed lengths. Theorem A removes these restrictions. It holds uniformly over the whole row 0 ≤ k ≤ N, including the bins where the unfinished last run is long, and for every relation between the two stable scales. We do not use their result.

We use this bound, applied to the centered run lengths, to control the bins outside the bulk in Theorem A. Their theorem concerns a fixed number of summands, while A_N counts a random number of runs and the last of them is cut off by the horizon.

> Berger [Ber19, Theorem 3.3] proves a strong renewal theorem for multivariate walks with different coordinate scalings along the direction of the mean.
