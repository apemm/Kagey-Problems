## 0. Summary and status

1. **O1(a) as stated is false.** The first counterexample is at d = 5 (Section 6). Call a set
   B of states a *memoryless block* if:
   - every state of B leaves B at the same rate, with the same landing law outside B;
   - every jump into B lands with law pi_B / pi(B).

   Then reversing Q inside B gives a generator Q^[B] with the same stationary occupation
   law at every T, and the same discrete counts at every N.
   - **Concrete case.** d = 5, B = {1,2,3}. Here Q^[B] is neither Q nor Q*, and it has the
     principal minors of -Q.
   - **Exact checks.** F_Q - F_{Q^[B]} = 0 holds as a rational function, and the count laws
     agree for N <= 6.
   - **Why the plan's evidence missed it.** Random fits never land on such chains.
2. **Proved: the correct positive statements.**
   - **Theorem 1 (one T is enough).** For any start, the law of O(T0) at a single T0 > 0
     determines the law at every T. Proof: exponential-separation asymptotics, plus the
     transcendence of e^{algebraic}.
   - **Theorem 2, O1(b) (generic start).** For a start outside a finite union of proper
     subspaces, the law at one T determines Q. The proof is direct and needs neither facemax
     nor full support.
   - **Theorem 3, O1(c) (second moments).** This is the strongest form.
     - For d <= 4, pi together with E_pi[O_i O_j] up to order T^4 determines Q up to reversal,
       for every irreducible Q.
     - For d >= 5, the same holds up to order T^5 on an explicit open dense, Lebesgue-full
       set of generators.
   - **Theorem 4, O1(a) corrected.** The stationary law at one T determines Q up to
     reversal when d <= 4, when Q has no cut, or when Q is generic in the sense of
     Theorem 3.
   - **Proposition 5 (exact gluing formula across a cut).** For the cut-transpose Q':
     F_Q - F_{Q'} = (p1 - p2)(q1 - q2)/(1 - alpha beta).

     So Q' keeps the law exactly when one side of the cut is "balanced". A side of size 2
     is balanced only if it is symmetric.
   - **Theorem 6.** Reversing a memoryless block keeps the law.
3. **Side fact.** Every cut-transpose generator point has the same stationary law pi as Q.
   This is easy, but the paper does not state it.
4. **Open.**
   - Conjecture A: the law fiber is exactly the set generated by Q -> Q* and by reversals of
     memoryless blocks.
   - Conjecture B: the characterization of balanced sides.
   - What one pair (N, T) of the discrete chain determines.

**Recommendation for C2.** The honest headline is: "the exact occupation law determines a
finite chain up to time reversal and the reversal of memoryless blocks". In particular it
determines the chain up to time reversal for d <= 4, for chains without a cut, and
generically, and in these cases already through the second moments. Do not claim "every Q".

## 1. Setting and the data

Q is an irreducible generator on [d], with stationary law pi and D = diag(pi). Put
e = sqrt(pi) and G = D^{1/2} Q D^{-1/2}, so that G e = 0 = e^T G. The time reversal
Q* = D^{-1} Q^T D corresponds to G^T. Put S = (G + G^T)/2 and K = (G - G^T)/2. For a start mu
and lam in C^d, with Lam = diag(lam), set

    F^mu_Q(lam) = mu (Lam - Q)^{-1} 1,      F_Q = F^pi_Q = e^T (Lam - G)^{-1} e.

**Lemma 1.1 (reductions).**
- (i) Continuous time. The laws of O(T) under (Q, mu), for all T >= 0, are equivalent to
  F^mu_Q. Indeed E_mu exp(-w.O(T)) = mu e^{T(Q - W)} 1, its Laplace transform in T is
  F^mu_Q(s1 + w), and O(T) is bounded, so its moment generating function is entire.
- (ii) Discrete time. Let P be irreducible stochastic. Then
  sum_{N>=1} y^N E_mu[prod_i z_i^{n_i(N)}] = F^mu_P(lam) with lam_i = 1/(y z_i). For
  P = I + hQ we have F^mu_P(lam) = h^{-1} F^mu_Q((lam - 1)/h).

  So, at a fixed h = T/N, the count laws for all N are equivalent to the continuous laws for
  all T. Every result below therefore holds verbatim for the counts of I + (T/N)Q.
- (iii) Second moments. E_pi[O(T) O(T)^T] = sum_{m>=0} T^{m+2}/(m+2)! Sigma_m, where
  Sigma_m = D Q^m + (D Q^m)^T = 2 D^{1/2} sym(G^m) D^{1/2}.

  So the stationary second moments on any interval of T are equivalent to pi together with
  sym(G^m) for m >= 1. Proof: E_pi[O_i O_j] = int_0^T (T - r)[pi_i P_ij(r) + pi_j P_ji(r)] dr.
- (iv) A fixed N. For the counts of I + hQ at a fixed N, we have E_pi[n n^T] =
  N D + sum_{k=1}^{N-1} h^k C(N+1, k+2) Sigma_k. This uses
  sum_r (N - r) C(r, k) = C(N+1, k+2).

  So the law at one fixed N, for N distinct values of h, gives Sigma_1, ..., Sigma_{N-1}.

## 2. One T determines everything

**Lemma 2.1 (the denominator).** Let P(s,w) = det(sI - Q + W). Then F^mu_Q(s1 + w) = N_mu/P,
with N_mu = mu adj(sI - Q + W) 1, and this fraction is in lowest terms. Hence F^mu_Q, from
any start (full support not needed), determines every principal minor of -Q.

*Proof.*
- P(s,w) = R(-(s1 + w)), where R(v) = det(-Q - diag v) is irreducible over C (Al Ahmadieh,
  Lemma 5.1; C2 Lemma 2.1).
- In the variables (s, lam = s1 + w), the polynomial P does not involve s. So in any
  factorization the s-degrees of the factors add to 0, and P is irreducible in C[s,w].
- N_mu has s-degree d - 1 and leading coefficient mu.1 = 1, so N_mu != 0 and P does not
  divide N_mu. The reduced denominator, normalized to be monic in s, is therefore P. Its
  coefficients are the principal minors. QED

**Theorem 1 (one horizon).** Let Q be irreducible, let Q' be any generator, and let mu, mu'
be starts. Suppose O(T0) has the same law under (Q, mu) and under (Q', mu') for one T0 > 0.
Then Q' has the principal minors of -Q (so Q' is irreducible), and F^{mu'}_{Q'} = F^mu_Q.
That is, the laws agree at every T.

*Proof.*
1. **The data.** The law at T0 is h(w) = mu e^{T0(Q - W)} 1 for all w in R^d (an entire
   function), and h' likewise.
2. **Isolating one exponential.** Take v in R^d with distinct entries and v_1 smallest. For
   large real t the matrix Q - tV has simple eigenvalues mu_i(tv) = -t v_i + O(1). Each
   mu_i(tv) + t v_i, and each residue C_i, is analytic in 1/t near 0.

   Write h(tv) = sum_i C_i(tv) e^{T0 mu_i(tv)}. Then e^{T0 t v_1} h(tv) equals
   phi(1/t) := C_1 e^{T0(mu_1 + t v_1)} up to O(e^{-c t}). So phi is determined by h: its
   Taylor coefficients are the asymptotic coefficients.

   Letting v vary over an open set shows that C_1 e^{T0 mu_1} = C'_1 e^{T0 mu'_1} on an open
   set U of w.
3. **The residue function.** Here C_1 = C(mu_1(w), w), where C = N_mu/(d P/d s) is a
   rational function on the hypersurface {P = 0}.

   C is not identically 0. Otherwise every simple root of P(., w) is a root of N_mu(., w),
   which is impossible by degree, since N_mu != 0. So, shrinking U, C_1 != 0.
4. **Transcendence.** On U, e^{T0(mu_1 - mu'_1)} = C'_1/C_1, and the right side is
   algebraic. Restrict to a complex line through U.

   One-variable lemma: if f and g are algebraic and e^f = g on a disc, then f is constant.
   *Proof of the lemma.* Continue both to the compact Riemann surface of C(z, f, g). If f is
   nonconstant, it has a pole p, with f ~ a tau^{-m} in a local coordinate. Then e^f grows
   faster than any power along a ray, while g is meromorphic at p.

   So mu'_1 = mu_1 + c, with c a real constant, on U.
5. **The minors.** P'(mu + c, w) vanishes on an open piece of the irreducible hypersurface
   {P = 0}, so it is divisible by P. Both are monic of degree d in mu, so
   P'(mu + c, w) = P(mu, w). Hence -Q' + cI and -Q have the same principal minors.

   Both Q and Q' are generators, so all their eigenvalues have real part <= 0. Also -c is an
   eigenvalue of Q, and c is an eigenvalue of Q'. Therefore c = 0.
6. **The residues.** Now C'_1 = C_1 on U, so C' = C on {P = 0}. Partial fractions at the
   simple roots (generic w) give mu'(sI - Q' + W)^{-1} 1 = mu(sI - Q + W)^{-1} 1. QED

Remarks.
- Nothing in the proof uses stationarity. It applies to every start, and so to (b) as well.
- **Discrete time.** By Lemma 1.1(iv), one N and all h give Sigma_k for k <= N - 1. At
  N = 2 this is only the symmetrized fluxes. For d = 3, uniform pi, P = I + h(S + kC) with C
  the cyclic circulation, every k gives the same N = 2 law, for every h. So one N is not
  enough in general (`check_balanced_halves.py` (C)).
- **Open.** I did not settle what a single pair (N, T) determines.

## 3. O1(b): a generic start

**Theorem 2.** Let Q be irreducible, and let Fib be the finite set of generators with the
principal minors of -Q (C2 Theorem 2.5(a)). Put v_Q(lam) = (Lam - Q)^{-1} 1, and for
Q' in Fib \ {Q} let Z_{Q'} = {mu : mu.(v_{Q'} - v_Q) == 0}. Then:
- each Z_{Q'} is a proper linear subspace;
- for every start mu outside Z = union of the Z_{Q'}, and every generator Q' with the law
  of O(T0) under (Q', mu) equal to that under (Q, mu) for one T0 > 0, we have Q' = Q;
- Z meets the simplex in a closed nowhere dense set. The start need not have full support.

*Proof.*
- **Z_{Q'} is proper.** Expand at large lam with m = 1/lam: the second-order term of
  v_Q is diag(m) Q m, whose coefficient of m_i m_j is q_ij. So v_Q determines Q, and
  v_{Q'} - v_Q != 0.
- **The main claim.** Theorem 1 and Lemma 2.1 put Q' in Fib, and F^mu_{Q'} = F^mu_Q means
  mu is in Z_{Q'} unless Q' = Q.
- **The simplex.** A proper subspace cannot contain the affine hyperplane {mu.1 = 1},
  since that hyperplane spans R^d.

QED. pi lies in Z_{Q*} whenever Q != Q*, and in Z_{Q^[B]} for every memoryless block
(Section 6).

## 4. O1(c): the second moments

By Lemma 1.1(iii), the data are pi together with sym(G^m). From them:
- sym(G) = S.
- sym(G^2) = S^2 + K^2, since SK + KS is antisymmetric. So N := K^2 is known.
- sym(G^3) = S^3 + SN + NS + KSK. So M := KSK is known.

**Theorem 3.**
- (i) If d <= 4, then pi, Sigma_1 and Sigma_2 determine Q up to time reversal, for every
  irreducible Q. Equivalently, E_pi[O_i(T) O_j(T)] to order T^4 does.
- (ii) Let d >= 2, and suppose:
  - the nonzero eigenvalues of N = K^2 each have multiplicity exactly 2;
  - the graph on the 2-dimensional eigenplanes E_k of N, with k ~ l when
    P_k S P_l != 0, is connected.

  Then pi, Sigma_1, Sigma_2 and Sigma_3 determine Q up to time reversal.
- (iii) For every pi, the conditions in (ii) hold off a closed, nowhere dense, Lebesgue-null
  subset of the open set of generators with all rates positive.

*Proof.*
- **The kernel.** If Q' has the same data, then pi' = pi, S' = S, K'^2 = N and
  K' S K' = M. For a real antisymmetric K', |K'x|^2 = -x^T K'^2 x, so K' kills ker N. Also
  K' commutes with N, so it preserves each eigenplane E_k (eigenvalue -sigma_k^2).
- **One plane.** On a plane, an antisymmetric map with square -sigma_k^2 is
  +-sigma_k J_k. So K' = sum_k eps_k K_k with eps_k = +-1, where K_k = K restricted to E_k.
- **(i).** For d <= 4, e-perp has dimension <= 3, so K has rank <= 2. Then there is at most
  one plane, eps = +-1, K' = +-K, and G' is G or G^T. This needs Sigma_1 and Sigma_2 only.
- **(ii).** P_k M P_l = K_k S K_l and P_k K' S K' P_l = eps_k eps_l K_k S K_l. Each K_k is
  invertible on E_k, so eps_k = eps_l whenever P_k S P_l != 0. Connectivity gives K' = +-K.
- **(iii), algebraic failure set.** Failure is contained in the zero set of a polynomial in
  the entries of G. That polynomial is the product of:
  - the discriminant and constant term of the reduced polynomial psi, with
    det(tI - K) = t^eps psi(t^2) after removing the forced zero roots;
  - prod_{k<l} ||P_k S P_l||^2, written as a symmetric function of the sigma_k^2 and
    cleared of denominators.

  Composed with the real-analytic map Q -> G, it is real-analytic on the connected open set
  of positive-rate generators. So it suffices to give one witness for each pi.
- **(iii), the witness.** Let f_1, ..., f_{d-1} be an orthonormal basis of e-perp. Take:
  - S0 = -(I - e e^T), whose off-diagonal entries e_i e_j are positive;
  - S1 = sum_{i,j} f_i f_j^T;
  - K = sum_k sigma_k (f_{2k} f_{2k-1}^T - f_{2k-1} f_{2k}^T), with distinct sigma_k > 0;
  - G = S0 + eps S1 + delta K, with eps and delta small.

  Then G e = 0 = e^T G and all off-diagonal entries are positive. The planes have distinct
  sigma, and P_k S P_l = eps P_k S1 P_l != 0, because S0 is scalar on e-perp. QED

**Exceptions.** Theorem 3(ii) can fail in two ways:
- **Coupling.** The memoryless example of Section 6 has K^2 eigenvalues -0.875 (twice) and
  -0.75 (twice). These are distinct, but S does not couple the two planes, and the
  conclusion fails (the full law fails too).
- **Repeated eigenvalue.** Isoclinic K (a repeated sigma) is the other way.

I do not know whether the full second-moment sequence (all m) and the full law always have
the same fiber.

## 5. O1(a): the stationary start, positive part

**Theorem 4.** Let Q and Q' be irreducible, and suppose O(T0) has the same stationary law
under both, for one T0 > 0. Then Q' is Q or Q* in each of the following cases:
- (i) d <= 4;
- (ii) Q has no cut;
- (iii) Q satisfies the conditions of Theorem 3(ii).

Conversely, Q* always has the same stationary law at every T (C2 Proposition 2.7, by path
reversal).

*Proof.*
- (i) and (iii): Theorem 1 gives the law at all T, which contains the second moments.
  Apply Theorem 3.
- (ii): Theorem 1 and Lemma 2.1 give the principal minors, and C2 Theorem 2.5(b) (Loewy)
  does the rest. QED

The route the plan proposes (J_V, then the finite fiber) is thus replaced by Lemma 2.1,
which gives the minors exactly with no large deviations. For d = 4, (i) settles the cut
case that Theorem A leaves open: the paper's own d = 4 cut-transpose is separated by
Sigma_1, the symmetrized fluxes.

## 6. Cuts: the gluing formula, memoryless blocks, the counterexample

**Normal form.** Let X | Y be a cut. Write G_XY = x1 y1^T and G_YX = y2 x2^T. Summing the
rows X of G e = 0 and the columns X of e^T G = 0 gives
(x1.e)(y1.e) = (x2.e)(y2.e) = -e^T G_XX e. So the factors can be rescaled until
x1.e = x2.e and y1.e = y2.e.

Put A = G_XX and B = G_YY, and define

    flip_Y G = [[A, x1 y2^T], [y1 x2^T, B^T]],     flip_X G = (flip_Y G)^T with G^T for G.

**Properties of the flip.**
- flip_Y G has e as a two-sided null vector (direct check with A e = -b x1,
  e^T A = -b x2^T, B e = -a y2, e^T B = -a y1^T).
- It is the paper's cut-transpose (eq:cut-transpose) with a = x1, b = y1, c = y2, u = x2.
  So by (N), D^{-1/2} flip_Y G D^{1/2} is the generator point of the cut-transpose of -Q.
- **In particular, every cut-transpose generator point has stationary law pi.** In the
  coordinates of Q: Q'_X = Q_X, Q'_Y = Q*_Y, q'_{xy} = a_x pi_y c_y and
  q'_{yx} = b_y u_x / pi_y, with pi_X.a = u.1 and b.1 = pi_Y.c.

**Proposition 5 (gluing).** Put M_X = Lam_X - A and M_Y = Lam_Y - B, and

    p1 = e^T M_X^{-1} x1,  p2 = x2^T M_X^{-1} e,  alpha = x2^T M_X^{-1} x1,
    q1 = y1^T M_Y^{-1} e,  q2 = e^T M_Y^{-1} y2,  beta  = y1^T M_Y^{-1} y2,
    E_X = e^T M_X^{-1} e,  E_Y = e^T M_Y^{-1} e.

In the coordinates of Q, p1 = pi_X (Lam - Q_X)^{-1} a, p2 = u (Lam - Q_X)^{-1} 1, and so on.
Then

    F_Q = E_X + E_Y + (p1 q1 + p2 q2 + alpha q1 q2 + beta p1 p2)/(1 - alpha beta),
    F_Q - F_{flip_Y Q} = (p1 - p2)(q1 - q2)/(1 - alpha beta).

*Proof.*
- **F_Q.** Lam - G = K0 - U V^T, with K0 = diag(M_X, M_Y), U = [(x1;0), (0;y2)] and
  V = [(0;y1), (x2;0)]. Then V^T K0^{-1} U = [[0, beta], [alpha, 0]]. Woodbury gives the
  first line.
- **The flip.** It sends B -> B^T and y1 <-> y2. This fixes E_Y and beta and swaps q1 and
  q2, which gives the second line. QED (Exact check on the d = 4 example:
  `check_gluing_and_counterexample.py` (1).)

The rational functions form an integral domain, and 1 - alpha beta -> 1. So the
cut-transpose keeps the stationary law iff p1 == p2 (the X side is *balanced*) or
q1 == q2. Also flip_Y Q = Q iff the Y side is symmetric (B = B^T, y1 = y2), and
flip_Y Q = Q* iff the X side is symmetric. A symmetric side is balanced.

**Corollary 5.1 (necessary conditions for balance).** Expand p1 - p2 in m = 1/lam.
- Order 1 forces x1 = x2 =: x. In Q terms, the entrance law into X is proportional to
  pi_i a_i.
- Order 2 forces (A_ij - A_ji)(rho_i - rho_j) = 0 with rho = x/e. In Q terms, every edge of
  X that is not in detailed balance joins two states with the same exit rate to Y.
- Since then A e = A^T e, K_X e_X = 0. So a side with |X| = 2 is balanced only if it is
  symmetric, and with d = 4 no nontrivial cut-transpose keeps the law (consistent with
  Theorem 4(i)).
- These two conditions are not sufficient. `check_balanced_halves.py` (A) gives a
  four-state side with two-valued rho and a circulation on the three-state level set, and
  there p1 - p2 != 0.

**Definition.** B, with 2 <= |B| <= d - 1, is a *memoryless block* of Q if there are
kappa > 0, a law nu on B^c and c >= 0 on B^c with:
- q_{iy} = kappa nu_y for i in B, y not in B;
- q_{yi} = c_y pi_i/pi(B) for y not in B, i in B.

**Theorem 6 (reversal of a memoryless block).** Let Q^[B] be Q with Q_B replaced by
Q*_B = D_B^{-1} Q_B^T D_B, and the cross blocks kept. Then:
- Q^[B] is an irreducible generator with stationary law pi;
- F_{Q^[B]} = F_Q, so the stationary law of O(T) is the same at every T, and so are the
  counts of I + hQ at every N and h.

Moreover:
- Q^[B] = Q iff Q_B is in detailed balance on B, which is automatic if |B| = 2;
- Q^[B] = Q* iff |B^c| = 1, or the side B^c is symmetric.

*Proof.*
1. **Two identities.** Flux balance across B gives kappa pi(B) = sum_y pi_y c_y. The
   balance at j in B then gives pi_B Q_B = -kappa pi_B, and Q_B 1 = -kappa 1.
2. **Q^[B] is a generator with law pi.**
   - Rows of B: Q*_B 1 = D_B^{-1} (pi_B Q_B)^T = -kappa 1, so the rows sum to 0.
   - Columns j in B: pi_B Q*_B = (Q_B 1)^T D_B = -kappa pi_B, which is cancelled by the
     inflow pi_j kappa.
   - The other entries are unchanged.
3. **The law, case |B^c| = 1.** Then q*_{iy} = pi_y c_y/pi(B) = kappa and
   q*_{yi} = pi_i c_y/pi(B). So Q^[B] = Q*, and Proposition 2.7 of C2 applies.
4. **The law, case |B^c| >= 2.** B | B^c is a cut. In symmetrized coordinates x1 and x2 are
   both proportional to e_B (exit rates constant, entrance proportional to pi_B), so after
   normalization x1 = x2 = theta e_B. Then p1 = theta E_B = p2, and Proposition 5 gives
   F_Q = F_{flip_{B^c} Q}.

   Transposition does not change F: e^T (Lam - H^T)^{-1} e = e^T (Lam - H)^{-1} e. Also
   (flip_{B^c} G)^T is G with A replaced by A^T, and desymmetrized this is Q^[B].
5. **The equality cases.** They follow from the flip identities above. QED

**Probabilistic reading.** The B-sojourns have Exp(kappa) lengths, independent of what
happens inside them, and their insides are stationary segments of the chain Q_B + kappa I.
The occupation law of a stationary segment of fixed length is reversal-invariant
(C2 Proposition 2.7), and the moves out of and into B do not see the state. So reversing
the inside of B changes nothing in the law of O(T), while it does change the path law.

**Corollary 7 (counterexample to O1(a) as stated).**
- **The chain.** d = 5, B = {1,2,3}, Y = {4,5}:
  - inside B: q12 = q23 = q31 = 2 and q21 = q32 = q13 = 1;
  - each i in B goes to 4 at rate 1 and to 5 at rate 2;
  - 4 goes to each i at rate 1, and 5 at rate 2;
  - q45 = 1 and q54 = 4.

  Then pi = (4,4,4,6,3)/21.
- **The other generator.** Q^[B] is Q with the B block transposed (pi_B is uniform).
- **What is checked.** Q^[B] != Q, Q^[B] != Q*, the 31 principal minors agree,
  F_Q - F_{Q^[B]} = 0 identically, Sigma_1, Sigma_2 and Sigma_3 agree, and the discrete
  count laws agree for N = 1, ..., 6 at h = 1/20.
- **Generality.** Any d >= 5 admits such examples: take |B| >= 3 with Q_B not in detailed
  balance, and B^c (of size >= 2) not symmetric. For d <= 4 none exist (Theorem 4(i)).

**Conjecture A (the law fiber).** The irreducible Q' with the stationary law of Q are
exactly the generators reached from Q or Q* by reversing memoryless blocks, applied in
succession.
- **Proved cases.** d <= 4, no cut, generic Q, and single cut-transposes, where the balance
  criterion is exact.

**Conjecture B.** A side X is balanced iff x1 = x2, and the antisymmetric part of G_XX is a
sum of parts each supported in a memoryless block of Q contained in X.
- **Sufficiency** follows from Theorem 6 (see `check_balanced_halves.py` (B)).
- **Necessity** is open beyond Corollary 5.1.
