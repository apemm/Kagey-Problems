#### P2-1. The lattice-rounding explanation claims more precision than the displayed approximate saddle supplies

**Locations:** `prop:nextterm` explanation, `sec_secondorder.tex:811–816`; `app:nextterm`, `app_laplace.tex:359–370`, particularly the last sentence. The proof rounds the point with displacement \(x_0=\Sigma_F\gamma_F/T\), whereas that point maximizes the quadratic model, not necessarily the full function \(G_T\). [Main explanation](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex:811); [actual rounding argument](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:359).

**Diagnosis:** Analyticity and the Taylor bounds allow \(\nabla G_T(x_0)=O(T^{-1})\). Rounding this approximate saddle can therefore produce an additional \(O((TN)^{-1})\) contribution, besides the quadratic \(O(T/N^2)\) contribution. The displayed argument actually proves the sufficient bound \(G_T(n^0/N)=c_F/T+O(T^{-2}+T/N^2)\). It does not isolate the rounding loss as only \(O(T/N^2)\). **This does not invalidate `eq:logmaxnext`: its existing \(O(T^{-2}+T^2/N)\) remainder absorbs the issue.** [Taylor bounds and `eq:GTnear`](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:294).

**Proposed fix:** Delete the final “flat to first order at its maximizer” sentence and state the bound already displayed in the proof. In the main explanation, say “rounding the quadratic-model maximizer preserves the claimed remainder.” Alternatively, introduce the actual continuous maximizer \(x_T=\Sigma_F\gamma_F/T+O(T^{-2})\); rounding that point does have a quadratic loss. The first option is shorter and sufficient.

#### Appendix B.2: independent targeted check of \(c_F=-(k-1)/8\)

**Verdict: sound in the calculation checked.** I used symbolic algebra with a free positive symbol \(m=k-1\), not a sweep over sizes or numerical fits. I independently expanded the phase to degree four and the logarithmic amplitude to degree two from the displayed closed-form integrand, checked the Gaussian substitution identities, and simplified the resulting moments. All residuals were exactly zero. The only failures encountered were local Python-runtime/package discovery; the actual calculation ran successfully with Python 3.14 and SymPy 1.14.0. [Integrand and Taylor coefficients, lines 463–511](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:463).

The reasoning is as follows. With \(\varpi=m/k\), Sherman–Morrison gives \(g=s/(1-s)\), \(b=\mu_0(1-s)^{-2}\); after \(1-s=(1+Y)/k\), these yield exactly the phase and amplitude printed at lines 477–478. Symmetry of \(C_F\) at the center gives \(\gamma_F=0\); thus the coefficient of the maximum equals the local coefficient. The covariance is \(\mathcal J^{-1}=\sigma_0(I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/k)\), \(\sigma_0=k^2/(2m^2)\), realized by centered independent Gaussians. [Symmetry](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:450); [Gaussian representation](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:510).

Exchangeability and \(\sum_iw_i=0\) give \(\mathbb E[w_1W_3]=\mathbb E[w_1W_3W_2]=0\). The necessary nonzero moments are

\[
\mathbb E W_3^2=\frac{6m(m-1)}{k},\qquad
\mathbb E[w_1^2(W_2-2)^2]=\frac{m(m^2+2m+4)}{k}.
\]

In particular, the mixed cubic term vanishes for a valid symmetry reason, not by an unjustified independence assumption. From the symbolic calculation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\mathcal P_4&=\frac{m^4+6m^3-m^2-2}{16mk},\\
\mathbb E\mathcal L_2&=-\frac{m^2}{2k},\\
\frac12\mathbb E(\mathcal P_3+\mathcal L_1)^2
&=-\frac{m^4+m^2-2}{16mk}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Their sum is \(-m/8=-(k-1)/8\). The square in the last expression is an ordinary square, not an absolute square; the imaginary cubic/amplitude term is why this contribution is negative. The formulas include \(m=1\), giving the symmetric-pair value \(-1/8\). These agree with every displayed moment and final simplification in B.2. [Moment calculation and final sum, lines 527–550](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:527).

The independent Bessel check for a symmetric pair also has the right sign: the NIST expansion gives coefficients \(+1/8\) for \(I_0\) and \(-3/8\) for \(I_1\), hence \(-1/(8aT)\) after combining them. I checked the cited expansion, not the entire external Paper A proof. [Pair calculation](C:/Users/arjun/OneDrive/Documents/GitHub/Kagey-Problems/kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/app_laplace.tex:390); [NIST DLMF 10.40.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40#E1).
