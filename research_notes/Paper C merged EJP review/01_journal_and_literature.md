## 2. Does the merged manuscript make a convincing EJP submission?

### Takeaway

**Credible EJP candidate, with substantial proof-burden and length risk even at 64 pages plus 14 pages of supplement.** I would support EJP submission once the short literature-positioning and final packaging checks are complete; further unlimited searching or an enlarged theorem programme is not justified by this review. The material has a coherent probability question and several potentially substantial results; a specialist must still judge whether their advance over existing occupation-density and spectral methods warrants the length. The completed trims and reported precision fixes strengthen the case. This is an editor-style inference from the claims and journal standard, not a finding that all proofs are correct. Sources: [introduction](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>), [local theory](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>), [shared charter](https://imstat.org/journals-and-publications/electronic-communications-in-probability/electronic-communications-in-probability-editorial-policy/).

### Cited Findings

- **The general question is clear.** For `I+(T/N)Q`, C asks which exact support carries the largest point probability of an occupation-count vector. Its first-order competition is `tau*lambda_F + |F|-1`; its irreducible-face maximum has an explicit constant multiplying `N^(-(k-1))*T^((k-1)/2)*exp(-T*lambda_F)`. This is more precise than merely asking where the occupation measure concentrates. [Introduction, Theorems A/B](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>).
- **The complete graph gives a substantive test of the general framework.** All support sizes tie at the same leading crossing scale, so the constants determine the actual winner. C additionally states exact balancing, exact finite-N crossing uniqueness for the symmetric model, asymptotic direct vertex-to-full-support selection, and effects of starts/weak fields. These are claimed theorems, not independently re-proved here. [Theorem C and surrounding discussion](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>), [complete-graph section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_complete.tex>).
- **The examples expose behavior beyond the complete graph.** The 4-cycle has an intermediate adjacent-pair phase, while the examples table includes community selection, changes of the selected community, and a finite-N path example whose phase order differs from its limiting order. The table explicitly distinguishes text/supplement proofs, Lean-only claims and numerical observations. [Examples section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_examples.tex>).
- **The scope boundaries are stated.** The local law is on compact subsets of the open face under (H); boundary uniformity and a general reducible-face sharp expansion remain open. General crossing uniqueness is not claimed. [Local-law section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>), [open questions](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_verify.tex>).
- **Some existing theory is already appropriately credited.** The introduction disclaims novelty of the exact complete-graph count law as a special Markov-multinomial law, discusses Markov-binomial multimodality, and distinguishes its question from occupation-measure LDPs. It also explicitly distinguishes the Guillin–Nectoux–Wu conditional-rate conjecture from C's finite-state variational identity. [Related work](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>).
- **Two particularly relevant references are absent from the checked bibliography:** Hervé–Ledoux on finite-state local-time density LLTs and Bouguet–Cloez on freezing-chain empirical measures. [Current bibliography](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/problem131_switching.tex>), [Hervé–Ledoux](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.5353), [Bouguet–Cloez](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.02121).

### Inferences

#### Editorial promise versus desk risk

The best central claim is that **the support of the single most likely count vector can change even while ordinary occupation-measure asymptotics do not resolve that competition**. A lattice cell's dimension penalty competes with survival cost at `T~log N`, and constants then resolve degeneracies. The complete graph and a small nonsymmetric example make this worth explaining to a general probabilist. This is the manuscript's most persuasive route to specialist review. Source for the claims being evaluated: [current introduction](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>).

The main desk risk is **a large proof apparatus for a fixed-dimensional finite-chain problem that an expert may regard as a standard spectral/saddlepoint consequence**. The convex-hull minimization is attractive but not, by itself, a large technical advance once facewise estimates are available. Likewise, the half-log-log correction comes from the Gaussian prefactor: its graph-independent form is useful, but its novelty should not be oversold independently of sharp constants, uniformity, global maximization and reducible counterexamples. The paper should make those genuinely demanding parts visible early. Sources for this assessment: [first-order section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>), [second-order section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).

The merged B material improves the general theory's payoff because it resolves the model where the leading rule is maximally degenerate. It also adds a second regime near a vertex, with Laguerre-polynomial asymptotics. That regime can remain in the merged paper, but should have a lower narrative priority than the support-selection mechanism. Otherwise the submission can read as a catalogue of formulas and crossing problems rather than one probability contribution. Sources: [complete-graph section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_complete.tex>), [vertex section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_vertex.tex>).

Lean verification, exact computer algebra and numerical checks can improve auditability. They do not establish significance, and a referee still needs a conventional route through the principal asymptotic proofs. For the graph catalogue, moving optional Lean-only rows to supplementary material or adding short mathematical explanations would reduce friction without questioning their formal validity. Source for the distinctions already made: [verification section](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_verify.tex>), [outcomes table and remark](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_examples.tex>).

#### Independent literature-gap checks, ranked by editorial importance

1. **Hervé–Ledoux is the first addition to make.** Loïc Hervé and James Ledoux, *A local limit theorem for densities of the additive component of a finite Markov additive process*, Statistics & Probability Letters 83 (2013), 2119–2128, DOI 10.1016/j.spl.2013.05.032. Theorem 2.2 is a quantitative density LLT; Proposition 3.1 applies to finite-jump-process local times and assumes irreducibility of the generator and its one-state-deleted subgenerators. The companion's Sections 5/6 check local-time assumptions and give compact-family uniformity. Both full HTML texts were accessible. This is not an automatic subsumption of C: compare C's graph hypotheses, killed faces, relative errors, explicit constants, discretization and global modes. Conversely, merely saying “our chain depends on N” does not dispose of their continuous-time benchmark. [Main full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.5353), [companion full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5644v2), [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167715213002009).

2. **Exact range-conditioned local-time densities are established prior art.** Brydges, van der Hofstad and König, Annals of Probability 35 (2007), 1307–1332, DOI 10.1214/009171906000001024, give a joint density for a fixed finite range and beyond-exponential-scale upper estimates.
