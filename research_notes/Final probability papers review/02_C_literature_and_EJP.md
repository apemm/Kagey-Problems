## 1. What is the contribution, and how close are the primary precedents?

### Takeaway

The strongest defensible positioning is a theorem about the **support of a global lattice mode**, obtained by comparing the maximum individual count probability on every admissible face. Occupation large deviations, local-time densities, spectral tilting, and tree expressions already have substantial precedents; the paper needs to explain the additional estimates that make the comparison across dimensions and boundaries valid. This judgment is based on the manuscript results and comparisons below, rather than a claim of exhaustive priority clearance. [Manuscript introduction](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>); [first-order theorem](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>); [local and maximum theorems](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).

#### Closest precedents, with reading depth and precise differences

1. **Hervé–Ledoux (2013): closest general local-limit precedent.** Read introduction, assumptions, Theorem 2.2, Remarks 2.2–2.3, Proposition 3.1, and Remark 3.1 (PDF pp. 1–6), plus relevant references; not the full Fourier proof. Theorem 2.2 gives an absolute Gaussian-density approximation, with `O(t^(-1/2))` plus a domain term. Remark 2.3 explicitly includes a uniform version over a compact family of irreducible aperiodic matrices under U1–U3. Proposition 3.1 treats local times when the generator and every one-state-deleted subgenerator are irreducible. Thus “they have no uniform theorem” would be wrong. C adds its relative killed-face estimate, discrete approximation, boundary exclusion for maxima, and comparison across supports. Its assumption (H) alone does not require irreducibility after deleting every state. [Primary paper, Theorem 2.2, Remark 2.3, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.5353); [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2013.05.032).

   **Interpretation:** the triangular matrices approaching the identity are not directly a compact family bounded within the irreducible class in their uniform remark. However, rescaling to a fixed continuous-time generator and applying a Doob transform is a natural route toward related estimates. Explain what C proves beyond that route; “killing” or “triangular array” alone is not an adequate novelty argument. [C's relative law and maximum proof](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).

2. **Brydges–van der Hofstad–König (2007): closest exact-range and pointwise-density precedent.** Read introduction, Theorem 2.1, Proposition 2.5, Proposition 3.1 and Remark 3.2, and opening of Section 3; skimmed formula derivations rather than auditing them. Theorem 2.1 already gives the exact joint local-time density on an event with **specified range R** and final state. Proposition 3.1 gives pointwise large-deviation upper bounds, beyond the exponential scale; Remark 3.2 simplifies them for symmetric generators. C must not imply that earlier density theory ignored faces or only provided coarse neighborhood probabilities. The incremental question is a global **discrete count mode** across all ranges, with the lattice-volume penalty and matching constants. [Primary published-text preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611525); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/009171906000001024).

3. **Dekking–Kong (2011): closest modal precedent.** Read introduction, Proposition 4.1, Propositions 4.2–4.3, and Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 1–2 and 7–12); sampled proofs of interior log-concavity. Proposition 4.3 gives log-concavity of the interior Markov-binomial probabilities; Theorem 4.1 classifies one-, two-, and three-peak shapes using endpoint and neighboring probabilities. This is real modal analysis and should be called that. It concerns two states, without C's multistate all-support variational rule or logarithmic crossing expansion. Their local peaks should not automatically be described as tied **global** maximizers. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.3613); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1239/jap/1324046011).

4. **Dupuis–Liu (2015): rate function is prior theory.** Read introductory scope, Section 3.1, Eq. (3.2), and Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 1–3 and 7–8); did not audit the LDP proof. Their explicit reversible pure-jump occupation rate yields C's reversible expression after the killing contribution is included. The complete-graph expression `d-(sum sqrt(alpha_i))^2` is consequently not a new rate function. An LDP for empirical measures supplies neither the `N^(-(|F|-1))` cell factor nor sharp comparisons of individual lattice maxima across dimensions. [Primary published-text preprint, Eq. (3.2), Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.6647); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-AOP883).

5. **Guillin–Nectoux–Wu (2024 preprint): closest conditioning-on-a-set comparison.** Read introduction and Section 2.2, including Theorem 2, Corollary 1, Eq. (2.15), and Corollary 2 (PDF pp. 1–8); not the proof sections. Their Theorem 2 concerns occupation measures **conditioned on survival in a prescribed set**, with rate minimized at the quasi-ergodic law. Corollary 2 identifies the reversible rate as the Dirichlet form minus the exit eigenvalue. They explicitly conjecture the general conditional-rate identity in (2.15). C's finite-matrix identity `inf I_F=lambda_F` should not be represented as solving that general conjecture. The draft's existing distinction is sound and can be shortened. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17216v1); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17216).

6. **Bertini–Gabrielli–Landim (2024), Landim (2023), Kim–Landim (published 2025): metastable rate-function expansions.** For BGL, read the publisher's abstract/metadata, not its full proof; for Landim, checked formal metadata and the comparison in Kim–Landim, without independently reading its full theorem; for Kim–Landim, read introduction and main-results Section 2, especially Theorem 2.3. That theorem is a Gamma-convergence statement for rescaled measure-current rate functionals. These works concern hierarchies of metastable dynamics/rate functionals as rates vary. They should not be portrayed as only fixed-rate results, but they do not supply C's all-face lattice-mode comparison. [BGL publisher issue, pp. 3820–3869](https://www.imstat.org/publications/aap/aap_34_4/aap_34_4.pdf); [Landim DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2023.09.001); [Kim–Landim primary theorem text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13515v2); [Kim–Landim publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2025.104733).

7. **Dietz–Sethuraman (2007) and Bouguet–Cloez (2018): rare switching, different time architecture.** Read Dietz–Sethuraman's introduction, setup and Theorems 1.3–1.5 (PDF pp. 1–7); read Bouguet–Cloez's introduction, setup and Theorem 2.9 (PDF pp. 1–8), not their proof sections. The former uses time-inhomogeneous `I+G/n^zeta`; at `zeta=1` Theorems 1.3–1.5 give a non-atomic full-simplex occupation limit, including Dirichlet examples. The latter studies freezing along a single trajectory, including nonstandard fluctuations. C fixes `I+(T_N/N)Q` throughout each row of length N. Matching the order of the number of switches does not make these models identical. [Dietz–Sethuraman primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701798); [Bouguet–Cloez primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.02121).

8. **Carugno–Vivo–Coghi (2022) and Huang–Kious–Sidoravicius–Tarrès (2018): exact combinatorial/tree formulas are prior art.** For CVC read introduction and the explanation around Eq. (30), with its large-n use in Eqs. (31)–(33); not the full derivation. Eq. (30) is exact at finite time and explicitly offers a route toward subleading asymptotics. For HKST read setup, Theorem 1, Remarks 1–2 and Proposition 2.1 (PDF pp. 1–4); skimmed the remaining proof. HKST assumes symmetric conductances and expresses joint local times, cycling numbers and last-exit tree through Bessel functions. These are important foundations, rather than the same global support-selection theorem. Avoid claiming that the appearance of trees itself is new. [CVC primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00582); [HKST primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06930).

9. **Stadje (1999) and a fresh 2025 check.** For Stadje only author-institution metadata and abstract scope were accessible; I did **not** independently verify C's attribution to his Theorems 3–4 or the assertion that all other transition rows stay fixed. The abstract does describe one asymptotically absorbing state with exit probabilities of order `1/N`; keep detailed theorem claims only after checking the full source. Shah's 2025 note was read through setup and Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 1–3): it gives an exact two-state visit-count formula, without an all-support or rare-switching global-mode theorem in the inspected material. This does not warrant expanding C's bibliography just to cite a recent formula. [Stadje institutional record](https://osnascholar.ub.uni-osnabrueck.de/handle/unios/6932); [Stadje DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmva.1999.1814); [Shah primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03073).

### Inferences

- **Why an LDP is insufficient:** its fixed-neighborhood probabilities do not compare individual lattice atoms belonging to supports of different dimensions. When `T=tau log N`, the occupation cost and the cell-volume cost are both polynomial in N. Passing from a central local density approximation to a maximum over a face also needs an upper bound that rules out near-boundary count vectors. These are the conceptual steps to foreground. [C, Theorems 3.3, 4.4, 4.6](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>); [C first-order rule](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>).
- **Convex hull versus substantive result:** the finite envelope construction is elementary once all the face maxima have the asserted asymptotics. Present it as a useful representation of the probabilistic theorem, rather than a new general convex-geometric principle. [C, Theorem 3.4](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>).
- **Recommended two-sentence novelty framing:**

  > For the homogeneous triangular family `P_N=I+(T/N)Q` with a fixed finite generator, we compare the largest individual occupation-count probabilities on every admissible support and show that, when `T=tau log N`, every global mode has a support minimizing `tau lambda_F+|F|-1`. For irreducible competing supports with positive initial mass, explicit prefactors locate their crossings through the `log log N` and constant terms; on complete graphs these comparisons distinguish a direct vertex-to-full-support jump from intermediate-support phases caused by a start law or a field of order `1/N` per visit.

  This is proposed manuscript language, grounded in the manuscript theorem statements, not a quote from another work. It deliberately makes no “first ever,” uniqueness, or all-N claim. [C first-order result](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>); [C crossings](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>); [C complete-graph consequences](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_complete.tex>).

#### Scope checklist, stated as mathematical limits

- The general model keeps `d,Q,mu` fixed. It does not cover `d` growing with N or arbitrary rates changing within a row. [C conventions](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_model.tex>).
- First order allows reducible admissible faces and arbitrary fixed start laws. The Gaussian prefactor and quasi-ergodic localization require irreducibility/positive initial mass; do not weaken the first-order result unnecessarily, or extend the sharp result to all reducible faces. [C Theorem 3.3](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_firstorder.tex>); [C Theorems 4.4, 4.6](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).
- Generic crossing roots exist and lie in an interval of length `O(1/log N)`; Theorem 4.7 does not show there is only one. Coincident envelope lines need a finer comparison. [C Theorems 4.7–4.8](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).
- The complete-graph one-jump statement is for sufficiently large N, fixed d and a logarithmic range. The draft supplies an `N=4,d=3` proper-face counterexample and no effective threshold. Equal limiting scores need not give exact finite-N coexistence. [C Corollary 5.6, Remark 5.7, Corollary 5.14](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_complete.tex>).
- A weak field here reweights paths by `exp(<xi,n>/N)` before normalization. It is not a theorem about every other interpretation of an external-field transition matrix. [C weak-field definition and proposition](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_secondorder.tex>).
- A local expansion on compact open-face subsets is different from a uniform expansion all the way to a lower-dimensional stratum. Likewise a mode of a linear projection adds masses of many count vectors. [C local-law and closing discussion](<../../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_verify.tex>).
