A Markov chain on $d$ states runs for $N$ steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$. On each face whose states communicate within it and carry start mass, we prove a local limit theorem for the occupation counts with an explicit constant and relative error $O(1/T)$, uniformly for $1\le T\le\varepsilon N$ with $\varepsilon\max_iq_i<1$ and on compact subsets of the open face. Up to $O(1)$, $-\log P_N(n)$ is $|F|-1$ times $\log(N/\sqrt T)$ plus $N$ times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the chain $I+(T/N)Q$ at $n/N$. Every admissible face has an explicit power of $T$ in its largest probability for large $T\le N^{1/3}$, and a constant given by a finite-dimensional variational formula. For $T=\tau\log N$ the face of the mode minimizes $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$ at first order, with $\lambda_F$ the exit rate. If two admissible faces differ by $\Delta\ge1$ states and their exit rates by $\delta>0$, we locate every crossing of their largest probabilities there up to $O(1/\log N)$. For faces whose states communicate, the crossing is unique for $T\le\varepsilon N$, with coefficient $1/2$ of $\log\log N$. Near a facet, the continuous-time occupation density is the facet density times an explicit kernel, with relative error $O(1/T)$ at times $x\le cT$ off the facet, with $c$ small. The counts obey a lattice form with extra error $T^2/N$. The kernel is Bessel for $x=o(T)$ at the facet profiles where the entry and exit laws of the removed state agree. At a profile where the rate has a first correction, of order $m\ge3$, it is Bessel if and only if $x=o(T^{1-2/m})$. On the complete graph with the uniform start, each face crosses a vertex exactly once.
