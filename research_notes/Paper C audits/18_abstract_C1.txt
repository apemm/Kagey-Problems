A Markov chain on $d$ states runs for $N$ steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$, so it makes about $T$ switches. We describe the law of its occupation counts. On each face we prove a local limit theorem with an explicit constant, uniformly for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$, with relative error $O(1/T+T^2/N)$. Its exponent is one power of $N$ per dimension plus $T$ times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the whole chain, which on the face is that of the killed chain. For $T=\tau\log N$ the face of the mode minimizes $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$ at first order, where $\lambda_F$ is the exit rate. We locate each crossing of two irreducible faces up to $O(1/\log N)$, with a coefficient $1/2$ of $\log\log N$ for every chain. If just two sizes of face compete at each switch, this locates every mode change. Near an irreducible facet with start mass, the law on an irreducible face is the facet law times an explicit kernel, with relative error $O(1/T)$ while the time $x$ off the facet is below a small multiple of $T$. For reversible chains the kernel is Bessel while $x=o(T)$. Otherwise the Bessel form holds in logarithmic form, and in relative form for $x=o(T^{1/3})$, but for a cycle on three states not when $x/T^{1/3}\to\infty$. On the complete graph each face crosses a vertex exactly once at every $N$, the most likely counts on a face are balanced for $T\le N/d$, and the mode changes exactly once for large $N$ and $T\le C\log N$.
