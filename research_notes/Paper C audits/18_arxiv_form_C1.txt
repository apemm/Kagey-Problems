Abstract (the abstract of the paper after the proofreading fixes of audit 35 of October 25, 2026, 1701 characters and 263 words with each formula counted as one word, math in dollar signs. 18_abstract_C1.txt holds the same text. The journal abstract is the same without its last sentence, 248 words):
A Markov chain with exit rates $q_i$ runs for $N$ steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$. On each irreducible face that carries start mass, we prove a local limit theorem for the occupation counts with an explicit constant and relative error $O(1/T)$, uniformly for $1\le T\le\varepsilon N$ with $\varepsilon\max_iq_i<1$ and on compact subsets of the open face. Up to $O(1)$, minus the log-probability of counts $n$ with support $F$ is $|F|-1$ times $\log(N/\sqrt T)$ plus $N$ times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of $I+(T/N)Q$ at $n/N$. Every admissible face has an explicit power of $T$ in its largest probability for large $T\le N^{1/3}$, and a constant given by a finite-dimensional variational formula. For $T=\tau\log N$ the face of the mode minimizes $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$ at first order, with $\lambda_F$ the exit rate. If an admissible face has $\Delta\ge1$ more states than another and an exit rate smaller by $\delta>0$, we locate every crossing of their largest probabilities for large $T\le N^{1/3}$ up to $O(1/\log N)$. For two irreducible faces the crossing is unique for large $N$ in $1\le T\le\varepsilon N$, with coefficient $1/2$ of $\log\log N$. Near a facet, the continuous-time occupation density is the facet density times an explicit kernel, with relative error $O(1/T)$ at times $x\le cT$ off the facet, $c$ small. The counts obey a lattice form with extra error $T^2/N$ for $T\le\varepsilon_1\sqrt N$. The kernel is Bessel for $x=o(T)$ at facet profiles where the removed state has equal entry and exit laws, and at a profile where the rate has a first correction, of order $m\ge3$, if and only if $x=o(T^{1-2/m})$. On the complete graph with the uniform start, each face of at least 2 states crosses a vertex exactly once.
