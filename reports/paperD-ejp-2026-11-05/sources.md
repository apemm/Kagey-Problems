The new local three-halves law follows from the density's certified derivative

## Recent EJP presentation choices

The closest comparison is the GLRS article above. Its opening question and
conditional unimodality result are immediately identifiable. This supports
putting the conjecture and answer ahead of mutation approximations in Paper D.

Jacek Wszoła's [First passage locations for two-dimensional lattice random walks
and the bell-shape](https://doi.org/10.1214/26-EJP1498), EJP 31 (2026), paper 44,
is another recent shape paper. The [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14393)
was read for its introduction, main theorems and proof organization. It presents
the structural shape claim before developing the auxiliary analytic classes.
The methodological analogy is limited: its rational-function and convolution
arguments concern different laws. Paper D follows that presentation choice,
not those hypotheses or conclusions. Its theorem-first organization and a
separate proof-architecture section help explain why a long proof is needed.

These comparisons inform an editorial judgment. They do not establish that a
59-page paper will be accepted or that its length is immaterial.

## Current EJP instructions

The [official submission page](https://imstat.org/journals-and-publications/electronic-journal-of-probability/electronic-journal-of-probability-manuscript-submission/)
requires LaTeX using the EJP template and PDF submission through EJMS. It allows
potential-referee suggestions. The [production page](https://imstat.org/journals-and-publications/electronic-journal-of-probability/electronic-journal-of-probability-preparation-of-manuscripts/)
asks for the class and source after acceptance, favors an inline bibliography,
and says to check overfull equations and figures. These official pages were
available through indexed text; direct retrieval was intermittently blocked.

The [journal overview](https://imstat.org/journals-and-publications/electronic-journal-of-probability/)
describes full-length probability articles, directs papers shorter than twelve
pages to ECP, and says author/publication fees are not required. No maximum
length or numerical abstract cap was found on the official pages checked.
The current [template repository](https://vtex-soft.github.io/texsupport.ims-ejpecp/)
provided the class used here. Older cached sample files and conflicting
third-party fee summaries were not treated as current journal policy.

## Unresolved access

Drezner–Farnum's complete 1993 paper remains the main historical-access gap.
Heyde's 2004 full text was also unavailable in the earlier audit; its general
limit-regime description is supported by the publisher/author abstract and
the later full-text comparison. These limitations prevent claiming that all
historical literature has been independently read in full.
