## Exact-support competition gives the merger a persuasive central question

For transition matrix I+(T/N)Q and T=τ log N, the first-order competition minimizes τλ_F+|F|−1 over admissible supports. The exit cost competes with the number of lattice compositions sharing a face's mass. On an irreducible face with positive initial mass, the sharp maximum has an explicit constant multiplying N^{−(k−1)}T^{(k−1)/2}exp(−Tλ_F). This turns an intuitive survival-versus-spreading picture into quantitative support selection. The complete graph makes the B material integral to C: all sizes tie at first order, so the explicit constants decide the winner. Starts and weak fields then demonstrate how intermediate supports can become optimal. ([Main theorem presentation](<../kagey-problems/problem131/paperC/sec_intro.tex>))

**The defensible novelty claim is this combination of exact-support optimization, sharp constants, lattice approximation and tie resolution.** Occupation-density local limits, Markov-additive sharp asymptotics and dependent-count modes already have substantial precedents. Hervé–Ledoux supplies a particularly relevant finite-state density LLT benchmark, including local-time applications with irreducibility assumptions on deleted subgenerators. Bouguet–Cloez studies freezing chains whose rates change during a trajectory; C instead fixes the rate within each N-step trajectory and varies it with N. Both comparisons are now included. Iltis is also added, appropriately restricted to the existence of earlier multivariate sharp asymptotics: the research accessed publisher abstract/snippets, so a detailed theorem-level separation is not established. ([Hervé–Ledoux](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.5353), [uniformity companion](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5644v2), [Bouguet–Cloez](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.02121), [Iltis publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167715299001819))

The editorial vulnerability is that a specialist could view much of the machinery as a lengthy application of familiar methods. Emphasize the global maximization and boundary control, rather than treating the convex-hull picture or Gaussian half-log-log correction alone as the principal advance. EJP has published both quantitative finite-chain local-limit work and freezing-chain occupation results, establishing subject fit without settling C's significance. The bounded search found no matching general support-selection theorem; that is not a priority certificate. ([Chiclana–Peres, EJP 2024](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-EJP1149), [Bouguet–Cloez, EJP 2018](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-EJP130))

## The audited proof chain addresses the difficult local-to-global step

The strongest mathematical reassurance is that **the face maximum is not inferred from an interior local limit alone**. The proof excludes compositions outside a fixed interior neighborhood using an exponential rate gap and finitely many tilts, then locates maximizers through quadratic bounds and a nearby lattice point. The discrete–continuum comparison controls run-count moments rather than assuming typical paths have order T runs. The audit also checked the admissibility/SCC argument for arbitrary starts, the covariance in the correct deleted-coordinate representation, and exclusion of competing torus saddles for directed or periodic graphs. These arguments should remain visible through any further shortening. ([Mathematical review and coverage ledger](<../research_notes/Paper C merged EJP review/02_mathematical_review.md>))

The next-order coefficient c_F=c(α_F*)+½γ_FᵀΣ_Fγ_F was checked through completion of the square. An independent symbolic calculation with arbitrary k−1 confirmed the complete-face value **c_F=−(k−1)/8**; it was not merely a numerical sweep over small sizes. The shortened weak-field proof was reread and approved: compact field bounds preserve exterior exclusion, and the amplitude tilt supplies the stated location shift. Kim–Landim now pins v2, the version containing the cited Lemma 5.7. ([Coefficient and field audit](<../research_notes/Paper C merged EJP review/02_mathematical_review.md>), [Kim–Landim v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13515v2#S5))

Completed corrections include the approximate-saddle rounding explanation, the condition resolving c_F/T against discretization error, the zero-generator case, irreducibility/start qualifications, and the distinction between identical envelope lines and actual face changes. Field hypotheses, normalization constants, Appendix D domains, crossing coordinates and historical B boundary wording are also corrected. **These are completed revisions, not outstanding defects.** ([Integration record](<../research_notes/Paper C merged EJP review/05_integration.md>), [notation changes](<../research_notes/Paper C merged EJP review/03_notation_edits.md>))
