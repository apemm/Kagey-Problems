# Literature for the general version of Problem 137

Date 2026-10-02. Setup as in `ledger.md`: f(x) = x + 1, g_m(x) = -1/(m x), root 0, X = sqrt(m) x,
lambda_q = 2 cos(pi/q). In X coordinates the maps are X -> X + sqrt(m) and X -> -1/X, so the group is
Hecke's G(lambda) with lambda = sqrt(m). For m = 1, 2, 3 this is lambda_q with q = 3, 4, 6.

Access levels used below.
- FULL: we read the source itself (PDF or full text).
- PARTIAL: we read the abstract, the introduction, or the theorem statements only.
- REVIEW: we read a zbMATH or JFM review, not the paper.
- SECONDARY: we could not open the paper. Its content is reported only through the named sources,
  which we did read.

No computation was run for this file. Nothing here changes the ledger.

## 1. Primary sources on Hecke groups

### Hecke 1936
E. Hecke, "Über die Bestimmung Dirichletscher Reihen durch ihre Funktionalgleichung",
Math. Ann. 112 (1936), 664-699. DOI 10.1007/BF01565437. JFM 63.0264.03, Zbl 0014.01601.
- Access: REVIEW (full JFM review read). The paper itself was not opened.
- What it proves (from the JFM review): the group G(lambda) generated by tau -> tau + lambda and
  tau -> -1/tau is of Schottky type (infinite covolume) for lambda > 2 and a Fuchsian group of the
  first kind ("Grenzkreisgruppe") otherwise. G(2) is the theta group. For 0 < lambda < 2 the discrete
  case is lambda = 2 cos(pi/q), q >= 3. Satz 6 (p. 689) gives dimensions of the form spaces.
- Bearing: the source for "G(lambda_q) is discrete" and "G(2) is the theta group". The free product
  structure C_2 * C_q is not stated in the review. We cite it from the secondary sources below.

### Free product structure C_2 * C_q (secondary statements we read)
- Short and Walker (Section 2 below), p. 1: "The generators sigma and tau satisfy the relations
  sigma^2 = (tau sigma)^q = I ... and all other relations in sigma and tau are consequences of these
  two. It follows that G_q is isomorphic as a group to the free product C_2 * C_q." FULL (arXiv).
- Mayer, Mühlenbruch, Strömberg, "The transfer operator for the Hecke triangle groups",
  arXiv:0912.2236 (published in Discrete Contin. Dyn. Syst. per the arXiv record, not checked),
  Section 2.1: "The only relations in the group G_q are given by S^2 = (S T_q)^q = 1." PARTIAL.
- Winsor, arXiv:2605.30064 (Section 2 below), p. 4: the image of G_q in PSL_2(R) is C_2 * C_q with
  presentation <S, U_q | S^2 = U_q^q = I>, U_q = S T_q, and unique reduced words in S, U
  (citing Magnus, Karrass, Solitar, Theorem 4.1). FULL.
- Bearing: confirms the vision file's claim that x -> x + sqrt(m), x -> -1/x generate C_2 * C_q with
  q = 3, 4, 6 for m = 1, 2, 3, and that g f (= U up to the conjugation and direction of composition)
  has order q. Route 2 of the vision file (normal forms in C_2 * C_q) can cite Magnus, Karrass,
  Solitar for uniqueness of reduced words.

### Rosen 1954
D. Rosen, "A class of continued fractions associated with certain properly discontinuous groups",
Duke Math. J. 21 (1954), no. 3, 549-563. DOI 10.1215/S0012-7094-54-02154-7. Zbl 0056.30703.
- Access: SECONDARY. Paywalled on Project Euclid. zbMATH has no review text.
- What it proves, as reported by sources we read:
  - Every real number has a lambda_q-continued fraction x = r_0 lambda + e_1/(r_1 lambda + ...) from
    a nearest-multiple algorithm, and the cusps G_q(infinity) are exactly the numbers with a finite
    expansion (Winsor p. 9; Hanson et al. zbMATH summary; Mayer et al.).
  - A "reduced" expansion is unique, defined on p. 555 by restricting the blocks
    B(s) = (lambda, -1/lambda, ..., -1/lambda) (Rosen and Schmidt 1992, Section 1.2).
  - Corollary 1: for even q every matrix of G_q has the form [[a, b lambda], [c lambda, d]] or
    [[a lambda, b], [c, d lambda]] with a, b, c, d in Z[lambda^2] (Winsor p. 7).
  - lambda/2 is a cusp (Winsor p. 9).
- Bearing: our reduced words are finite Rosen fractions with all digits c_i >= 1 (c_1 >= 0) and all
  signs minus. Rosen's uniqueness is for a different normal form (nearest multiple, signed digits).
  It does not give our rank formula or our run rule. Corollary 1 with Z[lambda^2] = Z for q = 4, 6 gives
  the matrix form used in Section 3 below.

### Leutbecher 1967
A. Leutbecher, "Über die Heckeschen Gruppen G(lambda)", Abh. Math. Semin. Univ. Hamb. 31 (1967),
199-205. DOI 10.1007/BF02992399. Zbl 0161.27601.
- Access: SECONDARY (through the introduction of Leutbecher 1974, which we read).
- What it proves: Rosen's conjecture that the cusps of G(lambda_5) are infinity and all of Q(sqrt 5).
- Bearing: only for q = 5 (lambda_5 is the golden ratio). Relevant to the vision file's "q = 5 and
  beyond" paragraph: for q = 5 the group orbit of 0 is all of Q(sqrt 5).

### Leutbecher 1974
A. Leutbecher, "Über die Heckeschen Gruppen G(lambda). II", Math. Ann. 211 (1974), 63-86.
DOI 10.1007/BF01344143. Zbl 0292.10020.
- Access: PARTIAL. Scanned PDF from GDZ (PPN235181684_0211, LOG_0020). We read the introduction and
  Satz 1 (pp. 63-64).
- What it states and proves:
  - Introduction: G(lambda) has a fundamental domain of finite area exactly when lambda = 2 or
    lambda = lambda_q. The four cases lambda = 1, sqrt 2, sqrt 3, 2 are commensurable with PSL_2(Z).
    Borho and Rosenberger showed that G(lambda_q) has all of Q(lambda_q) as cusps at most when
    q = 2^n + 1, and then the class number of Q(lambda_q) is 1.
  - Satz 1: congruence subgroups of SL_2 over the maximal order of Q(x) containing [[1,1],[0,1]] and
    [[1,0],[x,1]].
  - Satz 2 (stated in the introduction): the cusp set of G(lambda_q) is lambda_q Q(lambda_q^2) together
    with infinity when lambda_q^2 has degree 2 over Q, that is q = 5, 8, 10, 12.
- Bearing: q = 4, 6 are not covered by Satz 2 because lambda_q^2 = 2, 3 is rational. Those cases are
  the commensurable ones and are handled in Section 3 below.

### Rosen and Schmidt 1992
D. Rosen and T. A. Schmidt, "Hecke groups and continued fractions", Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 46
(1992), 459-474. DOI 10.1017/S0004972700012120.
- Access: FULL (Cambridge PDF).
- What it proves: periodic lambda-fractions of sqrt(D)/C, Pell equations over Q(lambda_q), a
  Legendre-type constant. Section 1.2 recalls Rosen's reduced form. The introduction says there is
  "a paucity of cases in which one knows the exact cusp set of a given G_q".
- Bearing: background only.

### Burton, Kraaikamp, Schmidt 2000
R. M. Burton, C. Kraaikamp, T. A. Schmidt, "Natural extensions for the Rosen fractions",
Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 352 (2000), no. 3, 1277-1298. DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-99-02442-3.
Zbl 0938.11036.
- Access: PARTIAL (zbMATH review read in full, theorem statements of the AMS PDF scanned).
- What it proves: planar natural extensions of the Rosen interval maps f_q on [-lambda/2, lambda/2)
  (Theorem 3.1 for even q, Theorem 3.2 for odd q), invariant measures, ergodicity, and Diophantine
  results (Theorem 4.1, Haas-Series and Lehner Hurwitz constants, Theorems 4.2 to 4.6).
- Bearing: metric theory of the nearest-multiple Rosen map. Nothing on word length or counting.

## 2. Continued fractions, paths, and words in Hecke groups

### Short and Walker 2016
I. Short and M. Walker, "Geodesic Rosen continued fractions", Q. J. Math. 67 (2016), no. 4, 519-549.
DOI 10.1093/qmath/haw025. Zbl 1416.11126. arXiv:1310.1585.
- Access: FULL (arXiv v2).
- What it proves: Rosen fractions [b_1, ..., b_n]_q = b_1 lambda - 1/(b_2 lambda - ...) correspond to
  paths from infinity in the Farey graph F_q (Theorem 2.1). The nearest-integer expansion is
  geodesic, that is uses the fewest terms (Theorem 1.1). At most F_n geodesic expansions (Theorem 1.2).
  Theorem 1.3 (q = 2r): [b_1, ..., b_n]_q is geodesic iff b_2, ..., b_n has no 0, no block
  +-1^[r], and no block +-(1^[r-1], 2, 1^[r-2], 2, ..., 2, 1^[r-1]). Theorem 7.1 is the odd case.
  Lemma 7.3: T_1^r = sigma tau^{-1} sigma T_{-1}^{r-2} tau^{-1} for q = 2r, where T_b = tau^b sigma.
- Bearing: this is the closest source. In X coordinates our word f^{c_1} g ... g f^{c_k}(0) is the
  Rosen fraction [c_1, ..., c_k]_q with all digits positive. Short and Walker measure length by the
  number of terms n. Our rank weights each term by c_i + 1 and allows only forward moves, so it is a
  different metric. The forbidden blocks differ. For q = 4 (r = 2) both forbid "1, 1", but they also
  forbid (1, 2, ..., 2, 1), which our rule allows. For q = 6 they forbid 1^[3], we forbid 1^[4].
  Their Lemma 7.3 is the same relation (tau sigma)^q = I that gives our run rule, used with signed
  digits. Their Theorem 10.1 (q = infinity, even-integer fractions, the Farey graph is a tree) is the
  analogue of our free case.

### Hanson, Merberg, Towse, Yudovina 2008
E. Hanson, A. Merberg, C. Towse, E. Yudovina, "Generalized continued fractions and orbits under the
action of Hecke triangle groups", Acta Arith. 134 (2008), no. 4, 337-348. DOI 10.4064/aa134-4-4.
Zbl 1219.11105.
- Access: REVIEW (zbMATH summary).
- What it proves: the orbit of 0 corresponds to finite lambda-fractions. For even q there are
  infinitely many G_q-orbits in Q(lambda). For odd q, lower bounds via reduction mod 2.
- Bearing: for q = 4, 6 the orbit of 0 inside Q(lambda) = Q(sqrt 2), Q(sqrt 3) is not everything.
  This does not conflict with Section 3, since the orbit is lambda Q, a proper subset of Q(lambda).

### Janvresse, Rittaud, de la Rue 2011 (arXiv)
E. Janvresse, B. Rittaud, T. de la Rue, "Dynamics of lambda-continued fractions and beta-shifts",
arXiv:1103.6181 (journal version not checked).
- Access: FULL (arXiv v1).
- What it proves: for 0 < lambda < 2, a map T_lambda on [0, infinity) coding positive "minus"
  fractions [[b_1, ..., b_l]]_lambda = 1/(b_1 lambda - 1/(b_2 lambda - ...)) with b_i >= 1, conjugate
  to a beta-shift (Theorem 4.6). Lemma 3.9 writes endpoints as blocks (e + 2, 1, ..., 1).
  Proposition 2.6: i_lambda = k - 2 for lambda in (lambda_{k-1}, lambda_k]. For lambda = lambda_k,
  beta = k - 1. Their earlier paper (Ann. IHP 46 (2010), 135-158, not opened) builds "generalized
  Stern-Brocot intervals" for lambda_k whose level-n partitions match base (k - 1) expansions.
- Bearing: these are exactly the backward, all-minus, positive-digit lambda-fractions of the vision
  file. Their depth counts digits, not our weight sum c_i + k - 1, so their counts ((k - 1)^n) are not
  ours. Good to cite for the "backward Rosen fraction" form.

### Taha 2019 (arXiv)
D. Taha, "On cross sections to the geodesic and horocycle flows on quotients of SL(2,R) by Hecke
triangle groups G_q", arXiv:1906.07250, and "The Boca-Cobeli-Zaharescu map analogue for the Hecke
triangle groups G_q", arXiv:1810.10668 (the second not opened).
- Access: PARTIAL (first paper, introduction and Sections 1 to 4).
- What it proves: a G_q-Stern-Brocot process for the "visible lattice points" Lambda_q = G_q (1,0)^T,
  and cross sections for the geodesic and horocycle flows.
- Bearing: the only "Hecke Stern-Brocot" construction we found. It inserts mediant-type vectors.
  It does not count by word length in x -> x + lambda, x -> -1/x.

### Winsor 2026 (arXiv)
K. Winsor, "Hecke triangle groups and special hyperbolic elements", arXiv:2605.30064.
- Access: FULL.
- What it states: G_q(infinity) is contained in lambda_q Q(lambda_q^2) together with infinity
  (equation (2)). If q is in {3, 4, 6} then "G_q is commensurable with SL(2,Z) and it is easy to show
  that the containment in (2) is an equality". For q in {5, 8, 10, 12} equality holds by Leutbecher
  and McMullen. For q >= 7 with K_q of degree >= 3 the question is "wide open".
- Bearing: confirms the vision file's table for the group orbit. No proof is given there for
  q = 4, 6 (see Section 3).

### Fairchild and Hanusa 2026 (arXiv)
S. Fairchild and C. R. H. Hanusa, "Densities of arithmetic Hecke triangle group orbits",
arXiv:2607.22142.
- Access: FULL.
- What it proves: Proposition 2 (citing C. L. Lang and M. L. Lang, J. Algebra 460 (2016), 392-417,
  Remark 3.9, not opened): for q = 4, 6 the orbit Gamma_q e_1 is the set of (a, b lambda) with
  gcd(a, b) = 1 and lambda^2 not dividing a, together with (a lambda, b) with gcd(a, b) = 1 and
  lambda^2 not dividing b. Theorem 1: the densities 4 sqrt 2 / pi^2 and 3 sqrt 3 / pi^2.
  It also cites Takeuchi 1977 for "q = 3, 4, 6 are the only arithmetic Hecke triangle groups".
- Bearing: Proposition 2 gives the cusp set lambda Q at once, since every primitive pair occurs.

### Im and Lee 2022 (arXiv)
B.-H. Im and W. Lee, "Non-holomorphic Eisenstein series for certain Fuchsian groups and class
numbers", arXiv:2207.05325 v1 (publication status not checked).
- Access: FULL for Sections 1 and 2.
- What it proves: R(N) = R(N)^+ union R(N)^-, with R(N)^+ = {[[a, b sqrt N], [c sqrt N, d]]} and
  R(N)^- = {[[a sqrt N, b], [c, d sqrt N]]}, a, b, c, d in Z, determinant 1. Lemma 2.1 gives
  generators. After Lemma 2.1: "the Hecke triangle groups Gamma(2,4,infinity) and Gamma(2,6,infinity)
  coincide with groups R(2) and R(3)". Introduction: for N = 2, 3 the Atkin-Lehner group Gamma_0^+(N)
  is the Fricke group, conjugate to the Hecke triangle groups of type (2, 2N, infinity), genus 0.
  Proposition 2.8: every cusp of R(p) is equivalent to infinity (one cusp). The elliptic points for
  m = 4, 6 are i and (1 + i)/sqrt 2, resp. i and (sqrt 3 + i)/2.
- Bearing: settles the vision file's "from memory" claim. See Section 3 for the exact form in our
  x coordinates.

### Mayer, Mühlenbruch, Strömberg (arXiv:0912.2236), cusp and theta group facts
- Access: PARTIAL. p. 29: "the group Gamma_theta has two cusps whereas all the Hecke triangle groups
  have only one cusp", and Gamma_theta (z -> -1/z, z -> z + 2) "is conjugate to the Hecke congruence
  subgroup Gamma_0(2)". Abstract: G_q is non-arithmetic for q not in {3, 4, 6}.
- Bearing: the m = 4 row. Our m = 4 tree lives in G(2), the theta group, with two cusp classes, so
  the group orbit of 0 cannot be all of Q there. This matches G10 (the parent map cycles at 1/2).

## 3. The group orbit for m = 2, 3, in x coordinates (what the paper can say)

Conjugating Im and Lee's R(m) by X = sqrt(m) x sends R(m)^+ to the matrices [[a, b], [m c, d]] with
ad - mbc = 1, which is Gamma_0(m), and R(m)^- to [[m a, b], [m c, m d]] with determinant m, which is
the coset Gamma_0(m) W_m of the Fricke involution. Our f is [[1,1],[0,1]] in Gamma_0(m) and our g_m is
x -> -1/(m x), the Fricke involution itself. So for m = 2, 3:

  <f, g_m> = Gamma_0(m)^+ (the Fricke group), acting on x, exactly and not only up to conjugacy.

This uses Im and Lee's statement that R(2), R(3) are the Hecke groups. We did this short
verification by hand. It is not written in any source we read in this form.

The group orbit of 0 is all of Q. A direct argument the paper can print: let x = a/c in lowest terms.
If m does not divide c, pick integers b, d with m a d - b c = 1 (possible since gcd(m a, c) = 1); then
the R^- coset matrix sends infinity to x. If c = m c', pick b, d with a d - m b c' = 1 (possible since
gcd(a, m c') = 1); then [[a, b], [m c', d]] in Gamma_0(m) sends infinity to x. Since g_m(infinity) = 0,
the orbit of 0 equals the orbit of infinity. The same follows from Fairchild and Hanusa,
Proposition 2. Status: proved (elementary, by hand), consistent with Winsor's remark.

What no source gives: that the forward monoid (f and g only, no f^{-1}) reaches every rational from 0.
That is the analogue of Theorem 3.6 and stays our own result (computer-checked as G7, proof needed).

## 4. Growth series of free products

- Paris and Varghese, "The growth series of Dyer groups", arXiv:2305.09796, p. 5. FULL for Section 2.
  States, citing A. Mann, How Groups Grow (LMS Lecture Note Ser. 395, Cambridge 2012, not opened):
  1/G_{G*H, S_G union S_H}(t) = 1/G_{G,S_G}(t) + 1/G_{H,S_H}(t) - 1. Also Lewin (Comm. Algebra 19
  (1991), 2405-2418, not opened) for amalgams.
- Fujii and Sakasai, arXiv:2511.22817. PARTIAL. Growth series of <x_1, ..., x_n | x_1^{p_1} = ... =
  x_n^{p_n}> with symmetric generators. Cites Gill (1999), Edjvet and Johnson (1992), Shapiro (1994)
  and Johnson, Kim, Song (1995) for the trefoil group G(2,3).
- Bearing: the free product formula needs a generating set that is a union of generating sets of the
  factors, for example {g} and {u, u^{-1}} with u = g f of order q. Our generating set is {g, f} with
  f = g u of infinite order, used only forward, and we count points (cosets of Stab(0) = <g f g>),
  not group elements. So the formula does not apply directly. We found no source that counts forward
  words, or orbit points, of a Hecke group by word length in {x + lambda, -1/x}. The Fibonacci count
  for m >= 4 is elementary (words with no g g that start with f), and Section 5 has a published
  relative.

## 5. Trees of rationals generated by x + 1 and an involution

### Kimberling and Moses 2014
C. Kimberling and P. J. C. Moses, "The infinite Fibonacci tree and other trees generated by rules",
Fibonacci Quart. 52 (2014), no. 5, 136-150. DOI 10.1080/00150517.2014.12427864. Zbl 1396.11036.
- Access: FULL (PDF from the Fibonacci Quarterly site).
- What it contains:
  - Theorem 3.2: root 1, rules x -> x + k and x -> k/x. If k = 1 then |g(n)| = F(n), and if k > 1
    then |g(n)| = F(n+1). Proved (short induction).
  - Theorem 3.3: root 1, x -> x + 1 and x -> k/x give every positive rational. The remark before it
    says the generation sizes "appear to be not linearly recurrent" for k > 1.
  - Example 4.1: x -> x + 1 and x -> -1/(x + 1) from 0, |g(n+1)| = 2 F(n).
  - Example 4.2: root 1, x -> x + 1 and x -> -1/x (the root-1 version of Problem 137, OEIS A226130).
    Sizes 1, 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 22, ... and the claim |g(n)| = |g(n-1)| + |g(n-3)| for n >= 7,
    stated with a one-line reason about S(n, i), not a full proof.
  - Example 4.4: x -> x + 1 and x -> -m/x (m >= 2): "S contains every rational number", no proof.
- Bearing, three points.
  1. Example 4.2 predates Kagey's 2025 question and asserts the q = 3 recurrence for the root-1 tree.
     The existing problem137.tex does not cite it. It should be cited in both papers (this file does
     not edit problem137.tex).
  2. The vision file's "x -> x + 1 and x -> 1/x gives Fibonacci, up to a correction at the root" is
     Theorem 3.2 with k = 1 (root 1). It is already proved there.
  3. Example 4.4 is a different family. With X = x / sqrt(m) the maps are X -> X + 1/sqrt(m),
     X -> -1/X, so lambda = 1/sqrt(m) < 1 and the group is not discrete. (Check by hand: (-m/x)
     composed with x + 1 is [[0, -m], [1, 1]], trace^2/det = 1/m. A finite-order elliptic has
     trace^2/det = 2 + 2 cos(2 pi k/q), and if that is rational it is an integer in {0, ..., 4} by
     Niven's theorem. 1/m is not an integer for m >= 2, so the composite is elliptic of infinite
     order and the group is not discrete.) It is not our -1/(m x). Their Theorem
     3.2 for k = 2, 3 with x -> k/x (an orientation-reversing involution, positive rationals only) is
     also outside our family.

## 6. Answers to the four questions in the task

1. Do x -> x + sqrt(m), x -> -1/x generate H_q for m = 1, 2, 3 with q = 3, 4, 6? Yes, by definition
   of Hecke's G(lambda) with lambda = 2 cos(pi/q) = 1, sqrt 2, sqrt 3 (Hecke 1936 via JFM review,
   Leutbecher 1974 introduction). The isomorphism with C_2 * C_q is stated in Short and Walker,
   Mayer et al., Winsor. In x coordinates the group is the Fricke group Gamma_0(m)^+ for m = 2, 3
   (Im and Lee plus the conjugation in Section 3). Each has one cusp (Im and Lee, Prop. 2.8; Mayer et
   al. p. 29). For m = 4 the group is the theta group, conjugate to Gamma_0(2), with two cusps.
   For m >= 5 it is of Schottky type (Hecke via JFM).
2. Is the orbit of 0 all of Q for m = 2, 3? For the group, yes. Winsor states it, Fairchild and
   Hanusa's Proposition 2 implies it, and Section 3 has a three-line proof. For the forward tree
   (f and g only) no source says so. This is new and is our G7 (computer-checked, proof needed).
3. Is the growth of words in C_2 * C_q with this generating set known? Not in what we found. The
   standard free product formula covers generating sets that split over the factors. {g, f} does not,
   and we count points of a forward orbit, not group elements. Short and Walker count terms of Rosen
   fractions (a different length). Janvresse, Rittaud, de la Rue count digits of positive minus
   fractions (also different).
4. Has anyone counted the tree ranks for m = 2 or 3? We found no such count in the OEIS, zbMATH,
   arXiv or by web search. The nearest published counts are Kimberling and Moses 2014 (q = 3, root 1,
   and Fibonacci trees with x + k, k/x).

## 7. What is known and what would be new

Known (with sources above):
- The groups, their free product structure, discreteness, one cusp for m = 2, 3, the Fricke group
  identification, and that the group orbit of 0 is Q for m = 2, 3 (proper subset for m = 4).
- Rosen fractions with signed digits, their nearest-multiple normal form, geodesics in the Farey
  graph F_q counted by number of terms (Short and Walker), positive minus fractions as a beta-shift
  (Janvresse, Rittaud, de la Rue).
- The q = 3 recurrence for the root-1 tree, asserted without full proof (Kimberling and Moses,
  Example 4.2). Fibonacci counts for x + k and k/x (Kimberling and Moses, Theorem 3.2, proved).
- 1/D_4 is A060961, and psi_4 is A293506.

New, as far as we can tell:
- The rank counts a_2(n), a_3(n) and their generating functions N_q/D_q, with denominator
  1 - t - t^3 - ... - t^{2q-3} depending only on q.
- The run rule (no q - 2 consecutive interior 1's, with the stated end exception) as the
  characterization of shortest forward words, and the rank formula d(x) = c_1 + ... + c_k + k - 1.
- Reachability of all of Q by forward moves for m = 2, 3 (the analogue of Theorem 3.6).
- The colors statement (reached by g iff negative) for m = 2, 3.
- The counting identity N_q/D_q for the reduced-word language for every q >= 3 (already proved in
  checks_report.md), and the q-independence of the count.

Could not access: Rosen 1954 (paywall), Leutbecher 1967 (not located open), Hecke 1936 full text (not
opened, JFM review read), Mann's book, Lang and Lang 2016, Takeuchi 1977, Lewin 1991, the journal
versions of the arXiv papers. Statements attributed to these are second-hand and marked SECONDARY.
