\begin{abstract}
We study the Markov chain on the complete graph with $d$ states, which is the
one-dimensional $d$-state Potts chain. It stays in its state with probability
$p$ and otherwise moves to each other state with probability $r=T/N$. We
compare the most likely count vector on each face of the simplex with a vertex.
Every face ties with the vertices at first order when $T$ is near $\log N$, and
we resolve the tie at second order. A balanced bin with $k$ states crosses a
vertex at $T=z$, where $z+\frac12\log z=\log N+a_k+1/(8z)+O((\log N)^{-2})$.
The factor $e^{-(k-1)a_k}$ equals the mass of a face divided by a Gaussian
volume, and we write it with square roots of weighted spanning-tree sums on
$K_k$.
Since $a_2>a_3>\cdots$, for large $N$ and $T=O(\log N)$ the mode changes
exactly once, from the vertices straight to the full centers. A start law or a
weak Potts field shifts the constants. A start can make an edge win on 3
states, and a field can make every support size win in turn. A Bessel majorant
holds for every positive count vector, and near a vertex with a large count $b$
a product of Laguerre-type polynomials gives the crossing uniformly over rare
counts of total $o(b)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Problem 131 of Kagey's Open Problem Collection~\cite{kagey131} asks about a
Galton board where the ball tends to keep going the same way, and in
particular when the middle bin becomes as likely as an endpoint. In this paper
the ball has $d$ directions. We run a Markov chain $X_1,\ldots,X_N$ on $d$
states that starts uniformly, stays in its state with probability $p$ and
otherwise moves to each of the other $d-1$ states with probability
$r=(1-p)/(d-1)$. The count vector $K_N$ records how often each state is
visited, and it plays the role of the bin. A bin with $k$ positive coordinates
lies on a face of the simplex with $k$ states. A vertex $Ne_i$ has only one
path, which never switches. When $p$ is close to $1$ the vertices are the most
likely bins. We ask where the best bin on each face overtakes the vertices, and
which face holds the global mode. We write $L=\log N$ and $T=Nr$, so that the
transition matrix is $I+(T/N)Q$ with $Q=J-dI$, where $J$ is the all-ones
matrix. Then $T$ will be of order $L$.

In this model every face ties with the vertices at first order. Let $C_{N,k}$
be the probability of a
balanced bin with $k$ positive coordinates, which means that they differ by at
most $1$, and let $V_N=p^{N-1}/d$ be the probability of a vertex.
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-window} shows that, uniformly for $cL\le T\le CL$,
\[
\log\frac{C_{N,k}}{V_N}=(k-1)\Bigl[T-L+\frac12\log T-a_k\Bigr]
-\frac{k-1}{8T}+O\Bigl(\frac1{T^2}+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr),\qquad
a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-\frac{k+\frac12}{k-1}\log k .
\]
So with $T=\tau L$ a face $F$ with $k$ states has
$\log C_{N,k}=-L[\tau\lambda_F+k-1]+O(\log L)$, where $\lambda_F=d-k$ is the
rate at which the chain leaves $F$. The general theory of Paper~C~\cite{paperC}
selects the faces that minimize $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$. On the complete graph
every face attains the minimum $d-1$ at $\tau=1$, so the first-order theory
cannot choose. Even the terms in $\log T$ are proportional to $k-1$, and only
the constants $a_k$ separate the faces. Adding a state to a face has 2 effects
of the same size. The chain no longer has to
avoid that state, which gains a factor of about $e^T$, since a path avoids a
given state for $N$ steps with probability about $(1-r)^N\approx e^{-T}$. But
the mass of the face is now spread over one more direction with about
$N/\sqrt T$ bins, so a single bin loses a factor of about $\sqrt T/N$. At
$T=L$ the factors $e^T$ and $1/N$ cancel, and the factor $\sqrt T$ is the same
for every new state.

This paper resolves the tie at second order. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}, for every $N\ge k$ the balanced bin of a
face with $k$ states crosses the vertices at exactly one $p_{N,k}$, which
increases with $N$, and the value $z=z_{N,k}$ of $T$ there satisfies
\[
z+\frac12\log z=L+a_k+\frac1{8z}+O(L^{-2}).
\]
For $k=2$, $a_2=\frac12\log(\pi/8)$ is the constant $c_0$ of
Paper~A~\cite{paperA}. Since $a_2>a_3>\cdots>a_d$, the full center crosses
first as $p$ decreases. Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} shows that for large
$N$ and all $T\le CL$ the global mode changes exactly once, from the $d$
vertices straight to the balanced bins with $d$ positive coordinates, and that
all face-center crossings lie in a $p$-interval of length
$(d-1)(a_2-a_d+o(1))/N$. At small $N$ this can fail
(Example~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode}).

The same method gives the crossing at every interior composition $\alpha$ of a
face (Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-interior}), with a constant $D(\alpha)$ that
equals $D_k=e^{-(k-1)a_k}$ at the center. Proposition~\ref{prop:kirchhoff}
writes it as
\[
D(\alpha)=\frac{A^2}{\sqrt{\det(2\pi\Sigma_\alpha)}}
=\frac{A^2}{(4\pi)^{(k-1)/2}}\,\frac{\sqrt{\tree(\alpha)}}{\prod_i\alpha_i},
\qquad A=\sum_i\sqrt{\alpha_i},
\]
where $\Sigma_\alpha$ is the occupation covariance of a tilted chain and
$\tree(\alpha)=(\prod_i\sqrt{\alpha_i})A^{k-2}$ is the sum over the spanning
trees of $K_k$ of the products of the edge weights $\sqrt{\alpha_i\alpha_j}$.
At the center each of Cayley's $k^{k-2}$ trees has weight $k^{1-k}$, so
$\tree=1/k$ and the tree count enters $D_k$ under a square root.
Proposition~\ref{prop:mass-balance} reads $D_k=k\det(2\pi\Sigma_k)^{-1/2}$ as
the mass of the face divided by the Gaussian volume of its fluctuations.

A start law $\mu$ changes the constants to
$a_k(\mu)=a_k+\frac1{k-1}\log(k\mu_i/\mu(F))$ for a face $F$ and a vertex $i$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:start-crossings}), and the modes in the window follow from
explicit lines (Remark~\ref{rem:start-modes}). On 3 states with
$\mu=(\frac12,\frac12,0)$ an edge holds the mode on a window of width
$\log(16/(9\sqrt3))=0.026$ in $T$, whose lower end is Paper~A's crossing for
large $N$ (Remarks~\ref{ex:K3-start} and~\ref{rem:K3-finite}). Paper~C proves this window from its general
tie-window theorem. So the start can decide which face wins. A Potts
field of size $h/N$ per site is an exponential tilt of the law of $K_N/N$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:field-tilt}). It adds to the line of each face the
field averaged over the face (Theorem~\ref{thm:weakfield-phases}), and one
field makes every support size win in turn
(Corollary~\ref{cor:weakfield-full-hierarchy}).

Near the boundary of the simplex a Bessel majorant has relative error
$O(L^2/N)$ for every positive count vector when $T=O(L)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-simplex-majorant}). When at least 2 coordinates are
of order $N$, a small coordinate starts to matter once it has size $N/L^2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-boundary-crossover}). Near a vertex, where one count
$b$ is large and the other counts $a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ have total $|a|=o(b)$, the
crossing has $u=r/p=\sqrt{y_a/b}\,(1+O(\sqrt{(|a|+1)/b}))$, where $y_a$ is the
root of a product of Laguerre-type polynomials
(Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root}).

This is the second of 4 papers on Problem 131, and Figure~\ref{fig:program}
shows the crossings of all 4. The present paper treats the complete graph,
which is the one-dimensional $d$-state Potts chain. Paper~A~\cite{paperA}
treats the walk with 2 states, which is the zero-field one-dimensional Ising
chain with free boundaries. It gives explicit uniform Bessel bounds for every
bin and brackets the crossing $p_N$ for every $N\ge2$. The bracket is analytic
for $N\ge175$ and rests on exact certificates below that, and all of it is
checked in Lean. This gives the crossing equation
$z_N+\frac12\log z_N=L+c_0+1/(8z_N)+R_N$ with an explicit $R_N$, where
$z_N=N(1-p_N)$. Paper~A also proves a uniform local law for every switching
probability $q\le\frac12$, from the diffusive to the ballistic regime. For
$d=2$ our $z_{N,2}$ is this $z_N$. Paper~C~\cite{paperC} gives the general
theory for a chain with transition matrix $P=I+(T/N)Q$ and $T=\tau L$. To first
order the mode sits on a face $F$ that minimizes $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$, where
$\lambda_F$ is the principal Dirichlet eigenvalue of $-Q$ on $F$. At second
order a strongly connected face has a constant $K_F$, and the crossings satisfy
a crossing equation. The present paper is the case where the first-order
selection is silent, with $K_F=\mu(F)k^{k-1/2}(4\pi)^{-(k-1)/2}$ for a face with
$k$ states. The application in Paper~C is the planar persistent walk.
Paper~D~\cite{paperD} asks what memory changes. The elephant random walk
crosses at $x_n=2z_n-\log(2\pi)+o(1)$, where $z_n$ is Paper~A's crossing at
$N=n$. This is about twice the Markov value, and the profile at the crossing
has 2 humps. Runs with heavy tails have a threshold at the golden ratio, and a
short remark treats aging walks. The 4 papers share the notation $N$, $L$,
$T$, $z_N$, $\lambda_F$ and $K_F$ of Section~\ref{sec:multistate-model}.

Section~\ref{sec:multistate-model} sets up the model with a general start and
writes the exact law in 3 forms. Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds} proves
the tie and resolves it, writes the constants as Gaussian volumes and tree
sums, and finds the global mode. Section~\ref{sec:general-start} treats a
general start and Section~\ref{sec:frontier-modes} a weak field.
Section~\ref{sec:frontier-boundary} drops the assumption that the coordinates
are of order $N$. Section~\ref{sec:verify} describes the checks and lists open
questions, and the longer proofs are in Appendices~\ref{app:thresholds}
to~\ref{app:vertex}. We do not treat a growing number of states, the modes
when $T$ is much larger than $L$, or the global modes at finite $N$ below the
$N_0$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The law belongs to the theory of Markov multinomial distributions. Wang and
Yang~\cite{wangyang1995} extended the Markov Bernoulli model of
Edwards~\cite{edwards1960} to finitely many states. We have seen neither paper,
and we describe both through Wang and Tang~\cite{wangtang1997,wangtang2003}.
The chain of Wang and Yang starts from a law $\mu$ and moves from $i$ to $j$
with probability
$\theta\delta_{ij}+(1-\theta)\mu_j$, and the generating function of the sum of
its states is a product of matrices. Our chain with the uniform start is the
case where $\mu$ is uniform and $\theta=\sigma=(dp-1)/(d-1)$. So the exact law
for the uniform start is a special case of this theory, and we do not claim it
as new. Our general start changes only the initial law and keeps the uniform
switching, so it is a Wang--Yang chain only when $\mu$ is uniform.
Hsiau~\cite{hsiau1997} and Wang and Tang~\cite{wangtang2003} prove compound
Poisson limits for sums over multistate chains. Whittle~\cite{whittle1955} gave
the exact law of the transition counts of a finite chain. The count vector is
determined by the transition counts and the first state, so $P_N$ could also
be summed from his formula.

Brydges, van der Hofstad and K\"onig~\cite{brydges2007} give occupation
densities for continuous-time chains on the visited faces of the simplex.
Huang, Kious, Sidoravicius and Tarr\'es~\cite[Theorem~1]{huang2018} give the
joint density of the local times, the last-exit spanning tree and the net
crossing numbers of a walk with symmetric conductances, with Bessel functions.
So in continuous time the trees appear directly, but we do not use their
formula.
Stadje~\cite{stadje1999} gives generating
functions for the joint numbers of visits of a discrete chain and limit laws
when one state is nearly absorbing. Dietz and Sethuraman~\cite{dietz2007} study
chains that switch from $i$ to $j$ at time $n$ with probability $G(i,j)/n$,
where every $G(i,j)>0$. Their chains make about $\log N$ switches by time $N$
but only $O(1)$ of them after time $N/2$, and their occupation fractions have a
limit law with full support on the simplex, which is a Dirichlet law when
$G(i,j)$ depends only on $j$. Near our crossings the chain makes about
$(d-1)\log N$ switches, spread evenly in time. Dupuis and
Liu~\cite{dupuisliu2015} give the rate function of the occupation measure,
which we compare with our exponent in Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds}.
Kassan-Ogly and Filippov~\cite{kassanogly2003} solve the Potts chain in a
field in the thermodynamic limit. For Whittle, Hsiau, Stadje and Kassan-Ogly
and Filippov we have read only the abstracts, and we use nothing beyond them.
None of these works compare one count vector with a vertex when $p$ depends on
$N$, and we did not find the face-center crossings or the tie in the
literature. We take the Bessel expansions from~\cite{dlmf}.

\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

The proofs in this paper do not rely on computation. All 4 papers on
Problem 131 share one verification supplement, \texttt{SUPPLEMENT.md} in the
Problem 131 folder of \url{https://github.com/apemm/Kagey-Problems}. It
describes the checks, and \texttt{python -B verify\_all.py --paper b} in that
folder runs the ones for this paper (\texttt{--full} adds a run of about 3
minutes). The scripts use only the standard library. They compare the exact
law with dynamic programs and with enumeration of all words, and they check the
crossings, the constants and their tree forms, the factor $e^{\mathcal E}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-boundary-crossover} and the statements that we mark
as numerically verified or with the proof omitted, where possible in 2
independent ways. Before the newer
computations we wrote the predictions in ledgers, which keep the 2 predictions
that failed. Many exact identities and finite bounds, among them the exact
law for the uniform start, balancing, the order of the constants $a_k$ and the
bracket near a vertex,
are also proved in Lean~4 in the folder \texttt{lean/PaperB}. The asymptotic
results are not in Lean.

Several questions remain open. We have not proved the discrete term
$-\frac{(k-1)^2}2z^2/N$, the second-order ends of the $K_3$ window in
Remark~\ref{rem:K3-finite}, or that balancing fails at all large $N$ for every
start that is not constant on the face. Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} has no effective
$N_0$, and we say nothing about the modes when $T$ is much larger than $L$. Near
a vertex we know the leading crossing uniformly for $|a|=o(b)$, but not the
next term uniformly in that range, and not with a start that is not uniform. We
have not treated the crossover with a start that is not constant on the
support, in particular one that sits only on the rare coordinates, or a growing
number of states. Switching that is not uniform is the subject of
Paper~C. Example~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode} shows that
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} does not extend to every $N$.
