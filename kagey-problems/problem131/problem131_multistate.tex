\begin{abstract}
Take a Markov chain on $d$ states that starts uniformly, stays in its
current state with probability $p$, and otherwise moves to one of the other
$d-1$ states at random. We count how often it visits each state and compare
the probability of a given count vector with the probability of a vertex,
where every visit is to the same state. Writing the law as a sum with
positive terms, we get a Bessel approximation whose relative error is
bounded for every positive count vector, even near the boundary of the
simplex. From it we find the crossing points for balanced faces and for
interior points, with explicit constants. When at least two coordinates
are of order $N$, a small coordinate starts to matter once it has size
$N/(\log N)^2$. Near a vertex, where one count $b$ is much larger than the
total $A=o(b)$ of the others, the crossing is given by a product of
Laguerre polynomials up to a relative error $O_\ell(\sqrt{(A+1)/b})$, and
this can be true even when the matching approximation to the probabilities
fails. We also find the volumes of the superlevel sets, and a weak field
under which every support size is the mode in turn. The exact law and the
normal limit were known before, and we include them for reference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When $p$ is close to $1$ the chain rarely switches, so the most likely
count vector is a vertex. As $p$ decreases, at some point a vector in the
interior becomes more likely instead. We want to know where that happens.
Note that we compare the probability of one vector, not of a whole face,
and this makes the answer depend a lot on coordinates that are small
compared with $N$. We keep the number of states fixed but let the count
vector approach a vertex at any rate.

This is the version of Kagey's Problem 131~\cite{kagey131} with $d$
directions instead of 2. The binary case, including free and periodic
boundaries, is in a companion paper~\cite{companion}, but we do not use it
here and we prove every estimate we need. One way to picture the model is
a board shaped like a simplex, where each bounce goes in one of $d$ fixed
directions and a switch goes to each other direction equally often. A
board with different rules at its walls would give a different process.

There are three regimes. On a fixed face of the simplex, the balanced
crossing is at $Nu=\log N-\tfrac12\log\log N+O(1)$, with a constant that
depends on the size of the support. A Bessel bound that holds for all
positive count vectors then handles the case where some coordinates have
size $N/(\log N)^2$. Finally, if one count is $b$ and the remaining counts have
total $A=o(b)$, a single polynomial equation gives the crossing with
relative error $O_\ell(\sqrt{(A+1)/b})$, uniformly over their individual
sizes. This holds even in a regime where the corresponding approximation
to the probabilities is not uniform. For the modes, we find the volumes of
the spatial superlevel sets and give an explicit weak field under which
every support size occurs in turn.

\paragraph{Relation to established results.}
The law belongs to the theory of Markov multinomial distributions
\cite{wangyang1995,wangtang2003}, and the exact finite sum and the normal
limit below are included for reference. We cite the Wang--Yang article
through Wang and Tang, since we have not been able to obtain it.
Brydges, van der Hofstad and K\"onig~\cite{brydges2007} give occupation
densities for continuous-time chains on the visited faces of the simplex.
The square-root quantity that appears below is also classical: if $Q$ is
the continuous-time generator of the complete graph with off-diagonal
entries one, then its symmetric occupation rate is
\[
 I_Q(\alpha)=-\langle\sqrt\alpha,Q\sqrt\alpha\rangle
 =d-\left(\sum_i\sqrt{\alpha_i}\right)^2.
\]
Hence the exponent $(\sum_i\sqrt{\alpha_i})^2-1$ is the difference
between the rate at a vertex and the rate at $\alpha$. We take the
Bessel expansions from~\cite{dlmf} and prove the rest ourselves. What we
add is that the estimates hold uniformly when $p$ depends on $N$, and what
that says near the boundary and about the modes.

\paragraph{Organization.}
In Section~\ref{sec:multistate-model} we write down the exact law, a few
simple bounds, the covariance and the normal limit for fixed $p$.
Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds} has the face crossings and the
balancing theorem. Sections~\ref{sec:frontier-boundary}
and~\ref{sec:vertex-completion} deal with small coordinates and the
near-vertex law, and Section~\ref{sec:frontier-modes} with superlevel
sets and weak fields. We do not find every global mode at finite $N$, and
the number of states stays fixed. In fact, at finite $N$ the mode does not
have to be a vertex or a full center (Example~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode}).

\section{The model and exact formulas}\label{sec:multistate-model}

Fix $d\ge2$ and let $(X_j)_{j\ge1}$ be a Markov chain on
$\{1,\ldots,d\}$. The initial state is uniform.
At each subsequent step the chain repeats its state with probability
$p\in[0,1]$ and moves to each other state with probability
$r=(1-p)/(d-1)$. Put $q=1-p$ and
$\sigma=p-r=(dp-1)/(d-1)$. Let $K_N=(K_{N,1},\ldots,K_{N,d})$
be the occupation vector over $N$ visits, and write
$P_N(k)=\Prob(K_N=k)$. For a word $w$ of length $N\ge1$ with
$j$ changes, its probability is $d^{-1}p^{N-1-j}r^j$.
The vertex probability is $V_N=p^{N-1}/d$.
For $p>0$ put $u=r/p$, and for a positive vector
$n=(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ with total $N$, write
\[
 S_n(u)=\frac{P_N(n_1,\ldots,n_k,0,\ldots,0)}{V_N}
 =\sum_j W_n(j)u^j,
\]
where $W_n(j)$ is the number of words on the $k$ states with these
counts and $j$ changes. Note that this ratio does not depend on $d$,
only on the positive coordinates.
Set $P_0(0,\ldots,0)=1$ separately.

\begin{proposition}[$d$ directions]\label{prop:dgf}
Let $P_N(k_1,\dots,k_d)$ be the probability that direction $i$ is taken $k_i$ times. Put
$\sigma=p-\frac{q}{d-1}=\frac{dp-1}{d-1}$ and $\mathcal R=\sum_{i=1}^d\frac{x_it}{1-\sigma x_it}$. Then
\[
\sum_{N\ge0}\sum_{k_1+\dots+k_d=N}P_N(k_1,\dots,k_d)\,x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_d^{k_d}\,t^N
=1+\frac{\mathcal R/d}{1-\frac{q}{d-1}\mathcal R}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $F_i$ be the generating function of words ending in state $i$, and $S=\sum_iF_i$.
Conditioning on the last bounce, $F_i=x_it\bigl(\tfrac1d+pF_i+\tfrac{q}{d-1}(S-F_i)\bigr)$, that is
$F_i(1-\sigma x_it)=x_it\bigl(\tfrac1d+\tfrac{q}{d-1}S\bigr)$. Summing over $i$ gives
$S=\mathcal R\bigl(\tfrac1d+\tfrac{q}{d-1}S\bigr)$, and the generating function is $1+S$.
\end{proof}

For $d=2$, setting $x_1=x$ and $x_2=1$ gives the binary generating function studied in the companion~\cite{companion}.

\begin{proposition}[finite bin probabilities in $d$ directions]\label{prop:dpmf}
Put $r=q/(d-1)$ and $\sigma=p-r$. For $N\ge1$, let $k=(k_1,\ldots,k_d)$ have
nonnegative integer coordinates summing to $N$, and put $H=\{i:k_i>0\}$. Sum over
$m_i=0$ for $i\notin H$ and $1\le m_i\le k_i$ for $i\in H$, and set $M=\sum_i m_i$.
Then
\[
P_N(k)=\frac1d\sum_m\frac{M!}{\prod_i m_i!}\,
r^{M-1}\sigma^{N-M}\prod_{i\in H}\binom{k_i-1}{m_i-1}.
\]
All exponents are nonnegative, and $0^0=1$. Set $P_0(0,\ldots,0)=1$ separately.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $z_i=x_it$, Proposition~\ref{prop:dgf} becomes the formal power series
\[
1+\frac1d\sum_{M\ge1}r^{M-1}
\left(\sum_i\frac{z_i}{1-\sigma z_i}\right)^M.
\]
Expand by the multinomial theorem and use, for $k\ge m\ge1$,
\[
[z^k]\left(\frac{z}{1-\sigma z}\right)^m
=\binom{k-1}{m-1}\sigma^{k-m}.
\]
For $m=0$ only $k=0$ contributes. Multiplying the coefficients proves the formula.
\end{proof}

At $d=3$ this is a triple sum, with the support convention covering edge bins.
At $p=1/d$, $\sigma=0$ and only $M=N$ survives, giving the ordinary multinomial law.
At $p=1$, only $M=1$ survives, giving mass $1/d$ at each $Ne_i$.
The formula also holds at $p=0$. For $\sigma<0$ its terms can cancel, so for numerical work we
use dynamic programming or exact arithmetic. This model assumes fixed global directions and
uniform switching to the other directions, and a board with a different rule need not have
this law.



\subsection{Elementary bounds used below}

For $x\ge0$, the modified Bessel function used here is
\[
 I_1(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(x/2)^{2j+1}}{j!(j+1)!}.
\]
Its coefficients give $I_1(x)\le\sinh x\le e^x$.
We also use the standard expansion~\cite[Eq.~10.40.1]{dlmf}
\[
 I_1(x)=\frac{e^x}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}
 \left(1-\frac{3}{8x}+O(x^{-2})\right),\qquad x\to\infty.
\]
The Laguerre--Bessel comparison needed near the boundary is proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel}.

\begin{lemma}[clipped product deficit]\label{lem:clipped-product}
For any finite list of nonnegative real numbers $t_1,\ldots,t_m$,
with $(x)_+=\max(x,0)$,
\[
 0\le1-\prod_{j=1}^m(1-t_j)_+\le\sum_{j=1}^m t_j.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If some $t_j\ge1$, the product is zero and the sum is at least one.
Otherwise all factors lie in $[0,1]$ and induction follows from
$1-(1-a)(1-b)=a+b-ab\le a+b$. The empty product gives equality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Covariance and the fixed-persistence normal limit}

We record these formulas for comparison with the later sections. Note
that the normal limit at fixed $p$ says nothing about individual
occupation probabilities when $p$ depends on $N$.

\begin{remark}[covariance and the tetrahedral case]\label{rem:tetra}
For the board with $d$ directions of Proposition~\ref{prop:dgf} let $K_a$ be the number of bounces in
direction $a$. The direction chain has transition matrix $\sigma I+(1-\sigma)\tfrac1dJ$ with $J$ the
all-ones matrix, so the indicator of direction $a$ at time $i$ and of direction $b$ at time $i+m$ have
covariance $\sigma^m\bigl(\tfrac{\delta_{ab}}d-\tfrac1{d^2}\bigr)$. Summing over the
two time indices and evaluating the finite geometric sum gives
\[
\Cov(K_a,K_b)=\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{ab}}d-\frac1{d^2}\Bigr)
\Bigl(N\,\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}-\frac{2\sigma(1-\sigma^N)}{(1-\sigma)^2}\Bigr),
\qquad \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}=\frac{d-2+dp}{d\,(1-p)} .
\]
These formulas hold for $p<1$, and at $p=1$ the covariance is
$N^2(\delta_{ab}/d-1/d^2)$. The asymptotic multiplier of the multinomial covariance is
$(d-2+dp)/(d(1-p))$, which is $(1+3p)/(3(1-p))$ for the tetrahedral board, $d=3$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[simplex normal limit]\label{thm:dclt}
Let $d\ge3$ and $0\le p<1$, or $d=2$ and $0<p<1$. Set
\[
\pi=(1/d,\ldots,1/d),\quad C=\frac1d I-\frac1{d^2}J,
\quad c=\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}=\frac{d-2+dp}{d(1-p)}.
\]
Then $(K_N-N\pi)/\sqrt N$ converges in distribution to $\mathcal N_d(0,cC)$.
This Gaussian is supported on the sum-zero hyperplane and is nondegenerate there.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$ and $T=\sigma I+(1-\sigma)\Pi$. Fix a real vector $a$ and
put $f_i=a_i-d^{-1}\sum_j a_j$, so $\pi f=0$ and $Tf=\sigma f$.
For $D(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{itf_i})$ and $B(t)=TD(t)$, stationarity gives
\[
\E\exp\left(it\sum_{j=1}^N f(X_j)\right)
=\pi D(t)B(t)^{N-1}\mathbf1.
\]
At zero, $B(0)=T$ has the simple eigenvalue $1$ and all other eigenvalues equal
$\sigma$, with $|\sigma|<1$. Analytic perturbation gives an eigenvalue $\lambda(t)$
and right eigenvector $h(t)$ with $\lambda(0)=1$, $h(0)=\mathbf1$ and $\pi h(t)=1$.
The rest of the spectrum is uniformly exponentially small for small enough $t$. To see
this, take a circle of radius $\eta<1$ around the rest of the spectrum but not around
$\lambda(t)$, and use the resolvent formula for matrix powers. Thus the characteristic
function equals $b(t)\lambda(t)^{N-1}+O(\eta^N)$ with $b(t)\to1$.

Differentiate $Bh=\lambda h$ at zero. Since $B'(0)=iT\operatorname{diag}(f)$,
\[
\lambda'(0)=0,\qquad (I-T)h'(0)=i\sigma f,
\qquad h'(0)=\frac{i\sigma}{1-\sigma}f.
\]
Differentiating again and multiplying by $\pi$ gives
\[
\lambda''(0)=\pi B''(0)\mathbf1+2\pi B'(0)h'(0)
=-\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\pi(f^2)=-c\,\pi(f^2).
\]
Hence $\lambda(t)=1-c\pi(f^2)t^2/2+O(t^3)$. Substitute $t=s/\sqrt N$ to obtain
the scalar normal characteristic function with variance $c\pi(f^2)=ca^{\mathsf T}Ca$.
L\'evy's theorem and the Cram\'er--Wold device give the result.
\end{proof}

For $d=3$ the first two centered coordinates have limiting covariance
\[
\frac{1+3p}{27(1-p)}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix};
\]
the third is minus their sum. Unlike the binary case, $p=0$ is included for $d\ge3$.
At $d=2,p=0$ bounded alternation gives the zero limit after division by $\sqrt N$.
At $p=1$ the centered sequence on this scale is not tight.

\section{Verification and open questions}

The proofs above do not rely on computation. The Python scripts for this
paper use only the standard library and check the results at finite size.
\texttt{verify\_geometry.py} compares 6,733 exact rational probabilities in
237 distributions with a dynamic program, and 158 of the distributions also
with exhaustive enumeration. It checks normalization, means, covariances,
and the values at $p=0,1$ for $d=2,3,4$. \texttt{verify\_simplex\_threshold.py}
checks the positive refresh formula, the balancing transfers, the finite-size
edge mode, and the crossing expansions. \texttt{verify\_frontier\_boundary.py}
and \texttt{verify\_frontier\_modes.py} check the uniform comparison, the
rare-coordinate formulas, the spatial profiles, and the weak-field results
against finite calculations. Finally, \texttt{verify\_vertex\_completion.py}
makes 462 exact identity comparisons, 200 exact checks of the elasticity
bounds, 16 root-bracket checks, and 4 large cases with growing rare counts.
Everything they print is saved in \texttt{data/}. The decimal checks are
only numerical, and none of the proofs rely on them.

Some things are still open. We know the leading near-vertex crossing for
all of $A=o(b)$, but not the next term uniformly in that range. We also
have not looked at a growing number of colors, at switching that is not
uniform, or at the full mode diagram for finite $N$ outside the critical
window. Example~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:vertex-probability-failure} show that the simplest
strengthenings of our results are false.
