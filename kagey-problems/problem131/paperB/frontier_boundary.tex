\section{Near the boundary of the simplex}
\label{sec:frontier-boundary}

So far every positive coordinate was of order $N$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-interior} needs each to be at least $\varepsilon N$).
In this section we drop that assumption. We first give an upper bound that holds
for every positive count vector (Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-simplex-majorant}).
Then we find the scale at which a small coordinate starts to matter. It is
$N/L^2$ when at least two coordinates are of order $N$
(Remark~\ref{rem:crossover-scale}). Near a vertex the crossing moves to odds of
order $N^{-1/2}$, and the small coordinates alone decide it
(Subsection~\ref{sec:vertex-completion}).

The start is uniform, but $S_n(u)$ does not involve the start, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dpmf} gives $P^\mu_N(n)\le(\max_i\mu_i)\,p^{N-1}S_n(u)$.
So every upper bound below also holds for a general start.

\begin{lemma}[a uniform Laguerre--Bessel inequality]
\label{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel}
For every integer $a\ge1$ and real $w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-laguerre-bessel}
0\le1-\frac{B_a(w)}{w\Phi(aw)}\le\frac w2,
\qquad
B_a(w)=\sum_{m=1}^{a}\binom{a-1}{m-1}\frac{w^m}{m!}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

By~\cite[Eq.~18.5.12]{dlmf}, $B_a(w)=(w/a)L^{(1)}_{a-1}(-w)$ with the Laguerre
polynomial $L^{(1)}_{a-1}$, so the lemma is a quantitative form of the limit of
Laguerre polynomials to Bessel functions. The proof, in
Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}, compares coefficients with
Lemma~\ref{lem:clipped-product}.

\begin{theorem}[a uniform Bessel majorant on every simplex stratum]
\label{thm:uniform-simplex-majorant}
Let $n_1,\ldots,n_k$ be arbitrary positive integers with sum $N$, and let
$0<u<1$, $v=u/(1-u)$. Define
\[
\mathcal Q_n(u)=(1-u)^{N-1}v^{k-1}
\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^k\prod_{i=1}^k\Phi(n_i vt)\,dt.
\]
Then, with no lower bound on any $n_i/N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-simplex-majorant}
0\le1-\frac{S_n(u)}{\mathcal Q_n(u)}
\le k(k+1)v+\frac{k}{2}v^2\left(\sum_i\sqrt{n_i}\right)^2
\le k(k+1)v+\frac{k^2}{2}Nv^2.
\end{equation}
Thus this approximation has relative error $O_k(L^2/N)$ whenever
$u=O(L/N)$, uniformly over every positive composition of $N$.
\end{theorem}

For the central bin of the segment $\mathcal Q_n$ is a bound of the same kind as the uniform Bessel bound of
Paper~A~\cite[Theorem~3.3]{paperA}. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}.
It applies Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel} to each factor of an integral
form of $S_n$, and an integration by parts bounds the resulting mean.

\begin{remark}[the scale $N/(\log N)^2$]\label{rem:crossover-scale}
A small coordinate $a$ adds its own runs. Each run needs about two switches,
which gives a factor $u^2$, and it can go in about $N$ places. So what matters
is $aNu^2$. At the crossings of Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds} the value
$Nu$ is of order $L$, and then $aNu^2$ is of order $aL^2/N$. So when at least
two coordinates are of order $N$, a rare count starts to matter once it has
size $\rho N/L^2$ with $\rho$ fixed.

Appendix~\ref{app:boundary} proves this. Take $k_1\ge2$ large coordinates with
fractions $\beta_i$ among themselves and $\ell$ rare counts $a_j=\rho_jN/L^2$.
With $z=Nu$, Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-boundary-crossover} gives $S_n(z/N)$ up to
a factor $1+O(L^{-2}+L^2/N)$. Each rare count contributes a factor
$\Phi(A^2a_jz^2/N)$, where $A=\sum_i\sqrt{\beta_i}$, and the complete correction
of relative order $1/L$ is a factor $e^{\mathcal E(z)}$ with three terms. The
theorem also gives the crossing as the root of an implicit equation, and as an
explicit expansion in $L$, which converges slowly. Rare counts with
$a_j=o(N/L^2)$ change that expansion only by $o(1)$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-rare-invisibility}). With only one large coordinate
the growth factor $e^{(A^2-1)z}$ is gone, since $A=1$. Then the crossing moves
to $u$ of order $b^{-1/2}$, where $b$ is the large count. This is the case of
Subsection~\ref{sec:vertex-completion}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Near a vertex}\label{sec:vertex-completion}

Now let one count $b$ be large and let the other positive counts
$a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ be small compared with $b$, with $\ell\ge1$ fixed. In
this subsection and in Appendix~\ref{app:vertex} we put
$|a|=a_1+\cdots+a_\ell$. These rare counts are not the constants $a_k$ of
Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds}, which do not appear here. With
$B_a$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel}, put
\[
\mathcal H(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}B_{a_i}(y),\qquad
\kappa(y)=\frac{y\mathcal H'(y)}{\mathcal H(y)}.
\]
The polynomial $\mathcal H$ has positive coefficients, $\mathcal H(0)=0$ and
$\mathcal H(1)\ge1$, so $\mathcal H(y_a)=1$ has exactly one positive root
$y_a$, and $y_a\le1$. Write $S_{(b,a)}$ for $S_n$ with
$n=(b,a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)$. It is a polynomial in $u$ with nonnegative
coefficients, zero constant term and a positive coefficient at $u^\ell$, so
$S_{(b,a)}(u)=1$ has exactly one positive root $u_{b,a}$.

Each rare run again needs about two switches and has about $b$ places, so the
rare counts enter through $bu^2$. For fixed rare counts the bounds of
Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-randomization} give $S_{(b,a)}(\sqrt{y/b})\to\mathcal H(y)$
as $b\to\infty$. So the crossing is near $\sqrt{y_a/b}$, and $Nu$ is of order
$\sqrt N$ instead of $L$. The next theorem shows that this stays correct
uniformly as long as $|a|=o(b)$.

\begin{theorem}[all growing rare counts near a vertex]
\label{thm:vertex-uniform-root}
Fix $\ell\ge1$ and put
\[
 c_\ell=\ell+\sqrt\ell+1,\quad
 \varepsilon=\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}},\quad \delta=16c_\ell\varepsilon.
\]
For all positive counts $b,a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ with $\delta\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-uniform-bracket}
 (1-\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}\ \le u_{b,a}\le
 (1+\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b},\qquad \mathcal H(y_a)=1.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-uniform-root}
 \biggl|\frac{u_{b,a}}{\sqrt{y_a/b}}-1\biggr|\le16c_\ell\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}}
 \qquad\text{whenever }\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}}\le\frac1{32c_\ell},
\end{equation}
so $u_{b,a}=\sqrt{y_a/b}\,(1+o(1))$ as $(|a|+1)/b\to0$. The constants are not
optimized.
\end{theorem}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:vertex}. It rests on two facts.
First, $\log\mathcal H$ is concave, and for $0<y\le1$ its elasticity $\kappa(y)$
lies between $\max\{\ell,\frac12\sqrt{|a|y}\}$ and $\ell+\sqrt{\ell|a|y}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-elasticity}). Second,
$S_{(b,a)}(u)=(1-u)^{|a|}\E[\mathcal H(v\mathcal X)]$ exactly, where
$v=u/(1-u)$ and $\mathcal X$ is a gamma variable plus a compound binomial sum
with mean $2+(b-1)u$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-randomization}). Jensen's
inequality then gives $S_{(b,a)}(u)\ge(1-u)^{|a|}\mathcal H(bu^2)$, which
exceeds $1$ at $u=(1+\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}$. The tangent line of $\log\mathcal H$
at $bu^2$ and the moment generating function of $\mathcal X$ give an upper bound
that is below $1$ at $u=(1-\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}$.
