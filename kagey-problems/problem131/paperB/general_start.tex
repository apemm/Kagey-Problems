\section{A general start}\label{sec:general-start}

Suppose now that the ball starts in state $a$ with probability $\mu_a$, and
moves as before. The start affects only the first run, but it changes the
constants $a_k$ by an amount of order $1$, the order at which the faces are
separated. So a start can decide which face wins.

For a count vector $n$ with support $F$, Proposition~\ref{prop:dpmf}(b) gives
\[
 P^\mu_N(n)=p^{N-1}\sum_{a\in F}\mu_aS^{(a)}_n(u)
 =p^{N-1}S_n(u)\sum_{a\in F}\mu_a\omega_a(n),\qquad
 \omega_a(n)=\frac{S^{(a)}_n(u)}{S_n(u)}.
\]
So the start enters only through the weights $\omega_a(n)$, which sum to $1$. A
vertex has $V^\mu_i=\mu_ip^{N-1}$. Throughout this section, $F$ is a face with
$k=|F|\ge2$ states and $\mu(F)>0$ (if $\mu(F)=0$, every bin with support $F$ has
probability $0$), and $i$ is a vertex with $\mu_i>0$.

\begin{lemma}[start weights]\label{lem:start-weights}
Let $n$ be a positive count vector on $F$.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item If $n_a=n_b$, then $\omega_a(n)=\omega_b(n)$. So $\omega_a(n)=1/k$ for
all $a\in F$ when $n$ is balanced and $k$ divides $N$.
\item Fix $C>0$. There are $C_1$ and $N_0$, depending only on $d$, $k$ and $C$,
such that for $N\ge N_0$, $0<T\le CL$ and every balanced bin $n$ of $F$,
\[
 \Bigl|\sum_{a\in F}\mu_a\omega_a(n)-\frac{\mu(F)}k\Bigr|
 \le\mu(F)\,C_1\frac{(1+T)^2}N.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

So a balanced bin forgets where the path started, up to a relative error
$O(T^2/N)$. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:start-field}. For other bins we
look at the first run of the path.

\begin{lemma}[the first run]\label{lem:first-run}
Let $n$ be a positive count vector on $F$ and let $a\in F$. Then, as
polynomials in $u$,
\[
 S^{(a)}_n(u)=u\sum_{j=1}^{n_a}(1-u)^{j-1}S_{n-je_a}(u).
\]
Here $S_{n-n_ae_a}$ is the polynomial of the $k-1$ positive coordinates of
$n-n_ae_a$, and it equals $1$ when $k=2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A word $x_1\cdots x_N$ with $i$ changes has weight $u^i$ in $S_n$. Note that
$u^i=\prod_{s<N}c(x_s,x_{s+1})$ with $c(x,y)=(1-u)\mathbf 1\{x=y\}+u$, since
$c(x,x)=1$ and $c(x,y)=u$ for $x\ne y$. Expand every factor into its two terms.
The term with no $u$ is nonzero only for a constant word, and a word that uses
$k\ge2$ states is not constant. For a word that starts with $a$ and a nonzero
term of its expansion, let $j$ be the first step at which the term $u$ is
chosen. The steps before $j$ give $(1-u)^{j-1}\mathbf 1\{x_1=\cdots=x_j=a\}$,
so $x_1=\cdots=x_j=a$ and $j\le n_a$. Step $j$ gives $u$, whatever $x_{j+1}$
is. The later factors, summed over their terms, give the weight of the word
$x_{j+1}\cdots x_N$, which can be any word with counts $n-je_a$. Summing over
these words gives $S_{n-je_a}(u)$.
\end{proof}

In the refresh reading of Section~\ref{sec:multistate-model}, the path stays in
$a$ until its first refresh, and after it the path no longer depends on where it
started.

\begin{proposition}[where a conditioned path starts]\label{prop:start-weights}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $0<c<C$, and put $\alpha=n/N$ and
$A=\sum_{b\in F}\sqrt{\alpha_b}$. Uniformly for positive count vectors $n$ on
$F$ with every $\alpha_b\ge\varepsilon$, for $a\in F$ and for $cL\le T\le CL$,
\[
 \omega_a(n)=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_a}}A+O\Bigl(\frac1L\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

Under the uniform start, $\omega_a(n)$ is the probability that the chain starts
in $a$ given $K_N=n$. So a path conditioned to have fractions $\alpha$ starts in
$a$ with probability close to $\sqrt{\alpha_a}/A$, and not $\alpha_a$. Note that
$\sqrt\alpha$ is the Perron vector $\phi$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:kirchhoff}.
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:start-field}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:first-run}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-interior}, removing $j$ visits from $a$ lowers
$S_n$ by a factor of about $e^{-ju(A/\sqrt{\alpha_a}-1)}$, and the sum over $j$
gives $\sqrt{\alpha_a}/A$. For $d=2$, $\alpha_1=0.6$, $z=L-\frac12\log L$ and
$N=2000$ the error is $-0.0020$, about $-0.015/L$ (numerically verified, see
Section~\ref{sec:verify}).

\begin{theorem}[face-center crossings with a start]\label{thm:start-crossings}
Let $n$ be a balanced bin of $F$.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item For every $N\ge k$, the ratio $P^\mu_N(n)/V^\mu_i$ is a polynomial in $u$
with nonnegative coefficients, zero constant term and lowest term
$(\mu(F)/\mu_i)(k-1)!\,u^{k-1}$. So it equals $1$ at exactly one $p\in(0,1)$, and
the bin is more likely than the vertex below this $p$. If $k$ divides $N$, then
$P^\mu_N(n)=\frac{\mu(F)}k\,p^{N-1}S_n(u)=\frac{d\mu(F)}k\,P_N(n)$.
So the crossing solves $S_n(u)=k\mu_i/\mu(F)$, its $u$ decreases strictly along
$N\in k\Z$, and at $N=k$ it is $u=(\mu_i/(\mu(F)(k-1)!))^{1/(k-1)}$.
\item Fix $C>0$ and let $N_0$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:start-weights}(2). For
$N\ge N_0$, uniformly for $0<T\le CL$ and over all $\mu$,
$P^\mu_N(n)=\frac{\mu(F)}k\,p^{N-1}S_n(u)\bigl(1+O((1+T)^2/N)\bigr)$.
\item Put $a_k(\mu)=a_k+\frac1{k-1}\log\frac{k\mu_i}{\mu(F)}$. Fix $0<c<C$. For
large $N$, uniformly for $cL\le T\le CL$ and over all $\mu$,
\[
 \log\frac{P^\mu_N(n)}{V^\mu_i}=(k-1)\bigl[\Psi_N(T)-a_k(\mu)\bigr]
 -\frac{k-1}{8T}+O\Bigl(\frac1{T^2}+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr).
\]
Fix also $\Delta>0$. Uniformly over the $\mu$ with $|\log(k\mu_i/\mu(F))|\le\Delta$, the
value $T=T^\mu_N(n)$ of $T$ at the crossing of (1) satisfies
$T+\frac12\log T=L+a_k(\mu)+\frac1{8T}+O(L^{-2})$ and the expansion
\eqref{eq:simplex-root-expansion} with $a_k(\mu)$ in place of $a_k$, and with
$T=L-\frac12\log L+t$ the ratio tends to $e^{(k-1)(t-a_k(\mu))}$, uniformly for
$t$ in compact sets. Here the constants depend also on $\Delta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

So a start changes only the constant of each crossing. It lowers $a_k$
by $\frac1{k-1}\log(\mu(F)/(k\mu_i))$, the logarithm of the average start
weight on the face against the start weight of the vertex, shared among the
$k-1$ new states. When $k$ does not divide $N$ the crossing can depend on the
balanced bin $n$, which is why we write $T^\mu_N(n)$.

\begin{proof}
(1) Each $S^{(a)}_n$ has nonnegative coefficients, zero constant term and lowest
term $(k-1)!\,u^{k-1}$, from the $(k-1)!$ orders of the states that start with
$a$, with each state in a single run. So the ratio increases strictly from $0$
to $\infty$ in $u$, and $u=r/p$ decreases from $\infty$ to $0$ on $(0,1)$. If
$k$ divides $N$, Lemma~\ref{lem:start-weights}(1) gives
$\sum_a\mu_a\omega_a(n)=\mu(F)/k$. The rest of (1) is about $S_n$, which
increases strictly along $N\in k\Z$ at every $u>0$ and equals $k!\,u^{k-1}$ at
$N=k$, by the proof of the exact part of Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}.

(2) is Lemma~\ref{lem:start-weights}(2), divided by $\mu(F)/k$.

(3) By (2), $\log(P^\mu_N(n)/V^\mu_i)=\log S_n(u)-\log(k\mu_i/\mu(F))+O(T^2/N)$,
and $S_n(u)=C_{N,k}/V_N$. So Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-window}(i) gives the
display. The rest follows as in Step~3 of the proof of the asymptotic
assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}, with $a_k(\mu)$ in place of
$a_k$, since $|a_k(\mu)-a_k|\le\Delta$ and the ratio increases strictly in $T$
by (1).
\end{proof}

If $\mu$ is constant on $F$, say $\mu_a=m$ for $a\in F$, then
$P^\mu_N(n)=m\,p^{N-1}S_n(u)$ for every $n$ with support in $F$. So the
statements of Sections~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds}
and~\ref{sec:frontier-boundary} about such bins hold verbatim, with $V_N$
replaced by $V^\mu_a=m\,p^{N-1}$ for a vertex $a\in F$. In particular
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} holds on $F$. Otherwise balancing can fail.

\begin{example}[balancing can fail]\label{ex:start-balance}
Let $d=2$, $N=4$ and $\mu=(\frac12+\varepsilon,\frac12-\varepsilon)$ with
$0<\varepsilon\le\frac12$. Counting the changes in the four words with counts
$(3,1)$ and the six with counts $(2,2)$ gives $P^\mu_4(3,1)=p^3(u+2\mu_1u^2)$
and $P^\mu_4(2,2)=p^3(u+u^2+u^3)$. So
$P^\mu_4(3,1)-P^\mu_4(2,2)=p^3u^2(2\varepsilon-u)$, and the unbalanced bin is
more likely when $u<2\varepsilon$.
\end{example}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:start-crossings}(2), a balanced bin of $F$ has the share
$\mu(F)/k$ of $d\,C_{N,k}$, up to a factor $1+O(T^2/N)$. The next theorem shows
that no bin of $F$, balanced or not, does better by more than a factor
$1+O(1/L)$.

\begin{theorem}[face maxima with a start]\label{thm:start-maxima}
Let $F$ be a face with $k\ge2$ states.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Fix $0<c<C$. For large $N$, uniformly for $cL\le T\le CL$ and over all
$\mu$ with $\mu(F)>0$,
\[
 \max_{\supp n=F}P^\mu_N(n)=\frac{d\mu(F)}k\,C_{N,k}
 \Bigl(1+O\Bigl(\frac1L\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
So with $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$, uniformly for $t$ in compact sets and over all
such $\mu$,
\[
 \log\frac{\max_{\supp n=F}P^\mu_N(n)}{p^{N-1}}
 =\log\frac{\mu(F)}k+(k-1)(t-a_k)+o(1).
\]
\item For $n$ with support $F$ put $y=\sqrt L\,(n/N-\frac1k\mathbf1)$. Fix a compact set $\mathcal J$ of $t$ and $R>0$. For
large $N$, uniformly for $t\in\mathcal J$ and over all $\mu$ with $\mu(F)>0$,
every bin that maximizes $P^\mu_N$ among the bins with support $F$ has
$|y|\le R$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

So a start can move the best bin of $F$ away from the balanced bins, but it
gains at most a factor $1+O(1/L)$, and in the window the best bin stays within
$o(L^{-1/2})$ of the center. The bound holds for every bin of $F$, including bins
with coordinates that are $o(N)$. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:start-field},
and it rests on Lemma~\ref{lem:first-run}. By Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance}, each
$S_{n-je_a}(u)$ is at most the balanced value at total $N-j$, which is about
$e^{-(k-1)ju}$ times the balanced value at total $N$. So $S^{(a)}_n(u)$ is at
most about $u\sum_j(1-u)^{j-1}e^{-(k-1)ju}\approx1/k$ times the balanced value,
and $1/k$ is the start weight of each state at a balanced bin.

Each face now gets a line in the window coordinate $t$, and the highest line
wins.

\begin{corollary}[global modes with a start]\label{cor:start-modes}
Order the start weights as $\mu_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge\mu_{(d)}$, put
$M_k=\mu_{(1)}+\cdots+\mu_{(k)}$ and $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$, and define the
lines
\[
 \ell^\mu_1(t)=\log\mu_{(1)},\qquad
 \ell^\mu_k(t)=\log\frac{M_k}k+(k-1)(t-a_k)\quad(2\le k\le d).
\]
Fix a compact set $\mathcal J$ of $t$ on which $\max_k\ell^\mu_k(t)$ is attained
only at $k=k^*$. For large $N$ and all $t\in\mathcal J$, every global mode of
$P^\mu_N$ has exactly $k^*$ positive coordinates, and its support $S$ has
$\mu(S)=M_{k^*}$. If $k^*=1$, the global modes are exactly the vertices $i$ with
$\mu_i=\mu_{(1)}$. If $k^*\ge2$ and $\mu$ is constant on such a support $S$,
every global mode with support $S$ is a balanced bin, and all balanced bins of
$S$ have the same probability.
If $k^*\ge2$, then for every fixed $R>0$ and large $N$, every global mode $n$
has $|n_i/N-1/k^*|\le R/\sqrt L$ for each $i$ in its support.
\end{corollary}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:start-field}.

\begin{remark}[the order of the winners]\label{rem:start-lines}
The winning size is nondecreasing in $t$. Indeed, if $k_1$ attains
$\max_k\ell^\mu_k$ at $t_1$ and $k_2$ attains it at $t_2>t_1$, adding the two
inequalities $\ell^\mu_{k_1}(t_1)\ge\ell^\mu_{k_2}(t_1)$ and
$\ell^\mu_{k_2}(t_2)\ge\ell^\mu_{k_1}(t_2)$ gives $(k_2-k_1)(t_2-t_1)\ge0$. Put
$t^*=a_d+\frac1{d-1}\log(d\mu_{(1)})$, where $\ell^\mu_1$ and $\ell^\mu_d$ cross.
Since the slope $k-1$ of $\ell^\mu_k$ lies strictly between the slopes $0$ and
$d-1$, a size $2\le k\le d-1$ can be the unique winner at some $t$ only if
$\ell^\mu_k(t^*)>\ell^\mu_1(t^*)$. For the uniform start $t^*=a_d$ and
$\ell^\mu_k(t^*)-\ell^\mu_1(t^*)=(k-1)(a_d-a_k)<0$, in line with
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[the start decides which face wins]\label{ex:K3-start}
Let $d=3$ and $\mu=(\frac12,\frac12,0)$, and put
$t_{\rm ef}=\log\frac32+2a_3-a_2=\frac12\log\frac{32\pi}{243}$. Fix $t$ and let
$N$ be large. If $t<a_2$, the global modes are $(N,0,0)$ and $(0,N,0)$. If
$a_2<t<t_{\rm ef}$, they are exactly the balanced bins of the edge $\{1,2\}$. If
$t>t_{\rm ef}$, every global mode has three positive coordinates. The window
$(a_2,t_{\rm ef})=(-0.4673558,-0.4412978)$ has width
$\log\frac{16}{9\sqrt3}=0.0260580$, while with the uniform start no proper face
wins (Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}).
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
Here $M_1=\frac12$ and $M_2=M_3=1$, so the lines of
Corollary~\ref{cor:start-modes} are $\ell^\mu_1=\log\frac12$,
$\ell^\mu_2=\log\frac12+t-a_2$ and $\ell^\mu_3=\log\frac13+2(t-a_3)$. Hence
$\ell^\mu_2>\ell^\mu_1$ exactly when $t>a_2$, $\ell^\mu_2>\ell^\mu_3$ exactly
when $t<t_{\rm ef}$, and $\ell^\mu_3>\ell^\mu_1$ exactly when $t>t^*$, where
$t^*=a_3+\frac12\log\frac32$, which is the point $t^*$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:start-lines}. By~\eqref{eq:intro-ak},
$t_{\rm ef}-a_2=\log\frac32+2(a_3-a_2)=4\log2-\frac52\log3
=\log\frac{16}{9\sqrt3}$. This is positive, since
$16^2=256>243=(9\sqrt3)^2$. Also $t^*-a_2=\frac12(t_{\rm ef}-a_2)$. So
$a_2<t^*<t_{\rm ef}$, and the unique winning size is $1$ for $t<a_2$, $2$ for
$a_2<t<t_{\rm ef}$ and $3$ for $t>t_{\rm ef}$. Now apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:start-modes} with $\mathcal J=\{t\}$. The vertices with
$\mu_i=\frac12$ are $1$ and $2$. The only edge with $\mu(S)=1$ is $\{1,2\}$, and
$\mu$ is constant on it. So for $a_2<t<t_{\rm ef}$ every global mode is a
balanced bin of $\{1,2\}$. Hence the global maximum equals the maximum over the
bins with support $\{1,2\}$, and since all balanced bins of this edge have the
same probability, each of them attains it.
\end{proof}

Dynamic programs over all bins at $N=90$, $300$ and $1002$ show the three phases at
finite $N$, with the window moved to larger $t$ (numerically verified, see
Section~\ref{sec:verify}). The next remark explains the shift.

\begin{remark}[the window at finite $N$]\label{rem:K3-finite}
Let $u_N$ be the unique positive root of
$S_{(\lceil N/2\rceil,\lfloor N/2\rfloor)}(u)=1$, which is the crossing odds
$(1-p_N)/p_N$ of Paper~A~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{paperA}. Since $\mu$ is constant on
$\{1,2\}$, we have $P^\mu_N(n_1,n_2,0)/P^\mu_N(N,0,0)=S_{(n_1,n_2)}(u)$, so for
every $N\ge2$ the balanced bins of the edge cross the vertices exactly at
$u=u_N$, that is, at $T=Nu_N/(1+2u_N)$. This differs from Paper~A's
$z_N=Nu_N/(1+u_N)$ by $O(L^2/N)$. Near $t=a_2$ the lines of the full face and of
the edges through $3$ stay below those of the vertices, so for large $N$ this
crossing is the lower end of the window. For the two ends at finite $N$ we use
$z=Nu=T/p$. A heuristic calculation, which we do not prove, puts them at
$z-L+\frac12\log z=a_2+\frac1{8z}+\frac{z^2}{2N}+\cdots$ and
$t_{\rm ef}+\frac1{12z}+\frac{3z^2}{2N}+\cdots$. Only $a_2+\frac1{8z}$ is
proved, by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}. The terms $z^2/N$ come from a
discrete term $-\frac{(k-1)^2}2\frac{z^2}N$ in the logarithm
of~\eqref{eq:simplex-uniform-ratio}. Exact computations give the widths
$0.0843$, $0.0324$, $0.0223$ and $0.0214$ at $N=300$, $3000$, $30000$ and
$300000$, and the heuristic gives $0.0802$, $0.0321$, $0.0226$ and $0.0217$
(numerically verified, see Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{remark}
