\section{Derivative limits for a fixed start}\label{app:derivative-limits}

We need first-difference convergence for the strict slope at zero.
The same argument works at every fixed order and for any prescribed
finite initial history. It uses the higher-difference recurrence
of~\cite[(4.10)]{tv2026}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:derivative-limits}
Fix $a\in(1/2,1)$ and a finite initial history. Suppose the rescaled
count law converges weakly to a law with smooth density $f$.
For each fixed integer $r\ge0$, linearly interpolate
\[
 \frac{n^{(r+1)a}}{2^{r+1}}\nabla^rP_n(k)
 \quad\hbox{at}\quad (2k-n-r)n^{-a},
 \qquad \nabla P_n(k)=P_n(k)-P_n(k-1).
\]
These interpolants converge locally uniformly to $f^{(r)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Repeated subtraction in \eqref{eq:P-rec} gives
\[
 \nabla^rP_{n+1}(k)
 =\left(\frac{1+a}{2}-\frac{ak}{n}\right)\nabla^rP_n(k)
 +\left(\frac{1-a}{2}+\frac{a(k-r-1)}n\right)\nabla^rP_n(k-1).
\]
For sufficiently large $n$, all coefficients multiplying nonzero
entries are nonnegative. Interior row sums are $1-(r+1)a/n$.
At either outer endpoint the only nonzero term has coefficient
$(1+a)/2$, which is no larger once $n$ is large.
Iteration gives
\[
 \sup_k|\nabla^rP_n(k)|\le C_{a,r}n^{-(r+1)a}.
\]
The bounds at orders $r$ and $r+1$ give uniform bounds and Lipschitz
constants for the interpolants. They are therefore compact for
locally uniform convergence. Discrete summation by parts against
a smooth compactly supported function identifies every subsequential
limit with the distributional derivative of order $r$ of the weak
limit. The lattice spacing is $2n^{-a}$, so the displayed
normalization is exactly its power $-(r+1)$.
Since $f$ is smooth, this distributional derivative is $f^{(r)}$.
Every subsequence has the same limit, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
