\subsection{Derivative limits}\label{app:derivative-limits}

We first pass finite differences to density derivatives. Trinas and
Valle~\cite[Proposition~2.1 and Theorem~3.1]{tv2026} prove the
corresponding bounds and local limit at orders zero and one.
The following argument derives the recurrence and treats every
fixed order, also after a prescribed finite history.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:derivative-limits}
Fix $a\in(1/2,1)$ and a finite initial history. Extend $P_n$ by zero
outside $\{0,\ldots,n\}$. Suppose the rescaled
count law converges weakly to a law with smooth density $f$.
For each fixed integer $r\ge0$, linearly interpolate
\[
 \frac{n^{(r+1)a}}{2^{r+1}}\nabla^rP_n(k)
 \quad\hbox{at}\quad (2k-n-r)n^{-a},
 \qquad \nabla P_n(k)=P_n(k)-P_n(k-1).
\]
These interpolants converge locally uniformly to $f^{(r)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Repeated subtraction in \eqref{eq:P-rec} gives
\[
 \nabla^rP_{n+1}(k)
 =\left(\frac{1+a}{2}-\frac{ak}{n}\right)\nabla^rP_n(k)
 +\left(\frac{1-a}{2}+\frac{a(k-r-1)}n\right)\nabla^rP_n(k-1).
\]
For sufficiently large $n$, both coefficients are nonnegative for
$1\le k\le n+r$, with sum $1-(r+1)a/n$.
At $k=0$ and $k=n+r+1$, only one summand can be nonzero and
its coefficient is $(1+a)/2$, no larger than that sum once $n$ is large.
Iterating these row-sum bounds gives
\[
 \sup_k|\nabla^rP_n(k)|\le C_{a,r}n^{-(r+1)a}.
\]
The bounds at orders $r$ and $r+1$ give uniform bounds and Lipschitz
constants for the interpolants. Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence converging
locally uniformly. To identify its limit, sum by parts against
a smooth compactly supported function. Weak convergence then gives
the distributional derivative of order $r$ of the limiting law. The lattice spacing is $2n^{-a}$, so the displayed
normalization is exactly its power $-(r+1)$.
Since $f$ is smooth, this distributional derivative is $f^{(r)}$.
Every subsequence has the same limit, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
