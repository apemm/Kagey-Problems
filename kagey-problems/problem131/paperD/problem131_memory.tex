\ABSTRACT{We prove the conjecture of Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon that
the bimodal initial biases form a (possibly empty) open interval centered at one half
for each superdiffusive memory of the elephant random walk.
The interval's endpoints vary continuously with memory, and outside it
the limit density has one local maximum. There is a unique memory threshold,
enclosed near $0.6688128600146$, at or below which the interval is empty.
The fair density has two inflection points below and at this threshold
and four above it. At the threshold the maximum is quartic.
The two modes then separate on a square-root scale, while the bias
interval opens on a three-halves scale. A fourth-moment argument proves
bimodality for memory at least $0.79$ without computation.
Near memory one, two Landau components approximate the density in total
variation and scaled uniform distance, uniformly over the initial bias.

For rare reversals, we give an explicit total variation approximation
by a truncated Luria--Delbr\"uck law and a Landau local limit theorem
with a rate. The bounds locate every mode. For even length $N$, the origin is as
likely as each end at exactly one reversal probability, with $N\varepsilon=2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+o(1)$.
For every even $N\ge8$, this crossing law has two symmetric modal
plateaus, with the center as its only interior minimum.}

\section*{A note on the use of AI}
I wrote the original draft of this paper and its mathematics. I used Claude
Opus 5.5 (Anthropic, through Claude Code) for literature searches, citation
checks and referee-style reviews that informed the framing and abstract.
I read every source cited in the paper, except that for Drezner and
Farnum~\cite{dreznerfarnum1993} and Heyde~\cite{heyde2004} I relied on their
abstracts and the account of James, James and Qi~\cite{jamesjamesqi2008}.
I proposed and drafted the following
additions: Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic-density}; the strictness and
inflection-point clauses of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes}; the fourth-moment
extension of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one} to $a\ge4/5$; the Jensen
lower bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(a); and the weaker
weak-convergence hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:landau}(c).
I used GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI, through Codex) to revise the manuscript and to
revise the proofs of these additions. Proofs aside from the aforementioned were not altered by either model.
The associated Lean files in
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperD} were originally written by me and edited using
GPT-6 Astra through Codex. Section~\ref{sec:verify} distinguishes the formal
proofs, their named classical hypotheses and the separate numerical checks.
I take responsibility for the claims, proofs and computations in the paper.
