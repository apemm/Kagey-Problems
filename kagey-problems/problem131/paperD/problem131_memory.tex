\begin{abstract}
In the elephant random walk each step copies a uniformly chosen earlier step
and reverses it with probability $\varepsilon$. For a walk with an even number
$N$ of steps we study the crossing, the value of $\varepsilon$ at which the
walk is as likely to end at the origin as at $N$. We prove that the crossing
is unique and that $N\varepsilon$ at the crossing equals
$2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+o(1)$. This is about twice the value for the
persistent random walk, which copies its last step instead, because a walk
that ends at the origin usually has a reversal among its first few steps. We
also describe the whole law at the crossing. The number of steps that disagree
with the first step is close to a compound Poisson law of Luria--Delbr\"uck
type, and after centering and scaling it converges to the Landau law. So the
law of the final position has a hump near each of $\pm N$, and we locate the
humps. Its second difference changes sign at most four times. For every large
even $N$, and for every even $N$ from $10$ to $2000$, the origin is less likely
than every other position the walk can reach, except $\pm N$. The threshold
for large $N$ is not explicit, so the even $N$ above $2000$ and below it remain
open.
\end{abstract}
