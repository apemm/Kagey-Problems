\begin{abstract}
In the elephant random walk each step copies a uniformly chosen earlier step
and reverses it with probability $\varepsilon$, as in broadcasting on a random
recursive tree. We let $\varepsilon\to0$ as the number $N$ of steps grows and
ask when the walk is as likely to end at the origin as at $N$. For even $N$ we
prove that this crossing value of $\varepsilon$ is unique and that
$N\varepsilon$ at the crossing equals $2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+o(1)$, about
twice the value for the persistent random walk. Explicit two-sided bounds
show that the walk ends at the origin with probability
$4\varepsilon/(N+2)$ up to a factor $1+O(\varepsilon\log N)$. To first order
in $\varepsilon$, such a walk reverses within its first few steps. We also describe the whole law at
the crossing. The number of steps that disagree with the first step is within
an explicit distance of a compound Poisson law of Luria--Delbr\"uck type, and
after centering and scaling it obeys a local limit theorem with the Landau
law. So the law of the final position has a hump near each of $\pm N$, which
we locate. For every $N$ and every $\varepsilon<\frac12$ its second difference
changes sign at most four times. For every large even $N$, at the crossing,
the origin is less likely than every other position the walk can reach except
$\pm N$, and a computer-assisted proof gives the same for every even $N$ from
$10$ to $2000$. The even $N$ between $2000$ and the threshold for large $N$,
which is not explicit, remain open.
\end{abstract}
