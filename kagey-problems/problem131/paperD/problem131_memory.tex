\ABSTRACT{Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon conjectured that the
initial biases giving the superdiffusive elephant random walk a
bimodal limit law form an interval centered at one half. We prove
this. For every superdiffusive memory, strongly biased starts are
unimodal, and the limit density has two local maxima exactly when
the bias lies in a proper open interval. Outside it, including
its endpoints, the density has a unique local maximum. The
interval is nonempty exactly above a single memory threshold,
numerically about $0.6688$, whose uniqueness follows from a
positive-weight integral for the central curvature of the
fair-start density. We control sign changes in the finite walk's
first and second differences, including those created at the
boundary, and pass them to the limit. The bias boundary is
continuous and tends to zero as memory tends to one.}

\section*{A note on the use of AI}
I wrote the original draft of this paper and its mathematics. I used Claude
Opus 5.5 (Anthropic, through Claude Code) for literature searches, citation
checks and referee-style reviews. The reviews informed the framing and
abstract and suggested proving that the bias boundary is positive, that
the transition is unique, and that the critical top is quartic without
computation. I read every source cited in the paper except
Eisenberger~\cite{eisenberger1964} and Robertson and
Fryer~\cite{robertsonfryer1969}, for which I relied on the publisher abstracts.
I proposed and drafted the following
additions: Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic-density}; the strictness and
inflection-point clauses of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes}; the fourth-moment
extension of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one} to $a\ge4/5$; the Jensen
lower bound in Theorem~4.5(a) of the archived combined version; and the weaker
weak-convergence hypothesis in its Theorem~5.9(c).
I used GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI, through Codex) to revise the manuscript and to
revise the proofs of these additions. I also used it to develop and draft
proofs, including the analytic quartic-sign argument, the memory-regularity
argument, and continuity and local decrease of the bias boundary.
The associated Lean files in
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperD} were generated and extended
with Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra through Codex. I also used Codex to run
the Lean build and axiom audit recorded in the verification archive.
Section~\ref{sec:verify} and the coverage map distinguish the formal proofs,
their hypotheses, the written proofs and the separate numerical checks.
I take responsibility for the claims, proofs and computations in the paper.
