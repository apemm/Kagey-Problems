\begin{abstract}
In the elephant random walk each step copies a uniformly chosen earlier step and
reverses it with probability $\varepsilon$. We let $\varepsilon$ tend to zero as the
number of steps $N$ grows, with $N\varepsilon$ of order $\log N$. The number of steps
that disagree with the first step is approximated by a truncated Luria--Delbr\"uck
law, with an explicit total variation bound. Its Landau local limit has an
explicit rate and locates every mode near one of the two ends. The same Landau
law arises from the fixed-memory superdiffusive limit as the memory tends to one.
For even $N$, the origin and each end cross exactly once, at an expected reversal
count of $2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+o(1)$. We bound the relative error of the
origin probability $4\varepsilon/(N+2)$. An exact sign argument proves that, at the
crossing, the origin is the unique interior minimum for every even $N\ge8$.
The law has exactly two symmetric intervals of maximizing bins, with strict
increase from the ends and strict decrease toward the center. This uses
certified endpoint comparisons at finitely many lengths. For a reversible
fluctuation assay with one sensitive founder and symmetric switching at birth,
the total variation bound controls the error of the compound Poisson experiment.
We also prove finite-sample concentration and mean-bias bounds for its
constrained maximum-likelihood estimator, and uniform consistency when the
number of independent cultures grows as the cube of the logarithm of their size.
\end{abstract}
