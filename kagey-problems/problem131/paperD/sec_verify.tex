\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

Apart from short exact ratios such as Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp},
the proofs use four numerical certificates.
First, the 60-term sum in Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(b) lies in
$[102.586396,102.586398]$, as checked by \nolinkurl{certify_elephant.py}.
With the analytic tail, it is below $102.59$, giving the constant $5200$.
This value fixes the explicit thresholds in Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}
and the second-order results. Second, the same script certifies the
Landau derivative and curvature enclosures of
Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}. Lean checks these independently.

Third, \texttt{certify\_center.py} supplies the finite endpoint-dip
input of Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} for $10\le N\le2000$. It brackets
each crossing and checks by an exact rational comparison that the lower end
of the bracket exceeds $2/(N+2)$. It also checks the center minimum
independently, which Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min} no longer needs. The
script \texttt{certify\_convex.py} supports only the finite convexity window
\ifPaperDcombined
of Section~S5 (Proposition~\ref{prop:convex-certified}).
\else
of the extended version supplied with this manuscript.
\fi
The dated outputs and rational witnesses in \nolinkurl{data/} record
script hashes and software versions. Certification scripts reject failed
checks and, where applicable, empty ranges.

Fourth, \texttt{certify\_threshold.py} proves
the classification outside the bracket in Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}
(Appendix~\ref{app:threshold}). It
imports \nolinkurl{threshold_taylor.py}, \nolinkurl{threshold_hankel.py} and
\nolinkurl{threshold_pointwise.py}. Its 9 runs are recorded in
\nolinkurl{data/threshold_*.txt}, with source hashes, versions, exit status
and signed accepted intervals. The mode
\texttt{check} verifies that the records join into the cover used in the
proof, with the sign $-$ on all of $(\frac12,z_-]$ and $+$ on all of
$[z_+,0.6712]$. The soundness checks raise exceptions and are not
Python \texttt{assert} statements, so \texttt{python -O} does not remove them.
Theorems~\ref{thm:two-modes} and~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one} use no
computation. The script
\texttt{verify\_elephant\_modes.py} checks the rational arithmetic in the
fourth-moment proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one}, retains checks
of the earlier two-moment bound for $a\ge39/40$, and computes the finite-$N$ numbers of
Remark~\ref{rem:modes-threshold}, which are not proof inputs. Its versioned
output is \nolinkurl{data/verify_modes_output.txt}.

The extension \texttt{certify\_transition.py} closes the unresolved bracket
by certifying $0.68<\partial_a g_a''(0)<0.70$ and
$-0.09<g_a^{(4)}(0)<-0.07$ on a larger interval containing it.
It bounds the actual parameter derivative through the differentiated
smoothing equation. The source hashes, interval enclosures and checks are in
\nolinkurl{data/transition_derivative_certificate.json}; the independent
rerun at higher precision is recorded in
\nolinkurl{data/transition_derivative_run.txt}. All checks remain active
under \texttt{python -O}.

The Lean~4.33.1 library in
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperD}, with Mathlib revision
\texttt{0df444a360ea}, checks the finite identities, crossing bounds,
complete modal intervals, maximum-height window and sign arguments.
It uses only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. Generated integer certificates use exact outward
rounding, without \texttt{native\_decide}.
The bulk and eventual-shape proofs retain six explicit classical inputs:
Fourier inversion, the compound Poisson characteristic function,
Efron--Stein, Scheff\'e~\cite{scheffe1947}, class-$L$ unimodality and
Hoeffding's hypergeometric bound. The enlarged weak-Landau range uses
only the first three, with Mathlib's L\'evy theorem; the Jensen bound
has no additional classical input. The statement-by-statement map,
including these dependencies, is in \nolinkurl{MAP.md} and
\nolinkurl{coverage_tables.tex}.

The entire extension and strict shape theorem are formalized from the
limit density, convergence in law, the conditional characteristic
function's smoothing inequality and the density's Fourier representation.
Its continuity, unit bound, symmetry and decay follow from a probability
measure with density. The fourth-moment proof for $a\ge4/5$ uses the
raw moment formulas of~\cite{glr2026}; Lean proves the unimodal kurtosis
inequality directly. The all-bias first-difference theorem and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dominant-half} use the actual finite law, including
both boundary histories. The lattice local limit~\cite{tv2026}, conditional
unimodality and Fourier representation then give the centred bias interval
in Theorem~\ref{thm:biased-limit-modes}. Its no-flatness, half-line shape
and mode-count bounds are conclusions, not hypotheses.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:near-one-profile}, Portmanteau supplies interval
masses from the stated weak bridge. Lean then proves localization,
peak-height convergence, uniform profiles and $L^1$ convergence, using
the fair curvature theorem, conditional unimodality and Landau density
facts. Reflection and mixing give uniformity in the bias, the two peak
windows of Corollary~\ref{cor:biased-near-one-peaks}, and $b_a\to0$.

For the transition, Lean checks the inhomogeneous maximum argument,
dominated Fourier differentiation, rational slack inequalities, and the
passage from the derivative bounds to a unique transition with unimodal
critical density. The detailed differentiated-smoothing integral estimates
and Arb transcendental enclosures remain separate written and numerical
proofs. Their bounds are explicit inputs to the formal interface.
The total variation upgrade of Theorem~\ref{thm:cp} and the martingale
proof of the fixed-memory weak bridge also remain unformalized; the
earlier pointwise compound Poisson bound is formalized.
The current source inventory and full axiom audit are in
\nolinkurl{reports/paperD-transition-2026-11-01}.

Each numerical record identifies its software versions. The derivative
extension was rerun with Python~3.12.14 and python-flint~0.8.0, at
$1600$-bit precision with $1100$ moments. Figures and other numerical
illustrations are not proof inputs. The repository gives rerun commands.
