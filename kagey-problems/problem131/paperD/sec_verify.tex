\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

The proofs use four certified computations, apart from short exact
ratios such as those in Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}.
First, \texttt{certify\_elephant.py} encloses the 60-term sum in
Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(b) in $[102.586396,102.586398]$.
With the analytic tail, it is below $102.59$, giving the constant $5200$.
Any finite bound suffices asymptotically, but this value determines
the explicit thresholds in Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} and the
second-order results. Second, the same script certifies the
Landau derivative and curvature enclosures of
Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}. Lean checks these independently.

Third, \texttt{certify\_center.py} supplies the finite endpoint-dip
input of Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} for $10\le N\le2000$. It brackets
each crossing and checks by an exact rational comparison that the lower end
of the bracket exceeds $2/(N+2)$. It also checks the center minimum
independently, which Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min} no longer needs. The
script \texttt{certify\_convex.py} supports only the finite convexity window
\ifPaperDcombined
of Section~S5 (Proposition~\ref{prop:convex-certified}).
\else
of the extended version supplied with this manuscript.
\fi
The outputs and rational witnesses are in
\nolinkurl{data/certify_center_output_2026-10-17.txt},
\nolinkurl{data/certify_convex_output_2026-10-17.txt},
\nolinkurl{data/witnesses_center_2026-10-17.jsonl} and
\nolinkurl{data/witnesses_convex_2026-10-17.jsonl}.
The witnesses record script hashes and software versions.
Each certification script fails if a required check fails, and
range-based scripts also reject empty ranges.

Fourth, \texttt{certify\_threshold.py} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} as described in Appendix~\ref{app:threshold}. It
imports \texttt{threshold\_taylor.py}, \texttt{threshold\_hankel.py} and
\texttt{threshold\_pointwise.py}. Its 9 runs are recorded in
\nolinkurl{data/threshold_*.txt}, each with a header that records the hashes
of the 4 scripts and the software versions, and with the exit status as its
last line. Each accepted interval is printed with its sign. The mode
\texttt{check} verifies that the records join into the cover used in the
proof, with the sign $-$ on all of $(\frac12,z_-]$ and $+$ on all of
$[z_+,0.6712]$. The soundness checks raise exceptions and are not
Python \texttt{assert} statements, so \texttt{python -O} does not remove them. Theorems~\ref{thm:two-modes}
and~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one} use no computation. The script
\texttt{verify\_elephant\_modes.py} checks the rational arithmetic in the
fourth-moment proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one}, retains checks
of the earlier two-moment bound for $a\ge39/40$, and computes the finite-$N$ numbers of
Remark~\ref{rem:modes-threshold}, which are not proof inputs. Its output,
with a header that records the hash of the script and the software versions,
is in \nolinkurl{data/verify_modes_output.txt}.

The Lean~4.33.1 library in
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperD} proves the finite
identities, crossing bounds, sign arguments and certificate checks.
The existing formal bulk and eventual-shape proofs use six named classical inputs,
namely Fourier inversion, the compound Poisson characteristic function,
Efron--Stein, Scheff\'e's theorem~\cite{scheffe1947}, unimodality of class-$L$ laws, and Hoeffding's
hypergeometric bound. Their sources and precise formal status are in
\nolinkurl{MAP.md} and \nolinkurl{coverage_tables.tex}.
The library uses Mathlib revision \texttt{0df444a360ea}. The recorded
kernel build reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}
and \texttt{Quot.sound}. Generated certificate entries are checked with
exact outward-rounded integer arithmetic, without \texttt{native\_decide}.
The kernel checks the proof terms independently of who wrote or edited them;
matching the formal statements to the manuscript remains a separate check.
This does not formalize the six classical inputs. Lemma~\ref{lem:center-no-holes} and the full
all-even shape in Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min} are kernel-checked,
including both complete intervals of maximizers. The composed theorem
\texttt{shape\_at\_crossing\_all\_even} needs only $N=2h$ with $h\ge4$.
The maximum-height window in Corollary~\ref{cor:height-window} and
Lemmas~\ref{lem:descartes}, \ref{lem:sp-limit} and~\ref{lem:sp-derivative} are also
kernel-checked. The November revision formalizes the analytic argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic-density}, including an entire extension, and the
strict monotonicity, exact local maxima and two-or-four inflection-point
classification of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes}. Its explicit inputs from
the limit-law theory are the density and convergence in law, the
smoothing inequality for the conditional-first-step characteristic
function, and the Fourier representation of the fair-start density.
Continuity, the unit bound, symmetry and decay of the characteristic
function follow in Lean from a probability measure with a measurable
density. The smoothing inequality is never imposed on the fair-start law.
The fourth-moment extension to $a\ge4/5$ is also kernel-checked, from the
explicit second- and fourth-moment formulas of~\cite{glr2026}; the
unimodal kurtosis bound is proved directly for a decreasing even density.
The sharper Jensen bound has no additional classical input. The enlarged
weak-Landau range is formalized from LFI, CP and ES by characteristic
functions and L\'evy's continuity theorem from Mathlib; it does not require
the separate Scheff\'e hypothesis. The total variation strengthening of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}, the fixed-memory bridge and
Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} remain unformalized. The earlier pointwise
compound Poisson bound remains valid. The revision's source inventory,
build log and full axiom audit are recorded in
\nolinkurl{reports/paperD-formalization-2026-11-01}.

The archived certification runs used Python~3.14.3, python-flint~0.9.0
(FLINT~3.6.0, including Arb), mpmath~1.3.0, numpy~2.4.2 and scipy~1.17.1.
The figures, tables and other numerical illustrations are not proof
inputs. Further checks and rerun commands are documented in the
repository named under Code availability.
