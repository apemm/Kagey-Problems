\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

{\sloppy
All computations are in Python, and the main ones were done by two independent
methods (for example, forward and backward recursions for the elephant center
agree to about $10^{-14}$ at $N=12800$). Apart from short exact computations
that can be checked by hand, such as the ratios $C_N/E_N$ for $N=2$, $4$, $6$
and $8$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}, the proofs use computation in three
places. First, some constants are values of
explicit sums and integrals, such as the 60-term sum in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(b), which we enclosed in ball arithmetic. It lies
in $[102.586396,102.586398]$, so the series is below the bound $102.59$ used
in the proof even with the tail added. Any finite bound suffices for the
asymptotic statements. The value $5200$ matters for the thresholds $1688$ and
$1689$ in Propositions~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} and~\ref{prop:odd}(c), and for
the second-order bound in Online Resource~1. Second,
Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode} rests on enclosures of $f_L'$ at four points and
of $f_L''$ on an interval, computed in ball arithmetic with
rigorous error bounds. These two computations are in
\texttt{certify\_elephant.py}. Third, for every $10\le N\le2000$ the script
\texttt{certify\_convex.py} encloses the crossing $\varepsilon_N$ in ball
arithmetic and then encloses the whole law $P_N$ at the crossing.
Proposition~\ref{prop:convex-certified} rests on this computation (about an
hour for the whole range, with its output in
\nolinkurl{data/certify_convex_output_2026-10-17.txt}). The script
\texttt{certify\_center.py} repeats it for every even $N$ in the range and
proves Proposition~\ref{prop:center-small} (about half an hour, with its
output in \nolinkurl{data/certify_center_output_2026-10-17.txt}). It also
checks, by an exact rational comparison, that the lower end of the enclosure
of $\varepsilon_N$ lies above $2/(N+2)$ for every even $10\le N\le2000$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:mlr} this gives $C_N<E_N$ at $\varepsilon=2/(N+2)$,
which is Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} for $10\le N<1688$. With the option
\texttt{-{}-witness} the two scripts also write a record for each $N$, with the
rational ends of the enclosure of $\varepsilon_N$, the indices that can be a
mode and the certified margins, under a header with the SHA-256 hashes of the
scripts and the versions of Python and python-flint. The records of these runs
are in \nolinkurl{data/witnesses_convex_2026-10-17.jsonl} and
\nolinkurl{data/witnesses_center_2026-10-17.jsonl}. Each of the three
\texttt{certify} scripts exits with nonzero status if any required
certification fails, and the two scripts that take a range of $N$ also refuse
an empty range. In \texttt{certify\_center.py} the direct comparison of every
bin with the center is a diagnostic. It is required only for an $N$ at which
the test on the modes fails, and in the run of October~17 it succeeded for
all $996$ values of $N$. The
outputs of earlier runs are kept in the folder \nolinkurl{data}, and the file
\nolinkurl{paperD/README.md} lists the statement numbers they use. The tables,
the figures and the numbers quoted outside the proofs rest on computation
alone, and so does the evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:convex} beyond what
Section~\ref{sec:convex} proves. The script \texttt{verify\_elephant\_odd.py} checks the identity
of Proposition~\ref{prop:odd}(a) in exact rational arithmetic for six values of $N$, and it
computes the numbers of Remark~\ref{rem:convex-open} for the Landau density in floating point.

Many statements of the paper are also proved in Lean~4, and the Lean kernel
checks every proof. They are Lemmas~\ref{lem:erw-reduction} and~\ref{lem:urn},
Propositions~\ref{prop:mlr} and~\ref{prop:first-mutation}, and
Theorems~\ref{thm:erw-bounds} and~\ref{thm:erw-crossing}. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-center} is proved in Lean as stated. Lean proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(b) for every even $N\ge2$, and the last display of
that theorem for $N\ge64$. For smaller $N$ the display follows from parts~(a)
and~(b) by taking logarithms. The constant $5200$
needs no ball arithmetic there, since Lean bounds the 60-term sum by exact
partial sums. Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-markov} is proved in Lean with its one input
from~\cite{paperA} as a hypothesis. That input is Proposition~3.8
of~\cite{paperA}, and the Lean files of that paper prove it. Lean also has the
random recursive tree with its marks, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:tree},
\ref{lem:split} and~\ref{lem:firstmark} are proved for it. This includes the
relabeling in Lemma~\ref{lem:split}. Lemma~\ref{lem:unimodal},
Theorem~\ref{thm:dip}, Corollary~\ref{cor:dip} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} are proved. So are Lemmas~\ref{lem:D-rec},
\ref{lem:interleave} and~\ref{lem:level-one}, Theorems~\ref{thm:five-blocks}
and~\ref{thm:no-holes}, Corollary~\ref{cor:one-point},
Lemma~\ref{lem:center-convex}, Propositions~\ref{prop:convex-certified}
and~\ref{prop:center-small}, and the first paragraph and parts~(a) and~(c) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:odd}. Appendices~\ref{app:erw} and~\ref{app:signs} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:B-D} are proved too. In Appendix~\ref{app:erw} the one exception
is the reading of Lemma~\ref{lem:A-polya}(a) as a probability for $c>0$, which
the proofs do not use. So parts~(a), (b) and~(d) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-shape} are proved in Lean.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp} Lean uses three arguments. For
$8\le N\le200$ it checks $C_N<E_N$ at $\varepsilon=2/(N+2)$ by an exact integer
recursion. For $N\ge1688$ the claim follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}(b). For $200<N<1688$ Lean uses checkers whose
soundness it proves. They enclose the law $P_N$ in integer arithmetic with
outward rounding, for every $\varepsilon$ in a short interval at once. Two runs
at the ends of the interval show that it contains $\varepsilon_N$, and a third
run on the whole interval gives the inequalities. The same checkers prove
Propositions~\ref{prop:convex-certified} and~\ref{prop:center-small} in Lean
for every $N$ in their range. The ends of the intervals come from the witness
files, and a script chooses the precision of each run and writes it into the
Lean files. Nothing that the script writes is trusted. For $N>100$ each row of
the law is packed into one large integer, so that a step of the recursion
takes a few operations on exact integers. The kernel evaluates every step of
every run. For the longest runs the Lean files also give the rows at a few
intermediate times, and the kernel recomputes each stretch between two of them
and compares. So no proof rests on compiled code, and none uses
\texttt{native\_decide}.

The bulk is not formalized. This covers Theorems~\ref{thm:cp}
and~\ref{thm:landau}, Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}, whose proof is a computation
in ball arithmetic, and what rests on them. That is
Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-location}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main-bulk},
Appendix~\ref{app:bulk} and Appendix~\ref{app:shape} apart from
Lemma~\ref{lem:B-D}. For Theorem~\ref{thm:convex-large},
Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min} and Proposition~\ref{prop:odd}(d) Lean proves
only the last step, with Steps~1 to~3 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex-large} as hypotheses, since these steps use
Theorem~\ref{thm:landau} and Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-location}. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-shape}(c) is not formalized. Neither are
Proposition~\ref{prop:odd}(b) and Online Resource~1. The classical tools of the
proofs for the bulk, such as Fourier inversion, L\'evy's continuity theorem and
the inequality of Efron and Stein, are not formalized either. The scripts in
Python and Arb are separate from Lean. The file \nolinkurl{MAP.md} in the
folder \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperD} lists each statement
of the paper with its Lean names and its status. The same library also holds
the Lean files of a separate paper. A recorded build of October~18, 2026, whose
source hashes match the present files, checks every theorem and lemma of the
library. It reports only the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}
and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The Lean files use Lean~4.33.1 and Mathlib at commit
\texttt{0df444a360ea} (the full revision is in the file
\nolinkurl{lake-manifest.json} of the Lean project). The scripts ran under Python~3.14.3 with
python-flint~0.9.0 (which contains FLINT~3.6.0 and with it Arb),
mpmath~1.3.0, numpy~2.4.2 and scipy~1.17.1.

The scripts and the Lean files are in the repository named under ``Code
availability'' in the Statements and Declarations, which also says how to rerun the
checks.
\par}
