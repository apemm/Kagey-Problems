\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon proved that the superdiffusive
elephant random walk has a unimodal limit density when its first step
is fixed~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. A biased first step mixes this
density with its reflection. They conjectured that the biases giving
two modes form an open interval centered at one half
\cite[Remark~2.1(b)]{glrs2025}. Trinas and Valle subsequently disproved
the related conjecture of eventual log-concavity~\cite{tv2026}.
We prove the bias-interval conjecture and locate the unique memory at
which the fair-start density acquires two modes.

The same finite shape bounds explain both this transition and the
two humps produced by rare reversals. In either regime, extra modes
cannot appear in the interior of the count law. The point is to control
what enters at its boundaries. This also gives the exact number of
inflection points of the fair limit density.

\subsection{Setting and notation}\label{sec:notation}

At each step the elephant walk~\cite{schutz2004} copies a uniformly chosen earlier step
and reverses it with probability $\varepsilon$. The walk takes $N$ steps $X_1,\dots,X_N\in\{+1,-1\}$, with $+1$ denoting right.
Put $A_N=\#\{i\le N:X_i=+1\}$ and $P_N(k)=\Prob(A_N=k)$. The position
$2A_N-N$ is at the origin for $A_N=N/2$ and at an end for $A_N\in\{0,N\}$.
Unless stated otherwise, $\Prob(X_1=+1)=\frac12$, giving
$P_N(k)=P_N(N-k)$. We write $\Prob_+$ and $\E_+$ for conditioning on $X_1=+1$.
For even $N$ put $h=N/2$, $C_N=P_N(h)$ and $E_N=P_N(N)=P_N(0)$, the center
and end masses. A reference to $C_N$ presupposes even $N$; the crossing solves
$C_N=E_N$. All logarithms are natural, $L=\log N$,
$H_k=\sum_{j\le k}1/j$ is the $k$-th harmonic number, and $\gamma$ is Euler's
constant. In the rare-reversal results, limits, $O(\cdot)$ and $o(\cdot)$
refer to $N\to\infty$, over even
$N$ when $C_N$ appears. The constants implied by $O(\cdot)$ are absolute
unless we name what they depend on.

The reversal probability is $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, $x=N\varepsilon$, and
the crossing is $\varepsilon_N$, with $x_N=N\varepsilon_N$.


For fixed memory put $a=1-2\varepsilon\in(1/2,1)$ and
$S_N=2A_N-N$. Conditional on $X_1=1$, the almost-sure limit of
$S_N/N^a$ has density $h_a$. With $\Prob(X_1=1)=q$, its density is
\[
 g_{a,q}(u)=q h_a(u)+(1-q)h_a(-u),\qquad g_a=g_{a,1/2}.
\]
The copying probability in the original notation is $p=(1+a)/2$.
In the finite recurrences we use $r_t(k)=\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)k/t$.

\subsection{The bias interval and its two limiting regimes}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{theorem}[the bias interval and the memory threshold]\label{thm:main-limit}
For each $a\in(1/2,1)$ there is $b_a\in[0,1/2]$ such that $g_{a,q}$
has exactly two local maxima if and only if $b_a<q<1-b_a$.
Otherwise it is unimodal with a unique local maximum, including at the
two endpoints. The interval is empty exactly when $a\le a_*$, where
$a_*$ is unique and
\[
 0.668812860014597754<a_*<0.668812860014601029.
\]
The fair density has exactly two inflection points for $a\le a_*$
and four for $a>a_*$. Bimodality for $a\ge79/100$ has a proof
without computation. At $a_*$ the top is quartic, and the two fair
modes emerge at distance proportional to $\sqrt{a-a_*}$. The bias
interval has half-width asymptotic to a positive constant times
$(a-a_*)^{3/2}$, and strictly widens just above the threshold.
\end{theorem}

The interval assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:biased-limit-modes}.
Theorems~\ref{thm:two-modes}--\ref{thm:threshold} give the fair-start
classification and its certified bracket. Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-shape}
describes the critical top. The equation for $b_a$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:bias-boundary} makes the bias interval explicit in
terms of the conditional density.
Proposition~\ref{prop:bias-cusp} gives the three-halves law and local
monotonicity of its width.

\begin{theorem}[the profile near memory one]\label{thm:main-near-one}
Put $\varepsilon=(1-a)/2$ and
$A_\varepsilon=1+2\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Let $f_L$ be the
Landau density with characteristic function
$\exp\{-it\log|t|-\pi|t|/2\}$, and let $\lambda_{\mathrm L}$ be its mode.
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the density
\[
 \frac{q}{2\varepsilon}f_L\left(\frac{A_\varepsilon-u}{2\varepsilon}\right)
 +\frac{1-q}{2\varepsilon}f_L\left(\frac{A_\varepsilon+u}{2\varepsilon}\right)
\]
approximates $g_{1-2\varepsilon,q}$ in $L^1$ and in supremum norm
multiplied by $2\varepsilon$, uniformly over $q\in[0,1]$.
In particular $b_a\to0$. For a fair start the positive mode is
$A_\varepsilon-2\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathrm L}+o(\varepsilon)$ and
its height is $(f_L(\lambda_{\mathrm L})+o(1))/(4\varepsilon)$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:near-one-profile} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:biased-near-one-peaks} prove these assertions.

\begin{theorem}[the finite shape bounds]\label{thm:shape-engine}
For every $N\ge1$, every $0<\varepsilon<1$ and every initial bias,
the count law has at most two modal plateaus, including its endpoints.
If $q\ge1/2$, it dominates its reflection on the right half and has
no strict valley there. For a fair start, $N\ge3$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$, the nonzero signs of
\[
 D_N(k)=P_N(k+1)-2P_N(k)+P_N(k-1),\qquad 1\le k<N,
\]
after equal neighbors are merged, form a subsequence of $(+,-,+,-,+)$.
\end{theorem}

These statements are Proposition~\ref{prop:all-bias-modes},
Proposition~\ref{prop:dominant-half} and Theorem~\ref{thm:five-blocks}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-shape-transfer} transfers the finite valley bounds
to a continuous limit using weak convergence alone. For curvature we
first smooth by a Gaussian, then use the same sign bound.

\subsection{Rare reversals}\label{sec:rare-results}

Now let $\varepsilon\to0$ with $x=N\varepsilon$ of order $\log N$.
The center and a specified end cross at about twice as many reversals
as in the persistent walk~\cite{paperA}. The reason is that an early elephant
reversal turns an entire family of descendants, not just one run.
Section~\ref{sec:erw} gives the calculation.

Let $M_N$ count steps opposite to $X_1$.
Its law $f_N(m)=\Prob(M_N=m)$ does not depend on $X_1$, and
$P_N(k)=\frac12[f_N(N-k)+f_N(k)]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:erw-reduction}). Let
$\CP_N$ be the law of $\sum_{d=1}^{N-1}d\,\mathcal N_d$, where the
$\mathcal N_d$ are independent Poisson variables with means $x/(d(d+1))$.
Put $\lambda_m=(m-x\log x)/x$, and let
\[
 f_L(\lambda)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\lambda t}e^{B(t)}\,dt,
 \qquad B(t)=-it\log\lvert t\rvert-\frac\pi2\lvert t\rvert,
\]
be the Landau density.

\begin{theorem}[the approximation]\label{thm:main-bulk}
For $N\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$ the total variation distance between
$f_N$ and $\CP_N$ is at most an explicit $\delta_\Sigma=O(x^2\log^2N/N)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}). Suppose that $x\to\infty$ and $x^3\log^2N/N\to0$.
Then, uniformly over integers $m\ge0$,
\[
 \bigl\lvert xf_N(m)-f_L(\lambda_m)\bigr\rvert
 =O\Bigl(\frac{1+\log x}{x}+\frac{x^3\log^2N}{N}\Bigr).
\]
The weak convergence $(M_N-x\log x)/x\Rightarrow f_L$ needs only
$x\to\infty$ and $x^2\log^2N/N\to0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:landau} gives explicit finite-$N$ bounds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the modes]\label{thm:main-modes}
The Landau density has a unique mode $\lambda_{\mathrm L}\approx-0.222783$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}, computer-assisted).
For $\log N\le x\le2\log N$ and all sufficiently large $N$, every mode
$a^*\ge N/2$ of $P_N$ satisfies
\[
 \bigl\lvert N-a^*-x\log x-\lambda_{\mathrm L}x\bigr\rvert
 \le 400\sqrt{x\log x},
\]
and the same bound holds for every mode of $f_N$ in place of $N-a^*$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-location}). The numerical constant is explicit, although the length threshold is not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the center and the crossing]\label{thm:main-center}
Let $N\ge2$ be even.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The ratio $C_N/E_N$ is strictly increasing in $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, and
the crossing $\varepsilon_N$ exists, is unique and lies in $(0,\frac12)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mlr}).
\item For $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$ we have
$C_N\ge\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1-\varepsilon)^{2-12/(N+4)}
\ge\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1-2\varepsilon)$. If
$\varepsilon(1+\log N)\le\frac1{100}$, then
$C_N\le\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}\,N^{2\varepsilon}e^{5200\varepsilon}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}, whose constant $5200$ uses an enclosure in ball
arithmetic).
\item As $N\to\infty$ over even $N$, the crossing satisfies
$x_N+\log x_N=2\log N-\log8+O(\log^2N/N)$, and so
\[
 x_N=2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+O\Bigl(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\Bigr)
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-crossing}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

So $C_N=\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1+O(\varepsilon\log N))$ when
$\varepsilon\log N$ is small, and the error factor is $1+O(\log^2N/N)$ at the
crossing.
For the shape between the humps put
$D_N(a)=P_N(a+1)-2P_N(a)+P_N(a-1)$ and $m^*=N-a^*$ for a mode $a^*\ge N/2$.

\begin{theorem}[the shape]\label{thm:main-shape}
For every even $N\ge8$, the crossing law has exactly two symmetric
intervals of maximizing points. It strictly rises to each interval and
strictly falls from it toward the center, its unique interior minimum
(Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min}). This uses the finite endpoint-dip
certificates of Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}. The whole statement,
certificates included, is kernel-checked in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{How the proofs fit together}\label{sec:architecture}

\paragraph{The boundary histories.}
The forward recurrence is a positive two-parent average. Inside the
row, the first and second differences satisfy recurrences of the same
kind, with shifted coefficients. Such an average cannot create an
extra alternating pattern. It can still inherit a change of sign
from either edge, so a bound based only on the interior recurrence
would be incomplete. We compute the boundary differences through
time and place those histories alongside the current row. The
augmented row then has a fixed sign pattern that induction preserves.
For first differences this allows two modal plateaus. For the fair
second difference it allows five sign blocks.

\paragraph{The half favored by the first step.}
Two possible modes alone do not imply an interval of bimodal biases.
For $q\ge1/2$ we also prove reflection dominance and absence of a valley
on the right half. Every valley must then be witnessed by a decrease
on the left half. For fixed points there, this decrease is an affine
function of $q$. It is absent at $q=1$, by conditional unimodality.
If it is present at one bias, it persists as that bias moves toward
one half. Taking the union over pairs of points gives an open interval.
Reflection gives its other half. Analyticity excludes flat intervals,
so the interval endpoints really are unimodal with one maximum.

\paragraph{Passing to the limit.}
There is no need to estimate individual lattice probabilities to
transfer a strict valley. If the limiting density had one, small
intervals around its witnesses would have the same strict inequalities
between their averages. Weak convergence transfers the interval
masses, and lattice counting normalizes their averages. Choosing a
minimum in the middle interval and maxima in the outer intervals
produces a forbidden finite valley. Five intervals exclude two valleys.
This is Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-shape-transfer}. To transfer curvature, we
convolve with a Gaussian. Descartes' rule for exponential sums preserves
the second-difference sign pattern, while summation by parts identifies
its limit. Removing the Gaussian gives the curvature partition.

\paragraph{Finding the unique transition.}
The shape alternative leaves one scalar quantity to check near the
transition, namely $g_a''(0)$. Exact moments and the smoothing equation
give rigorous Fourier-tail bounds. Taylor models in $a$ cover the
parameter intervals without treating cancellation as numerical error.
On the small interval containing the zero, we differentiate the
smoothing equation itself. This proves
$0.68<\partial_a g_a''(0)<0.70$ and
$-0.09<g_a^{(4)}(0)<-0.07$. Thus the zero is unique and the critical
density has a quartic maximum. The complete certificate proof is in
Appendices~\ref{app:threshold} and~\ref{or:threshold}.

\paragraph{Why the rare-reversal results belong here.}
The same finite shape bounds give the two humps and the unique central
minimum at every even crossing with $N\ge8$. To locate them we use the
recursive-tree representation. Ignoring repeated flips within a family
gives a compound Poisson approximation, with an explicit error for the
discarded interactions. Its Landau limit gives the peaks on the
$x\log x$ scale. The near-one fixed-memory theorem joins the two
descriptions through the same Landau profile. Its density convergence
uses the curvature partition to prevent a narrow spike at a mode.

\subsection{Scope and organization}\label{sec:organization}

All proofs of stated results are in this article. Section~\ref{sec:convex}
gives the finite shape bounds, Section~\ref{sec:bulk-applications} the
fixed-memory limits, and Sections~\ref{sec:erw} and~\ref{sec:shape} the
rare-reversal crossing and approximation. Section~\ref{sec:verify}
separates written proofs, numerical certificates and formal checks.
The auxiliary second-order, tail, odd-length and likelihood results
from earlier versions are not used here.

We do not claim global monotonicity of $b_a$ in memory, a unique finite-length
memory threshold, or a density approximation uniform from rare reversals
to every fixed memory. These would require parameter or derivative
estimates beyond the shape bounds. The multidimensional limit and the
Drezner--Farnum model with a nonfair innovation probability likewise
need different shape arguments.
\subsection{Prior work}\label{sec:prior}

The counting chain predates the elephant walk. Drezner and Farnum's
generalized binomial model~\cite{dreznerfarnum1993}, in the formulation
of James, James and Qi~\cite[p.~2339]{jamesjamesqi2008}, has next-success
probability $(1-\theta)p+\theta b/t$. Taking $p=1/2$ and
$\theta=a=1-2\varepsilon$ gives our $r_t(b)=\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)b/t$.
For $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$ this is the same transition rule; the fair-start
chain also has their initialization. Other initial biases change only that
initialization. Heyde~\cite{heyde2004} established the fixed-parameter limit
regimes, including almost-sure convergence for $\theta>1/2$.
James, James and Qi~\cite[Theorem~2.2]{jamesjamesqi2008} treat time-varying
dependence parameters. Drezner and Farnum's abstract already identifies
bimodal members of this family.
Our claims concern the precise limit-shape classification and the
rare-reversal bounds, rather than the observation of bimodality.

Further fixed-memory results include
\cite{coletti2017,bercu2018,kubotatakei2019}. Murahara and
Tokumitsu~\cite{muraharatokumitsu2026} give optimal Gaussian local and
central limit rates for fixed copying probabilities at most $3/4$;
Vishwakarma~\cite{vishwakarma2026} studies moment recurrences in the three
regimes. The superdiffusive limit has a smooth positive density with known
moments~\cite[Theorems~1.3 and~1.4]{glr2026}, unimodal conditional on the
first step~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. Trinas and Valle's uniform local
limit theorem~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{tv2026}, an unrefereed preprint result,
would also transfer our first-difference bound to every initial bias;
our proof uses only weak convergence (Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-shape-transfer}).
Their exact moments and certified recursion
disprove the log-concavity conjecture in~\cite[Remark~2.3]{glrs2025}
\cite[Theorem~4.4]{tv2026}; conditional unimodality alone does not control
the mixture. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes} treats its fair-start shape and curvature.

Engl\"ander and Volkov's coin-turning walk switches at time $n$ with
probability $p_n$; for $p_n=c/n$ eventually, its occupation fraction tends
to Beta$(c,c)$~\cite[Section~4, Theorem~1]{ev2018}. Fan and
Qi~\cite{fanqi2026} vary the elephant's memory within a walk. Our reversal
probability instead varies with its final length, giving a triangular
array, as in Roy, Takei and Tanemura's varying memory windows~\cite{rtt2025}.

K\"ursten's coupling~\cite{kursten2016} copies a parent with probability
$1-2\varepsilon$ and otherwise assigns a fair sign; Baur and
Bertoin~\cite{baurbertoin2016} give the urn connection. Our regime is
strongly supercritical in Baur's classification~\cite{baur2016}, following
Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014}. Baur's Corollary~1 gives a Poisson limit for the
largest nonroot clusters. Miyazaki and Takei derive root-cluster limits via
the minimal elephant walk~\cite[Theorems~2.3, 2.4 and Section~2.3]{miyazakitakei2020}.
These results do not estimate our exact central mass.

Bokes, Hlubinov\'a and Singh~\cite{bhs2023} already derived the
first-order mutation profile for the same counting chain. We bound its
error uniformly in the rare-reversal regime and use it to locate the
crossing.

The untruncated law is the Lea--Coulson Luria--Delbr\"uck law. Pakes proved
its weak Landau limit~\cite[p.~993]{pakes1993}; M\"ohle gave density formulas
and a uniform lattice local limit for integer parameters, without a
rate~\cite[Theorem~4.1 and Corollaries~4.1--4.2]{mohle2005}. Kessler and
Levine give the small-parameter profile, Landau limit and dip threshold,
with mode near $\mu N(\log\mu N-0.22)$~\cite[Section~2 and Eqs.~(14),
(18), (19)]{kesslerlevine2015}. Bertoin proves the same weak limit for
vertices cut off from a recursive-tree root at cut probability of order
$k^{-1/4}$ on a tree of size $k$~\cite[Proposition~1]{bertoin2014ejp}.
In the Iksanov--M\"ohle coupling~\cite[Section~2 and
Proposition~1]{iksanovmohle2007}, the Meir--Moon cutting
procedure~\cite{meirmoon1974} removes subtrees whose sizes have law
$1/(j(j+1))$ independently up to first passage. These weights also give the expected
$N/(d(d+1))$ families of size $d$ in Section~\ref{sec:shape}.
Schweinsberg~\cite{schweinsberg2012} and Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014ejp}
obtain this stable law for root-cluster fluctuations at retention
$1-c/\log n$. Our additions are the total variation bound, explicit local
rate with truncation at $N-1$, transfer to the elephant walk and shape theorems.

Broadcasting papers study root-color reconstruction~\cite{adlv2022,ahs2026},
color-count limits and martingale error bounds~\cite{dhw2023,ksw2026}.
We estimate the tie probability as the flip probability tends to zero.
Gut and Stadtm\"uller's count~\cite[(1.5)]{gs2026} has next-step
probability $pk/n$, whereas ours has
$\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)k/n$, so their chain cannot leave zero.
The irreversible mutation expansion of Kessler and
Levine~\cite[Eqs.~(10), (13), (14)]{kesslerlevine2013} has the same
first derivative at $\varepsilon=0$ as our law, but not our uniform
relative bound at $N\varepsilon\asymp\log N$.


For comparison, Dekking and Kong~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{dekkingkong2011} classify the
Markov binomial law as unimodal, bimodal or trimodal. Their dependence is
on the previous state, whereas the present walk samples its whole past.
The two shape classifications therefore concern different laws.
Trinas~\cite{trinas2026return} studies last returns to zero at fixed
superdiffusive memory. His leading constant involves $g_{a,q}(0)$, which
is independent of $q$ by reflection. The bias changes the shape away
from zero even though it leaves that value unchanged.
