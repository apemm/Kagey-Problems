\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In the elephant random walk of Sch\"utz and Trimper~\cite{schutz2004},
each step copies a uniformly chosen earlier step and reverses its sign
with probability $\varepsilon$. The first step is fair. We study rare
reversals. The reversal probability $\varepsilon$ tends to $0$ as the
number of steps $N$ grows, and $x=N\varepsilon$ is of order $\log N$.
So reversals become rarer even though their expected number
$x-\varepsilon$ grows. Most limit theorems instead fix
$\varepsilon$~\cite{coletti2017,bercu2018,kubotatakei2019,glrs2025,tv2026}.

Let $M_N$ be the number of steps that disagree with the first step. Our
main result is a total variation bound of order $x^2\log^2N/N$ between
the law of $M_N$ and a truncated Luria--Delbr\"uck law, namely the
compound Poisson law with jumps $d\le N-1$ at rates $x/(d(d+1))$. From it
we derive a Landau local limit theorem with an explicit rate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-bulk}). The modes, the shape of the law and the
crossing below are consequences. Every mode lies at distance about
$x\log x-0.2228\,x$ from one of the two ends
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-modes}). The second difference of the law changes
sign at most 4 times (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-shape}).

The count $M_N$ is the resistant-cell count in Bokes, Hlubinov\'a and
Singh's reversible fluctuation assay~\cite{bhs2023}, with one sensitive
founder, uniform parent choice and equal switching probabilities at
birth. Their first-switch and P\'olya-urn calculation already gives the
first-order center coefficient $4/(N+2)$~\cite[Eqs.~(6)--(10)]{bhs2023}.
Their matched asymptotics give the Landau shape for one sensitive
founder~\cite[Eqs.~(50)--(52)]{bhs2023}, and two Landau densities for a
random start~\cite[Eqs.~(58)--(60)]{bhs2023}. These results are formal. Our
contribution is quantitative. We give explicit error bounds, and from them
exact crossing and shape results.

For even $N$ we compare the probability of the origin with that of each
end $\pm N$. Near $\varepsilon=0$ the ends are more likely, while at
$\varepsilon=\frac12$ the steps are independent and the origin is more
likely. We prove that the two probabilities cross exactly once, and we
locate the crossing at $x=2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+o(1)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-center}). At the crossing, for every even $N\ge8$,
the origin is the unique interior minimum and the law has exactly 2
symmetric intervals of modes (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-shape}). The persistent
walk, which copies the last step rather than a uniform earlier step,
crosses at about $\log N$ switches~\cite{paperA}.

The sign theorem holds for every $N$ and every $\varepsilon<\frac12$, so it
also applies at fixed memory $a=1-2\varepsilon\in(\frac12,1)$, where
$(2A_N-N)/N^a$ has a superdiffusive limit law. With a fair first step this law
has at most 2 modes for every $a$, and exactly 2 for $a\ge\frac{39}{40}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main-limit}). A computer-assisted proof shows that it is
unimodal for $a\le0.668812860014597$ and bimodal for $a\ge0.668812860014602$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}). Up to flat modal intervals, this settles the
fair-start case of a conjecture of Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and
Simon~\cite[Remark~2.1(b)]{glrs2025}, with the threshold located to within
$3.3\cdot10^{-15}$.
A weak limit connects our Landau law to this limit law as the memory tends
to 1 (Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-memory-bridge}).

For comparison of conventions, the original memory parameter
is $1-\varepsilon$, the parameter $a$ of~\cite{glrs2025} is
$1-2\varepsilon$, and the broadcasting flip probability is
$\varepsilon$. The broadcasting tree representation is recalled in
Lemma~\ref{lem:tree}~\cite{adlv2022,ahs2026}.

\subsection{Setting and notation}\label{sec:notation}

The walk takes $N$ steps $X_1,\dots,X_N\in\{+1,-1\}$, where
$+1$ means right. Let $A_N=\#\{i\le N:X_i=+1\}$ be the number of right steps,
and write $P_N(k)=\Prob(A_N=k)$ for the probability of the value $k$. The
position $2A_N-N$ is at the origin when $A_N=N/2$ and at an end when
$A_N\in\{0,N\}$. Unless we say otherwise the first
step is fair, $\Prob(X_1=+1)=\frac12$, and the rules treat the two directions
alike, so $P_N(k)=P_N(N-k)$. We write $\Prob_+$ and $\E_+$ for probability and
expectation given $X_1=+1$. For even $N$ we put $h=N/2$ and call
$C_N=P_N(h)$ the center and $E_N=P_N(N)=P_N(0)$ the end. Whenever $C_N$
appears, $N$ is even. The crossing is the value of the parameter at which
$C_N=E_N$. All logarithms are natural, $L=\log N$,
$H_k=\sum_{j\le k}1/j$ is the $k$-th harmonic number, and $\gamma$ is Euler's
constant. Limits, $O(\cdot)$ and $o(\cdot)$ refer to $N\to\infty$, over even
$N$ when $C_N$ appears. The constants implied by $O(\cdot)$ are absolute
unless we name what they depend on.

The elephant walk (Definition~\ref{def:erw}) has the reversal probability
$\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $x=N\varepsilon$. Its crossing is $\varepsilon_N$,
and $x_N=N\varepsilon_N$. The symbols that only one section uses are defined in that section, where
they first appear.

\subsection{Main results}\label{sec:results}

We first state the approximation, then its consequences for the modes
and the shape, and then the crossing.

The law $f_N(m)=\Prob(M_N=m)$ does not depend on $X_1$, and
$P_N(k)=\frac12[f_N(N-k)+f_N(k)]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:erw-reduction}). Let
$\CP_N$ be the law of $\sum_{d=1}^{N-1}d\,\mathcal N_d$, where the
$\mathcal N_d$ are independent Poisson variables with means $x/(d(d+1))$.
Put $\lambda_m=(m-x\log x)/x$, and let
\[
 f_L(\lambda)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\lambda t}e^{B(t)}\,dt,
 \qquad B(t)=-it\log\lvert t\rvert-\frac\pi2\lvert t\rvert,
\]
be the Landau density.

\begin{theorem}[the approximation]\label{thm:main-bulk}
For $N\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$ the total variation distance between
$f_N$ and $\CP_N$ is at most an explicit $\delta_\Sigma=O(x^2\log^2N/N)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}). Suppose that $x\to\infty$ and $x^3\log^2N/N\to0$.
Then, uniformly over integers $m\ge0$,
\[
 \bigl\lvert xf_N(m)-f_L(\lambda_m)\bigr\rvert
 =O\Bigl(\frac{1+\log x}{x}+\frac{x^3\log^2N}{N}\Bigr),
\]
and $(M_N-x\log x)/x$ converges in law to the density $f_L$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:landau} gives explicit finite-$N$ bounds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the modes]\label{thm:main-modes}
The Landau density has a unique mode $\lambda_{\mathrm L}\approx-0.222783$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}, computer-assisted).
For $\log N\le x\le2\log N$ and all sufficiently large $N$, every mode
$a^*\ge N/2$ of $P_N$ satisfies
\[
 \bigl\lvert N-a^*-x\log x-\lambda_{\mathrm L}x\bigr\rvert
 \le K\sqrt{x\log x},
\]
and the same bound holds for every mode of $f_N$ in place of $N-a^*$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-location}). We do not compute $K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the center and the crossing]\label{thm:main-center}
Let $N\ge2$ be even.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The ratio $C_N/E_N$ is strictly increasing in $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, and
the crossing $\varepsilon_N$ exists, is unique and lies in $(0,\frac12)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mlr}).
\item For $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$ we have
$C_N\ge\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1-2\varepsilon)$. If
$\varepsilon(1+\log N)\le\frac1{100}$, then
$C_N\le\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}\,N^{2\varepsilon}e^{5200\varepsilon}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds}, whose constant $5200$ uses an enclosure in ball
arithmetic).
\item As $N\to\infty$ over even $N$, the crossing satisfies
$x_N+\log x_N=2\log N-\log8+O(\log^2N/N)$, and so
\[
 x_N=2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+O\Bigl(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\Bigr)
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-crossing}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

So $C_N=\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1+O(\varepsilon\log N))$ when
$\varepsilon\log N$ is small, and the error factor is $1+O(\log^2N/N)$ at the
crossing. For the persistent walk with switch probability $q$, the crossing has
$T=Nq$ close to $\log N$. We compare the two crossings to the next order
in \arxivonly{Theorem}{thm:erw-markov}.

At the crossing each hump is about $x\log x-0.2228\,x$ steps from its end.
For the shape between them put
$D_N(a)=P_N(a+1)-2P_N(a)+P_N(a-1)$ and $m^*=N-a^*$ for a mode $a^*\ge N/2$.

\begin{theorem}[the shape]\label{thm:main-shape}
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item For $N\ge3$ and $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$, delete zeros from the signs
of $D_N$ and merge equal neighbors. The resulting pattern is a
subsequence of $(+,-,+,-,+)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:five-blocks}).
\item For every even $N\ge8$, the crossing law has exactly two symmetric
intervals of maximizing points. It strictly rises to each interval and
strictly falls from it toward the center, its unique interior minimum
(Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min}). This uses the finite endpoint-dip
certificates of Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}. The whole statement,
certificates included, is kernel-checked in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Between the humps we expect more, namely strict convexity on
$[2m^*,N-2m^*]$. The arXiv version proves this window for every large $N$,
and at the crossing for $5\le N\le2000$ by a certified computation
(\arxivonly{Section}{or:window}).

The shape of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-shape}(b) needs no such window. It holds
for every even $N\ge8$. The computational inputs are described in
Section~\ref{sec:verify}, with the result each one supports. The tables and
figures illustrate the theorems and are not used in their proofs.

\begin{theorem}[the fixed-memory limit law]\label{thm:main-limit}
Fix $0<\varepsilon<\frac14$ and put $a=1-2\varepsilon$. With a fair first step,
$(2A_N-N)/N^a$ converges almost surely to a limit with an even density $g_a$.
Either $g_a$ is nonincreasing on $[0,\infty)$, or there is $c>0$ such that
$g_a$ is nondecreasing on $[0,c]$ and nonincreasing on $[c,\infty)$ with
$g_a(0)<g_a(c)$. So $g_a$ has at most two modes
(Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes}). The second case holds for
$0<\varepsilon\le\frac1{80}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes-near-one}). With
explicit rationals $z_-<z_+$ such that $z_-=0.66881286001459775\ldots$ and
$z_+-z_-<3.3\cdot10^{-15}$, the first case holds for $a\le z_-$ and the second
for $a\ge z_+$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}, computer-assisted).
\end{theorem}

The proof of the threshold combines Taylor models in $a$ with a bound on the
characteristic function that improves itself through the fixed-point equation
of Gu\'erin, Laulin and Raschel (Remark~\ref{rem:modes-threshold}).

\paragraph{Why twice as many reversals?}
An endpoint has mass about $\frac12e^{-x}$. In the persistent walk,
let $q$ be the switch probability and $T=Nq$.
Its runs have typical length $N/T$, and the right-step count spreads
over about $N/\sqrt T$ values. Its central mass is of order $\sqrt T/N$,
which balances $e^{-T}$ at $T\approx\log N$.
An elephant reversal instead starts a family of descendants, governed
by P\'olya's urn. Reaching the middle requires an early reversal whose
family occupies about half the walk. This costs order $\varepsilon$,
and the exact middle costs another $1/N$. Thus the center has mass
about $4\varepsilon/N=4x/N^2$, balancing the endpoint at
$x\approx2\log N$. Most of the roughly $2\log N$ reversals there
occur too late to produce a large family.

\subsection{Prior work}\label{sec:prior}

Fixed-parameter limits for the elephant walk are well
developed~\cite{coletti2017,bercu2018,kubotatakei2019}.
In the superdiffusive regime the limit law has a smooth positive density
with known moments~\cite[Theorems~1.3 and~1.4]{glr2026}, and it is unimodal
given the first step~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. Trinas and Valle prove a
uniform local limit theorem~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{tv2026}, and disprove a
log-concavity conjecture of~\cite[Remark~2.3]{glrs2025} with exact moments and
a certified study of the recursion~\cite[Theorem~4.4]{tv2026}. They note that
unimodality need not pass to the mixture given by a random first step. Our
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes} treats this mixture for a fair first step, through
the sign pattern at finite $N$. Engl\"ander and Volkov's coin-turning walk instead switches
at time $n$ with probability $p_n$. For $p_n=a/n$ eventually, its
occupation fraction tends to Beta$(a,a)$~\cite[Section~4, Theorem~1]{ev2018}.
Fan and Qi~\cite{fanqi2026} let the memory parameter of the elephant walk
vary with time. We vary the reversal probability with the final length,
not with time within one walk. So our walks form a triangular array, as do
those of Roy, Takei and Tanemura~\cite{rtt2025}, whose memory window varies
with $n$.

K\"ursten's percolation coupling~\cite{kursten2016} copies a parent with
probability $1-2\varepsilon$ and otherwise gives the new vertex a fair
sign. Baur and Bertoin~\cite{baurbertoin2016} give the urn connection.
Our regime is strongly supercritical in Baur's
classification~\cite{baur2016}, following
Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014}. These works describe cluster sizes.
Baur's Corollary~1 gives a Poisson limit for the largest nonroot
clusters. Miyazaki and Takei obtain related root-cluster limits via
the minimal elephant walk~\cite[Theorems~2.3, 2.4 and Section~2.3]{miyazakitakei2020}.
They do not estimate the exact central mass in our regime.

Without truncation, our compound Poisson law is the Lea--Coulson
Luria--Delbr\"uck law. Its weak Landau limit as the mean grows is due to
Pakes~\cite[p.~993]{pakes1993}. M\"ohle proves it again with density
formulas~\cite[Theorem~4.1 and Corollary~4.1]{mohle2005}, and credits it to
Pakes. His Corollary~4.2 gives a uniform lattice local limit for integer
parameters, without a rate. Kessler and Levine describe the small-parameter
profile and the Landau limit~\cite[Eqs.~(14), (18), (19)]{kesslerlevine2015},
as well as the dip threshold and mode near
$\mu N(\log\mu N-0.22)$~\cite[Section~2]{kesslerlevine2015}. On random
recursive trees, Bertoin proves a weak limit of the same form for the number
of vertices cut off from the root by rare cuts, when the cut probability is
of order $k^{-1/4}$ on a tree of size $k$~\cite[Proposition~1]{bertoin2014ejp}.
His proof uses the coupling of Iksanov and M\"ohle~\cite[Section~2 and
Proposition~1]{iksanovmohle2007}. In it the subtrees removed by the cutting
procedure of Meir and Moon~\cite{meirmoon1974} have the sizes of independent
steps with law $1/(j(j+1))$, up to a first passage time. The same weights give
the mean number $N/(d(d+1))$ of families of size $d$ in
Section~\ref{sec:shape}. Schweinsberg~\cite{schweinsberg2012}
and Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014ejp} obtain the same stable law for root-cluster
fluctuations at retention $1-c/\log n$. What is new here is the total
variation bound, the explicit local rate with truncation at $N-1$, the
transfer to the elephant walk, and the shape theorems that follow.

Broadcasting papers study reconstruction of the root color from the
terminal tree~\cite{adlv2022,ahs2026}. Color-count limits and
martingale error bounds are also available~\cite{dhw2023,ksw2026}.
Here the center is the probability of a color tie, relevant to
majority-vote error, with the flip probability tending to zero.
Gut and Stadtm\"uller's count~\cite[(1.5)]{gs2026} has next-step
probability $pk/n$, whereas ours has
$\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)k/n$, so their chain cannot leave zero.
The irreversible mutation expansion of Kessler and
Levine~\cite[Eqs.~(10), (13), (14)]{kesslerlevine2013} has the same
first derivative at $\varepsilon=0$ as our law, but not our uniform
relative bound at $N\varepsilon\asymp\log N$.
We found no such central estimate in the literature reviewed.
