\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Fix the first step of a superdiffusive elephant random walk.
Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon prove that its limit density is
unimodal~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. Randomizing the first step
mixes this density with its reflection. They conjecture that the
initial biases giving two peaks form an open interval centered at
one half~\cite[Remark~2.1(b)]{glrs2025}. We prove this and show
that the interval is nonempty exactly above a unique memory threshold.

Their finite-walk induction proves conditional unimodality. For an
arbitrary start, new signs can enter at the ends, so we retain the
boundary values needed at later lengths in each row. This gives
at most two peaks and no valley on the half favored by the first
step. An affine comparison in the initial bias then gives the
interval. We also prove that the conditional density has positive
slope at zero for every superdiffusive memory, which makes the
bimodal interval a proper subinterval of $(0,1)$. To determine when
it opens, we use the transform parametrization of
\cite[Proposition~3.2]{glrs2025}. A Mellin calculation converts it
to a positive-weight formula for the central curvature, whose
single crossing gives the unique threshold.

\subsection{The walk and its parameters}\label{sec:notation}

The elephant walk~\cite{schutz2004} takes steps $X_i\in\{+1,-1\}$.
At each new step it chooses an earlier step uniformly and copies
its direction, except with probability $\varepsilon$, when it
reverses it. The choices and reversal coins are independent, also
of the first step. Put
\[
 A_N=\#\{i\le N:X_i=+1\},\qquad S_N=2A_N-N,\qquad
 P_N(k)=\Prob(A_N=k).
\]
We use $a=1-2\varepsilon$ for memory and $q=\Prob(X_1=1)$ for the
initial bias. The original copying probability is $p=(1+a)/2$.
The next-step probability at count $k$ is
$r_t(k)=\varepsilon+ak/t$. A fair start means $q=1/2$.
The notation $\Prob_+$ and $\E_+$ conditions on $X_1=1$.

For $a\in(1/2,1)$, $S_N/N^a$ has an almost-sure limit
\cite{baurbert2016,bercu2018}. Denote its conditional density by
$h_a$. For initial bias $q$, write
\[
 g_{a,q}(u)=q h_a(u)+(1-q)h_a(-u),\qquad g_a=g_{a,1/2}.
\]

\subsection{The bias interval and its threshold}\label{sec:results}

\begin{theorem}[the bias interval]\label{thm:main-limit}
For every $a\in(1/2,1)$ there is $b_a\in(0,1/2]$ such that
$g_{a,q}$ has exactly two local maxima if and only if
$b_a<q<1-b_a$. Outside this open interval, including its endpoints,
the density is unimodal with a unique local maximum.
\end{theorem}

Thus strongly biased starts are always unimodal. The next theorem
determines which memories admit two peaks.

\begin{theorem}[the analytic threshold]\label{thm:main-analytic}
There is exactly one $a_*\in(2/3,69/100)$ with $g_{a_*}''(0)=0$.
The central curvature is negative below $a_*$ and positive above
it, and $\partial_a g_a''(0)|_{a=a_*}>0$.
The bias interval is empty exactly when $a\le a_*$.
The function $a\mapsto b_a$ is continuous on $(1/2,1)$,
continuously differentiable on $(a_*,1)$, strictly decreasing
immediately above $a_*$, and tends to zero as $a\uparrow1$.
The fair density has exactly two inflection points when unimodal
and four when bimodal. At $a_*$ it has a quartic top. For positive
constants $K_*,Q_*$, its positive mode and bias boundary satisfy
\[
 \kappa_a\sim K_*\sqrt{a-a_*},\qquad
 \tfrac12-b_a\sim Q_*(a-a_*)^{3/2}\quad(a\downarrow a_*).
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorems~\ref{thm:two-modes} and~\ref{thm:biased-limit-modes}
give the shape classification. Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} proves
the unique transition, Theorem~\ref{thm:bias-continuity} gives the
boundary regularity, and Corollary~\ref{cor:bias-boundary-rate}
gives the limit at one. None of these arguments uses a numerical
certificate. Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic-quartic} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:critical-shape} and~\ref{prop:bias-cusp}
give the critical shape and opening laws. Global decrease of $b_a$ remains open.

\begin{proposition}[numerical constants, computer-assisted]\label{thm:main-threshold}
The threshold is $a_*\approx0.6688$. The positive constants in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-analytic} satisfy
$K_*\approx7.224$ and $Q_*\approx9.867$.
\end{proposition}

Propositions~\ref{prop:critical-shape} and~\ref{prop:bias-cusp}
give the expansions. Appendix~\ref{app:threshold} records the
certified enclosures, with their error analysis in the supplement.

\subsection{From finite signs to the limiting shape}\label{sec:architecture}

The interior recurrence takes positive sums of neighbors, so it
cannot create a sign change. The ends need separate treatment.
We enlarge each row by the boundary values that will enter later
(Figure~\ref{fig:augmented-rows}). Bounding their signs first
closes the induction for the whole row and for the half favored
by the initial bias. Lemma~\ref{lem:derivative-limits} passes the
resulting bounds directly to the density and its derivatives.

For $q\ge1/2$, the right half has no valley and dominates its
reflection. A second peak therefore occurs exactly when the
density decreases somewhere on the left. At two fixed points
there, the difference is affine in $q$ and has the opposite sign
at $q=1$. Once a decrease occurs, it persists as $q$ moves toward
one half. Taking all pairs and reflecting gives the bias interval.

For the memory threshold, an independent exponential scale gives
the explicit transform of~\cite[Proposition~3.2]{glrs2025}.
Two integrations by parts in its Mellin transform express the
central curvature as a weighted mean minus a constant. Rational
bounds fix the sign outside $[2/3,69/100]$. Within that interval,
an increasing score and a covariance identity give exactly one
crossing (Appendix~\ref{app:analytic-threshold}).

\subsection{Prior work}\label{sec:prior}

The counting chain predates the elephant walk. The Drezner--Farnum
model~\cite{dreznerfarnum1993}, in the formulation of
James, James and Qi~\cite[p.~2339]{jamesjamesqi2008}, has
next-success probability $(1-\theta)p+\theta k/t$.
Taking $p=1/2$ and $\theta=a$ gives our transition rule.
Their family includes bimodal laws. Heyde~\cite{heyde2004}
established fixed-parameter limit regimes, and James, James and
Qi treat time-varying dependence. Baur and Bertoin~\cite{baurbert2016}
derive functional limits through the connection with P\'olya-type
urns. Further limit and fluctuation results include
\cite{coletti2017,bercu2018,kubotatakei2019}.

We use the density and moments of
\cite[Theorems~1.3 and~1.4]{glr2026} and conditional unimodality
from~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}.
Kiss and Vet\H{o}~\cite{kissveto2022} give low-order moments for
general steps. Recent preprints study transition probabilities,
Gaussian rates and moment recurrences
\cite{peresqin2026,muraharatokumitsu2026,vishwakarma2026}.
Trinas and Valle~\cite{tv2026} prove a superdiffusive local limit
and disprove conditional log-concavity. Their difference recurrence
also yields our derivative-limit lemma, whose proof is included
here, so no result depends on the unrefereed preprint.

Mohandas et al.~\cite[Supplemental Material, Section~IV.A]{mohandas2025}
observe the fair-start transition in a growing-network model.
They extrapolate finite-length central curvatures to a copying
probability near $0.836$. Our threshold is
$(1+a_*)/2\approx0.8344$ in their parameter.

Which mixing weights give two peaks is a classical question.
Eisenberger~\cite{eisenberger1964} and Robertson and
Fryer~\cite{robertsonfryer1969} study it for normal mixtures.
Here the components are a non-Gaussian limit density and its
reflection. The finite sign argument uses classical variation
diminution~\cite{karlin1964,karlin1968}, with the extra work of
controlling boundary histories before applying the positive
interior recurrence.
