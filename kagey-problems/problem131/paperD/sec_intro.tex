\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{quote}\small\itshape
An elephant and an ant are playing hide and seek. The elephant counts while
the ant hides among many identical anthills, then opens its eyes and knows
at once which one the ant is in. The ant's shoes are outside the door.
\end{quote}
\hfill\textup{(Anonymous)}
\medskip
\end{minipage}

Fix the first step of a superdiffusive elephant random walk.
Gu\'erin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon prove that its limit density is
unimodal~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. Randomizing the first step mixes
this density with its reflection. The mixture can have two peaks,
although each component has one. They ask which biases give two,
conjecturing an open interval
centered at one half~\cite[Remark~2.1(b)]{glrs2025}.
We prove their conjecture and locate the unique memory threshold
where two peaks first appear under a fair start. A related conjecture,
eventual log-concavity, is disproved by Trinas and Valle in a recent
preprint~\cite{tv2026}.

We prove the shape bounds by tracking signs in the finite law's first
and second differences. Positive sums of neighbors cannot add a sign
change in the interior. Retaining earlier endpoint values lets the
induction cover the ends as well. These bounds give the rare-reversal
shape directly and the fixed-memory shape after passage to the limit.

\subsection{The walk and its parameters}\label{sec:notation}

The elephant walk~\cite{schutz2004} takes steps
$X_1,\dots,X_N\in\{+1,-1\}$, with $+1$ meaning right.
At each step it chooses uniformly from its earlier steps and repeats
the chosen direction, except with probability $\varepsilon$, when it
goes the other way. Write $U_t$ for the step chosen at time $t+1$,
and call it the parent of that step. We choose all parents and all
reversal coins independently, also independently of $X_1$.

It is convenient to count right steps instead of tracking position.
Put $A_N=\#\{i\le N:X_i=+1\}$ and $P_N(k)=\Prob(A_N=k)$.
Its position is $2A_N-N$, with $A_N=N/2$ at the origin and
$A_N\in\{0,N\}$ at the two ends (all steps in one direction).
We take $\Prob(X_1=+1)=\frac12$ unless we specify another bias.
Reflection then gives $P_N(k)=P_N(N-k)$.
For the walk started to the right, we use $\Prob_+$ and $\E_+$,
namely probability and expectation conditional on $X_1=+1$.
For even $N$ put ${k_{\mathrm c}}=N/2$, $C_N=P_N({k_{\mathrm c}})$ and $E_N=P_N(N)=P_N(0)$, the center
and end masses. The center mass $C_N$ is used only for even $N$. We call the solution of
$C_N=E_N$ the crossing.

All logarithms are natural,
$H_k=\sum_{j\le k}1/j$ is the $k$-th harmonic number, and $\gamma$ is Euler's
constant. For rare reversals we take $N\to\infty$, restricting to even
$N$ in statements about $C_N$. This is also the limit in $O(\cdot)$
and $o(\cdot)$. Any dependence in the constants implied by $O(\cdot)$
is stated explicitly (otherwise they are absolute).

The reversal probability is $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, $x=N\varepsilon$, and
the crossing is $\varepsilon_N$, with $x_N=N\varepsilon_N$.


For fixed memory put $a=1-2\varepsilon\in(1/2,1)$ and
$S_N=2A_N-N$. Conditional on $X_1=1$, the almost-sure limit of
$S_N/N^a$ has density $h_a$. With $\Prob(X_1=1)=q$, its density is
\[
 g_{a,q}(u)=q h_a(u)+(1-q)h_a(-u),\qquad g_a=g_{a,1/2}.
\]
The copying probability in the original notation is $p=(1+a)/2$.
In the finite recurrences we use $r_t(k)=\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)k/t$.

\subsection{Fixed memory}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{theorem}[the bias interval and the memory threshold]\label{thm:main-limit}
For each $a\in(1/2,1)$ there is $b_a\in[0,1/2]$ such that $g_{a,q}$
has exactly two local maxima if and only if $b_a<q<1-b_a$.
Otherwise it is unimodal with a unique local maximum, including at the
two endpoints. The interval is empty exactly when $a\le a_*$, where
\[
 0.668812860014597754<a_*<0.668812860014601029.
\]
The boundary $b_a$ is continuous in $a$, with $b_a\to0$ as $a\to1$.
The fair density has exactly two inflection points for $a\le a_*$
and four for $a>a_*$. Bimodality for $a\ge79/100$ has a proof
without computation. At $a_*$ the top is quartic, and the two fair
modes emerge at distance proportional to $\sqrt{a-a_*}$. The bias
interval has half-width asymptotic to $Q_*\approx9.86698769$ times
$(a-a_*)^{3/2}$, and strictly widens just above the threshold.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:biased-limit-modes} proves the interval assertion.
Theorems~\ref{thm:two-modes}--\ref{thm:threshold} classify the fair density
and enclose its threshold. Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-shape} gives
the shape at that threshold. Proposition~\ref{prop:bias-boundary}
gives the variational equation for $b_a$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:bias-continuity} proves global continuity.
Proposition~\ref{prop:bias-cusp} gives the three-halves law and local
monotonicity of its width.

\begin{theorem}[the profile near memory one]\label{thm:main-near-one}
Put $\varepsilon=(1-a)/2$ and
$A_\varepsilon=1+2\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Let $f_L$ be the
Landau density with characteristic function
$\exp\{-it\log|t|-\pi|t|/2\}$, and let $\lambda_{\mathrm L}$ be its mode.
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the density
\[
 \frac{q}{2\varepsilon}f_L\left(\frac{A_\varepsilon-u}{2\varepsilon}\right)
 +\frac{1-q}{2\varepsilon}f_L\left(\frac{A_\varepsilon+u}{2\varepsilon}\right)
\]
approximates $g_{1-2\varepsilon,q}$ in $L^1$ and in supremum norm
multiplied by $2\varepsilon$, uniformly over $q\in[0,1]$.
In particular $b_a\to0$, with the explicit upper rate
$b_a\le8(1-a)+O((1-a)^2)$ from Corollary~\ref{cor:bias-boundary-rate}.
For a fair start the positive mode is
$A_\varepsilon-2\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathrm L}+o(\varepsilon)$ and
its height is $(f_L(\lambda_{\mathrm L})+o(1))/(4\varepsilon)$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:near-one-profile} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:biased-near-one-peaks} prove these assertions.

\begin{theorem}[the finite shape bounds]\label{thm:shape-engine}
For every $N\ge1$, every $0<\varepsilon<1$ and every initial bias,
the count law has at most two modal plateaus, including its endpoints.
If $q\ge1/2$, it dominates its reflection on the right half and has
no strict valley there. For a fair start, $N\ge3$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$, the nonzero signs of
\[
 D_N(k)=P_N(k+1)-2P_N(k)+P_N(k-1),\qquad 1\le k<N,
\]
after equal neighbors are merged, form a subsequence of $(+,-,+,-,+)$.
\end{theorem}

The proofs are Proposition~\ref{prop:all-bias-modes},
Proposition~\ref{prop:dominant-half} and Theorem~\ref{thm:five-blocks}.
Weak convergence is enough to carry the valley bounds to a continuous
limit (Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-shape-transfer}). For the curvature bound,
we first smooth by a Gaussian and then apply the sign bound.

\subsection{Rare reversals}\label{sec:rare-results}

Now let $\varepsilon\to0$ with $x=N\varepsilon$ of order $\log N$.
The center and a specified end cross at about twice as many reversals
as in the persistent walk~\cite{paperA}, which copies or reverses only
its immediately preceding step. An early reversal changes an entire family of descendants (all the
steps that copy it, directly or indirectly). The calculation is in
Section~\ref{sec:erw}.

Let $M_N$ count steps opposite to $X_1$.
Its law $f_N(m)=\Prob(M_N=m)$ does not depend on $X_1$, and
$P_N(k)=\frac12[f_N(N-k)+f_N(k)]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:erw-reduction}). Let
$\CP_N$ be the law of $\sum_{d=1}^{N-1}d\,\mathcal N_d$, where the
$\mathcal N_d$ are independent Poisson variables with means $x/(d(d+1))$.
Put $\lambda_m=(m-x\log x)/x$. We use the Landau density $f_L$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-near-one}.

\begin{theorem}[compound Poisson and Landau approximations]\label{thm:main-bulk}
For $N\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$ the total variation distance between
$f_N$ and $\CP_N$ is at most an explicit $\delta_\Sigma=O(x^2\log^2N/N)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}). Suppose that $x\to\infty$ and $x^3\log^2N/N\to0$.
Then, uniformly over integers $m\ge0$,
\[
 \bigl\lvert xf_N(m)-f_L(\lambda_m)\bigr\rvert
 =O\Bigl(\frac{1+\log x}{x}+\frac{x^3\log^2N}{N}\Bigr).
\]
The weak convergence $(M_N-x\log x)/x\Rightarrow f_L$ needs only
$x\to\infty$ and $x^2\log^2N/N\to0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:landau} gives explicit finite-$N$ bounds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[mode locations]\label{thm:main-modes}
Suppose $x\to\infty$ and $x^3\log^2N/N\to0$. For every mode
$\kappa_N\ge N/2$ of $P_N$, and every mode $m$ of $f_N$,
\[
 N-\kappa_N=x\log x+\lambda_{\mathrm L}x+o(x),\qquad
 m=x\log x+\lambda_{\mathrm L}x+o(x).
\]
Both errors are
$O\bigl(\sqrt{x(1+\log x)}+x^{5/2}\log N/\sqrt N\bigr)$.
Here $\lambda_{\mathrm L}\approx-0.222783$ is the unique Landau mode
(Lemma~\ref{lem:landau-mode}, computer-assisted).
Corollary~\ref{cor:wider-mode-range} proves the assertion.
For $\log N\le x\le2\log N$, Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-location}
also gives the bound $50\sqrt{x\log x}$ for sufficiently large $N$
(an explicit threshold is in Remark~\ref{rem:bulk}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the center and the crossing]\label{thm:main-center}
Let $N\ge2$ be even.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The ratio $C_N/E_N$ is strictly increasing in $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, and
the crossing $\varepsilon_N$ exists, is unique and lies in $(0,\frac12)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mlr}).
\item Uniformly as $\varepsilon\log N\to0$,
$C_N=\frac{4\varepsilon}{N+2}(1+O(\varepsilon\log N))$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-bounds} gives explicit two-sided bounds.
\item As $N\to\infty$ over even $N$, the crossing satisfies
$x_N+\log x_N=2\log N-\log8+O(\log^2N/N)$, and so
\[
 x_N=2\log N-\log\log N-\log16+O\Bigl(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\Bigr)
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:erw-crossing}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

At the crossing the relative error in (b) is $O(\log^2N/N)$.
For the shape between the humps put
$D_N(k)=P_N(k+1)-2P_N(k)+P_N(k-1)$ and $m_N=N-\kappa_N$ for a mode $\kappa_N\ge N/2$.

\begin{theorem}[shape at the crossing]\label{thm:main-shape}
For every even $N\ge8$, the crossing law has exactly two symmetric
intervals of maximizing points. On either half, moving from the end
to the center, the probabilities strictly increase up to the maximizing
interval and strictly decrease after it. The center is the only
interior minimum (Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-min}). The proof uses the
finite endpoint-dip certificates in Proposition~\ref{prop:dip-hyp}.
Lean checks these certificates as well as the deduction of the shape
statement (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proof overview}\label{sec:architecture}

\paragraph{Signs at the ends.}
The recurrence alone is not enough at the ends. We append the earlier
end values to each row, so that the induction keeps track of where a
new sign could enter. In this enlarged row, an entry is either copied
or is a positive sum of neighbors from the preceding row. We can
therefore bound the signs of the entire row. For first differences
this gives at most two modal plateaus. For second differences under
a fair start it gives at most five sign blocks.

\paragraph{The half favored by the first step.}
Take $q\ge1/2$. The right half has no valley and dominates its
reflection. A second peak therefore requires a decrease on the left,
and this is also sufficient. Fix two points on the left and compare
their values. The right value minus the left is affine in the bias
and nonnegative at $q=1$, by conditional unimodality. Once negative,
it stays negative as the bias moves toward one half. Taking all such
pairs gives an interval, and reflection gives its other half.
Analyticity excludes flat pieces. We later use the two-mode bound
again, this time to control the boundary ratio and prove continuity.

\paragraph{Passing to the limit.}
If the limit density had a forbidden valley, average over three short
intervals around its high and low points. The inequalities remain
strict, so weak convergence gives them for the finite law too.
The smallest atom in the middle interval and the largest in each
outer interval then give a forbidden finite valley.
For two valleys we use five intervals instead
(Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-shape-transfer}).
For curvature we first smooth the second differences with a Gaussian.
Descartes' rule controls their signs. Summation by parts then gives
the limit for a fixed Gaussian width, after which we let that width
tend to zero.

\paragraph{Locating the threshold.}
Near the transition, the question reduces to the sign of $g_a''(0)$.
We evaluate its Fourier integral and bound the tail using exact
moments and the smoothing equation. We use a Taylor model on each
interval of $a$ because direct interval evaluation loses cancellations
in the integral. In the small interval containing the zero, we also
differentiate the smoothing equation, obtaining
$0.68<\partial_a g_a''(0)<0.70$ and
$-0.09<g_a^{(4)}(0)<-0.07$. The first inequality makes the zero unique.
The second identifies the first nonconstant term at the critical maximum,
namely the quartic term. At the threshold we have the sharper bounds
in Table~\ref{tab:critical-constants}. All estimates used by the
certificate are proved in
Appendices~\ref{app:threshold} and~\ref{or:threshold}.

\paragraph{The rare-reversal limit.}
The finite bounds already give two humps with just one interior minimum
at every even crossing with $N\ge8$. For their locations, view each
step as a vertex joined to its parent. A reversal then affects a whole
family. Ignoring repeated flips in a family leads to a compound
Poisson approximation, with an explicit bound for the discarded
interactions. Its Landau limit locates the peaks on the $x\log x$
scale. We obtain the same profile from the fixed-memory density as
memory tends to one. Here the curvature partition matters, since
weak convergence alone would not rule out a thin spike at a mode.

\subsection{Prior work}\label{sec:prior}

The counting chain predates the elephant walk. Drezner and Farnum's
generalized binomial model~\cite{dreznerfarnum1993}, in the formulation
of James, James and Qi~\cite[p.~2339]{jamesjamesqi2008}, has next-success
probability $(1-\theta)p+\theta b/t$. Taking $p=1/2$ and
$\theta=a=1-2\varepsilon$ gives our $r_t(b)=\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)b/t$.
For $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$ this is the same transition rule.
The fair-start chain also has their initialization. Other initial biases change only that
initialization. Heyde~\cite{heyde2004} established the fixed-parameter limit
regimes, including almost-sure convergence for $\theta>1/2$.
James, James and Qi~\cite[Theorem~2.2]{jamesjamesqi2008} treat time-varying
dependence parameters. Drezner and Farnum's abstract identifies bimodal members of this
family. We classify the limiting shapes and prove quantitative bounds
when reversals are rare.

Fixed-memory results include
\cite{coletti2017,bercu2018,kubotatakei2019}. Murahara and
Tokumitsu~\cite{muraharatokumitsu2026} give optimal Gaussian local and central limit rates for fixed copying
probabilities at most $3/4$. Vishwakarma~\cite{vishwakarma2026} studies moment recurrences in the three
regimes. We use the density and moments
of~\cite[Theorems~1.3 and~1.4]{glr2026} and conditional unimodality
from~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{glrs2025}. The local limit in Trinas and Valle's
preprint~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{tv2026} could replace our weak-transfer
argument. Their Theorem~4.4 disproves the log-concavity conjecture
of~\cite[Remark~2.3]{glrs2025}.

Engl\"ander and Volkov's coin-turning walk switches at time $n$ with
probability $p_n$. For $p_n=c/n$ eventually, its occupation fraction tends
to Beta$(c,c)$~\cite[Section~4, Theorem~1]{ev2018}. Fan and
Qi~\cite{fanqi2026} vary the elephant's memory within a walk. Our reversal
probability varies with the final length, giving a triangular array,
as in Roy, Takei and Tanemura's varying memory windows~\cite{rtt2025}.

K\"ursten's coupling~\cite{kursten2016} copies a parent with probability $1-2\varepsilon$ and otherwise assigns
a fair sign. Baur and Bertoin~\cite{baurbertoin2016} give the urn connection. Our regime is
strongly supercritical in Baur's classification~\cite{baur2016}, following
Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014}. Baur's Corollary~1 treats the largest nonroot
clusters. Miyazaki and Takei derive root-cluster limits via
the minimal elephant walk~\cite[Theorems~2.3, 2.4 and Section~2.3]{miyazakitakei2020}.
These results do not estimate our exact central mass.

Bokes, Hlubinov\'a and Singh~\cite{bhs2023} derived the first-order
mutation profile for this counting chain. We bound its
error uniformly in the rare-reversal regime and use it to locate the
crossing.

The untruncated law is the Lea--Coulson Luria--Delbr\"uck law. Pakes proved
its weak Landau limit~\cite[p.~993]{pakes1993}. M\"ohle gave a uniform
lattice local limit for integer parameters, without a
rate~\cite[Corollaries~4.1--4.2]{mohle2005}. Kessler and
Levine give the small-parameter profile, Landau limit and dip threshold,
with mode near $\mu N(\log\mu N-0.22)$~\cite[Section~2 and Eqs.~(14),
(18), (19)]{kesslerlevine2015}. Bertoin proves the same weak limit for
vertices cut off from a recursive-tree root at cut probability of order
$k^{-1/4}$ on a tree of size $k$~\cite[Proposition~1]{bertoin2014ejp}.
The Iksanov--M\"ohle coupling~\cite[Proposition~1]{iksanovmohle2007}
represents the Meir--Moon subtree cuts~\cite{meirmoon1974} by independent
sizes of law $1/(j(j+1))$ up to first passage. The same weights appear
in our expected family counts.
Schweinsberg~\cite{schweinsberg2012} and Bertoin~\cite{bertoin2014ejp}
obtain the stable root-cluster fluctuations at retention $1-c/\log n$.
We add total variation and local error bounds for the truncated law,
transfer them to the elephant walk and locate its modes.

Broadcasting papers study root-color reconstruction~\cite{adlv2022,ahs2026},
color-count limits and martingale error bounds~\cite{dhw2023,ksw2026}.
We estimate the tie probability as the flip probability tends to zero.
Gut and Stadtm\"uller's count~\cite[(1.5)]{gs2026} has next-step
probability $pk/n$, whereas ours has
$\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)k/n$, so their chain cannot leave zero.
The irreversible mutation expansion of Kessler and
Levine~\cite[Eqs.~(10), (13), (14)]{kesslerlevine2013} has the same
first derivative at $\varepsilon=0$ as our law, but not our uniform
relative bound at $N\varepsilon\asymp\log N$.


Dekking and Kong~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{dekkingkong2011} classify the Markov
binomial law as unimodal, bimodal or trimodal. It depends on the previous
state, so it differs from the walk here.
Trinas~\cite{trinas2026return} studies last returns to zero at fixed
superdiffusive memory. His leading constant involves $g_{a,q}(0)$, which
is independent of $q$ by reflection. The bias changes the shape away
from zero even though it leaves that value unchanged.
