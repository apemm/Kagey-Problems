Dear Editors,

Please consider "The elephant random walk with rare reversals: a Luria–Delbrück approximation and the shape of the law" for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

The paper studies the elephant random walk when the reversal probability tends to 0 with the number of steps N, with N times that probability of order log N. The main result is an explicit total variation bound between the number of steps that disagree with the first step and a truncated Luria–Delbrück law. From it we derive a Landau local limit theorem with an explicit rate. The modes, the shape of the law and a crossing between the origin and the ends follow as consequences. We prove a unique crossing and locate it. For every even length at least 8 the law at the crossing has exactly 2 symmetric modal intervals and a unique interior minimum. The sign theorem behind this shape holds at every fixed reversal probability below 1/2, and it passes to the classical superdiffusive limit law. With a fair first step that law has at most 2 modes for every memory parameter, and exactly 2 when the memory is close to 1. A computer-assisted proof then locates the change from 1 mode to 2 modes to within 3.3e-15, at a memory of about 0.6688128600146. Unless the density of that law is constant on some interval, this settles the fair-start case of a conjecture of Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon (Electron. J. Probab. 30 (2025), Remark 2.1(b)). A short weak-limit theorem connects the Landau law to the same limit law as the memory tends to 1.

The paper builds on the reversible fluctuation-assay model of Bokes, Hlubinová and Singh and on the Luria–Delbrück and elephant-walk literature. The weak Landau limit of the Luria–Delbrück law goes back to Pakes (1993), and Bertoin (2014) proves a tree analogue. The contribution is the total variation bound, the explicit local rate, the transfer to this walk, and the shape, crossing and two-mode results that follow. The preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats a different walk, the persistent random walk. The article uses it only for a heuristic comparison.

Every proof the article uses is in the article and its appendices, with one exception. The threshold theorem rests on a certified computation, and Appendix G states its steps and outlines their proofs. The arXiv version gives these proofs in full, in Section S6, and we would ask the referees to read that section as well. The code and its records are in the public repository named in the article. There is no supplementary material. A longer arXiv version adds the second-order center and crossing, the tail, further results for odd lengths and a convexity window between the two modes. The verification section and the repository map separate the kernel-checked Lean results, their classical hypotheses and the certified computations.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
