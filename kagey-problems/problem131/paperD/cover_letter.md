Dear Editors,

Please consider “The bias interval of the elephant random walk” for the Electronic Journal of Probability.

The paper proves the conjecture of Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon in EJP 30 (2025), Remark 2.1(b). For every superdiffusive memory, the initial biases giving two modes form a possibly empty open interval centered at one half. The classification and uniqueness of the memory threshold have analytic proofs. A positive-weight integral for the central curvature reduces uniqueness to a strict likelihood-ratio comparison. Separate interval certificates locate the threshold and bound the derivatives governing the transition. At the threshold the fair density has a quartic top. Its modes separate on a square-root scale and the bias interval opens on a three-halves scale.

The argument starts with the finite walk. Boundary histories complete the variation-diminishing induction for first differences, giving at most two modal plateaus and no valley on the half favored by the initial bias. Weak convergence carries these properties to the limit. A second-difference bound controls the inflection points. The paper also proves that the bias boundary is positive and continuously differentiable wherever the interval is nonempty. This interior regularity is analytic. Continuity at the memory threshold uses the certified critical expansion. A short general proposition combines the augmented-row sign argument with weak transfer, and a diagram shows how the boundary histories enter.

The manuscript is 35 pages and includes every proof of its stated results. The verification section separates written proofs, formal deductions and interval certificates. The rare-reversal and finite-threshold developments from the earlier combined version have been retained in a repository archive.

The paper credits the classical variation-diminishing theory, the mixture-modality studies of Eisenberger and Robertson–Fryer, Drezner and Farnum's observation of bimodal laws, the existing conditional-unimodality theorem, and the numerical transition observed by Mohandas and coauthors. It discusses the recent preprints of Trinas and Valle and of Trinas.

Potential referees with relevant expertise include Bernard Bercu, Naoki Kubota, Erich Baur and Cristian Coletti.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. My related preprint arXiv:2610.06935, “Uniform Bessel bounds for magnetization crossings in finite Ising chains,” is under consideration at J. Stat. Mech. It concerns the persistent walk. My related paper, “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, support selection and the boundary layer,” is under consideration at the Journal of Theoretical Probability and concerns a different Markov occupation model.

Thank you for considering the paper.
