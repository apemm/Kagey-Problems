Dear Editors,

Please consider “The bias interval of the elephant random walk” for the Electronic Journal of Probability.

The paper resolves the conjecture of Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon in EJP 30 (2025), Remark 2.1(b). For every superdiffusive memory, the biases giving two modes form a proper, possibly empty open interval centered at one half. We prove analytically that it is nonempty exactly above a unique memory threshold. The boundary is continuous, is continuously differentiable above the threshold, and strictly decreases immediately above it.

The proof extends the finite-walk induction to augmented rows that retain the boundary histories. This bounds both the number of modes and the inflection points. A Mellin calculation based on the GLRS transform gives a positive-weight curvature integral and its unique crossing. A further integration by parts proves the negative fourth derivative at the transition, so the quartic top and the square-root and three-halves laws are also analytic. Numerical certificates only locate the threshold and evaluate the constants.

The main paper is 27 pages. The seven-page supplement contains the complete certificate error analysis and an independent fourth-moment check. Please include these supplementary proofs in the refereeing. All analytic proofs are in the main paper, and the formalization is an independent check rather than a prerequisite for reading them.

Potential referees with relevant expertise include Bernard Bercu, Naoki Kubota, Erich Baur and Cristian Coletti.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. My related preprint arXiv:2610.06935, “Uniform Bessel bounds for magnetization crossings in finite Ising chains,” is under consideration at J. Stat. Mech. It concerns the persistent walk. My related paper, “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, support selection and the boundary layer,” is under consideration at the Journal of Theoretical Probability and concerns a different Markov occupation model.

Thank you for considering the paper.
