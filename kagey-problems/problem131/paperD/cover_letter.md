Dear Editors,

Please consider “Bimodality of the elephant random walk: the bias interval, the threshold and rare reversals” for the Electronic Journal of Probability.

The paper proves the conjecture of Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon in EJP 30 (2025), Remark 2.1(b). At every superdiffusive memory, the initial biases giving two modes form an open interval centered at one half. Its endpoints vary continuously with memory, by an analytic argument. The interval is empty up to a unique memory threshold. At the threshold the fair density has a quartic top, its two modes emerge on a square-root scale, and the bias interval opens on a three-halves scale. A certified enclosure locates the threshold near 0.6688128600146.

The argument begins with the actual finite walk. First differences give at most two modal plateaus and exclude a valley on the half favored by the initial bias. A second-difference bound controls inflection points. These bounds pass to the limit using weak convergence and lattice-window averages. They also give an all-even-length shape theorem at the rare-reversal crossing. The second part supplies explicit compound Poisson and Landau approximation bounds and locates that crossing and its modes. A common shape argument and the near-one Landau profile connect the two regimes.

Every proof of a stated result is included in the manuscript. No extended version or review-only supplement is needed. The verification section distinguishes the formal proofs and their explicit mathematical inputs from the interval computations. Source files, certificate records and the declaration audit accompany the tagged repository version.

The paper credits Drezner and Farnum's observation of bimodality, the first-order mutation profile of Bokes, Hlubinová and Singh, and the existing weak Landau limits. It also discusses the recent preprints of Trinas and Valle on local limits and log-concavity, and of Trinas on last returns. The contribution is the bias-interval classification, the unique modal transition and its local shape, together with the quantitative rare-reversal bounds.

Potential referees with relevant expertise include Bernard Bercu, Naoki Kubota, Erich Baur and Cristian Coletti.

Stat. Mech. It concerns the persistent walk and is cited here only for a comparison of crossing scales. My related paper, “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, support selection and the boundary layer,” is under consideration at the Journal of Theoretical Probability and concerns a different Markov occupation model.

Thank you for considering the paper.
