Dear Editors,

Please consider "Shape transitions and rare reversals in the elephant random walk" for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

The paper proves that, at every superdiffusive memory, the initial biases producing a bimodal limit density form one open interval centred at one half. Outside that interval, including its endpoints, there is a unique unimodal maximum. This proves the classification conjectured by Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon (Electron. J. Probab. 30 (2025), Remark 2.1(b)). The proof controls first differences and boundary histories of the actual finite count law, including a no-valley property on the half favoured by the initial bias. A separate parameter-derivative certificate proves a unique fair-start modal transition at memory 0.6688128600146, within a bracket shorter than 3.3e-15. The critical density is unimodal. The fair density also has exactly two or four inflection points according to its branch.

As memory tends to one, the paper upgrades the weak Landau bridge to a two-component density approximation, in total variation and scaled uniform distance, uniformly over the initial bias. The curvature partition controls the height at the mode, which weak convergence and unimodality alone would not justify. The result determines both the locations and the heights of the fair-start peaks.

In the rare-reversal regime, an explicit total variation approximation by a truncated Luria–Delbrück law gives a Landau local limit theorem with a rate and locates every mode. Separate center estimates locate the unique crossing between the probabilities of a color tie and a unanimous outcome in the broadcasting representation. At that crossing, the sign theorem and finite certificates give two symmetric modal intervals and a unique interior minimum for every even length at least 8. The explicit constants make this all-length conclusion possible.

The paper identifies the counting chain with the older correlated-Bernoulli model of Drezner and Farnum and credits the limit theory of Heyde and James, James and Qi. It distinguishes its shape and error bounds from that literature, the formal asymptotics of Bokes, Hlubinová and Singh, and the weak Landau limits of Pakes and Bertoin. The preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats the persistent random walk and is used only for a heuristic comparison in the journal text.

The submission includes the extended manuscript and a review-only supplement, `threshold_proofs_review.pdf`. The supplement has an identification cover followed by Section S6 and the bibliography, retaining the extended manuscript's page and statement numbers. It supplies the proofs behind the threshold certificate summarized in Appendix G. Referees therefore receive the complete argument with the submission. Sections S1 to S5 of the extended manuscript contain additional results not used by the journal paper. I would welcome the editor's guidance on whether the threshold proofs should become published supplementary information.

The source package includes editable LaTeX files and figures in PDF and EPS. The public code repository and verification section distinguish the Lean kernel checks, their explicit mathematical inputs, and the separate interval computations. The formal proofs of the profile upgrade use the stated weak bridge and published density facts as inputs; they do not formalize the entire cited limit-law theory.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
