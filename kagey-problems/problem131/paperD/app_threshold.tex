\section{Certifying the memory threshold}\label{app:threshold}

This appendix describes the certificate for Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}.
Appendix~\ref{or:threshold} proves the estimates it uses. Fix
$a\in(\frac12,1)$ and write $\phi(t)=\E e^{it\mathcal L_a}$. Put
$\theta_j=1$ for even $j$ and $\theta_j=a$ for odd $j$, let $m_0=m_1=1$ and
$m_n=(na-\theta_n)^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\theta_jm_jm_{n-j}$ for $n\ge2$. Then
$\E \mathcal L_a^n=n!\,c_n$ with $c_n=m_n/\Gamma(1+an)$~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{glr2026}. As
$\E e^{t\lvert \mathcal L_a\rvert}<\infty$ for real $t$~\cite[Corollary~2.11]{glr2026},
$\phi(t)=\sum_nc_n(it)^n$ for all real $t$. By~\cite[Theorem~1.1 and
(2.10)]{glr2026}, $\mathcal L_a$ has the law of $V^a\mathcal L_a'+\xi(1-V)^a\mathcal L_a''$, with $V$ uniform on
$[0,1]$, $\xi=\pm1$, $\mathcal L_a'$ and $\mathcal L_a''$ distributed as $\mathcal L_a$, and all four
independent. Since $\lvert\phi(-s)\rvert=\lvert\phi(s)\rvert$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thr-fixed}
 \lvert\phi(t)\rvert\le\int_0^1\lvert\phi(tv^a)\rvert\,
 \lvert\phi(t(1-v)^a)\rvert\,dv\qquad(t\in\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
The density $g_a$ of $\mathcal L^\circ_a$ has the characteristic function
$\operatorname{Re}\phi$.

\emph{Step 1, one value of $a$.} Lemmas~\ref{lem:thr-moments}
and~\ref{lem:thr-trunc} bound the moment-series remainder by a geometric
series. Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-cells} then bounds $|\phi|$ on frequency cells.
For $T_3\ge2^aT_2$, $\beta>3$ and $a\beta>1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-tail}
gives an explicit test $R<1$ that propagates
$|\phi(t)|\le Ct^{-\beta}$ from $[T_2,T_3]$ to $[T_2,\infty)$.
We integrate the moment series for
$g_a''(0)=-\pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^2\operatorname{Re}\phi(t)\,dt$
on $[0,T_3]$ and bound both the series remainder and Fourier tail
by Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-fpp}.

\emph{Step 2, intervals of $a$.} Direct interval evaluation loses
cancellations between terms of size about $10^{60}$. Instead, on
$I=[a_0-w,a_0+w]$ we expand in $a-a_0$. Choose $\rho=w/\vartheta$
with $0<\vartheta<1$. A complex bound $|h|\le M$ on the disc of
radius $\rho$ gives the degree-$K$ remainder
$M\vartheta^{K+1}/(1-\vartheta)$ on $I$.
The complex moment bound and gamma product formula in
Proposition~\ref{prop:thr-interval} give a polynomial $G$ and
$\eta$ with $|g_a''(0)-G(a-a_0)|\le\eta$ throughout $I$.

\emph{Step 3, $a$ near $\frac12$.} Set $y=2a-1$,
$\mu_n=m_ny^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ and
$\sigma^2=\E\mathcal L_a^2=1/(y\Gamma(2a))$.
The scaled moments are analytic for $\operatorname{Re}a>1/4$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-scaled}). Applying Steps~1 and~2 to
$\mathcal L_a/\sigma$ bounds $\sigma^3g_a''(0)$ up to $a=1/2$
by continuation. Its sign agrees with $g_a''(0)$ for $a>1/2$.
The unscaled density is not defined at $a=1/2$.

\emph{Step 4, the Hankel test.} This step uses no Fourier analysis.

\begin{lemma}[a Hankel test]\label{lem:thr-hankel}
Let $s_j=(2j+1)\E \mathcal L_a^{2j}$. Suppose that for some $k\ge1$ and
$x\in\mathbb R^{k+1}$ we have $\sum_{i,j\le k}x_ix_js_{i+j}<0$ or
$\sum_{i,j\le k}x_ix_js_{i+j+1}<0$. Then case (a) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes} fails, so case (b) holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In case (a), $\mathcal L^\circ_a$ is unimodal about $0$. By Khinchine's
theorem~\cite{dj1988} it has the law of $UZ$, with $U$ uniform on
$(0,1)$ and independent of $Z$. So $s_j=(2j+1)\E(\mathcal L^\circ_a)^{2j}
=\E W^j$ with $W=Z^2\ge0$, and the two sums are $\E(\sum_ix_iW^i)^2\ge0$ and
$\E W(\sum_ix_iW^i)^2\ge0$.
\end{proof}

The script \texttt{certify\_threshold.py} checks every hypothesis in ball
arithmetic~\cite{johansson2017}. It evaluates $R$ with $a$ as a ball over $I$
and evaluates $G$ on subintervals of $I$, bisecting until $G\pm\eta$ has one
sign on each. For the Hankel test it takes $k$ and a rational $x$ from a
floating-point eigenvector at the center of a box of $a$, and checks the sum
on the whole box with a Taylor model as in Step~2. Floating-point choices of $N$, the precision and $x$ only affect
whether the subsequent certificate succeeds.
The cells have half-width $\frac1{32}$, the Taylor order in $t$ is $12$,
$\beta=4$, and the degree $K$ is chosen so that $M\vartheta^{K+1}\le e^{-60}$.
Table~\ref{tab:thr-runs} lists the runs.

\emph{Step 5, the unique transition.} On
$I=[0.66881285,0.66881287]$, which contains $[z_-,z_+]$,
the differentiated smoothing equation propagates bounds on
$|\partial_t\phi_a|$ and $|\partial_a\phi_a|$.
Section~\ref{or:transition-unique} proves these bounds and the
parameter Taylor estimates. The resulting certificate gives
\[
 0.68<\partial_a g_a''(0)<0.70,\qquad
 -0.09<g_a^{(4)}(0)<-0.07
 \quad(a\in\operatorname{int}I).
\]

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}]
The 40 intervals of the first run cover $[\frac12,0.668]$. On each, the
scaled enclosure from Step~3 is negative, so $g_a''(0)<0$ for
$a\in(\frac12,0.668]$. The 8 intervals of the second run cover
$[0.668,0.6712]$. On the 2 intervals that cover $[0.668,0.6688]$ the enclosure
is negative, and on the 5 that cover $[0.6692,0.6712]$ it is positive. On
$[0.6688,0.6692]$ we have $\eta<1.1\cdot10^{-15}$. There the enclosure is
negative on closed pieces that end at $z_-$ and positive on closed pieces that
start at $z_+$, and only $(z_-,z_+)$ is undecided. The script prints these
signs and checks this arrangement. So $g_a''(0)<0$ on $(\frac12,z_-]$ and
$g_a''(0)>0$ on $[z_+,0.6712]$. The 115 boxes of the third run cover
$[0.6705,1]$, and on each the first sum of Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-hankel} is
negative.

By Step~1, $g_a$ is even with two continuous derivatives, so $g_a'(0)=0$. If
$g_a''(0)<0$, then $g_a'<0$ on some $(0,\delta)$, which rules out case (b), where $g_a$ is nondecreasing on
$[0,\kappa]$. If $g_a''(0)>0$, then $g_a'>0$ on
some $(0,\delta)$, which rules out case (a). Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-hankel}
gives case (b) on $[0.6705,1)$. By Step~2,
$a\mapsto g_a''(0)$ is continuous on $[0.6688,0.6692]$, so it has a zero in
$(z_-,z_+)$. Step~5 makes it strictly increasing throughout that bracket,
so the zero $a_*$ is unique. The signs already proved exclude other zeros
in $(\frac12,0.6712]$ and give the stated shape on either side of $a_*$.
At $a_*$, evenness and Step~5 give
$g_{a_*}'(x)=g_{a_*}^{(4)}(0)x^3/6+o(x^3)<0$ for small positive $x$.
This excludes case (b), so the critical density is unimodal too.
\end{proof}
