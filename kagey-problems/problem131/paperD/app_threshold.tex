\section{Locating the threshold and its critical derivatives}\label{app:threshold}

Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} proves the unique transition analytically.
Here we locate it and certify the derivatives used in the critical
expansions. Appendix~\ref{or:threshold} proves the error bounds.
Fix $a\in(1/2,1)$ and write $\phi(t)=\E e^{it\mathcal L_a}$.
Put $\theta_j=1$ for even $j$ and $\theta_j=a$ for odd $j$, let
$m_0=m_1=1$ and
\[
 m_n=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\theta_jm_jm_{n-j}}{na-\theta_n},
 \qquad c_n=\frac{m_n}{\Gamma(1+an)}\quad(n\ge2).
\]
Then $\E\mathcal L_a^n=n!c_n$~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{glr2026},
with the same definition of $c_0,c_1$.
The exponential moments in~\cite[Corollary~2.11]{glr2026} give
$\phi(t)=\sum_nc_n(it)^n$ for every real $t$.
The fixed-point equation~\cite[Theorem~1.1 and (2.10)]{glr2026}
and conjugate symmetry give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thr-fixed}
 |\phi(t)|\le\int_0^1|\phi(tv^a)|\,
                 |\phi(t(1-v)^a)|\,dv.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[a certified location]\label{cor:threshold-bracket}
The unique threshold in Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} satisfies
$z_-<a_*<z_+$, where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 z_-&=1838418790980431/2748779069440000
      =0.668812860014597754\ldots,\\
 z_+&=45960469774511/68719476736000
      =0.668812860014601028\ldots.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The width of this interval is less than $3.3\cdot10^{-15}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At each endpoint we integrate the real moment series and bound
the remainder and Fourier tail by Lemmas~\ref{lem:thr-moments}
to~\ref{lem:thr-fpp}. The recorded enclosures give
$g_{z_-}''(0)<0<g_{z_+}''(0)$. Continuity and
Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} place its unique zero between them.
\end{proof}

The endpoint computation bounds the series remainder by Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-trunc} and $|\phi|$ on frequency
cells by Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-cells}. Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-tail}
propagates $|\phi(t)|\le Ct^{-\beta}$ once its explicit test
$R<1$ succeeds. Lemma~\ref{lem:thr-fpp} bounds the omitted
curvature integral. The two endpoint checks use real-parameter
moments directly, independently of the parameter Taylor models.
Their records and exact inputs are in Appendix~\ref{app:data}.

To enclose derivatives over a memory interval, we expand in
$a-a_0$ before taking absolute values. This preserves cancellation
between large moment terms. Proposition~\ref{prop:thr-interval}
bounds the Taylor remainder using a complex moment majorant.
Section~\ref{or:transition-unique} differentiates the smoothing
equation and proves the uniform derivative-tail bounds.
On $[0.66881285,0.66881287]$ the resulting enclosures give
\[
 0.68<\partial_a g_a''(0)<0.70,\qquad
 -0.09<g_a^{(4)}(0)<-0.07.
\]
The refined evaluation in Table~\ref{tab:critical-constants}
uses a smaller neighborhood containing $[z_-,z_+]$.
These numerical bounds determine the quartic top and its constants.
They are not inputs to the analytic uniqueness proof.
