\section{Sign patterns and regularity}\label{app:modes}

We prove the lemmas used for Theorem~\ref{thm:two-modes}, with the notation
of Sections~\ref{sec:convex} and~\ref{sec:modes}. Deleting entries from a list
replaces its pattern by a subsequence. An alternating list $p$ satisfies
$p\preceq q$ for every alternating list $q$ with the same first sign and at
least as many entries.

\begin{lemma}[Descartes' rule for exponential sums]\label{lem:descartes}
Let $\lambda_1<\dots<\lambda_n$ and $b_1,\dots,b_n$ be real, with
$b=(b_1,\dots,b_n)\ne0$, and put $F(y)=\sum_{k=1}^nb_ke^{\lambda_ky}$. Then
$F$ has finitely many real zeros, and
$\operatorname{sp}(F(y_1),\dots,F(y_j))\preceq\operatorname{sp}(b)$ for all
$y_1<\dots<y_j$. The same holds for $\psi F$ for every positive function
$\psi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Z(F)$ be the number of distinct real zeros of $F$, and $S(b)$ the number
of sign changes of $b$. We show $Z(F)\le S(b)$ by induction on $S(b)$. If
$S(b)=0$, then $F$ has the sign of the nonzero $b_k$ everywhere. If $S(b)\ge1$,
choose $i$ such that the last nonzero $b_k$ with $k\le i$ and the first
nonzero $b_k$ with $k>i$ have opposite signs, and choose
$\lambda_i<\kappa<\lambda_{i+1}$. Then
\[
 G(y)=\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(e^{-\kappa y}F(y)\bigr)
 =\sum_{k=1}^nb_k(\lambda_k-\kappa)e^{(\lambda_k-\kappa)y}.
\]
The coefficient $b_k(\lambda_k-\kappa)$ has the sign of $-b_k$ for $k\le i$
and of $b_k$ for $k>i$, so the coefficients of $G$ have $S(b)-1$ sign changes.
By Rolle's theorem $G$ vanishes between any two zeros of $F$, so
$Z(F)\le Z(G)+1\le S(b)$.

If $b_{k_0}$ is the first nonzero $b_k$, then
$e^{-\lambda_{k_0}y}F(y)\to b_{k_0}$ as $y\to-\infty$. The zeros of $F$ cut the
line into at most $S(b)+1$ open intervals, on each of which $F$ has one sign.
With equal neighbors merged, these signs form an alternating list that starts
with the sign of $b_{k_0}$ and has at most $S(b)+1$ entries, so it is
$\preceq\operatorname{sp}(b)$. The nonzero values $F(y_1),\dots,F(y_j)$ lie in
these intervals in order. A positive factor $\psi$ changes no sign.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[pointwise limits]\label{lem:sp-limit}
Let $v_n$ and $v$ be real functions on $\mathbb R$ with $v_n(y)\to v(y)$ for
every $y$, and let $\Pi$ be an alternating list. If every $v_n$ has values at
most $\Pi$, then so has $v$. If every $v_n$ has increments at most $\Pi$, then
so has $v$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $y_1<\dots<y_j$, and let $I$ be the set of indices $i$ with $v(y_i)\ne0$.
For all large $n$, $v_n(y_i)$ has the sign of $v(y_i)$ for every $i\in I$.
Then $\operatorname{sp}(v(y_1),\dots,v(y_j))$ is the pattern of the list
$(v_n(y_i))_{i\in I}$, which is a subsequence of
$\operatorname{sp}(v_n(y_1),\dots,v_n(y_j))\preceq\Pi$. The increments of $v_n$
at $y_1,\dots,y_j$ tend to those of $v$, and the same argument applies to
them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one derivative fewer]\label{lem:sp-derivative}
Let $u$ be twice continuously differentiable on $\mathbb R$, with $u'(y)\to0$
as $y\to\pm\infty$. If $u''$ has values at most $\Pi_5$, then $u'$ has values
at most $\Pi_4$, and $u$ has increments at most $\Pi_4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to treat points $y_1<\dots<y_j$ at which the values of $u'$ are
nonzero and alternate in sign. Let $s$ be the sign of $u'(y_1)$. Choose $y_0<y_1$
with $\lvert u'(y_0)\rvert<\lvert u'(y_1)\rvert$, and $y_{j+1}>y_j$ with
$\lvert u'(y_{j+1})\rvert<\lvert u'(y_j)\rvert$. For $0\le i\le j$ the mean
value theorem gives $w_i\in(y_i,y_{i+1})$ with
$u''(w_i)(y_{i+1}-y_i)=u'(y_{i+1})-u'(y_i)$. The right side has the sign $s$
for $i=0$, the sign of $u'(y_{i+1})$ for $1\le i\le j-1$, and the sign of
$-u'(y_j)$ for $i=j$. In each case this is $(-1)^is$. So $u''$ takes $j+1$
alternating signs, starting with $s$, at $w_0<\dots<w_j$. This list is
$\preceq\Pi_5$, so $j+1\le5$, and $j+1\le4$ if $s$ is $-$. In both cases the
list of the $j$ signs of $u'(y_1),\dots,u'(y_j)$ is $\preceq\Pi_4$.

For the increments at $y_1<\dots<y_j$, each nonzero $u(y_{i+1})-u(y_i)$ has
the sign of $u'(\xi_i)$ for some $\xi_i\in(y_i,y_{i+1})$, by the mean value
theorem. So the pattern of the increments is a pattern of values of $u'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[absence of flat intervals]\label{lem:analytic-density}
For every $a\in(\frac12,1)$ and $1<r<1/a$, the characteristic function
$\phi_a$ of $\mathcal L_a$ satisfies
\[
 |\phi_a(t)|\le 2e^{-c|t|^r}\qquad(t\in\mathbb R)
\]
for some $c=c(a,r)>0$. For every $q\in[0,1]$, the density of the limit with first-step bias $q$
extends to an entire function and is not constant on an interval of
positive length.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-point equation of~\cite[Theorem~1.1 and (2.10)]{glr2026} gives
\[
 |\phi_a(t)|\le\int_0^1|\phi_a(tv^a)|\,
                  |\phi_a(t(1-v)^a)|\,dv.
\]
Here $\phi_a$ is the characteristic function conditional on a positive
first step. The random sign in the fixed-point equation causes no change
in this inequality, since $|\phi_a(-s)|=|\phi_a(s)|$.
Put $D(v)=v^{ar}+(1-v)^{ar}-1$. Since $ar<1$, we have $D(v)>0$ for
$0<v<1$. Dominated convergence therefore permits $K>1$ such that
\[
 R:=2\int_0^1e^{-K^r(\log2)D(v)}\,dv<1.
\]
The law of $\mathcal L_a$ has a density~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{glr2026}, so
the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma gives $T>0$ with
$|\phi_a(t)|\le2^{1-K^r}$ for $|t|\ge T$. Set
$c=(\log2)/T^r$ and $b(t)=2e^{-ct^r}$ for $t\ge0$.
On $[0,T]$ we have $b\ge1$, and on $[T,KT]$ we have
$b\ge2^{1-K^r}$. Thus $|\phi_a|\le b$ on $[0,KT]$.

For $t\ge KT$,
\[
 \frac{\int_0^1b(tv^a)b(t(1-v)^a)\,dv}{b(t)}
 =2\int_0^1e^{-ct^rD(v)}\,dv\le R<1.
\]
If the bound $|\phi_a|\le b$ first ceased to hold at a finite supremum
$t_*\ge KT$, continuity would give the bound on $[0,t_*]$. The
fixed-point inequality would then give
$|\phi_a(t_*)|\le Rb(t_*)<b(t_*)$, and continuity would extend the bound
beyond $t_*$. This contradiction proves the bound on $[0,\infty)$;
symmetry of its modulus gives the bound on $\mathbb R$.

Since $r>1$, the Fourier integral for the density converges absolutely
and locally uniformly for complex arguments: for every $B<\infty$,
$\int_{\mathbb R}e^{B|t|}|\phi_a(t)|\,dt<\infty$. It therefore defines
an entire function. At initial bias $q$, the characteristic function is
$q\phi_a(t)+(1-q)\phi_a(-t)$ and obeys the same bound by the triangle
inequality. Each resulting density is integrable with integral one, so
it is not constant on $\mathbb R$. The identity theorem excludes a
constant interval.
\end{proof}


\subsection{First differences for a biased start}

\begin{proposition}[Two modes for every initial bias]\label{prop:all-bias-modes}
Let $0<\varepsilon<1$, let $q\in[0,1]$ be the probability that the first
step is positive, and let $P_n(k)$ be the law of the number of positive
steps after $n\ge1$ steps. Extend $P_n$ by zero outside $\{0,\ldots,n\}$.
The first differences $d_n(k)=P_n(k+1)-P_n(k)$ satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{sp}\bigl(d_n(-1),d_n(0),\ldots,d_n(n)\bigr)
 \preceq (+,-,+,-).
\]
In particular, $P_n$ has at most two modal plateaus, including endpoint
modes. Moreover, there are no integers $i_0<\cdots<i_4$ for which
\[
 P_n(i_0)>P_n(i_1)<P_n(i_2)>P_n(i_3)<P_n(i_4).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=1-2\varepsilon$ and $r_t(k)=\varepsilon+ak/t$. The forward equation
is
\[
 P_{t+1}(k)=(1-r_t(k))P_t(k)+r_t(k-1)P_t(k-1),
 \qquad P_1=(1-q,q).
\]
Subtracting adjacent equations gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias-first-diff}
 d_{t+1}(k)=r_t(k-1)d_t(k-1)+(1-r_t(k+1))d_t(k),
 \qquad 1\le k\le t-1.
\end{equation}
Both coefficients are at least $\min(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)>0$:
$r_t(j)$ is a convex combination of $\varepsilon$ and $1-\varepsilon$
for $0\le j\le t$. We treat the two boundaries separately.

Suppose first that $q<1$. Since
$P_t(0)=(1-q)(1-\varepsilon)^{t-1}>0$, define
\[
 \xi_t=\frac{P_t(1)}{P_t(0)},\qquad
 b=\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}<1,\qquad
 A=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}>0.
\]
The boundary equation gives
\[
 \xi_{t+1}=(1-b/t)\xi_t+A,\qquad \xi_1=\frac q{1-q}.
\]
Thus $\delta_t=\xi_{t+1}-\xi_t$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias-edge-increments}
 \delta_{t+1}=\frac{t-b}{t+1}\delta_t+\frac A{t+1}.
\end{equation}
A nonnegative increment is followed by a positive increment. Consequently,
$\xi_t$ first decreases and then increases, allowing either stretch to be
empty. For $\ell_t=d_t(0)=P_t(0)(\xi_t-1)$ this proves
\[
 \operatorname{sp}(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_N)\preceq(+,-,+).
\]
If $q\le1/2$, then $\xi_1\le1$ and the sharper bound is
$\operatorname{sp}(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_N)\preceq(-,+)$.
In particular, a positive $\ell_i$ cannot be followed by a negative
$\ell_j$. Zeros do not affect either conclusion.

Write $r_t^*=d_t(t-1)$. Reflection of the walk gives
$r_t^*(q)=-\ell_t(1-q)$ and
$d_t^{(1-q)}(t-1-k)=-d_t^{(q)}(k)$.
For $0<q\le1/2$, the right boundary history therefore has pattern
contained in $(-,+,-)$. For $q=0$ it is nonpositive throughout: the
conditional count is at most $t-1$, so $r_t^*=-P_t(t-1)\le0$.

Fix $N$ and, for $1\le t\le N$, form the augmented row
\[
 Z_t=(\ell_N,\ldots,\ell_{t+1},
       d_t(0),\ldots,d_t(t-1),
       r_{t+1}^*,\ldots,r_N^*).
\]
For $q\le1/2$, its first row is contained in $(+,-,+,-)$.
Here is an explicit check that also handles zeros at
$\ell_1=r_1^*=2q-1$. If $Z_1$ contained four ordered signs $-,+,-,+$
and the second lay in the reversed left history, the first two would give
a positive left edge followed in time by a negative one, which is
impossible. Otherwise the last three lie in the right history and have
signs $+,-,+$. For $q>0$ their reflected negatives contradict the
$(-,+,-)$ exclusion for the left history at bias $1-q$; for $q=0$ they
contradict its nonpositive right history. This proves the asserted base
pattern, including $N=1$.

Every entry of $Z_{t+1}$ is a nonnegative combination of adjacent entries
of $Z_t$. The interior entries use \eqref{eq:bias-first-diff}; the two
new boundary entries and the remaining future edge values are copied
with coefficients $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$. Such a step cannot introduce an
alternating strict-sign pattern: each nonzero entry has a parent of the
same sign, and parents of successive opposite signs are strictly ordered.
Thus $\operatorname{sp}(Z_N)\preceq(+,-,+,-)$. Reflection and sign
reversal preserve this pattern, proving the claim also for $q\ge1/2$,
including $q=1$.

Finally, extension by zero adds $d_N(-1)=P_N(0)\ge0$ on the left and
$d_N(N)=-P_N(N)\le0$ on the right; neither enlarges the pattern.
Every modal plateau contributes a positive-to-negative change, so there
are at most two. If the last displayed inequalities in the statement
held, each of their four intervals would contain an adjacent difference
of the same strict sign, by telescoping. These four differences would
have signs $-,+,-,+$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
