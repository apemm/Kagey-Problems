\begin{abstract}
A coin-turning walk on the integers switches direction at step $k$ with
probability $c/k$. We compare the end, where all $N$ steps go right, with the
other bins of the number of right steps. With a fixed first step and $c=1$
this number is exactly uniform, and the ratio of each bin to the end increases
strictly in $c$. Hence every bin from 1 to $N-1$ ties with the end exactly at $c=1$, for every
$N$. The same holds for a walk on $d$ states that moves to each other state
with probability $\theta/(k+d-2)$. Its count vector is exactly uniform at
$\theta=1$. Both laws come from a chain that draws its state afresh or repeats
it, and its counts and last state have an explicit joint law for every weight.
With a fair first step the center ties with the end at
$c_N=1-\log2/(\log N+\gamma+2-2\log2)+O((\log N)^{-3})$, where $\gamma$ is
Euler's constant. The proof rests on a local limit theorem with a rate.
\end{abstract}
