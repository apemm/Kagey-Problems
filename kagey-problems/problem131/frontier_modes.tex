\section{Spatial superlevel sets and weak-field face phases}\label{sec:frontier-modes}

The interior-composition estimate also determines the geometry of the bins
whose individual probabilities exceed the vertex probability. We give the
result in a weak external field, since this perturbation exhibits additional
face phases that the symmetric critical window suppresses.

Fix $d$ and the symmetric transition model of
Section~\ref{sec:simplex-thresholds}. For $h=(h_1,\ldots,h_d)\in\mathbb R^d$,
define the \emph{tilted occupation law}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakfield-law}
 \widetilde P_{N,h}(n)=
 \frac{e^{h\cdot n/N}P_N(n)}
 {\sum_{m_1+\cdots+m_d=N}e^{h\cdot m/N}P_N(m)}.
\end{equation}
Thus the field on each step is $h_i/N$. This is a Gibbs tilt of the
occupation law; it is not a claim that the original homogeneous transition
probabilities have acquired this bias. The normalization cancels in every
bin comparison. A modal phase below specifies which face contains the
largest individual bin probabilities; it does not assert concentration of
the total probability mass on that face. Put $L=\log N$, retain
\[
 u_N(t)=\frac{L-\tfrac12\log L+t}{N},\qquad
 p_N(t)=\frac{1}{1+(d-1)u_N(t)},
\]
and write $h_* =\max_i h_i$ and
$\widetilde V_{N,h}=\max_i\widetilde P_{N,h}(Ne_i)$.
For a support $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}$ of cardinality $k\ge2$, set
\[
 \overline h_S=\frac1k\sum_{i\in S}h_i,
 \quad B_S(t,h)=(k-1)(t-a_k)+\overline h_S-h_*,
 \quad H_S=\{y\in\mathbb R^S:\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}y_i=0\}.
\]
Euclidean measure on $H_S$ is understood, and
$\omega_s=\pi^{s/2}/\Gamma(1+s/2)$ denotes the volume of its
$s$-dimensional unit ball when $s=k-1$.

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian superlevel geometry]\label{thm:frontier-superlevel}
For a count vector with support exactly $S$, put
\[
 y_i=\sqrt L\left(\frac{n_i}{N}-\frac1k\right),\qquad i\in S.
\]
Uniformly when $t,h,y$ range over fixed compact sets, with $y\in H_S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier-profile}
 \frac{\widetilde P_{N,h}(n)}{\widetilde V_{N,h}}
 =\exp\left(B_S(t,h)-\frac{k^2}{4}\sum_{i\in S}y_i^2+o(1)\right).
\end{equation}
Let $M_{N,S}(t,h)$ be the number of bins of support exactly $S$ whose
tilted probability is strictly greater than $\widetilde V_{N,h}$. Then,
uniformly for $(t,h)$ in compact sets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier-volume}
 \frac{L^{(k-1)/2}}{N^{k-1}}M_{N,S}(t,h)
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\omega_{k-1}}{\sqrt k}
 \left(\frac{4\,[B_S(t,h)]_+}{k^2}\right)^{(k-1)/2},
 \qquad [x]_+=\max(x,0).
\end{equation}
If $B_S(t,h)<0$, there are no such bins for all sufficiently large $N$.
At $B_S(t,h)=0$, the normalized count tends to zero; no eventual emptiness
is asserted at that boundary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $\alpha_i=1/k+y_i/\sqrt L$ and $\sum_i y_i=0$, Taylor expansion on
any fixed compact set in $H_S$ gives
\[
 \left(\sum_i\sqrt{\alpha_i}\right)^2
 =k-\frac{k^2}{4L}\sum_i y_i^2+O(L^{-3/2}),
 \qquad D(\alpha)=D_k(1+o(1)).
\]
Insert these expressions and $z=L-\tfrac12\log L+t$ into
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-interior}. Since
$\log D_k=-(k-1)a_k$, the logarithm of the untilted ratio is
\[
 (k-1)(t-a_k)-\frac{k^2}{4}\sum_i y_i^2+o(1).
\]
The logarithm of the field factor relative to the largest vertex is
$h\cdot\alpha-h_*=\overline h_S-h_*+o(1)$, uniformly on the stated
compact sets. This proves~\eqref{eq:frontier-profile}.

The local estimate must also exclude superlevel bins near the boundary of
the face. We supply that step rather than assuming that a local limit
controls all bins. Fix compact sets of $t,h$, and choose a sufficiently
large constant $R$. Starting from any positive count vector on $S$,
successively transfer one unit from a coordinate $b$ to a coordinate $a$
when $b\ge a+2$. These transfers preserve the support and terminate at
a balanced vector. By Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance}, the untilted
probability strictly increases at every transfer. Each transfer changes
the scaled vector $y$ by norm $\sqrt{2L}/N$ and decreases its squared
norm. If the starting vector has $\|y\|>R$, the first vector along this
path with norm at most $R$ has norm $R+o(1)$; it lies in a compact subset
of the relative interior of the face. Its untilted log ratio to a vertex
is at most
\[
 (k-1)(t-a_k)-k^2R^2/4+o(1).
\]
This is an upper bound for the starting vector's untilted log ratio.
For every composition $\alpha$, $h\cdot\alpha-h_*\le0$. Consequently
$R$ can be chosen uniformly so that every tilted superlevel bin satisfies
$\|y\|\le R$. The same argument, with a larger $R$ if necessary, bounds
all face maximizers when comparison is made with its balanced bin.

Inside this ball the logarithms in~\eqref{eq:frontier-profile} converge
uniformly. For any $\delta>0$, their positive set is sandwiched between
the lattice points in balls of squared radii
$4[B_S-\delta]_+/k^2$ and $4[B_S+\delta]_+/k^2$, with the inner ball
interpreted as empty when $B_S\le\delta$. The scaled count vectors form
a translate of the lattice
\[
 \frac{\sqrt L}{N}\{z\in\mathbb Z^S:\textstyle\sum_i z_i=0\}
 \quad\hbox{in }H_S.
\]
Its covolume is $\sqrt{k}(\sqrt L/N)^{k-1}$: the basis
$e_i-e_k$, $1\le i<k$, has Gram determinant $k$.
Lattice Riemann sums over balls therefore give the stated volume constant.
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves~\eqref{eq:frontier-volume}, including
$B_S=0$. The radii stay bounded on compact parameter sets, so the same
sandwich proves uniform convergence. If $B_S<0$, the uniform profile on
the confining ball is strictly below one for large $N$, proving emptiness.
\end{proof}

At zero field, all supports of the same size have the same count, so the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:frontier-volume} is multiplied by
$\binom dk$ when counting all bins with $k$ positive coordinates.
For $k=2$, writing $y=(y_1,-y_1)$ gives the profile
$\exp(t-a_2-2y_1^2)$ and the one-face count constant
$\sqrt{2[t-a_2]_+}$, agreeing with the free binary window after its
centering convention is taken into account.

\begin{theorem}[weak-field modal phase diagram]\label{thm:weakfield-phases}
Define the face scores
\[
 \lambda_S(t,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 h_i,& S=\{i\},\\
 (|S|-1)(t-a_{|S|})+\overline h_S,& |S|\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The largest bin weight, expressed relative to the untilted vertex before
normalizing~\eqref{eq:weakfield-law}, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakfield-maximum}
 \log\max_n\left\{e^{h\cdot n/N}\frac{P_N(n)}{V_N}\right\}
 \longrightarrow \max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}
 \lambda_S(t,h).
\end{equation}
For fixed $(t,h)$, every global mode has, for all sufficiently large $N$,
a support maximizing the limiting score. If the maximizing support is
unique, all global modes have exactly that support. On any selected
support $S$ with $|S|\ge2$, their locations satisfy
\[
 \max_{i\in S}\left|\frac{n_i}{N}-\frac1{|S|}\right|
 =o((\log N)^{-1/2}).
\]
These assertions do not identify the maximizing integer vectors exactly,
and equality of limiting scores does not assert finite-$N$ coexistence.

If the fields are sorted $h_1\ge\cdots\ge h_d$, the score maximization
reduces to the upper envelope of the $d$ lines
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakfield-lines}
 \lambda_1(t)=h_1,\qquad
 \lambda_k(t)=(k-1)(t-a_k)+\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k h_i,
 \quad 2\le k\le d.
\end{equation}
Away from ties, the winning support size is nondecreasing as $t$ increases.
If the fields are strictly ordered, the corresponding supports are the
nested sets $\{1,\ldots,k\}$. Every support size $k\in\{2,\ldots,d-1\}$
can be the unique winning size on an open interval for suitable fixed
fields. Thus proper-face phases can persist asymptotically under a field
of order $1/N$ per step.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each fixed support, the confinement argument in the preceding proof
applies also to its maximum, because the field factor is bounded above
and below uniformly in $N$. On that compact scaled region, the profile
relative to the untilted vertex is
$\exp(\lambda_S-k^2\|y\|^2/4+o(1))$.
Balanced integer vectors have $y=o(1)$, so the face maximum converges to
$e^{\lambda_S}$. There are finitely many supports. Taking their maximum
proves~\eqref{eq:weakfield-maximum}, and the positive gap to any
nonmaximizing score excludes its support for large $N$. If a sequence of
maximizers within a selected face had scaled norm bounded away from zero,
the uniform Gaussian profile would make it strictly smaller than that
face's balanced bin. This proves the location statement.

Among supports of a given size $k$, the largest field average is the
average of the $k$ largest fields. This proves~\eqref{eq:weakfield-lines}.
For $t_1<t_2$, a line of strictly larger slope cannot win at $t_1$ and
lose to a line of smaller slope at $t_2$: subtracting the two comparisons
would give $(k_1-k_2)(t_2-t_1)\le0$. Hence the winning size is
nondecreasing away from ties.

To realize a prescribed $2\le k<d$, set $h_1=\cdots=h_k=0$ and
$h_{k+1}=\cdots=h_d=-H$. Choose $t>a_k$ sufficiently close to $a_k$ so
that $t<a_{k-1}$ if $k>2$. The $k$-face score is positive; all smaller
nonvertex face scores are negative, and the largest vertex score is zero.
For $j>k$, the largest score is
\[
 (j-1)(t-a_j)-H\frac{j-k}{j}.
\]
Taking $H$ sufficiently large makes each of these strictly smaller than
the $k$-face score. Every other support of size $k$ includes a negative
field and also loses. All comparisons are strict, so they persist on
an open interval of $t$. Small perturbations can make the fields
strictly ordered without removing the phase.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[one field realizes the full face hierarchy]
\label{cor:weakfield-full-hierarchy}
Fix $d\ge2$ and $H>3/4$, and choose
\[
 h_i=-H(i-1)^2,\qquad 1\le i\le d.
\]
In particular $h_i=-(i-1)^2$ is a single explicit choice valid in every
fixed dimension. Put
\[
 c_k=(k+\tfrac12)\log k-\frac{k-1}{2}\log(4\pi),\qquad
 \tau_k=c_k-c_{k+1}+\frac{H(4k-1)}6\quad(1\le k<d),
\]
and set $\tau_0=-\infty$, $\tau_d=+\infty$. Then
$\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{d-1}$, and for every $k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$ the
support $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ is the unique winning support throughout the
nonempty interval $\tau_{k-1}<t<\tau_k$. Consequently, for each fixed
$t$ in this interval and all sufficiently large $N$, every global mode
of the tilted law has exactly those $k$ positive coordinates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $c_1=0$ and $c_k=-(k-1)a_k$ for $k\ge2$. Since
$k^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^k(i-1)^2=(k-1)(2k-1)/6$, the lines in
\eqref{eq:weakfield-lines} have intercepts
$c_k-H(k-1)(2k-1)/6$. The intersection of lines $k$ and $k+1$ is
therefore $t=\tau_k$. Extend $c_k$ to the displayed smooth function
$c(x)$ for $x\ge1$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 0<c''(x)=\frac{x-1/2}{x^2}\le\frac12.
\]
Integrating this bound twice over unit intervals yields
$c_{k+2}-2c_{k+1}+c_k\le1/2$. Hence
\[
 \tau_{k+1}-\tau_k
 =\frac{2H}{3}-(c_{k+2}-2c_{k+1}+c_k)>0.
\]
For $\tau_{k-1}<t<\tau_k$, summing consecutive differences
$\lambda_{j+1}(t)-\lambda_j(t)=t-\tau_j$ shows that line $k$
strictly exceeds every earlier and later line. The fields are strictly
ordered, so its unique best support is $\{1,\ldots,k\}$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakfield-phases} to obtain the finite-$N$ conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[a persistent triangular edge phase]\label{ex:weakfield-triangle}
For $d=3$ and $h=(0,0,-H)$, the three competing maximal scores are
\[
 0,\qquad t-a_2,\qquad 2(t-a_3)-H/3.
\]
If $H>6(a_2-a_3)$, the edge with support $\{1,2\}$ wins strictly for
\[
 a_2<t<H/3+2a_3-a_2.
\]
For every fixed $t$ in this interval, all global modes of the tilted law
lie on that edge for sufficiently large $N$. This differs from the
untilted law, where proper-face modes disappear from the asymptotic
critical window. It does not strengthen the finite-size counterexample
of Example~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode} into an untilted asymptotic claim.
\end{example}
