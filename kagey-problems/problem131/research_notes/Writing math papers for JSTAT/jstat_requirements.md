# JSTAT (Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment): submission requirements for a rigorous probability paper (as of Oct 2026)

Research note: all jstat.sissa.it and publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org pages below were fetched on 2026-10-15. iopscience.iop.org journal pages (scope, about, APC, metrics) were bot-blocked (Radware redirect) and could not be fetched, so APC amounts, the formal peer-review model label and journal metrics there are unverified. JSTAT's own author help page is the main source: https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author

## Scope and sections; is rigorous probability in scope?

### Takeaway
JSTAT's scope is "statistical physics" broadly, and it is explicitly not fixed. It has five keyword-defined sections, and "Exact results", "Rigorous results in statistical mechanics" and "Stochastic processes" are official keywords. A rigorous probability paper is in scope only when it is framed as statistical physics. The journal publishes no scope statement for pure mathematics and no explicit out-of-scope list.

### Cited Findings
- Scope (IOP publishing-support page): "JSTAT is targeted to a broad community interested in different aspects of statistical physics, which are roughly defined by the fields represented in the conferences called 'Statistical Physics'." It also says submissions from experimentalists with a "connection to statistical physics are also strongly encouraged." — [IOP Publishing Support, JSTAT](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/journal-of-statistical-mechanics-theory-and-experiment/)
- JSTAT About page: "The Journal covers different topics which correspond to the following keyword sections". It also says "The Journal's scope should not be regarded as fixed" and that the scope will change with research trends. — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- The five sections are: (1) Quantum statistical physics, condensed matter, integrable systems; (2) Classical statistical mechanics, equilibrium and non-equilibrium; (3) Disordered systems, classical and quantum; (4) Interdisciplinary statistical mechanics; (5) Biological modelling and information. There is no standalone "Probability" or "Mathematical physics" section. — [JSTAT keyword list](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=kwList)
- Acceptance criteria: "The criteria for acceptance are scientific quality, originality and relevance." Editors are expected to apply a strict quality filter, and "Serial publication should be avoided." — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- Referee criteria: contributions "must contain significant new material, must be of high quality and scientific interest" and "must be on subject matter covered by the Journal." — [JSTAT For Referees](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=referee)
- SISSA Medialab describes the journal this way: "JSTAT is targeted to the broad community of statistical physicists." It was launched in 2004 and is "jointly owned by IoPP and SISSA Medialab." — [SISSA Medialab JSTAT page](https://www.sissamedialab.it/en/node/17)
- No explicit out-of-scope list was found. The About page "does not list topics that are out of scope", and mathematical papers are not mentioned either way. — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)

### Inferences
- The audience is statistical physicists, not probabilists. A paper on a persistent random walk, a 1D Ising chain or a Galton board needs an introduction that states the statistical-physics question (for example persistence, first passage or exact solvability) and puts the theorems in that context.
- Because the acceptance criterion includes "relevance" to the community, an abstract-probability paper with no physical model or interpretation is the highest-risk profile.

### Gaps
- No JSTAT editorial was found that explicitly addresses pure-math or rigorous-probability submissions.
- The scope text on iopscience.iop.org could not be fetched because the site blocked the bot.

## Article types, length, abstract and title rules

### Takeaway
JSTAT has regular Papers, Special Issue papers, errata/addenda, and invited reviews and perspective reviews. No Letters type was found on the current pages. There is no word or page limit. The abstract must fit on the first page, and the title and abstract should contain no formulae or references.

### Cited Findings
- "JSTAT also publishes invited Special Issues, reviews and perspective reviews." — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- "The procedure for submitting standard research papers and papers for a special issue is the same." Errata and addenda are submitted as stand-alone articles and are "peer-reviewed, typeset and proofread before being published." — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Length: no word limit is stated. Conciseness is encouraged, but "clarity and consistency are more important". Editors judge whether the length is justified and may suggest cuts. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Abstract and title: "The abstract should briefly summarize the content of the contribution and must fit in the first page." Also: "Please avoid formulae and references in the title and abstract." — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Structure: "Please divide the text into sections." Use standard English and keep jargon and acronyms to a minimum. From March 2025, if the front matter runs past two pages, the full author list goes at the end of the article. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- The editor flowchart sets a target of "within 5 weeks" for the review procedure "for Papers". — [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow)
- At the generic IOP level, abstracts over 300 words "may lead to the manuscript being rescinded" (quoted from the J. Phys. A page, which uses generic IOP guidance). — [IOP Publishing Support, J. Phys. A](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/journal-of-physics-a-mathematical-and-theoretical/)

### Inferences
- Write a short, formula-free abstract (well under 300 words), for example "the probability that a persistent random walk crosses...", with no symbols such as $p$ or $\alpha$.
- JSTAT historically had a "Letters" category. It is not mentioned on the current pages, so treat it as unavailable or unverified.

### Gaps
- No explicit title-length rule was found.
- No page-count guidance for reviews was found.

## Mandatory keyword list and keywords for this paper

### Takeaway
Authors must pick 1 to 4 keywords from JSTAT's controlled list. The keywords decide which editor handles the paper. For a persistent-random-walk, 1D Ising or Galton board crossing paper, the best verified fits are "Exact results", "Persistence", "First Passage", "Stochastic processes" and "Rigorous results in statistical mechanics". "Random walks" is not on the list.

### Cited Findings
- "At the time of submission you must characterise your work with a number of keywords selected from the JSTAT keyword list. ... Please choose from 1 to 4 keywords which most accurately describe the subject of your work." — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- The keywords determine the handling editor. The system "automatically routes the paper to an editor based on the author's chosen keywords". Authors may indicate a preferred section, but the final placement is decided later. — [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow); [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Relevant keywords that appear verbatim in the list, by section:
  - **Classical statistical mechanics, equilibrium and non-equilibrium:** "Exact results", "Persistence", "First Passage", "Brownian motion", "Diffusion", "Renewal Processes", "Classical phase transitions", "Kinetic Ising models", "Correlation functions", "Stochastic particle dynamics", "Fluctuation phenomena", "Extreme value", "Large deviations in non-equilibrium systems", "Dynamical processes", "Stationary states"
  - **Quantum statistical physics, condensed matter, integrable systems:** "Rigorous results in statistical mechanics", "Solvable lattice models", "Topology and combinatorics", "Classical integrability"
  - **Interdisciplinary statistical mechanics:** "Stochastic processes", "First Passage", "Large deviation", "Renewal Processes", "Extreme value statistics"
  - Source for all of the above: [JSTAT keyword list](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=kwList)
- These candidate terms are not on the list: "random walks", "probability", "persistent random walk", "telegraph process" and "Galton board". — [JSTAT keyword list](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=kwList)

### Inferences
- Suggested set of 4 that targets the classical section: **Exact results; Persistence; First Passage; Stochastic processes**. Swap in "Rigorous results in statistical mechanics" if rigor is the main selling point.
- "Rigorous results in statistical mechanics" sits in the quantum and integrable section. Using it may route the paper to an integrable-systems or quantum editor. Combining it with classical keywords such as "Exact results" may split the routing, so check the preferred-section field at submission.
- If the 1D Ising mapping is central, "Classical phase transitions" fits poorly, because the 1D Ising chain has no finite-temperature transition. "Solvable lattice models" or "Correlation functions" fit better.

### Gaps
- It is not documented how a mixed-section keyword set is resolved for editor assignment.

## Manuscript format

### Takeaway
LaTeX with the IOP template is preferred, and JSTAT has its own "JSTAT class". References are numbered in square brackets in order of citation. A Data/Software/Code Availability Statement at the end is expected. Submission is by arXiv ID or file upload.

### Cited Findings
- "Manuscripts should be preferably prepared in LaTeX" using the IoP template. The template is at https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/questions/latex-template/ and the JSTAT homepage links it as "JSTAT class". — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author); [JSTAT home](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/)
- Packages: use standard packages. Common older packages are installed on the server, and unrecognised packages must be included in the submission. Include the .bbl file if you use BibTeX, and the .gls file if you use glossaries. MS Word is accepted but may not convert reliably. Use TrueType fonts only. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- References: use sequential numbers in square brackets, with the list in numerical order (not alphabetical). Citations of data, software and code are strongly encouraged, ideally with DOIs. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- "Data/Software/Code Availability Statements should be included at the end of articles submitted to JSTAT." — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Figures: .ps, .eps, .jpg, .png and .pdf are accepted. Every figure and table must be cited in the text and have a caption. Large tables, figures or multimedia can be uploaded as attachments; these are reviewed but not typeset. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Submission routes: arXiv-based submission (TeX/LaTeX only) or web upload. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)

### Inferences
- For a pure-math paper with no data, still add a short statement at the end, for example "No new data were generated; code used for numerical checks is available at [DOI]."
- amsthm theorem environments are standard. Bundle any non-standard style files with the submission.

### Gaps
- The template page itself was not fetched, so it is unverified whether the JSTAT class is a separate .cls file or the generic iopjournal class.

## Review model, timelines, editorial structure, appeals

### Takeaway
JSTAT uses traditional confidential peer review. Referees are anonymous and editors are anonymous to authors. Each paper has one handling editor, chosen by keywords, who uses "one or more" referees. The target is 5 weeks. Editors can declare a paper "Not suitable" with no full report, and that decision cannot be appealed. Ordinary rejections can be appealed once.

### Cited Findings
- Editors "should send the new preprints to one or more referee" within 5 days. Referees accept or decline within 3 days and review within 4 weeks. "Editors are requested to do their best to complete the review procedure for Papers within 5 weeks." — [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow); [JSTAT For Referees](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=referee); [JSTAT For Editors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=editor)
- "Editors take full responsibility for the submission." Editors may review a paper themselves or send it to referees. Editors and referees work anonymously but may reveal their identity. The flowchart describes the process as traditional peer review with guaranteed confidentiality. Referees are told: "Please remember not to sign the report." — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about); [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow); [JSTAT For Referees](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=referee)
- Desk rejection: the "Not suitable for the journal" decision is for articles "clearly suited for another journal or articles of an obviously poor quality". It should be issued "only if it is crystal clear that the article should not be considered in JSTAT". A brief motivation is expected, not a full report. Appeals cannot be processed after a not-suitable decision. — [JSTAT For Editors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=editor)
- Appeals after a normal rejection: authors "can request the editor-in-charge to reconsider their decision on the grounds of serious and not polemic scientific reasons." "Second appeals are not considered unless there is a clear history of unfair treatment of the manuscript." — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Revisions: authors get one month and may request an extension. Otherwise the paper is withdrawn and must be resubmitted as new. A cover letter replying to the referees is mandatory; enter it in the text box, not in the archive. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author); [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow)
- Editorial structure: "JSTAT has a large Editorial Board in order to provide competent coverage for all the areas". "The Chief Scientific Director is in charge of supervising the Editorial Board". — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- Named directors (from a search snippet, not verified on a fetched page): section scientific directors include Eduardo Fradkin and Giuseppe Mussardo (quantum and integrable), David Mukamel, Matteo Marsili and Mussardo (classical), Fradkin and Riccardo Zecchina (disordered), Marsili and Zecchina (interdisciplinary), and Marsili, William Bialek and Zecchina (biological). — [WebSearch summary of JSTAT About / IOP about_the_journal](https://iopscience.iop.org/1742-5468/page/about_the_journal)
- Mussardo describes himself as "Scientific Director" of JSTAT. — [Mussardo homepage](https://people.sissa.it/~mussardo/)
- SISSA Medialab disclosures for 2019 to 2023 list Marc Mézard as JSTAT Direttore (director). — [SISSA Medialab disclosure 2023](https://medialab.sissa.it/sites/default/files/societa-trasparente/03-consulenti-e-collaboratori/direttori-vicedirettori-scientific-advisor-2023.pdf)
- Wikipedia also lists Mézard as editor-in-chief. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Mechanics:_Theory_and_Experiment)

### Inferences
- In effect this is single-anonymous review: author names are visible and the referees are anonymous. No anonymisation of the manuscript is required.
- No editor with a dedicated probability or mathematical-physics remit was identified. Rigorous papers are handled by statistical-physics editors, so the keyword choice determines who reads the paper.

## Policies: preprints, prior rejection, cover letter, suggested referees, OA/APC, transfers

### Takeaway
arXiv posting is allowed and encouraged, and submission can be made directly from an arXiv ID. The submitted version must match the arXiv version. JSTAT is hybrid: authors can pay an APC, use a transformative agreement, or publish subscription-only. No suggested-referee field or prior-rejection policy is documented.

### Cited Findings
- "If you have posted your preprint on the Archives, please enter its ID number in the submission form". If an arXiv number is given, "the submitted version must be the same as the arXiv version". "JSTAT encourages authors to submit their accepted manuscript to arXiv at any time" (subject to the arXiv licence). — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- At the generic IOP level, preprints are accepted only if the author has not transferred copyright or granted an exclusive licence. — [IOP Publishing Support, J. Phys. A](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/journal-of-physics-a-mathematical-and-theoretical/)
- "Manuscripts submitted to JSTAT must not have been published or be under consideration" elsewhere. — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- IOP ethics on duplication: "It is unethical to submit the same, or essentially the same, article to a second primary research journal". Authors "should state in their cover letter if there are sections of the article that have already been published elsewhere." — [IOP ethical policy](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/ethical-policy-journals/)
- Open access options: "Open Access paid by Author" (APC) or "Open Access – Transformative Agreement". Gold OA articles use CC BY 4.0. Otherwise copyright transfers to IOP Publishing and SISSA Medialab on acceptance. The APC schedule is at https://iopscience.iop.org/journal/1742-5468/page/publication_charges and the APC contact is jstat@ioppublishing.org. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- Wikipedia describes JSTAT as hybrid OA and monthly. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Mechanics:_Theory_and_Experiment)
- Metrics (secondary aggregator, unverified): 2025 IF 1.9, 5-year IF 2.1, JCR Q2. — [journalmetrics.org](https://www.journalmetrics.org/journal/journal-of-statistical-mechanics-theory-and-experiment)
- Wikipedia gives a 2023 IF of 2.2. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Mechanics:_Theory_and_Experiment)
- Editage gives a CiteScore of 3.9. — [Editage](https://www.editage.com/journal/journal-of-statistical-mechanics-theory-and-experiment/)
- Communication goes through the site's "write to editor" and "write to Editorial Office" buttons. The Editorial Office address is jstat-eo@jstat.sissa.it. — [JSTAT Flowchart](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=flow); [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)

### Inferences
- JSTAT runs its own submission system at jstat.sissa.it, not IOP's ScholarOne. Automatic IOP transfer cascades probably do not apply, but this is unverified.

## AI policy

### Takeaway
JSTAT requires authors to declare AI use in the Methods or Acknowledgments section. IOP's 2026 policy is more specific: a separate AI disclosure statement in the Acknowledgements that names the tool and version, describes how it was used, and explains how outputs were verified. Research-process use must also be described in Methods. Routine language editing is exempt.

### Cited Findings
- JSTAT: authors "are thus required to declare and properly reference in detail the use of AI assisted technology in the preparation of the manuscript". The declaration goes in the Methods or Acknowledgments section, and authors remain responsible for the content. — [JSTAT For Authors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=author)
- IOP: "authors must disclose this in a separate AI disclosure statement as part of the Acknowledgement section of their article."
  - "The disclosure statement should identify the tool or model used, describe how it was used" and "explain the extent to which the authors verified, validated and critically reviewed the AI-assisted outputs."
  - Research use "should be additionally described in detail in the Methods".
  - "routine spelling, grammar and language editing that does not materially contribute to the manuscript" is exempt.
  - Template wording: "During the preparation of this manuscript, the authors used [Tool Name, Version] to support [describe use]."
  - "All authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality and integrity of all content in their submission".
  - Authors may not "Upload reports from reviewers to generative AI tools" or "Generate responses to reviewers".
  - Fabricated references are treated as strong evidence of irresponsible AI use.
  - Source: [IOP ethical policy for journals](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/ethical-policy-journals/)

### Inferences
- The two placements are compatible: put a separate "AI disclosure" paragraph inside the Acknowledgements. If AI was used for proofs, computations or symbolic checks, also describe that in a methods-type section, for example "Computational verification".
- The ban on AI-generated responses to reviewers matters at the revision stage.

### Gaps
- No JSTAT-specific editorial on AI was found beyond the author-page paragraph.

## Desk-rejection risks; JSTAT vs J. Stat. Phys. vs J. Phys. A

### Takeaway
JSTAT's desk-reject route ("Not suitable") targets papers "clearly suited for another journal" or of "obviously poor quality". For a mathematical paper the main risks are low relevance to statistical physicists, incremental or serial work, and a formula-heavy abstract. J. Phys. A is the IOP alternative explicitly framed around mathematical structures. J. Stat. Phys. (Springer) is the traditional home for rigorous probability in statistical mechanics.

### Cited Findings
- "Not suitable" covers articles "clearly suited for another journal or articles of an obviously poor quality". It should be used "only if it is crystal clear that the article should not be considered in JSTAT", and it cannot be appealed. — [JSTAT For Editors](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=editor)
- "Serial publication should be avoided." Acceptance criteria are "scientific quality, originality and relevance." — [JSTAT About](https://jstat.sissa.it/jstat/help/helpLoader.jsp?pgType=about)
- Generic IOP screening (from the J. Phys. A page): "Reporting incremental steps forward from previous work is usually not sufficient". Papers must be "scientifically rigorous" with "sound motivation and purpose". Abstracts over 300 words may be rescinded. Submissions featuring the Lena/Lenna image will not be considered. — [IOP Publishing Support, J. Phys. A](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/journal-of-physics-a-mathematical-and-theoretical/)
- J. Phys. A scope: "a major journal of theoretical physics" covering "the mathematical structures that describe fundamental processes of the physical world" and "the analytical, computational and numerical methods for exploring these structures." — [IOP Publishing Support, J. Phys. A](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/journal-of-physics-a-mathematical-and-theoretical/)
- JSTAT is published by SISSA (Medialab) and IOP Publishing. It is a different journal from Springer's Journal of Statistical Physics. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Mechanics:_Theory_and_Experiment); [SISSA Medialab](https://www.sissamedialab.it/en/node/17)

### Inferences
- Mitigations for desk-rejection risk:
  - Open with the physics question, citing the persistence, first-passage and Ising literature.
  - State the physical implications of the exact formulas.
  - Keep the abstract formula-free.
  - Add a figure or simulation check that statistical physicists will recognise.
  - Avoid splitting the work into a series of papers.
- JSTAT vs J. Phys. A for mathematical papers: J. Phys. A explicitly welcomes mathematical structures and methods. It has a "Mathematical physics" section and a "Statistical physics" section (section names from prior knowledge, not verified on a fetched page), and it runs on IOP's ScholarOne system. JSTAT is the statistical-physics community journal, edited through SISSA, with keyword-routed editors who judge "relevance" to statistical physicists. A theorem-proof paper whose interest is mainly mathematical fits J. Phys. A, or J. Stat. Phys., better than JSTAT.
- J. Stat. Phys. (Springer) has a long tradition of rigorous probability and mathematical statistical mechanics. Its policies were not researched here, so this characterisation comes from general knowledge and is unverified.

### Gaps
- No JSTAT editorial or editor statement on desk-rejection rates or on "too mathematical" rejections was found.
- No public desk-rejection statistics were found.
