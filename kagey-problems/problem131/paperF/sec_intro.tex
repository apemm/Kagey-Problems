\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

An on/off process is a two-state process in discrete time whose on periods and off periods
alternate and have independent lengths. Let the on periods have the law of $X_\plus$ and the
off periods the law of $X_\minus$, two laws on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ with regularly varying tails
of indices $\alpha_\plus$ and $\alpha_\minus$ in $(1,2)$, so that a period has a finite mean
and an infinite variance. Let $A_N$ be the number of time slots spent in the on state
among the first $N$. The law of $A_N$ in distribution is classical. Under a finite
variance $A_N$ is Gaussian at the scale $\sqrt N$~\cite{takacs1957,takacs1959,kesten1962},
under infinite means $A_N/N$ has the generalized arcsine law of
Lamperti~\cite{lamperti1958}, and in the regime of this paper $A_N-pN$ is stable at the
scale $N^{1/\alpha}$, where $p$ is the long-run fraction of on
time~\cite{takacs1957,godrecheluck2001}. These are statements about distribution
functions. We describe the mass function $P_N(k)=\Prob(A_N=k)$ at every point $k$ of its
range $\{0,\dots,N\}$. The bulk is a stable density, uniformly over the whole range
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk}). On each side of the bulk and up to the endpoint the law is given
by one kernel, which adds a complete giant period and the period in progress at time $N$,
and which contains a renewal boundary layer at each end and an atom at each endpoint
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump}). An arbitrary initial period enters each of these pieces
through a sharp criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation}), and observation from a later
time ages the atom, the layer and the big-jump zone but not the bulk
(Theorem~\ref{thm:aging}). The shape follows. The endpoint is the global mode above or
below the golden ratio (Theorem~\ref{thm:mode}), an interior minimum sits at the stable
scale of the opposite periods (Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode}), and the law has exactly
$M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes for all large $N$, where $M_\pm$ counts the local maxima of the two
boundary profiles (Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count}). Section~\ref{sec:exposure} reads these
theorems as the exposure law of an experiment that switches treatment at the regenerations
of a queue with heavy-tailed service.

The same process is a lattice L\'evy walk. Read an on slot as a step to the right and an off
slot as a step to the left. The walk moves in runs of alternating direction, its position
after $N$ steps is $2A_N-N$, and $P_N$ is the law of the walk on its natural lattice. L\'evy
walks are a standard model of superdiffusion~\cite{zdk2015}, and the same walk describes heat
transport in one-dimensional lattices~\cite{dsd2013,wlh2016}. In continuous time the propagator
of a L\'evy walk is a stable bulk between two ballistic peaks at the fronts, formed by the
walkers still in their first flight~\cite[Section~II.A.2 and Fig.~2]{zdk2015} (see
also~\cite{zdh2011,dzh2012}), and the law between the bulk and the fronts is the infinite
density of Rebenshtok, Denisov, H\"anggi and Barkai~\cite{rdhb2014,rdhb2014b}. On the lattice
the peaks are the endpoint atoms and the boundary layers, and the infinite density is
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} read at $k=vN$. The picture is known. What we add is its exact
form at a fixed time, its dependence on the start, and an exact count of its peaks. We keep
the vocabulary of the walk in the body of the paper. A period is a run, an on slot is a plus
step, an off slot is a minus step, and the two ends of the range are the plus end $k=N$ and
the minus end $k=0$.

\subsection{The four pieces of the law}\label{sec:intro-picture}

Figure~\ref{fig:atlas} shows $P_N$ for $N=5000$ on a logarithmic scale. Four pieces make up
the law.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The bulk. Near $pN$ the law is a stable density at the scale $B_N$ of the larger of
the two run-length fluctuations. Here $B_N$ grows like $N^{1/\alpha_*}$ up to a slowly
varying factor, with $\alpha_*$ the smaller index.
\item The big-jump zones. A count $k$ with $k-pN$ much larger than $B_N$ needs one plus run
of macroscopic length, a giant run, and the law of that run gives $P_N(k)$ its size, which is
of order $T_\plus(N)$ on the ballistic range, with $T_\plus$ the tail of a plus run. The giant
run is either a complete run in the interior or the run in progress at time $N$, and
$P_N(k)$ is the sum of the two contributions.
\item The boundary layers. At $k=N-j$ with $j$ fixed, the giant run is the one in progress at
time $N$, and the only question is where it started. It starts right after the $j$-th minus
step exactly when a minus run ends there, so the layer is $T_\plus(N)$ times the renewal mass
function $u_\minus(j)$ of the minus runs.
\item The atoms. The point $k=N$ is the event that the first run covers all $N$ steps, and
its mass is the tail of the first run.
\end{itemize}
The first run of the walk is its initial run. It may be an ordinary run, a run seen from a
typical time, the run in progress at the time the observation starts, or anything else, and
it enters each of the four pieces through its own criterion.

\subsection{Main results}\label{sec:intro-results}

\emph{Hypotheses.} We state them in Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}. They are regular variation
of the two tails with indices in $(1,2)$, written (RV), the local form
$f_\sgn(n)\sim\alpha_\sgn T_\sgn(n)/n$ of the mass functions, written (LR), and aperiodicity,
written (SPAN). Sections~\ref{sec:extrema} and~\ref{sec:mode-count} also use bounds on the
first and second differences of the mass functions, written (LD) and (LD2), and
Section~\ref{sec:mode-count} a comparison of the two tails, written (EQ). The run laws may
differ between the two signs, so the two stable scales $b_{\plus,N}$ and $b_{\minus,N}$ may
differ, and their ratio may oscillate. Every theorem below except Theorems~\ref{thm:window}
and~\ref{thm:mode-count} holds for every relation between the two scales. We write $s$ for
the sum of the two mean run lengths and $p$ for the fraction of the plus mean.

\emph{The bulk.} Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk} says that for a fresh start, under (RV) and (SPAN),
\[
 \sup_{k\in\Z}\bigl|B_NP_N(k)-h_N\bigl((k-pN)/B_N\bigr)\bigr|\to0,
 \qquad \sup_kP_N(k)\le\frac C{B_N},
\]
where $h_N$ is the density of $(1-p)\theta_{\plus,N}Z_\plus-p\,\theta_{\minus,N}Z_\minus$, with
$Z_\pm$ independent totally skewed stable variables. The supremum runs over the whole range
of the count. On a window $|k-pN|\le KB_N$ the local limit also follows from a strong renewal
theorem of Berger~\cite[Theorem~3.3]{berger2019}, and what is new is the uniformity over
every point under (RV) and (SPAN) alone. Outside the bulk the theorem says only that
$P_N(k)=o(1/B_N)$, and the next theorem says what $P_N(k)$ is there.

\emph{Big jumps and the boundary layer.} Put $L_k=(k-pN)/(1-p)$, the length of the plus run
that carries a typical configuration to the count $k$. Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} says that
for a fresh plus start, under (RV) and (SPAN) for both signs and (LR) for the plus sign,
\[
 P_N(k)=\Bigl[\gamma(N-k)\,f_\plus(\lfloor L_k\rfloor)+T_\plus(\lfloor L_k\rfloor)\,u_\minus(N-k)\Bigr](1+o(1))
\]
uniformly on $pN+K\lambda_NB_N\le k\le N$, in the sense that the error tends to zero as
$N\to\infty$ and then $K\to\infty$. Here $\gamma(j)$ is an explicit finite sum with
$\gamma(j)\sim js/\mu_\minus^2$, and $\lambda_N=1+\log(B_N/b_{\plus,N})$, which equals $1$ when
the plus scale is the larger one. The first term is a complete giant run and the second the
run in progress at time $N$. At $k=N-j$ with $j$ fixed the theorem reduces to
$P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)\to u_\minus(j)$, which needs only (RV) for the plus sign. As $j$ grows,
$u_\minus(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$ and the layer joins the big-jump zone continuously. Read at
$k=vN$ the theorem gives the infinite density
\[
 \frac{P_N(\lfloor vN\rfloor)}{T_\plus(N)}\to
 \frac1{\mu_\minus}\Bigl[\alpha_\plus(1-\ell)\ell^{-\alpha_\plus-1}+\ell^{-\alpha_\plus}\Bigr],
 \qquad \ell=\frac{v-p}{1-p},
\]
where the second term is the run in progress at time $N$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:infinite-density}). (LR) suffices for the theorem, and
Example~\ref{ex:rv-not-lr} shows that (RV) alone does not, since the kernel reads $f_\plus$ at
a single point. This parallels the optimality of the local hypothesis for
sums~\cite[Eq.~(2.4)]{berger2019ptrf}. When the plus scale is the larger one, the bulk profile
up to a slowly growing multiple of $B_N$ and the kernel beyond it give the whole plus side in a
single limit, while no fixed switching point does (Remark~\ref{rem:no-single-formula}). When
the plus scale is the smaller one, the window $KB_N\le k-pN\le K\lambda_NB_N$ lies between the
two theorems, and in general we have no relative error there. For a fresh start with
$b_{\minus,N}/b_{\plus,N}\to\infty$, Theorem~\ref{thm:window} narrows it. The kernel takes over
at $pN+(1+\delta)\xi_NB_N$ and not at $pN+(1-\delta)\xi_NB_N$, where $\xi_N=o(\lambda_N)$ comes
from a Cram\'er transform of the minus runs, and before it the law is a moderate deviation of
the minus runs, described on the logarithmic scale. For sums of independent variables with a
heavy left tail of index in $(1,2)$ and a lighter right tail, the same crossover at a
logarithmic multiple of the stable scale is due to Rozovski\u{\i}~\cite{rozovskii1997} for the
tail and to Denisov, Dieker and Shneer~\cite[Section~9.2]{dds2008} for local probabilities.
What Theorem~\ref{thm:window} adds is the count at a fixed time, where the number of runs is
random and the big jump may be made by the run in progress at time $N$.

\emph{The initial run.} Wang, Schulz, Deng and Barkai~\cite{wsdb2018} left ``the effect of
initial preparation on the rare fluctuations'' for future work. Their question covers all
the observables of their paper and also the range $0<\alpha<1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation}
answers it for the occupation count with $1<\alpha<2$, for every law of the initial run and
on every piece of the law. It rests on the first-run decomposition of
Proposition~\ref{prop:first-run}. Write $\nu_\sgn(n)$ for the probability that the first run
has sign $\sgn$ and length $n$, and $Q_\sgn(n)$ for the probability that it has sign $\sgn$ and
length at least $n$. On every macroscopic interior of the range of the count the bulk
approximation holds with no condition on the initial run. It holds uniformly over
$1\le k<N$ exactly when $B_n(\nu_\plus(n)+\nu_\minus(n))\to0$, which we call the bulk
criterion. The initial run is invisible in the boundary layer at the plus end exactly when
$\nu_\plus(n)=o(T_\plus(n))$. On the big-jump zone up to the end the same condition is sharp,
while on its interior only window averages of $\nu_\plus$ matter. The conditions are nested,
and when one fails the theorem gives the exact correction. The stationary initial run, the
run in progress at a typical time of a two-sided stationary process, satisfies the bulk
criterion and fails the other two, and Corollary~\ref{cor:stationary} gives its corrections
in closed form. Corollary~\ref{cor:no-trim} shows that no trimming of the range
$\{0,\dots,N\}$ by $o(N)$ points removes the dependence on the initial run for every initial
run at once.

\emph{Aging.} Let the process start fresh at time $0$ and count the plus steps among the
steps $t_a+1,\dots,t_a+N$, with $t_a/N\to\theta$. This is the aged occupation time of the
physics literature, whose law in distribution for $0<\alpha<1$ is due to Akimoto, Sera,
Yamato and Yano~\cite{asyy2020}. For $1<\alpha<2$ the run in progress at time $t_a$ is an
initial run whose law depends on $N$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:aging} gives the aged law at every
point by extending Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation} to such triangular arrays. The bulk does not
age, for every $t_a(N)$. The atom becomes $NT_\plus(N)(1-(1+\theta)^{1-\alpha_\plus})/(s(\alpha_\plus-1))$,
the boundary layer becomes
\[
 \frac{P_N(N-j)}{T_\plus(N)}\to p\,u_\minus(j)+\frac1s+\frac1s\bigl(1-(1+\theta)^{-\alpha_\plus}\bigr)\bigl(1+\mu_\plus u_\minus(j)\bigr),
\]
and the interior of the big-jump zone gains the term
$[T_\plus(L_k)-T_\plus(L_k+t_a)]/\mu_\minus$, the probability that the run in progress at time
$t_a$ is the giant run. At $\theta=0$ these are the fresh forms with the atom replaced by
$t_aT_\plus(N)/s$, and at $\theta=\infty$ they are the stationary forms of
Corollary~\ref{cor:stationary}. The third term of the layer is the complete initial run,
which the fresh start never sees.

\emph{The global mode and the golden ratio.} Theorem~\ref{thm:mode} compares the endpoint
atoms with the bulk for an initial run that satisfies the bulk criterion. An endpoint is
the global mode when its atom exceeds $H_N/B_N$ by a fixed factor, with $H_N$ the height of
the profile, and the bulk wins when both atoms are smaller by a fixed factor. For the
stationary initial run with equal indices this is the golden ratio threshold. The endpoints
win for $\alpha<\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, the bulk wins for $\alpha>\varphi$, and at
$\alpha=\varphi$ with pure power tails the bulk wins by the explicit factor
$1/\mathcal C(\varphi)=1.288\ldots$, with $\mathcal C$ as in~\eqref{eq:Calpha}. The threshold
is the exponent identity $\alpha-1=1/\alpha$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:aged-mode} shows that it
survives aging for every $\theta\in(0,\infty]$. For a fresh start the mode is always
interior.

\emph{The antimode and the extrema near the ends.} Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode} locates the
interior minimum between the bulk and the plus end for a fresh plus start. If
$u_\minus(j)>1/\mu_\minus$ for every $j\ge1$, which holds for every strictly log-convex law of
the minus runs, the minimum sits at $N-j^*_N$ with $T_\minus(j^*_N)\sim2\alpha_\plus s/N$, so
$j^*_N$ grows like the stable scale of the minus runs. It is asymptotic to the minimizer of
$u_\minus(j)+C(j)/N$, with $C(j)$ the exact second-order coefficient of
Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}. When $u_\minus$ dips below its limit the minimum stays at
bounded distance from the end. Section~\ref{sec:extrema} compares neighbouring values. A
difference form of the renewal theorem,
$u_\minus(j)-u_\minus(j-1)\sim-T_\minus(j)/\mu_\minus^2$ under (RV) and (SPAN), makes the end
profile eventually monotone whenever the initial run has $\nu_\minus(n)=(c+o(1))T_\minus(n)$
with $c\ne1/\mu_\minus$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:prep-diff}). Each local maximum of the profile at an
end is copied by $P_N$. So when $\nu_\sgn=o(T_\sgn)$ for both signs, $P_N$ has at least one
mode more than the two profiles together (Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}), and some prepared
starts of a log-convex law have at least 4 modes (Corollary~\ref{cor:four-modes}). Under (LD),
up to distance $N^{1-\eta}$ from each end the local extrema of $P_N$ are those of the
profile, followed by an odd number of extrema in a window of width $2\eta'a_\minus(N)$ around
the interior minimum, for every $\eta'>0$, and $P_N$ is strictly monotone in between
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}).

\emph{The exact number of modes.} Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count} assumes (LR), (SPAN) and the
second-difference bound (LD2) for both signs, and (EQ), which says that the two tails are
comparable. For a fresh or fair start with no ties in the end profiles, the first differences
of $P_N$ change sign in a fixed order for all large $N$, and $P_N$ has exactly
$M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes, where $M_\pm$ is the number of local maxima of the end profile at
the end of sign $\pm$. So $P_N$ has exactly 3 modes for all large $N$, a central peak and two
peaks at the ends, if and only if each end profile has a single local maximum
(Corollary~\ref{cor:three-modes}). This holds for all strictly log-convex run laws
(Corollary~\ref{cor:logconvex-three}). The criterion is not monotonicity of the renewal mass
function, and Lemma~\ref{lem:one-max} says when the two agree. The picture of 3 peaks fails
for every aperiodic law without runs of length $1$. Then the fair start has at least 5
modes, and for the law $f(n)\propto n^{-5/2}$ on $n\ge2$ it has at least $15$
(Example~\ref{ex:runs2}). The proof needs three steps beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}. A
second-order form of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-diff} gives a single antimode. A difference form
of the local big-jump theorem for sums of $m$ runs, uniform in $m$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-bigjump}), makes $P_N$ monotone on the big-jump zone, where $m$ is of
order $N$. A local limit theorem for the differences of $P_N$ in the bulk, together with the
bell shape of stable densities, gives a single bulk mode. Given equal indices, the comparison
(EQ) is needed only at the scale $N$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:sequences}). Unequal scales and a
bound for every $N$ remain open (Remark~\ref{rem:mode-open} and Section~\ref{sec:open}).

\emph{Exposure in switchback experiments.} A switchback experiment alternates two treatments
over time, and its exposure is the number of slots spent under one of them. If the system is
a slotted queue and the experiment switches at the regenerations, the exposure is $A_N$ with
the cycle laws of the two arms as run laws. Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle} shows that the cycle
law of a slotted queue with service of locally regularly varying tail satisfies (LR), (SPAN),
(LD) and (LD2), so that every theorem of the paper applies to it. The local tail of the
cycle is known~\cite{kortchemski2015,janson2012,denisovshneer2010}, and the difference bounds
are new. Corollaries~\ref{cor:exposure-bulk} to~\ref{cor:exposure-mode} give the exposure
imbalance at the scale $N^{1/\alpha}$, the probability of a severe imbalance, the probability
that the experiment never switches, the factor of order $N$ by which a start at an arbitrary
time inflates it, and the golden ratio rule for the most likely exposure. The designs in the
literature assume geometric mixing and so a Gaussian exposure at the scale $\sqrt N$. In the
language of statistics, the theorems of this paper are the exact sampling law of the
treated share $A_N/N$, including its dependence on when the experiment starts.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:intro-related}

\emph{On/off processes.} The occupation time of an alternating renewal process is a
classical object. Tak\'acs~\cite{takacs1957,takacs1959} and Kesten~\cite{kesten1962} proved
its limit laws in distribution, Lamperti~\cite{lamperti1958} the generalized arcsine law for
infinite means, with later refinements by Mitov and Yanev~\cite{mitovyanev2001}, and
Starreveld, Bekker and Mandjes~\cite{sbm2018} a central limit theorem and a large deviation
principle under exponential moments, with dependent sojourns. The heavy-tailed on/off source
is the standard model of long-range dependence in teletraffic. Heath, Resnick and
Samorodnitsky~\cite{hrs1998} derived the slowly decaying covariance of the stationary
indicator, Taqqu, Willinger and Sherman~\cite{tws1997} the self-similar limit of many
superposed sources, and Mikosch, Resnick, Rootz\'en and Stegeman~\cite{mrrs2002} the stable
and fractional Brownian limits according to the order of the limits. These works concern the
stationary indicator, its covariance, and aggregates of many sources. The object here is one
source at a fixed horizon, through its mass function at every point. Every statement in the
sources above is a statement in distribution, while every statement here concerns single
points.

\emph{Infinite densities and big jumps.} Rebenshtok, Denisov, H\"anggi and
Barkai~\cite{rdhb2014,rdhb2014b} derived the infinite density of a L\'evy walk from the
Montroll and Weiss equation, formally and in continuous time. For the two-state velocity
model their density~\cite[Eqs.~(8) and~(10)]{rdhb2014} has the shape
$|v|^{-1-\alpha}[\alpha-(\alpha-1)|v|]$. Schulz and Barkai~\cite[Eqs.~(25) and~(26)]{sb2015}
found the infinite density of the occupation time of a site for an alternating process with
two sojourn laws and matched it to the stable bulk, and Wang, Schulz, Deng and
Barkai~\cite[Eq.~(83)]{wsdb2018} found it for one law. A complete giant run alone would give
the linear term $-\alpha|v|$, and the run in progress adds $+|v|$, so these formulas already
contain the run in progress. Vezzani, Barkai and Burioni~\cite[Eqs.~(12) and~(13)]{vbb2019}
(see also~\cite{vbb2020}) derived the tail from a single big jump as the sum of exactly these
two terms. So the shape of the infinite density and its two-term mechanism are known
formally. What Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} and Corollary~\ref{cor:infinite-density} add is a
proof on the lattice at a fixed time, with the exact kernels $\gamma$ and $\kappa$ at finite
distance from the end, uniformly up to the endpoint, together with the boundary layer and the
role of the local hypothesis. In the physics model each flight chooses its direction
independently, and consecutive flights in the same direction merge into one run. This gives
the alternating walk with run laws thinned geometrically with parameter $\frac12$.

\emph{Aging and the initial condition.} The paper~\cite{wsdb2018} works in continuous time
through a double Laplace transform, with one law for both states and a start of fair random
sign. It finds a boundary layer of tail times renewal density for the backward recurrence
time when $1<\alpha<2$~\cite[Eq.~(52)]{wsdb2018} and an atom with a boundary layer for the
occupation time when $0<\alpha<1$~\cite[Section~VII.A]{wsdb2018}. Its authors write that
``the effect of initial preparation on the rare fluctuations is left for a future
work''~\cite[Section~VIII]{wsdb2018}. Wang, Vezzani, Burioni and Barkai~\cite{wvbb2019} then
compared the ordinary and the equilibrated start formally for a biased continuous-time random
walk. They found that the rare fluctuations remember the start, through an added term
$\xi^{-\alpha}$ in the infinite density and an atom at the origin~\cite[Eqs.~(28), (46)
and~(47)]{wvbb2019}. This is the structure of Corollary~\ref{cor:stationary}(a) and~(b),
where the stationary initial run adds the complete initial run to the kernel.
Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation} treats an arbitrary initial run at every point, with a sharp
criterion in each zone for when the start is invisible and the exact correction when it is
not. Akimoto, Sera, Yamato and Yano~\cite{asyy2020} proved the aged arcsine law in
distribution for $0<\alpha<1$, and Akimoto~\cite{akimoto2023} computed the moments of the
occupation time for the ordinary, the equilibrium and the aged start and showed that they
differ for $1<\alpha<2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:aging} is the local aging law for $1<\alpha<2$. Wang,
H\"oll and Barkai~\cite{whb2020} treat the infinite-mean case. The golden ratio appears in
Wang, Li and H\"anggi~\cite{wlh2016} as the exponent at which the side peaks and the central
peak decay at the same rate, with the edge exponent of Cipriani, Denisov and
Politi~\cite{cdp2005}, and Lee-Dadswell, Nickel and Gray~\cite{ldng2005} found the conduction
exponent $(3-\sqrt5)/2$ by a hydrodynamic argument. What Theorem~\ref{thm:mode} adds is the
comparison of single points as a theorem and the constant $\mathcal C(\varphi)$.

\emph{Local limit theorems and renewal theory.} Doney~\cite{doney1991} proved a bivariate
local limit theorem in the stable domain, and Berger~\cite{berger2019} proved multivariate
local large deviations and strong renewal theorems. His Theorem~3.3 applied to the walk of
run lengths, together with the fresh-plus identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:fresh-identity},
gives the window $|k-pN|\le KB_N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk}, and Section~\ref{sec:bulk}
explains what the rest of the theorem adds and why it needs new work. Melbourne, P\`ene and
Terhesiu~\cite{mpt2026} prove mixing local limit theorems with multivariate stable limits on
Young towers under the invariant measure. For the walk they give one point at the stable
scale for the stationary initial run, with the first and the last run restricted to fixed
lengths. In the i.i.d.\ renewal setting Theorems~\ref{thm:bulk} and~\ref{thm:preparation}
remove these restrictions. We use the local large deviation bound of Caravenna and
Doney~\cite{caravennadoney2019} (see also~\cite{melbourneterhesiu2022}) for sums of a fixed
number of variables. The local one-big-jump theorems for sums of independent variables are
due to Denisov, Dieker and Shneer~\cite{dds2008} and Berger, Birkner and Yuan~\cite{bby2023},
and the one under exactly the local condition (LR) is~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{berger2019ptrf}.
That theorem gives the probability that a fixed number of plus runs adds up to a given large
value. It does not give Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} directly. The number of runs before step
$N$ is random and tied to the run lengths, the big jump may be made by the run in progress
at time $N$, which no statement about complete sums sees, and the kernel must hold uniformly
up to the endpoint, where only boundedly many minus steps remain. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} does not use these theorems. The second-order coefficient rests on
the second-order renewal theorem of Frenk~\cite{frenk1982}, see also
Rogozin~\cite{rogozin1973} and Doney~\cite{doney2023}, and the monotonicity of $u_\minus$ on de
Bruijn and Erd\H{o}s~\cite{debruijnerdos1953}. Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-bigjump} is a difference
form of the local big-jump theorems of Doney~\cite{doney1989}, of Denisov, Dieker and Shneer
and of Berger, which give $f^{*m}(n)$ up to a factor $1+o(1)$ and so say nothing about its
differences. For a fixed number of summands the difference form is within reach of the
expansions of Barbe and McCormick~\cite{barbemccormick2009}, and what is new is the uniformity
in $m$. The bulk mode rests on Kwa\'snicki's theorem that stable densities are
bell-shaped~\cite{kwasnicki2020}. The infinite-mean case $0<\alpha<1$, where the local theory
rests on strong renewal theorems~\cite{doney1997,caravennadoney2019} and the bulk is the
Lamperti law, is the subject of the companion paper~\cite{paperG}, which uses the exact
identities of Section~\ref{sec:setting}.

\emph{The walk.} The walk is the directionally reinforced random walk of Mauldin, Monticino
and von Weizs\"acker~\cite{mmw1996}, with limit laws by Horv\'ath and
Shao~\cite{horvathshao1998}, and the persistent random walk of C\'enac, Le Ny, de Loynes and
Offret~\cite{clnlo2018,clnlo2019}. For geometric run lengths its crossings and a related walk
with a Landau profile are treated in~\cite{paperA,paperD}. Magdziarz, Scheffler, Straka and
\.Zebrowski~\cite{mssz2015} prove limit theorems for L\'evy walks in continuous space and
time, and Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk} is the local form of the marginal of these limits at one

\subsection{Structure of the paper}\label{sec:intro-structure}

Everything rests on the exact identities of Section~\ref{sec:setting}, which write $P_N(k)$
through products of renewal kernels of the two signs and reduce every initial run to the two
fresh laws. Section~\ref{sec:bulk} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk} and Section~\ref{sec:bigjump}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump}, with the infinite density, the examples and the window theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:preparation} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation}, its corollaries for the
stationary start, and Theorem~\ref{thm:aging}. Section~\ref{sec:shape} proves
Theorems~\ref{thm:mode} and~\ref{thm:antimode} and the results on the extrema near the ends,
and states Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count}. Section~\ref{sec:exposure} treats the queue.
Section~\ref{sec:open} lists the open problems and Section~\ref{sec:verify} describes the
computations. Appendix~\ref{app:classical} collects classical inputs, and
Appendix~\ref{app:modes} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count}.
