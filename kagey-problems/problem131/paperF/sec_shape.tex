\section{The shape of the law}\label{sec:shape}

This section reads three features of the shape off Theorems~\ref{thm:bulk}
to~\ref{thm:preparation}. The first is the global mode, where the golden ratio
enters, the second is the interior minimum between the bulk and the plus end, and the
third is the local extrema near the ends.

\subsection{The global mode}\label{sec:mode}

Let $P^{\max}_N=\max_{1\le k<N}P_N(k)$ be the largest interior value and
$H_N=\sup_xh_N(x)$ the height of the profile. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:first-run} the two endpoint atoms are
$P_N(N)=Q_\plus(N)$ and $P_N(0)=Q_\minus(N)$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the global mode]\label{thm:mode}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN), and let the initial run satisfy the bulk criterion
$B_n\Prob(D=n)\to0$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item $P^{\max}_N=(H_N+o(1))/B_N$, every interior maximizer lies in $pN+O(B_N)$, and
along any subsequence on which $h_N$ converges uniformly the scaled maximizers
$(k-pN)/B_N$ converge to the set of maximizers of the limit profile.
\item If $\liminf_NB_NQ_\sgn(N)/H_N>1$ for some sign $\sgn$, the endpoint atom of
sign $\sgn$ exceeds every interior value for all large $N$, so the global mode
of $P_N$ is an endpoint. If $\limsup_NB_NQ_\sgn(N)/H_N<1$ for both signs, the
global mode is interior for all large $N$.
\item If $Q_\sgn\in\RV_{-\rho_\sgn}$, the endpoint of sign $\sgn$ beats every
interior value for all large $N$ when $\rho_\sgn<1/\alpha_*$, and loses to the
largest interior value when $\rho_\sgn>1/\alpha_*$.
\item (The golden ratio.) For the stationary initial run,
$\rho_\sgn=\alpha_\sgn-1$. With equal indices $\alpha_\plus=\alpha_\minus=\alpha$,
the global mode is an endpoint for all large $N$ when $\alpha<\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$
and is interior for all large $N$ when $\alpha>\varphi$. For two equal laws
$T_\plus(n)=T_\minus(n)=n^{-\alpha}$ and the stationary initial run,
$B_NQ_\sgn(N)/H_N\sim\mathcal C(\alpha)N^{1-\alpha+1/\alpha}$ for each sign, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Calpha}
 \mathcal C(\alpha)=\frac{\pi\bigl(K_\alpha\lvert\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}2\rvert\bigr)^{1/\alpha}}
 {4(\alpha-1)\,\Gamma(1+\frac1\alpha)\,\zeta(\alpha)^{1+1/\alpha}} ,
\end{equation}
and at $\alpha=\varphi$ the ratio tends to $\mathcal C(\varphi)=0.7761317\ldots<1$, so the
global mode is interior. For a fresh start of sign $\sgn_0$,
$Q_{\sgn_0}=T_{\sgn_0}$, $Q_{\osgn_0}=0$ and $\rho_{\sgn_0}=\alpha_{\sgn_0}>1>1/\alpha_*$,
so the global mode is interior for all large $N$ and every pair of indices.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Put $h_*=\inf_NH_N$, which is positive by Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}(d). By Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation}(b) the bulk criterion
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-interior}
 \varepsilon_N=\sup_{1\le k<N}\bigl|B_NP_N(k)-h_N(x_{N,k})\bigr|\to0 .
\end{equation}
Hence $B_NP^{\max}_N=\max_{1\le k<N}h_N(x_{N,k})+O(\varepsilon_N)$, and
$\max_kh_N(x_{N,k})\le H_N$. For the lower bound, Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}(b) gives $K$
with $\sup_{|x|>K}h_N(x)<h_*/2$ for all $N$, so $H_N$ is the supremum of $h_N$ over
$[-K,K]$. For large $N$ the points $x_{N,k}$ with $1\le k<N$ cover $[-K,K]$ with mesh
$1/B_N$, since $pN/B_N\to\infty$ and $(1-p)N/B_N\to\infty$. The common Lipschitz
constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}(a) gives $\max_kh_N(x_{N,k})\ge H_N-C_2/B_N$. Hence
$B_NP^{\max}_N=H_N+o(1)$.

Let $k_N$ be an interior maximizer. Then $h_N(x_{N,k_N})\ge
B_NP^{\max}_N-\varepsilon_N=H_N-o(1)>h_*/2$ for large $N$, so $|x_{N,k_N}|\le K$, which
is the statement $k_N\in pN+O(B_N)$. Along a subsequence with $h_N\to h$
uniformly we have $H_N\to\sup h$ and $h(x_{N,k_N})\ge H_N-o(1)$. Every limit
point $x$ of the bounded sequence $x_{N,k_N}$ satisfies $h(x)=\sup h$ by
continuity. So the distance from $x_{N,k_N}$ to the set of maximizers of $h$
tends to zero.

(b) If $B_NQ_\sgn(N)\ge(1+\eta)H_N$ for all large $N$, then by (a)
$B_NQ_\sgn(N)-B_NP^{\max}_N\ge\eta H_N-o(1)\ge\eta h_*-o(1)>0$, so the endpoint atom
of sign $\sgn$ exceeds every interior value. If $B_NQ_\sgn(N)\le(1-\eta)H_N$ for
both signs and all large $N$, then (a) gives $B_NP^{\max}_N-B_NQ_\sgn(N)\ge\eta h_*-o(1)>0$
for both signs.

(c) By Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(v), $B_N\in\RV_{1/\alpha_*}$. Hence
$B_NQ_\sgn(N)\in\RV_{1/\alpha_*-\rho_\sgn}$. A regularly varying function with a
positive index tends to infinity and one with a negative index tends to
zero~\cite[Proposition~1.5.1]{bgt1989}. Since $H_N$ is bounded above and below by
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}(a) and~(d), part (b) applies.

(d) By Corollary~\ref{cor:stationary}(a) the stationary initial run satisfies the
bulk criterion and $Q_\sgn(N)\sim NT_\sgn(N)/(s(\alpha_\sgn-1))$, so
$\rho_\sgn=\alpha_\sgn-1$. With equal indices $\alpha_*=\alpha$, and $\alpha-1<1/\alpha$
if and only if $\alpha^2-\alpha-1<0$, that is, $\alpha<\varphi$. Part (c) then shows
that both endpoints beat the interior when $\alpha<\varphi$ and that both lose when
$\alpha>\varphi$. For unequal indices part (c) shows that the endpoint of sign $\sgn$
beats the interior if and only if $\alpha_\sgn-1<1/\alpha_*$, away from equality.

Now let $T_\plus(n)=T_\minus(n)=n^{-\alpha}$. Then $\mu_\sgn=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\alpha}=\zeta(\alpha)$,
$s=2\zeta(\alpha)$, $p=1/2$, $\theta_{\plus,N}=\theta_{\minus,N}=1$, and we may take
$a_\sgn(t)=t^{1/\alpha}$, so $B_N=(N/s)^{1/\alpha}$. The profile does not depend on $N$.
It is the density $h$ of $\frac12(Z_\plus-Z_\minus)$ with $Z_\plus$ and $Z_\minus$
independent copies of $Z_\alpha$. Its characteristic function is
\[
 \exp\bigl(K_\alpha(-it/2)^\alpha+K_\alpha(it/2)^\alpha\bigr)
 =\exp\bigl(-c_\alpha|t|^\alpha\bigr),\qquad c_\alpha=2^{1-\alpha}K_\alpha\bigl\lvert\cos\tfrac{\pi\alpha}2\bigr\rvert ,
\]
because $(-iy)^\alpha+(iy)^\alpha=2y^\alpha\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}2$ for $y>0$ and
$\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}2<0$. This function is positive and integrable, so by
Fourier inversion $h(x)\le h(0)$ for every $x$, and
\[
 H_N=h(0)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-c_\alpha t^\alpha}\,dt
 =\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac1\alpha)}{\pi\,c_\alpha^{1/\alpha}} .
\]
The stationary atom is $Q_\sgn(N)=s^{-1}\sum_{d\ge N}d^{-\alpha}\sim
N^{1-\alpha}/(2(\alpha-1)\zeta(\alpha))$. Multiplying,
\[
 \frac{B_NQ_\sgn(N)}{H_N}\sim\frac{N^{1/\alpha+1-\alpha}}{(2\zeta(\alpha))^{1/\alpha}\cdot2(\alpha-1)\zeta(\alpha)}
 \cdot\frac{\pi\,2^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\bigl(K_\alpha\lvert\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}2\rvert\bigr)^{1/\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\frac1\alpha)}
 =\mathcal C(\alpha)\,N^{1-\alpha+1/\alpha},
\]
since $2^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}2^{-1/\alpha}2^{-1}=2^{-2}$. At $\alpha=\varphi$ the
exponent vanishes, and $\alpha-1=1/\varphi$, $1+1/\alpha=\varphi$ and
$2-\alpha=\varphi^{-2}$, so $K_\varphi=\varphi\,\Gamma(\varphi^{-2})$ and
\[
 \mathcal C(\varphi)=\frac{\pi\varphi\bigl(\varphi\,\Gamma(\varphi^{-2})\lvert\cos\frac{\pi\varphi}2\rvert\bigr)^{1/\varphi}}
 {4\,\Gamma(\varphi)\,\zeta(\varphi)^{\varphi}}=0.7761317205\ldots .
\]
That $\mathcal C(\varphi)<1$ needs only four elementary bounds. Since $x^{-\varphi}$ is
convex, the trapezoid rule gives $\zeta(\varphi)\ge\frac12+1/(\varphi-1)=\varphi+\frac12$.
The minimum of $\Gamma$ on $(0,\infty)$ exceeds $0.8856$~\cite[Section~5.4(iii)]{dlmf},
so $\Gamma(\varphi)\ge0.8856$. Since $\Gamma\le1$ on $[1,2]$,
$\Gamma(\varphi^{-2})=\varphi^2\Gamma(1+\varphi^{-2})\le\varphi^2$. Finally
$\frac\pi2<\frac{\pi\varphi}2<\frac{5\pi}6$, so $\lvert\cos\frac{\pi\varphi}2\rvert\le\frac{\sqrt3}2$.
Together these give $\mathcal C(\varphi)\le\pi\varphi(\sqrt3\varphi^3/2)^{1/\varphi}/(4\cdot0.8856\,(\varphi+\frac12)^\varphi)=0.9513\ldots<1$,
and (b) shows that the global mode is interior.

For a fresh start of sign $\sgn_0$ we have $\Prob(D=n)=f_{\sgn_0}(n)\le T_{\sgn_0}(n)$,
and $B_nT_{\sgn_0}(n)\to0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(v), so the bulk criterion holds.
Moreover $Q_{\sgn_0}=T_{\sgn_0}\in\RV_{-\alpha_{\sgn_0}}$ with
$\alpha_{\sgn_0}>1>1/\alpha_*$, and the other atom vanishes. Part (c) concludes.
\end{proof}

For two equal laws $H_N=h(0)$, so $\mathcal C(\alpha)$ also compares the endpoint
with the center point. On the line $\rho_\sgn=1/\alpha_*$ the ratio
$B_NQ_\sgn(N)/H_N$ is slowly varying, the comparison depends on the slowly varying
factors, and we leave it open.

\subsection{The boundary layer at second order}\label{sec:second-order}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} the law follows the renewal mass function $u_\minus$
in the boundary layer, and $u_\minus(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$ by~\eqref{eq:renewal-thm}.
If $u_\minus$ stays above its limit, the layer lies above the level
$T_\plus(N)/\mu_\minus$ at which it meets the big-jump zone, while the big-jump kernel
increases as $k$ decreases from $N$. To locate the interior minimum between the two
we need the boundary layer at second order, with an exact coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[second order in the boundary layer]\label{thm:second-order}
Assume (RV) and (LR) for the plus sign, and let the start be fresh with sign
$\plus$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cj}
\begin{split}
 C(j)&=\alpha_\plus\Bigl[j\,u_\minus(j)+2\mu_\plus v_\minus(j)+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}u_\minus(i)\Bigr]\\
 &=\alpha_\plus\Bigl[j\,u_\minus(j)+\mu_\plus v_\minus(j)
 +\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}u_\minus(i)\bigl(1+\mu_\plus u_\minus(j-i)\bigr)\Bigr].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item For every fixed $j\ge0$,
\[
 \frac{P_N(N-j)}{T_\plus(N)}=u_\minus(j)+\frac{C(j)}{N}+o\Bigl(\frac1N\Bigr).
\]
\item For every $\eta\in(0,1)$,
\[
 \sup_{1\le j\le N^{1-\eta}}\frac Nj\,\Bigl|\frac{P_N(N-j)}{T_\plus(N)}-u_\minus(j)-\frac{C(j)}{N}\Bigr|\to0 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
If in addition (RV) and (SPAN) hold for the minus sign, then
$C(j)/j\to2\alpha_\plus s/\mu_\minus^2$ as $j\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

In the second form of $C(j)$ the terms $j\,u_\minus(j)+\mu_\plus v_\minus(j)$ come
from a giant run in progress at time $N$ that starts after $a$ steps, through
$T_\plus(N-a)/T_\plus(N)=1+\alpha_\plus a/N+o(a/N)$, and the sum comes from a complete
giant run followed by a fresh minus walk, through Lemma~\ref{lem:green}. We prove the
first form. The convergence of $C(j)/j$ is slow, of the order of $jT_\minus(j)$
by~\eqref{eq:second-renewal} below. For the zeta law of Section~\ref{sec:verify}
the limit is $3.0811$, while $C(j)/j$ is $4.968$, $3.762$ and $3.323$ at $j=5$, $40$
and $320$.

The proof uses the final-run identity~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} and two renewal
identities besides~\eqref{eq:cover}. Write $u=u_\minus$ and $v=v_\minus$. For $j\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{m=1}^{j}T_\minus(m)\,v(j-m)&=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}u(i),\label{eq:cover-v}\\
 \sum_{i=0}^{j-1}u(i)\,u(j-i)&=v(j).\label{eq:conv-v}
\end{align}
For~\eqref{eq:cover-v}, $v(j-m)T_\minus(m)=\sum_rr\Prob(S^\minus_r=j-m,\,X^\minus_{r+1}\ge m)$,
so the sum is the expectation of the index $r$ of the run that covers level
$j$, minus one. That index minus one equals the number of $r\ge1$ with
$S^\minus_r\le j-1$, whose expectation is $\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}u(i)$. For
\eqref{eq:conv-v}, $\sum_{i=0}^{j}u(i)u(j-i)=\sum_{a,b\ge0}u^\minus_{a+b}(j)=\sum_n(n+1)u^\minus_n(j)=v(j)+u(j)$,
and the term $i=j$ equals $u(j)$. By~\eqref{eq:conv-v} the two forms
of~\eqref{eq:Cj} agree.

We need two facts about sums of plus runs. Write $f=f_\plus$,
$T=T_\plus$, $\alpha=\alpha_\plus$, $\mu=\mu_\plus$ and $S_r=S^\plus_r$ in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:local-bigjump} and~\ref{lem:sums}. Under (LR) the sequence $f$
is regularly varying with index $-\alpha-1$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(i) gives, for
every $c>0$ and large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f-uct}
 \sup_{m\ge cn}\frac{f(m)}{f(n)}\le C_c,\qquad
 \sup_{0\le w\le w_n}\Bigl|\frac{f(n-w)}{f(n)}-1\Bigr|\to0\quad\text{whenever }w_n=o(n).
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(iv), $\E[X_\plus^2;X_\plus\le y]\le\sum_{n\le y}(2n-1)T(n)\le Cy^2T(y)$
and $\E[X_\plus;X_\plus>y]\to0$. Finally $nT(n)\to0$, since $nT(n)\in\RV_{1-\alpha}$.

\begin{lemma}[uniform local big jump]\label{lem:local-bigjump}
Assume (RV) and (LR) for the plus sign, and fix $\eta\in(0,1)$. Then
\[
 \sup_{1\le r\le n^{1-\eta}}\Bigl|\frac{f^{*r}(n)}{r\,f(n)}-1\Bigr|\to0\qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $r$ this is the local subexponentiality of $f$. Indeed, for $r\ge2$
split $f^{*r}(n)=\sum_mf^{*(r-1)}(m)f(n-m)$ at $m=n/2$. By~\eqref{eq:f-uct} and
dominated convergence the part $m\le n/2$ is $f(n)(1+o(1))$, and by induction on $r$
the part $m>n/2$, which is $\sum_{i<n/2}f(i)f^{*(r-1)}(n-i)$ up to one term of order
$f(n/2)^2=o(f(n))$, is $(r-1)f(n)(1+o(1))$. For growing $r$ we use the local
big-jump theorem of Denisov, Dieker and Shneer~\cite[Theorem~9.1]{dds2008} for the
centered step $\xi=X_\plus-\mu$ and intervals of length $T=1$. Its hypotheses, those
of~\cite[Proposition~9.1]{dds2008}, hold here. By Karamata's theorem
$\E[\xi^2;|\xi|\le x]\sim\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}x^2T(x)$, which gives their~(25), the left
tail of $\xi$ is bounded, which gives~(26), and~(27) follows from~(25),
$\E\xi=0$ and the index $-\alpha<-1$ of the tail~\cite[Lemma~9.1]{dds2008}. The function
$x\mapsto\Prob(x<\xi\le x+1)=f(\lfloor x+\mu\rfloor+1)$ is regularly varying with
index $-\alpha-1<-1$, so it is intermediate regularly varying. The theorem then
gives, for every $\varepsilon>0$, an $r_0$ such that
$|\Prob(S_r-r\mu\in(x,x+1])/(r\Prob(x<\xi\le x+1))-1|\le\varepsilon$ for all $r\ge r_0$ and
all $x\ge x_r=\frac12r^{1/(1-\eta)}$, because $rT(x_r)\to0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(ii). Take $x=n-r\mu-\frac12$. For $r\le n^{1-\eta}$ and large $n$ we
have $x\ge n/2\ge x_r$, the event is $\{S_r=n\}$, and
$\Prob(x<\xi\le x+1)=f(n')$ with $|n'-n|\le r\mu+1=o(n)$, so $f(n')=f(n)(1+o(1))$
by~\eqref{eq:f-uct}. Together with the finitely many $r<r_0$ this proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[sums of plus runs]\label{lem:sums}
Assume (RV) for the plus sign. There is a constant $C$ such that, for all $r\ge1$
and real $x>0$,
\[
 \E[S_r;S_r>x]\le r\Bigl(\E[X_\plus;X_\plus>x/2]+\frac{2r\mu^2}{x}\Bigr),
\]
and, if in addition $x\ge4r\mu$,
\[
 \Prob(S_r\ge x)\le C\,rT(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $S_r\ge x$, either some run is at least $x/2$, or the sum of the runs
truncated at $x/2$ is at least $x$. The truncated sum has mean at most
$r\mu\le x/4$ and variance at most $r\,\E[X_\plus^2;X_\plus<x/2]\le Crx^2T(x)$. By
Chebyshev's inequality the second event has probability at most
$(4/(3x))^2Crx^2T(x)$, and the first at most $rT(x/2)\le CrT(x)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(ii), since $x\ge4r\mu\ge4$. For the other bound, which uses no
condition on $x$, on $\{S_r>x\}$ either $X^\plus_i>x/2$ or $S_r-X^\plus_i>x/2$.
Hence $\E[X^\plus_i;S_r>x]\le\E[X_\plus;X_\plus>x/2]+\mu\Prob(S_r-X^\plus_i>x/2)$, and the
last probability is at most $2r\mu/x$ by Markov's inequality. Sum over $i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}]
We write $u=u_\minus$, $v=v_\minus$ and keep the notation of the two lemmas. By (LR),
$Nf(N)/T(N)\to\alpha$. We use $v(j)\le j\,u(j)\le j$, which holds because
$u^\minus_r(j)=0$ for $r>j$.

(b) Fix $\eta\in(0,1)$, put $J_N=N^{1-\eta}$ and $x_N=N^{1-\eta/2}$, and let $N$ be so
large that $2J_N\le x_N$, $4J_N\mu\le N/2$ and $J_N\le(N/2)^{1-\eta/2}$. Every $o(\cdot)$
below is uniform in $1\le r\le j\le J_N$. In~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} the first sum runs
over $1\le r\le j$, since $u^\minus_0(j)=0$ for $j\ge1$, and the second over $1\le m\le j$
and $0\le r\le j-m$.

The final plus run. Split the expectation in the first sum
of~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} at $S_r=N/2$. For integers $0\le x\le3N/4$ put
\[
 \mathcal T_N(x)=\frac{N}{T(N)}\bigl(T(N-x)-T(N)\bigr)=\frac{N}{T(N)}\sum_{n=N-x}^{N-1}f(n).
\]
By~\eqref{eq:f-uct} and $Nf(N)/T(N)\to\alpha$, $\mathcal T_N(x)=\alpha x(1+o(1))$ uniformly in
$x\le x_N$, and $\mathcal T_N(x)\le Cx$ for $x\le3N/4$, since $f(n)\le C_{1/4}f(N)$ for
$n\ge N/4$. Since $j\le N/4$,
\[
 \E\bigl[T(N-j-S_r);S_r\le N/2\bigr]=T(N)\Bigl(\Prob(S_r\le N/2)+\frac1N\E\bigl[\mathcal T_N(j+S_r);S_r\le N/2\bigr]\Bigr).
\]
On $\{S_r\le x_N/2\}$ we have $j+S_r\le x_N$. With $\E[j+S_r]=j+r\mu$ this gives
\[
 \bigl|\E[\mathcal T_N(j+S_r);S_r\le N/2]-\alpha(j+r\mu)\bigr|
 \le o(j+r)+C\,\E[j+S_r;S_r>x_N/2].
\]
By Markov's inequality and Lemma~\ref{lem:sums},
$\E[j+S_r;S_r>x_N/2]\le2jr\mu/x_N+r\bigl(\E[X_\plus;X_\plus>x_N/4]+4r\mu^2/x_N\bigr)=o(j)$,
since $r\le j\le J_N=o(x_N)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sums} and Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(ii),
$\Prob(S_r>N/2)\le CrT(N)$. On the other part,
\[
 \E\bigl[T(N-j-S_r);N/2<S_r\le N-j-1\bigr]=\sum_{1\le l<N/2-j}f^{*r}(N-j-l)\,T(l).
\]
Here $N-j-l\ge N/2$ and $r\le J_N\le(N-j-l)^{1-\eta/2}$. So Lemma~\ref{lem:local-bigjump}
with $\eta/2$ in place of $\eta$ and~\eqref{eq:f-uct} give $f^{*r}(N-j-l)=rf(N)(1+o(1))$
for $l\le x_N$, since then $j+l=o(N)$, and $f^{*r}(N-j-l)\le CrT(N)/N$ for all $l$ in the
range. Since $\sum_{l\le x_N}T(l)\to\mu$ and $\sum_{l>x_N}T(l)\to0$, the sum equals
$r\alpha\mu T(N)N^{-1}(1+o(1))$. Together,
\[
 \E\bigl[T(N-j-S_r);S_r\le N-j-1\bigr]=T(N)\Bigl(1-O\bigl(rT(N)\bigr)+\frac{\alpha(j+2r\mu)}{N}+o\Bigl(\frac jN\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Multiply by $u^\minus_r(j)$ and sum over $r$. Since $\sum_ru^\minus_r(j)=u(j)\le1$ and
$\sum_rru^\minus_r(j)=v(j)$, the first sum of~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} divided by $T(N)$
equals
\[
 u(j)-O\bigl(v(j)T(N)\bigr)+\frac{\alpha}{N}\bigl(j\,u(j)+2\mu v(j)\bigr)+o\Bigl(\frac{j}{N}\Bigr).
\]
Since $v(j)T(N)\le jT(N)=(j/N)\,NT(N)$ and $NT(N)\to0$, the second term is $o(j/N)$.

The final minus run. Since $r+1\le j\le J_N\le(N-J_N)^{1-\eta/2}$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-bigjump} with $\eta/2$ in place of $\eta$ and~\eqref{eq:f-uct} give
$u^\plus_{r+1}(N-j)=f^{*(r+1)}(N-j)=(r+1)\alpha T(N)N^{-1}(1+o(1))$. So the second sum
of~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} equals
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{\alpha T(N)}{N}(1+o(1))\sum_{m=1}^{j}T_\minus(m)\sum_r(r+1)u^\minus_r(j-m)\\
 &\qquad=\frac{\alpha T(N)}{N}(1+o(1))\sum_{m=1}^{j}T_\minus(m)\bigl(u(j-m)+v(j-m)\bigr),
\end{align*}
with $v(0)=0$. By~\eqref{eq:cover} and~\eqref{eq:cover-v} the last sum equals
$1+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}u(i)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}u(i)$, which is at most $j$. Adding the two
contributions gives $u(j)+C(j)/N+o(j/N)$ with the first form of~\eqref{eq:Cj}, uniformly
in $1\le j\le J_N$. This is (b).

(a) For $j=0$, \eqref{eq:final-explicit} gives $P_N(N)=T(N)$ exactly, and $C(0)=0$. For
fixed $j\ge1$, part (b) gives an error $o(j/N)=o(1/N)$.

The limit. By~\eqref{eq:renewal-thm} for the minus sign, $u(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$.
By~\eqref{eq:conv-v} and Ces\`aro's theorem, $v(j)/j=j^{-1}\sum_{i<j}u(i)u(j-i)\to\mu_\minus^{-2}$,
and $j^{-1}\sum_{i<j}u(i)\to1/\mu_\minus$. Hence
$C(j)/j\to\alpha_\plus(2/\mu_\minus+2\mu_\plus/\mu_\minus^2)=2\alpha_\plus s/\mu_\minus^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The antimode}\label{sec:antimode}

Two classical facts about the renewal mass function enter. The first is the
second-order renewal theorem. Under (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-renewal}
 u_\minus(j)-\frac1{\mu_\minus}\sim\frac{j\,T_\minus(j)}{\mu_\minus^2(\alpha_\minus-1)}\qquad(j\to\infty).
\end{equation}
This is Lemma~4 of Frenk~\cite{frenk1982}, stated there for an aperiodic
recurrent event whose tail $\Prob(X>n)\sim T(n)$ is regularly varying with index
$-\alpha$, $\alpha>1$ (see also~\cite{rogozin1973} and~\cite[Theorem~1]{doney2023}).
The second fact gives a simple
condition for the hypothesis of the next theorem.

\begin{lemma}[log-convex run laws]\label{lem:logconvex}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign. If $f_\minus(n)>0$ for all $n\ge1$
and $f_\minus(n-1)f_\minus(n+1)>f_\minus(n)^2$ for all $n\ge2$, then $u_\minus$ is
strictly decreasing and $u_\minus(j)>1/\mu_\minus$ for every $j\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~1 of de Bruijn and Erd\H{o}s~\cite{debruijnerdos1953}, the
renewal sequence of a strictly log-convex law is strictly log-convex, that
is, $u_\minus(m-1)u_\minus(m+1)>u_\minus(m)^2$ for $m\ge1$. Hence the ratios
$u_\minus(m+1)/u_\minus(m)$ increase strictly in $m$. If one of them were at
least $1$, all later ones would exceed some $q>1$, and $u_\minus$ would grow
geometrically, which is impossible since $u_\minus\le1$. So every ratio is
smaller than $1$, $u_\minus$ is strictly decreasing, and it decreases to its
limit $1/\mu_\minus$ given by~\eqref{eq:renewal-thm}.
\end{proof}

\begin{introtheorem}[the antimode]\label{thm:antimode}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for both signs and (LR) for the plus sign, and let the
start be fresh with sign $\plus$. Let $j^*_N$ be a minimizer of
$j\mapsto P_N(N-j)$ over $0\le j\le(1-p)N$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item If $u_\minus(j)>1/\mu_\minus$ for every $j\ge1$, then $j^*_N\to\infty$,
$j^*_N=o(N)$, and
\[
 T_\minus(j^*_N)\sim\frac{2\alpha_\plus s}{N} .
\]
Moreover $j^*_N/i_N\to1$ for every choice of minimizers $i_N$ of
$j\mapsto u_\minus(j)+C(j)/N$ over $j\ge0$. So $j^*_N$ is of the order of
$b_{\minus,N}$, the stable scale of the minus runs, and
$j^*_N\asymp N^{1/\alpha_\minus}$ up to a slowly varying factor.
\item If $\inf_{j\ge1}u_\minus(j)<1/\mu_\minus$, then $j^*_N$ is bounded and
every value that $j^*_N$ takes infinitely often is a minimizer of $u_\minus$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

The borderline case, in which $u_\minus\ge1/\mu_\minus$ with equality at some
$j\ge1$, is not covered. By Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex} part (a) applies to every
strictly log-convex law of the minus runs, for instance the zeta law
$f(n)=n^{-\alpha-1}/\zeta(\alpha+1)$.

\begin{proof}
Write $R_N(j)=P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)$, $u=u_\minus$ and
$c_{\mathrm a}=2\alpha_\plus s/\mu_\minus^2$. In this proof $\mu_\minus$ and $\mu_\plus$ are
always written with their signs. We first prove two lower bounds on $R_N$ and then
treat (a) and (b).

Lower bound in the layer. Let $1\le j\le N/(8\mu_\plus)$. In the first sum
of~\eqref{eq:final-explicit}, $T_\plus(N-j-S^\plus_r)\ge T_\plus(N-j)$, and
$\Prob(S^\plus_r\le N-j-1)\ge1-\Prob(S^\plus_r\ge N/2)\ge1-CrT_\plus(N)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sums}, since $4r\mu_\plus\le N/2$. Dropping the second sum,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anti-lower}
 R_N(j)\ge\frac{T_\plus(N-j)}{T_\plus(N)}\bigl(u(j)-C\,v(j)\,T_\plus(N)\bigr)
 \ge\frac{T_\plus(N-j)}{T_\plus(N)}\bigl(u(j)-C\,j\,T_\plus(N)\bigr).
\end{equation}
By (LR), $f_\plus(n)\ge\frac34\alpha_\plus T_\plus(n)/n\ge\frac34\alpha_\plus T_\plus(N)/N$
for $N/2\le n\le N$ and large $N$, because $T_\plus(n)/n$ is nonincreasing. Hence
$T_\plus(N-j)/T_\plus(N)\ge1+\frac34\alpha_\plus j/N$ for $j\le N/2$. We use
\eqref{eq:anti-lower} below only for $j\le\delta N$ with $\delta\le1/(8\mu_\plus)$ and
on ranges where $u(j)$ is bounded below by a positive constant. There
$CjT_\plus(N)\le C\delta NT_\plus(N)\to0$, so the second factor is positive for large
$N$. Since the first factor is at least $1$, we may then drop it or expand the
product.

Lower bound far from the layer. Fix $\delta\in(0,1-p)$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anti-far}
 \liminf_N\ \min_{\delta N\le j\le(1-p)N}R_N(j)\ge\frac{(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_\plus}}{\mu_\minus}.
\end{equation}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:bigjump-lower} there is $K$ such that
$P_N(k)\ge(1-\varepsilon)G^{(\plus)}_N(k)$ for $pN+KB_N\le k\le N$ and large $N$.
For $k=N-j$ with $j\ge\delta N$ we have $L_k=N-j/(1-p)\le(1-\delta)N$, so
$G^{(\plus)}_N(k)\ge T_\plus(\lfloor L_k\rfloor)u(j)\ge T_\plus(\lfloor(1-\delta)N\rfloor)u(j)$,
and $T_\plus(\lfloor(1-\delta)N\rfloor)/T_\plus(N)\to(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_\plus}$ by (RV).
For $pN\le k<pN+KB_N$, Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk}(c) and Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}(d)
give $B_NP_N(k)\ge\frac12\inf_N\inf_{[-K,K]}h_N>0$ for large $N$, so
$R_N(N-k)\ge c_K/(B_NT_\plus(N))\to\infty$, since $B_NT_\plus(N)\to0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(v). As $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary and $u(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$, the claim
follows.

(b) Let $m_*=\min_ju(j)<1/\mu_\minus$, which is attained on a finite set because
$u(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$, and let $\varepsilon_*=(1/\mu_\minus-m_*)/3$. Choose $J$ with
$u(j)\ge1/\mu_\minus-\varepsilon_*>0$ for $j\ge J$, and let $\delta=\min\{1/(8\mu_\plus),(1-p)/2\}$. For
$J\le j\le\delta N$, \eqref{eq:anti-lower} gives
$R_N(j)\ge u(j)-CjT_\plus(N)\ge1/\mu_\minus-\varepsilon_*-C\delta NT_\plus(N)$, which exceeds
$m_*+\varepsilon_*$ for large $N$, since $NT_\plus(N)\to0$. For $j\ge\delta N$,
\eqref{eq:anti-far} gives $R_N(j)\ge m_*+\varepsilon_*$ for large $N$. For $j<J$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}(a) gives $R_N(j)\to u(j)$. So for large $N$ every
minimizer is smaller than $J$, and a value $j'$ taken infinitely often with
$u(j')>m_*$ would give $R_N(j')>R_N(j_0)$ for large $N$, where $u(j_0)=m_*$.

(a) Let $\Lambda_N=a_\minus(N)$, so that $NT_\minus(\Lambda_N)\to1$ and
$\Lambda_N\in\RV_{1/\alpha_\minus}$. Put $w(j)=u(j)-1/\mu_\minus$ and
$c_{\mathrm r}=1/(\mu_\minus^2(\alpha_\minus-1))$, so $w(j)\sim c_{\mathrm r}jT_\minus(j)$
by~\eqref{eq:second-renewal}, and $w(j)>0$ for $j\ge1$. Let
\[
 \Upsilon(x)=c_{\mathrm r}x^{1-\alpha_\minus}+c_{\mathrm a}x,\qquad x>0 .
\]
The function $\Upsilon$ is strictly convex with a unique minimizer $x_*$, given by
$c_{\mathrm r}(\alpha_\minus-1)x_*^{-\alpha_\minus}=c_{\mathrm a}$, that is,
$x_*^{-\alpha_\minus}=c_{\mathrm a}\mu_\minus^2=2\alpha_\plus s$.

We fix the constants first. They depend only on $c_{\mathrm r}$, $c_{\mathrm a}$, $\alpha_\pm$ and
$\mu_\pm$. Choose $\varepsilon\in(0,x_*)$ so small that
$\frac{c_{\mathrm r}}5\varepsilon^{-(\alpha_\minus-1)/2}>\Upsilon(x_*)+2$, and choose $K>x_*$ so large
that $\alpha_\plus K/(2\mu_\minus)>\Upsilon(x_*)+1$. So $\varepsilon<x_*<K$.

Step 1 (the window). By~\eqref{eq:second-renewal} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(i),
$(N/\Lambda_N)\,w(j)=c_{\mathrm r}(j/\Lambda_N)^{1-\alpha_\minus}(1+o(1))$ uniformly in
$\varepsilon\Lambda_N\le j\le K\Lambda_N$, because
$NT_\minus(j)=NT_\minus(\Lambda_N)\cdot T_\minus(j)/T_\minus(\Lambda_N)$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}, $C(j)=c_{\mathrm a}j(1+o(1))$ as $j\to\infty$. Choose
$\eta>0$ with $1-\eta>1/\alpha_\minus$, so that $K\Lambda_N\le N^{1-\eta}$ for large
$N$. Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}(b) then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anti-window}
 \frac{N}{\Lambda_N}\Bigl(R_N(j)-\frac1{\mu_\minus}\Bigr)=\Upsilon\Bigl(\frac{j}{\Lambda_N}\Bigr)+o(1)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{N}{\Lambda_N}\Bigl(u(j)+\frac{C(j)}N-\frac1{\mu_\minus}\Bigr)=\Upsilon\Bigl(\frac{j}{\Lambda_N}\Bigr)+o(1),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\varepsilon\Lambda_N\le j\le K\Lambda_N$. Since $\varepsilon<x_*<K$, in
particular the minimum of $R_N$ is at most $1/\mu_\minus+(\Lambda_N/N)(\Upsilon(x_*)+o(1))$.

Step 2 (small $j$). We have $R_N(0)=1$, and $u(0)+C(0)/N=1$ for the comparison
function. Since $\mu_\minus>1$ and $\Lambda_N/N\to0$, both exceed
$1/\mu_\minus+(\Lambda_N/N)(\Upsilon(x_*)+1)$ for large $N$. Let
$1\le j\le\varepsilon\Lambda_N$. Then $u(j)>1/\mu_\minus$, and~\eqref{eq:anti-lower} gives
$R_N(j)\ge u(j)-C\varepsilon\Lambda_NT_\plus(N)=1/\mu_\minus+w(j)-o(\Lambda_N/N)$. Choose
$j_0$ with $w(j)\ge\frac{c_{\mathrm r}}2jT_\minus(j)$ for $j\ge j_0$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(ii) applied to $jT_\minus(j)\in\RV_{1-\alpha_\minus}$,
for $j_0\le j\le\varepsilon\Lambda_N$ and $j_0$ large,
\[
 \frac{jT_\minus(j)}{\Lambda_NT_\minus(\Lambda_N)}\ge\frac12\Bigl(\frac{j}{\Lambda_N}\Bigr)^{-(\alpha_\minus-1)/2}\ge\frac12\varepsilon^{-(\alpha_\minus-1)/2}.
\]
Since $\Lambda_NT_\minus(\Lambda_N)\sim\Lambda_N/N$, we get
$w(j)\ge\frac{c_{\mathrm r}}5\varepsilon^{-(\alpha_\minus-1)/2}\Lambda_N/N$ for large $N$.
For $1\le j<j_0$, $w(j)\ge\min_{i<j_0}w(i)>0$, which is much larger than
$\Lambda_N/N$. By the choice of $\varepsilon$,
$R_N(j)>1/\mu_\minus+(\Lambda_N/N)(\Upsilon(x_*)+1)$ for $1\le j\le\varepsilon\Lambda_N$ and
large $N$. The same bound holds for $u(j)+C(j)/N\ge1/\mu_\minus+w(j)$.

Step 3 (intermediate $j$). Let $\delta=\min\{1/(8\mu_\plus),(1-p)/2\}$ and
$K\Lambda_N\le j\le\delta N$. By~\eqref{eq:anti-lower} and $u(j)>1/\mu_\minus$,
\[
 R_N(j)\ge\Bigl(1+\frac{3\alpha_\plus j}{4N}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac1{\mu_\minus}-CjT_\plus(N)\Bigr)
 \ge\frac1{\mu_\minus}+\frac{\alpha_\plus j}{2\mu_\minus N}\ge\frac1{\mu_\minus}+\frac{\alpha_\plus K}{2\mu_\minus}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_N}{N}
\]
for large $N$, since $NT_\plus(N)\to0$. By the choice of $K$ this exceeds
$1/\mu_\minus+(\Lambda_N/N)(\Upsilon(x_*)+1)$. For the comparison function,
$C(j)\ge\alpha_\plus j\,u(j)\ge\alpha_\plus j/\mu_\minus$, so
$u(j)+C(j)/N\ge1/\mu_\minus+\alpha_\plus j/(\mu_\minus N)$ for every $j$, and the same bound
holds for all $j\ge K\Lambda_N$.

Step 4 (large $j$). By~\eqref{eq:anti-far}, $R_N(j)\ge(1+\varepsilon_\delta)/\mu_\minus$ for
$\delta N\le j\le(1-p)N$ and large $N$, with $\varepsilon_\delta=((1-\delta)^{-\alpha_\plus}-1)/2>0$, while
$\Lambda_N/N\to0$.

Conclusion. By Steps 2 to 4 every minimizer of $R_N$, and every minimizer of
$u(j)+C(j)/N$, lies in $[\varepsilon\Lambda_N,K\Lambda_N]$ for large $N$. Fix
$\eta'>0$. Since $\Upsilon$ is continuous with a unique minimizer, $\Upsilon\ge\Upsilon(x_*)+\varepsilon'$
on $[\varepsilon,K]\setminus(x_*-\eta',x_*+\eta')$ for some $\varepsilon'>0$, and
\eqref{eq:anti-window} shows that both minimizers lie in
$((x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N,(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N)$ for large $N$. Hence
$j^*_N/\Lambda_N\to x_*$ and $i_N/\Lambda_N\to x_*$, so $j^*_N/i_N\to1$. By (RV),
$NT_\minus(j^*_N)=NT_\minus(\Lambda_N)\cdot T_\minus(j^*_N)/T_\minus(\Lambda_N)\to x_*^{-\alpha_\minus}=2\alpha_\plus s$.
Finally $\Lambda_N=a_\minus(N)$ and $b_{\minus,N}=a_\minus(N/s)$ differ by a
bounded factor, and $a_\minus\in\RV_{1/\alpha_\minus}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local extrema near the ends}\label{sec:extrema}

Theorems~\ref{thm:mode} and~\ref{thm:antimode} compare values of $P_N$ in different zones.
Here we compare neighbouring values. A \emph{local maximum} of a finite or infinite
sequence $(y_i)$ is a maximal block $\{a,\dots,b\}$ of consecutive indices on which $y$ is
constant, such that $y_{a-1}<y_a$ unless $a$ is the first index and $y_{b+1}<y_b$ unless
$b$ is the last. Local minima are defined in the same way, and a \emph{mode} of $P_N$ is a
local maximum. So an endpoint is a mode when it beats its neighbour, and a plateau counts
once.

Let the initial run satisfy $\nu_\plus(n)=o(T_\plus(n))$, and let
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)=\lim_NQ_\plus(N)/T_\plus(N)$ exist. The \emph{end profile} is
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus=(\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0),\kappa_\plus(1),\kappa_\plus(2),\dots)$,
with $\kappa_\plus$ as in~\eqref{eq:kappa-prep}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:first-run} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:preparation}(d), under (RV) for the plus sign,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-limit}
 P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)\to\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)\qquad\text{for every fixed }j\ge0 .
\end{equation}
For a fresh plus start, a fresh minus start and the fair start, which is fresh with each
sign with probability $\frac12$, the profile is $(1,u_\minus(1),u_\minus(2),\dots)$,
$(0,u_\minus(1),\dots)$ and $(\frac12,u_\minus(1),\dots)$. The profile $\widetilde\kappa_\minus$
at the minus end is defined with the signs exchanged. We write
$D_N(j)=P_N(N-j-1)-P_N(N-j)$ and $\Delta y(j)=y(j+1)-y(j)$.

\begin{lemma}[difference renewal theorem]\label{lem:diff-renewal}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign, and put $d_n=u_\minus(n)-u_\minus(n-1)$ for
$n\ge0$, with $u_\minus(-1)=0$. Then $\sum_n|d_n|<\infty$ and
$d_n=-(1+o(1))T_\minus(n)/\mu_\minus^2$. In particular $u_\minus$ is eventually strictly
decreasing.
\end{lemma}

Summing over $n>j$ and using Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(iv) gives back~\eqref{eq:second-renewal}, so
the lemma is a local form of Frenk's theorem. It also shows that if $u_\minus$ is
nonincreasing on $j\ge1$, then $u_\minus(j)>1/\mu_\minus$ for every $j\ge1$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode}(a) applies, and the borderline case after
Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode} has a nonmonotone $u_\minus$.

\begin{proof}
Write $u=u_\minus$, $\mu=\mu_\minus$, $t_n=T_\minus(n+1)$ for $n\ge0$, and
$\hat a(z)=\sum_na_nz^n$. Since $t_0=1$ and $t_{n-1}-t_n=f_\minus(n)$, we have
$(1-z)\hat t=1-\hat f_\minus$, and $\hat u=1/(1-\hat f_\minus)$, so $\hat d=(1-z)\hat u=1/\hat t$.
On $|z|\le1$ the function $\hat t$ has no zero. At $z=1$ it equals $\mu$, and elsewhere a zero
would give $\hat f_\minus(z)=1$, which (SPAN) excludes. By Wiener's lemma~\cite{wiener1932}
the function $1/\hat t$ on the unit circle has absolutely summable Fourier coefficients. They
vanish at negative indices since $1/\hat t$ is continuous on the closed disk and analytic
inside. So they are the $d_n$, and $d\in\ell^1$.

For sequences on $\Z_{\ge0}$ put $\|a\|_1=\sum_n|a_n|$ and $\|a\|_t=\sup_n|a_n|/t_n$. The
sequence $t$ is positive, nonincreasing and regularly varying, so
$c_t=\sup_nt_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}/t_n<\infty$. Splitting a convolution at $n/2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 \|a*b\|_t\le c_t\bigl(\|a\|_1\|b\|_t+\|a\|_t\|b\|_1\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the following product rule. If $a,b\in\ell^1$, $a_n/t_n\to\chi_a$ and $b_n/t_n\to\chi_b$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-rule}
 (a*b)_n/t_n\to\hat a(1)\chi_b+\hat b(1)\chi_a .
\end{equation}
Indeed, in $\sum_{k\le n/2}a_kb_{n-k}/t_n$ each term tends to $a_k\chi_b$, since
$t_{n-k}/t_n\to1$, and the terms are dominated by $c_t\|b\|_t|a_k|$. The other half is the
same.

Split $t=t'+t''$ with $t'_n=t_n\ones_{\{n\le M\}}$, and take $M$ so large that
$2c_t\|d\|_1\|t''\|_1\le\frac14$. Then $1-\hat t''\hat d$ has the inverse
$\sum_{k\ge0}(\hat t''\hat d)^k$ with summable coefficients, and
$\hat t'=\hat t(1-\hat t''\hat d)$ has no zero on $|z|\le1$. So the coefficients $e$ of
$1/\hat t'=\hat d\sum_k(\hat t''\hat d)^k$ satisfy $\|e\|_1\le2\|d\|_1$. As $\hat t'$ is a
polynomial without zeros in the closed disk, $e_n$ decays geometrically, and $e_n=o(t_n)$.
Put $g=e*t''$. Since $t''_n=t_n$ for $n>M$, \eqref{eq:product-rule} gives
$g_n/t_n\to\hat e(1)$, and $c_t\|g\|_1\le\frac14$. By~\eqref{eq:weighted},
$x_k=\|g^{*k}\|_t$ satisfies $x_k\le\frac14x_{k-1}+c_t\|g\|_t4^{1-k}$, so $x_k\le Ck4^{-k}$.
By induction with~\eqref{eq:product-rule}, $(g^{*k})_n/t_n\to k\hat g(1)^{k-1}\hat e(1)$.
By dominated convergence over $k$, $h=\sum_{k\ge1}(-g)^{*k}$ satisfies
$h_n/t_n\to-\hat e(1)/(1+\hat g(1))^2$. Finally $\hat t=\hat t'(1+\hat g)$ gives
$\hat d=\hat e(1+\hat h)$, so $d=e+e*h$ and $d_n/t_n\to-\hat e(1)^2/(1+\hat g(1))^2$. Here
$\hat e(1)=1/\hat t'(1)$ and $1+\hat g(1)=(\hat t'(1)+\hat t''(1))/\hat t'(1)=\mu/\hat t'(1)$,
so the limit is $-1/\mu^2$. Since $t_n\sim T_\minus(n)$, the lemma follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the prepared profile]\label{lem:prep-diff}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign, and let $\nu_\minus(d)=(c+o(1))T_\minus(d)$ for
some $c\ge0$. Then
$\kappa_\plus(j)-\kappa_\plus(j-1)=\bigl((c\mu_\minus-1)/\mu_\minus^2+o(1)\bigr)T_\minus(j)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The generating function of $\kappa_\plus$ is $\pi_\plus(\hat u_\minus-1)+\hat\nu_\minus\hat u_\minus$,
so $\kappa_\plus(n)-\kappa_\plus(n-1)=\pi_\plus d_n+(\nu_\minus*d)_n$ for $n\ge2$. Apply
\eqref{eq:product-rule} with $a=\nu_\minus$, $\hat a(1)=\pi_\minus$, $\chi_a=c$, and $b=d$,
$\hat b(1)=\lim_nu_\minus(n)=1/\mu_\minus$, $\chi_b=-1/\mu_\minus^2$, and add $\pi_\plus d_n$.
\end{proof}

So $\kappa_\plus$ is eventually strictly decreasing when $c<1/\mu_\minus$, for instance when
$\nu_\minus=o(T_\minus)$ or for the stationary initial run ($c=1/s$), and eventually strictly
increasing when $c>1/\mu_\minus$. If $\nu_\minus=T_\minus/\mu_\minus$ exactly, then
$\kappa_\plus\equiv1/\mu_\minus$ on $j\ge1$ by~\eqref{eq:cover}.

\begin{proposition}[copies of the profile]\label{prop:copy}
Assume (RV) for the plus sign, let $\nu_\plus(n)=o(T_\plus(n))$, and let
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)$ exist.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item If $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j+1)\ne\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$ for $0\le j<J$, then for
large $N$ the sign of $D_N(j)$ is that of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j+1)-\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$
for every $0\le j<J$.
\item For every local maximum $\{a,\dots,b\}$ of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ and all large $N$,
$P_N$ has a local maximum inside $\{N-b,\dots,N-a\}$. The same holds for local minima.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) follows from~\eqref{eq:profile-limit}, since $D_N(j)=T_\plus(N)(\Delta R_N)(j)$ with
$R_N(j)=P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)$. For (b), let $\widetilde\kappa_\plus=y$ on $\{a,\dots,b\}$,
and let $3\varepsilon$ be the gap between $y$ and the larger of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(a-1)$
(absent if $a=0$) and $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(b+1)$. For large $N$, \eqref{eq:profile-limit}
puts $R_N(i)$ within $\varepsilon$ of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(i)$ for $a-1\le i\le b+1$. So the
largest value of $P_N$ on $\{N-b,\dots,N-a\}$ exceeds its values at $N-b-1$ and $N-a+1$, and
the maximal block on which $P_N$ takes this value and that meets an argmax lies inside
$\{N-b,\dots,N-a\}$. It is a local maximum of $P_N$. Minima are the same.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[a lower bound for the number of modes]\label{thm:modes-lower}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for both signs, let $\nu_\sgn(n)=o(T_\sgn(n))$ for both signs, and
let $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)$ and $\widetilde\kappa_\minus(0)$ exist. Then
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ has finitely many local maxima, $M_\plus\ge1$ of them, and
$\widetilde\kappa_\minus$ has $M_\minus\ge1$. For all large $N$ the law $P_N$ has at least
$M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes. In particular, if one end profile has two local maxima, then
$P_N$ has at least four modes for all large $N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:prep-diff} with $c=0$, $\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ is strictly decreasing on
some $[J,\infty)$, with the positive limit $1/\mu_\minus$. So its local maxima lie in
$[0,J]$, and there is at least one, the block of a global maximizer. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:copy}(b) each of them gives a mode of $P_N$ in
$\{N-J,\dots,N\}$, and distinct local maxima give modes in disjoint blocks. The same holds
at the minus end. The initial run satisfies the bulk criterion, since $B_nT_\sgn(n)\to0$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:mode}(a) the interior maximizers lie in $pN+O(B_N)$, and the block of
interior maximizers that contains one of them is a mode of $P_N$ at distance of order $N$
from both ends.
\end{proof}

For a fresh plus start $\widetilde\kappa_\plus=(1,u_\minus(1),\dots)$ has exactly one local
maximum if and only if $u_\minus$ is nonincreasing on $j\ge1$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal}. The preparation can add a mode, already for a log-convex law.

\begin{corollary}[a prepared start with four modes]\label{cor:four-modes}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for both signs, and let $f_\minus$ be strictly log-convex as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex}. Let the initial run be a fresh plus run with probability
$\frac12$ and a minus run of a fixed length $d_0\ge2$ with probability $\frac12$. Then $P_N$
has at least four modes for all large $N$, while for a fresh plus start
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ is strictly decreasing.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $\nu_\plus=f_\plus/2=o(T_\plus)$, $\nu_\minus$ is a point mass, $Q_\plus(N)=T_\plus(N)/2$
and $Q_\minus(N)=0$ for $N>d_0$. So $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)=\frac12$ and, by
\eqref{eq:kappa-prep}, $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)=\frac12(u_\minus(j)+u_\minus(j-d_0))$ for
$j\ge1$, with $u_\minus=0$ at negative arguments. Since $u_\minus(1)=f_\minus(1)<1$,
$\{0\}$ is a local maximum. By Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex}, $u_\minus(d_0)>1/\mu_\minus$, so
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus(d_0)=\frac12(1+u_\minus(d_0))$ exceeds both
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus(1)=\frac12u_\minus(1)$ and $1/\mu_\minus=\lim_j\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$.
So the largest value of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ on $j\ge1$ is attained, and only at points
$j\ge2$, and its block is a second local maximum. Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower} gives at
least $2+1+1$ modes. For a fresh plus start Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex} makes
$(1,u_\minus(1),\dots)$ strictly decreasing.
\end{proof}

To go further we need differences of the law itself. Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order}(b) gives
$R_N(j)=u_\minus(j)+C(j)/N+o(j/N)$, but near $j\asymp b_{\minus,N}$ both $N\Delta u_\minus(j)$
and $\Delta C(j)$ are of order one, so the error $o(j/N)$ says nothing about $\Delta R_N$. We
take the difference inside the exact identities, and (LD) controls the one term that is a
genuine difference of a convolution power.

\begin{lemma}[plus runs at the level of differences]\label{lem:plus-diff}
Assume (RV), (LR) and (LD) for the plus sign. Write $f=f_\plus$, $T=T_\plus$,
$\mu=\mu_\plus$, $V_m=f^{*m}$ with $V_0=\delta_0$, $\delta V_m=V_{m+1}-V_m$ and
$q_m=q^\plus_m$, and fix $\eta\in(0,1)$. As $n\to\infty$, uniformly in $0\le m\le n^{1-\eta}$,
the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $q_{m+1}(n)\le CT(n)$.
\item $|V_m(n-1)-V_m(n)|\le Cmf(n)/n$.
\item $\delta V_m(n)=f(n)(1+o(1))$.
\item $q_{m+2}(n)-q_{m+1}(n)=2\mu f(n)(1+o(1))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use Lemma~\ref{lem:local-bigjump} at arguments of order $n$, with $\eta/2$ in place of
$\eta$, and~\eqref{eq:f-uct}. So $V_m(x)\le Cmf(n)$ for $m\ge1$ and $x\ge n/2$, and
$mT(n)\to0$, $mf(n)\le C/n$ uniformly. Below we also apply (ii) and (iii) at arguments
$n'\ge n/2$. For large $n$ we have $n^{1-\eta}\le(n/2)^{1-\eta/2}\le(n')^{1-\eta/2}$, so there
we use them with $\eta/2$ in place of $\eta$.

(i) In $q_{m+1}(n)=\sum_{x<n}V_m(x)T(n-x)$ the part $x\le n/2$ is at most $T(n/2)\le CT(n)$,
and the part $x>n/2$ is at most $Cmf(n)\mu\le CT(n)$.

(ii) Let $m\ge1$, fix $K_0$ with $(K_0/4-1)(\alpha_\plus-1)/2>\alpha_\plus+3$, and put
$w=\lceil n/K_0\rceil$. On the event that no summand reaches $w$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:truncated} with $z=n/2-1$ bounds $\Prob(S^\plus_m=n')$ for $n'\in\{n-1,n\}$ by
$C(mT(w))^{K_0/4-1}\le Cn^{-\alpha_\plus-3}=o(f(n)/n)$, since
$mT(w)\le Cn^{-(\alpha_\plus-1)/2}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rv}(ii). Otherwise let $I$ be the
first index with $X^\plus_I\ge w$, and let $h_i(z)$ be the probability that $X^\plus_l<w$
for $l<i$ and that the sum of the $X^\plus_l$ with $l\ne i$ equals $z$. Then
$\Prob(S^\plus_m=n',I=i)=\sum_{y\ge w}f(y)h_i(n'-y)$, and the difference at $n-1$ and $n$ is
\[
 \sum_{z\le n-1-w}h_i(z)\bigl[f(n-1-z)-f(n-z)\bigr]-h_i(n-w)f(w).
\]
In the first sum $n-1-z\ge n/K_0$, so (LD) and~\eqref{eq:f-uct} bound the bracket by
$Cf(n)/n$, and $\sum_zh_i(z)\le1$. In the second term $h_i(n-w)\le V_{m-1}(n-w)\le Cmf(n)$,
and $mf(n)f(w)\le Cf(n)/n$. Summing over $i\le m$ gives (ii).

(iii) For $m=0$ this is $\delta V_0(n)=f(n)$. Let $m\ge1$. Then
$\delta V_m(n)=\sum_{x\ge1}f(x)[V_m(n-x)-V_m(n)]$. For $x\le n/2$, (ii) at the arguments
$n-x,\dots,n$ bounds the bracket by $Cmxf(n)/n$, so this part is at most
$C\mu mf(n)/n=o(f(n))$. The part $x>n/2$ equals
$\sum_{1\le y<n/2}f(n-y)V_m(y)-V_m(n)\sum_{x>n/2}f(x)$. The second term is at most
$Cmf(n)T(n/2)=o(f(n))$. In the first, $f(n-y)/f(n)$ is bounded and tends to one uniformly
in $y\le\varepsilon n$, while $\Prob(S^\plus_m<n/2)\to1$ and
$\Prob(S^\plus_m>\varepsilon n)\le m\mu/(\varepsilon n)\to0$. So it is $f(n)(1+o(1))$.

(iv) Putting one more run in front gives $q_{m+2}(n)=\sum_{x\ge1}f(x)q_{m+1}(n-x)$, with
$q_{m+1}=0$ at nonpositive arguments. Comparing the events in~\eqref{eq:q-cover} gives
$q_{m+1}(l-1)-q_{m+1}(l)=\delta V_m(l-1)$ for $l\ge1$. So
$q_{m+2}(n)-q_{m+1}(n)=\sum_xf(x)[q_{m+1}(n-x)-q_{m+1}(n)]$. For $x\le n/2$ the bracket is
$\sum_{i=1}^x\delta V_m(n-i)$, which is at most $Cxf(n)$ and is $xf(n)(1+o(1))$ for fixed
$x$, by (iii). Since $\sum_xxf(x)=\mu$, this part is $\mu f(n)(1+o(1))$. The part $x>n/2$
equals $\sum_{1\le y<n/2}f(n-y)q_{m+1}(y)-q_{m+1}(n)\sum_{x>n/2}f(x)$. The second term is
$O(T(n)^2)=o(f(n))$ by (i). In the first, $\sum_yq_{m+1}(y)=\E X^\plus_{m+1}=\mu$, and
$\sum_{y\ge\varepsilon n}q_{m+1}(y)\le\E[X_\plus;X_\plus\ge\varepsilon n/2]+\mu\Prob(S^\plus_m\ge\varepsilon n/2)\to0$.
So it is $\mu f(n)(1+o(1))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[first differences in the layer]\label{thm:first-diff}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign and (LR) and (LD) for the plus sign, and fix
$\eta\in(0,1)$. Let the start be fresh, with sign $\plus$ with probability $w_\plus$ and sign
$\minus$ with probability $w_\minus=1-w_\plus$. Put $R_N(j)=P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)$ and
$C_w(j)=C(j)-2\alpha_\plus\mu_\plus w_\minus u_\minus(j)$, with $C$ as in~\eqref{eq:Cj}. Then,
uniformly in $1\le j\le N^{1-\eta}$,
\[
 N\,\Delta R_N(j)=N\,\Delta u_\minus(j)+\Delta C_w(j)+o(1),
\]
and $\Delta C_w(j)\to c_{\mathrm a}=2\alpha_\plus s/\mu_\minus^2$ as $j\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By linearity it suffices to treat the two fresh starts. Write $u=u_\minus$, $v=v_\minus$,
$a_m=u^\minus_m$, $V_m=u^\plus_m$ and $n=N-j$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:fresh-identity} and its
minus form, $P_N(N-j)=\sum_{m\ge1}[a_m(j)Q_m(n)+V_m(n)b_m(j)]$, with $Q_m=q^\plus_{m+1}$ and
$b_m=q^\minus_m$ for a plus start, and $Q_m=q^\plus_m$ and $b_m=q^\minus_{m+1}$ for a minus
start. Taking the difference,
$D_N(j)=\mathrm I+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{IV}$ with
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm I&=\sum_m\Delta a_m(j)\,Q_m(n-1),&
 \mathrm{II}&=\sum_ma_m(j)\bigl[Q_m(n-1)-Q_m(n)\bigr],\\
 \mathrm{III}&=\sum_mV_m(n-1)\,\Delta b_m(j),&
 \mathrm{IV}&=\sum_m\bigl[V_m(n-1)-V_m(n)\bigr]b_m(j).
\end{align*}
Only $m\le j+1$ occurs, by (K2), so Lemma~\ref{lem:plus-diff} applies at $n-1\ge N/2$ with
$\eta/2$ in place of $\eta$. We use six facts on the minus side.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item $\sum_ma_m(j)=u(j)$ and $\sum_mma_m(j)=v(j)$.
\item The sum $\bar A_i(j)=\sum_{m>i}a_m(j)$ equals $\sum_{x<j}u^\minus_i(x)u(j-x)$. Hence
\[
 \Delta\bar A_i(j)=\sum_{x<j}u^\minus_i(x)\,d_{j+1-x}+u^\minus_i(j)\,u(1),
\]
and $\sum_{i\ge1}|\Delta\bar A_i(j)|\le1+\|d\|_1$, which is finite by
Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal}.
\item $(j+1)|\Delta u(j)|\le C$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal} and $jT_\minus(j)\to0$.
\item By~\eqref{eq:conv-v}, $v+u=u*u$, so $\Delta(v+u)(j)=\sum_{i\le j+1}d_iu(j+1-i)$ is the
coefficient of $z^{j+1}$ in $\hat u\hat d$, and $|\Delta v(j)|\le1+\|d\|_1$.
\item $\bar B_i(j)=\sum_{m>i}b_m(j)$ equals $\Prob(S^\minus_i<j)$ for a plus start and
$\Prob(S^\minus_{i+1}<j)$ for a minus start. So $\Delta\bar B_i(j)$ is $u^\minus_i(j)$,
respectively $u^\minus_{i+1}(j)$, and $\sum_m\Delta b_m(j)$ is $0$, respectively
$f_\minus(j)$.
\item $\sum_mmb_m(j)\le j$, since the index of the run that covers level $j$ is at most $j$.
\end{enumerate}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:plus-diff} and~\eqref{eq:f-uct}, every $\delta V_i(n-1)$ below is
$f_\plus(N)(1+o(1))$ and every $Q_{i+1}(n-1)-Q_i(n-1)$ is $2\mu_\plus f_\plus(N)(1+o(1))$,
uniformly in $i\le j+1$. Also $T_\plus(N-j-1)=T_\plus(N)+(j+1)f_\plus(N)(1+o(1))$.

II. Here $Q_m(n-1)-Q_m(n)$ is $\delta V_m(n-1)$, respectively $\delta V_{m-1}(n-1)$, so
$\mathrm{II}=f_\plus(N)u(j)+o(f_\plus(N))$ by (1).

III. Write $V_m=V_1+\sum_{i<m}\delta V_i$ and sum by parts. For a plus start
$\mathrm{III}=\sum_{i\ge1}\delta V_i(n-1)u^\minus_i(j)=f_\plus(N)u(j)+o(f_\plus(N))$ by (5). For a
minus start
$\mathrm{III}=V_1(n-1)f_\minus(j)+\sum_{i\ge1}\delta V_i(n-1)u^\minus_{i+1}(j)=f_\plus(N)[f_\minus(j)+u(j)-u^\minus_1(j)]+o(f_\plus(N))$,
which is the same since $u^\minus_1=f_\minus$.

IV. By Lemma~\ref{lem:plus-diff}(ii) and (6), $|\mathrm{IV}|\le Cf_\plus(N)j/N=o(f_\plus(N))$.

I. Write $Q_m=Q_1+\sum_{i<m}(Q_{i+1}-Q_i)$ and sum by parts. Then
$\mathrm I=Q_1(n-1)\Delta u(j)+\sum_{i\ge1}(Q_{i+1}-Q_i)(n-1)\,\Delta\bar A_i(j)$. Since
$\sum_{i\ge1}\bar A_i(j)=\sum_m(m-1)a_m(j)=v(j)-u(j)$, fact (2) makes the sum
$2\mu_\plus f_\plus(N)(\Delta v-\Delta u)(j)+o(f_\plus(N))$. For a plus start
$Q_1(n-1)=q^\plus_2(N-j-1)=T_\plus(N)+(j+1+2\mu_\plus)f_\plus(N)+o((j+1)f_\plus(N))$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:plus-diff}(iv) with $m=0$, and for a minus start
$Q_1(n-1)=T_\plus(N-j-1)=T_\plus(N)+(j+1)f_\plus(N)+o((j+1)f_\plus(N))$. By (3) the errors
times $\Delta u(j)$ are $o(f_\plus(N))$.

Adding the four parts,
\[
 D_N(j)=T_\plus(N)\Delta u(j)+f_\plus(N)\bigl[(j+1)\Delta u+2\mu_\plus\Delta v+2u-2\mu_\plus w_\minus\Delta u\bigr](j)+o(f_\plus(N)),
\]
with $w_\minus=0$ for a plus start and $w_\minus=1$ for a minus start. The bracket is bounded
by (3) and (4), and $Nf_\plus(N)/T_\plus(N)\to\alpha_\plus$ by (LR). Since
$\Delta C(j)=\alpha_\plus[(j+1)\Delta u+2\mu_\plus\Delta v+2u](j)$ by~\eqref{eq:Cj}, multiplying
by $N/T_\plus(N)$ gives the claim. For the limit, $(j+1)\Delta u(j)\to0$, $u(j)\to1/\mu_\minus$,
and $\Delta(v+u)(j)\to\hat d(1)/\mu_\minus=1/\mu_\minus^2$ by (4) and dominated convergence,
so $\Delta C_w(j)\to\alpha_\plus(2\mu_\plus/\mu_\minus^2+2/\mu_\minus)=c_{\mathrm a}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the extrema up to distance $N^{1-\eta}$]\label{thm:ends}
Assume (RV), (SPAN), (LR) and (LD) for both signs, and let the start be fresh with either
sign or the fair start. Let $J_\plus\ge1$ be such that $u_\minus$ is strictly decreasing on
$[J_\plus,\infty)$, and assume $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j+1)\ne\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$ for
$0\le j<J_\plus$. Put $\Lambda_N=a_\minus(N)$ and $x_*=(2\alpha_\plus s)^{-1/\alpha_\minus}$, and
fix $\eta\in(0,1-1/\alpha_*)$ and $\eta'\in(0,x_*)$. For all large $N$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item $D_N(j)$ has the sign of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j+1)-\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$ for
$0\le j<J_\plus$.
\item $D_N(j)<0$ for $J_\plus\le j\le(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N$.
\item $D_N(j)>0$ for $(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N\le j\le N^{1-\eta}$.
\item On $(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N<j<(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N$ the sign of $D_N(j)$ changes an odd
number of times, after the zeros of $D_N$ are deleted.
\end{enumerate}
The closed window $[(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N,(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N]$ contains every minimizer
of $P_N(N-j)$ over $J_\plus\le j\le N^{1-\eta}$. For a fresh plus start with
$u_\minus(j)>1/\mu_\minus$ for all $j\ge1$ it contains the minimizer $j^*_N$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode}. The same holds at the minus end with the
signs exchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) and (b) on $J_\plus\le j<J'$, for any fixed $J'$, follow from
Proposition~\ref{prop:copy}(a), since $\widetilde\kappa_\plus=u_\minus$ on $j\ge1$ for these
starts. By Theorem~\ref{thm:first-diff} and Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal},
\[
 \frac{N\,D_N(j)}{T_\plus(N)}=-\frac{NT_\minus(j+2)}{\mu_\minus^2}(1+\epsilon_j)+\Delta C_w(j)+o(1)
\]
uniformly in $1\le j\le N^{1-\eta}$, with $\epsilon_j\to0$ as $j\to\infty$. Here
$NT_\minus(x\Lambda_N)\to x^{-\alpha_\minus}$ for $x>0$, and
$x_*^{-\alpha_\minus}=c_{\mathrm a}\mu_\minus^2$. For (b) on $J'\le j\le(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N$, the
first term is at most $-(1-\epsilon)((x_*-\eta')^{-\alpha_\minus}+o(1))/\mu_\minus^2$ and
$\Delta C_w(j)\le c_{\mathrm a}+\epsilon$ once $J'$ is large, so the right side is negative for
$\epsilon$ small. For (c), the first term is at least
$-(1+\epsilon)((x_*+\eta')^{-\alpha_\minus}+o(1))/\mu_\minus^2$ and
$\Delta C_w(j)\ge c_{\mathrm a}-\epsilon$, so it is positive. The range of (c) is not empty,
since $\Lambda_N\in\RV_{1/\alpha_\minus}$ and $1/\alpha_\minus\le1/\alpha_*<1-\eta$.

For (d) and the minimizers, (b) and (c) at $\eta'$ are not enough, since they leave
$D_N(j)$ open for the integers $j$ in the open window nearest to its ends. We apply them
with $\eta'/2$ in place of $\eta'$. Put $L=(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N$ and $U=(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N$,
and let $j_L=\lfloor L\rfloor+1$ and $j_U=\lceil U\rceil-1$ be the first and last integers in
$(L,U)$. Since $\eta'\Lambda_N/2\to\infty$, for large $N$ we have
$j_L+1\le(x_*-\eta'/2)\Lambda_N$ and $j_U-1\ge(x_*+\eta'/2)\Lambda_N$. So $D_N(j_L)<0$ and
$D_N(j_U)>0$, and the sign of $D_N$ changes an odd number of times on $[j_L,j_U]$, which is
(d). Write $g(j)=P_N(N-j)$, so $D_N(j)=g(j+1)-g(j)$. By (b) at $\eta'/2$, $g$ decreases
strictly on $J_\plus\le j\le j_L+1$, so no minimizer is below $j_L+1$. By (c) at
$\eta'/2$, $g$ increases strictly on $j_U-1\le j\le N^{1-\eta}$, so no minimizer is above
$j_U-1$. Hence every minimizer lies in the open window, and so in the closed one. For the
fresh plus start, $j^*_N/\Lambda_N\to x_*$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:antimode}(a) and its proof. At the minus end the same argument uses (LR) and
(LD) for the minus sign.
\end{proof}

So within distance $N^{1-\eta}$ of the plus end the local extrema of $P_N$ are the copies of
those of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ and, inside the window of (d), alternating minima and
maxima with one more minimum than maximum, plateaus counted once.

\subsection{The exact number of modes}\label{sec:mode-count}

Fresh and fair starts satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}, which gives
at least $M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes. Here we prove the matching upper bound. Three steps are
needed beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}. The window of Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}(d) must hold a
single minimum. The law must be monotone on the big-jump zone, where the number of runs is of
order $N$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:local-bigjump} no longer applies. Finally $\Delta P_N$ must change
sign only once in the bulk. Each step needs one more order of smoothness, which is (LD2). The
last two also need the two scales to be comparable, which is (EQ). We keep
$\Lambda_N=a_\minus(N)$ and $x_*=(2\alpha_\plus s)^{-1/\alpha_\minus}$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}, and we write $x_\theta$ for the mode of the profile $h_\theta$, which is
unique by Lemma~\ref{lem:bell}.

\begin{introtheorem}[the exact number of modes]\label{thm:mode-count}
Assume (SPAN), (LR) and (LD2) for both signs, and (EQ). Let the start be fresh with either
sign or the fair start, and assume the no-tie condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:ends} at both
ends. Fix $\eta\in(0,1-1/\alpha_*)$ and $\eta'\in(0,x_*)$. There is $K_0$, which depends only on
the laws of the runs, such that for all large $N$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $D_N(j)$ has the sign of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j+1)-\widetilde\kappa_\plus(j)$ for
$0\le j<J_\plus$.
\item $D_N(j)<0$ for $J_\plus\le j\le(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N$.
\item $D_N$ is strictly increasing on $(x_*-\eta')\Lambda_N<j<(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N$. So its sign
changes exactly once there, and it vanishes at most once.
\item $D_N(j)>0$ for $(x_*+\eta')\Lambda_N\le j\le(1-p)N-K_0B_N$.
\item On $|x_{N,k}|\le K_0+1$ the sign of $\Delta P_N(k)=P_N(k+1)-P_N(k)$ changes exactly once,
from positive to negative, at a point $k_N$ with $x_{N,k_N}-x_{\theta_N}\to0$, and $\Delta P_N$
is strictly decreasing near $k_N$.
\end{enumerate}
The same holds at the minus end with the signs exchanged. Consequently $P_N$ has exactly
$M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes for all large $N$. There are $M_\plus$ of them within distance
$J_\plus$ of $N$, one in the bulk at $pN+(x_{\theta_N}+o(1))B_N$, and $M_\minus$ within distance
$J_\minus$ of $0$. Between the bulk mode and the modes near each end lies exactly one local
minimum, the antimode, in the window of Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}(d) or its mirror.
\end{introtheorem}

Parts (i), (ii) and the existence of a change of sign in (iii) are Theorem~\ref{thm:ends}.
Appendix~\ref{app:modes} proves the rest. The strict increase in (iii) comes from a second-order
form of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-diff} (Theorem~\ref{thm:layer-second}). Part (iv) comes from a
difference form of Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump}(b) (Theorem~\ref{thm:zone-diff}), which rests on
Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-bigjump} below. Part (v) comes from a local limit theorem for the first and
second differences of $P_N$ in the bulk (Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-diff}) together with the bell
shape of stable densities. Parts (i) to (iii) do not use (EQ).

\begin{proof}[Proof of the count]
We take parts (i) to (v) and their mirrors as given. Section~\ref{app:junctions} checks that
their ranges overlap and cover $0\le j<N$. Write $g(j)=P_N(N-j)$, so $D_N(j)=g(j+1)-g(j)$, and
let $S$ be the sequence of the nonzero signs among $D_N(0),\dots,D_N(N-1)$. The modes of $P_N$
are the local maxima of $g$. Each local maximum corresponds to exactly one of the following, an
adjacent pair $(+,-)$ in $S$, a first sign $-$, or a last sign $+$. Let $\sigma_\plus$ be the
sequence of signs of $\Delta\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ on $[0,J_\plus)$, all nonzero by the
no-tie condition, and define $\sigma_\minus$ at the minus end in the same way. At the minus end
$D_N(j)=-(P_N(i+1)-P_N(i))$ with $i=N-j-1$, and $i$ decreases as $j$ increases. So (i) to (v)
and their mirrors give
\[
 S=\sigma_\plus\,(-)^a\,(+)^b\,(-)^c\,(+)^d\,\bar\sigma_\minus,\qquad a,b,c,d\ge1,
\]
where $\bar\sigma_\minus$ is $\sigma_\minus$ read backwards with every sign reversed. The two
changes from $-$ to $+$ are the antimodes and the change from $+$ to $-$ is the bulk mode.
Since $\widetilde\kappa_\plus=u_\minus$ on $j\ge1$ is strictly decreasing on $[J_\plus,\infty)$,
the full sign sequence of $\Delta\widetilde\kappa_\plus$ is $\sigma_\plus$ followed by minus
signs. So a first sign $-$ and the pairs $(+,-)$ in $\sigma_\plus(-)$ count the local maxima
of $\widetilde\kappa_\plus$, which are $M_\plus$. The middle block $(-)^a(+)^b(-)^c(+)^d$ holds
one pair $(+,-)$. Reading a sequence backwards and reversing its signs keeps the pairs $(+,-)$
and exchanges a first sign $-$ with a last sign $+$. So the pairs in $(+)\bar\sigma_\minus$ and
a last sign $+$ count the local maxima of $\widetilde\kappa_\minus$, which are $M_\minus$. The
total is $M_\plus+1+M_\minus$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[three modes]\label{cor:three-modes}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count}, $P_N$ has exactly three modes for all
large $N$ if and only if each end profile has exactly one local maximum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count} the number of modes is $M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ for all large $N$,
and $M_\plus,M_\minus\ge1$.
\end{proof}

The ``only if'' half needs only Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}. The next lemma says when an end
profile of a fresh or fair start has one local maximum.

\begin{lemma}[one local maximum]\label{lem:one-max}
Assume (RV) and (SPAN) for the minus sign, let $c_0\in[0,1]$, and let
$y=(c_0,u_\minus(1),u_\minus(2),\dots)$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item If $u_\minus$ is nonincreasing on $j\ge1$, then $y$ has exactly one local maximum.
\item If $c_0>u_\minus(1)$, then $y$ has exactly one local maximum if and only if $u_\minus$
is nonincreasing on $j\ge1$.
\item If $c_0<u_\minus(1)$, then the local maxima of $y$ are those of the sequence
$(u_\minus(j))_{j\ge1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $u=u_\minus$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal} $u$ is eventually strictly decreasing, with
the positive limit $1/\mu_\minus$, so every maximal block of equal values of $y$ is finite.
(a) A block $\{a,\dots,b\}$ with $a\ge2$ is not a local maximum, since $y_{a-1}\ge y_a$. If
$c_0>u(1)$, the block $\{0\}$ is the only local maximum. Otherwise the block that contains $1$
is a local maximum, since it is followed by a strict decrease, and it is the only one. (b) The
``if'' part is (a). Let $u(i+1)>u(i)$ for some $i\ge1$. The values of $u$ on $[i+1,\infty)$ lie
above $1/\mu_\minus$ from some point on and tend to it, so their maximum is attained. The
maximal block of a maximizer starts after $i$, since $u(i)<u(i+1)$, and it is a local maximum
of $y$. So is $\{0\}$, since $c_0>u(1)$. (c) Since $c_0<u(1)$, no local maximum contains $0$,
and a block that starts at $1$ meets the condition at its left end.
\end{proof}

For a fresh start of sign $\sgn_0$ the profile at the end of sign $\sgn_0$ has $c_0=1>u(1)$,
so (b) applies, and this is the criterion stated after Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}. Part (b)
makes monotonicity necessary at every end whose profile starts above $u(1)$. This includes the
fair start, with $c_0=\frac12$, when $f(1)<\frac12$. The profile at the other end of a fresh
start has $c_0=0$, and (c) applies when $f(1)>0$. If $f(1)=0$ the profile starts with the tie
$0=u(1)$, and neither (b) nor (c) applies. Part (c) also applies to the fair start when
$f(1)>\frac12$. In case (c) monotonicity of $u$ is sufficient by (a). It is not necessary in
the example with $f(1)=0.6$ below, but there $u$ is checked only on a finite range. For the fair start and the zeta law of Section~\ref{sec:verify},
$\widetilde\kappa_\plus=(\frac12,u_\minus(1),u_\minus(2),\dots)$ with
$u_\minus(1)=1/\zeta(5/2)=0.745\ldots$. The profile is not monotone and has one local maximum,
and Corollary~\ref{cor:logconvex-three} below gives exactly three modes for all large $N$. Now
let $f(1)=0.6$, $f(2)=0.3$ and $f(n)=cn^{-5/2}$ for $n\ge3$ with $\sum_{n\ge3}f(n)=0.1$, for
both signs. This law satisfies (SPAN), (LR), (LD2) and (EQ). Here $u(1)=0.6<u(2)=0.66$, so $u$ is
not monotone, while the computed $u$ decreases strictly on $2\le j\le10^4$. The computed law of
the fair start has exactly three modes, at $k=2$, $N/2$ and $N-2$, for $N=1000$, $1500$ and
$2000$. For the fresh plus start the profile $(1,0.6,0.66,\dots)$ has at least two local maxima by (b),
so $P_N$ has at least four modes for all large $N$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}, and the
computed law has four.

\begin{corollary}[log-convex run laws]\label{cor:logconvex-three}
Assume (SPAN), (LR) and (LD2) for both signs and (EQ), and let $f_\plus$ and $f_\minus$ be
strictly log-convex as in Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex}. For a fresh start of either sign, and for
the fair start when $f_\plus(1)\ne\frac12$ and $f_\minus(1)\ne\frac12$, $P_N$ has exactly three
modes for all large $N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:logconvex}, $u_\minus$ is strictly decreasing, so $J_\plus=1$ is admissible.
The no-tie condition asks $u_\minus(1)=f_\minus(1)\ne\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)$. This holds
since $0<f_\minus(1)<1$ and $\widetilde\kappa_\plus(0)$ is $1$, $0$ or $\frac12$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-max}(a) the profile has one local maximum. The minus end is the same, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:three-modes} concludes.
\end{proof}

The zeta law $f(n)=n^{-\alpha-1}/\zeta(\alpha+1)$ with the same $\alpha$ for both signs satisfies
these hypotheses, and $f(1)=1/\zeta(\alpha+1)>\frac12$ for $\alpha>1$.

(EQ) enters the proof only at the scale $N$. So the theorem also holds along any sequence of
times at which the two scales are comparable.

\begin{corollary}[comparable scales along a sequence]\label{cor:sequences}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count} with (EQ) replaced by
$\alpha_\plus=\alpha_\minus$, and fix $\theta_0>0$. There is $N_0$ such that for every
$N\ge N_0$ with $\min_\sgn\theta_{\sgn,N}\ge\theta_0$ parts (i) to (v) hold and $P_N$ has
exactly $M_\plus+M_\minus+1$ modes.
\end{corollary}

Under (EQ) every large $N$ qualifies for some $\theta_0$ (Section~\ref{app:junctions}). The
corollary also covers equal indices with a ratio $T_\plus/T_\minus$ that oscillates between $0$
and $\infty$, at the times when the two scales are comparable.

In continuous time the propagator of a L\'evy walk is pictured as a central peak between two
side peaks at the fronts~\cite{zdk2015}. Corollary~\ref{cor:three-modes} makes this picture exact
on the lattice and gives its criterion. It also gives a family of laws where the picture fails.

\begin{example}[runs of length at least two]\label{ex:runs2}
If $f_\plus(1)=f_\minus(1)=0$, then for the fair start
$\widetilde\kappa_\pm=(\frac12,0,u_\mp(2),\dots)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:one-max}(b) gives at least
two local maxima at each end. So $P_N$ has at least five modes for all large $N$, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:modes-lower}. Now let $f_\sgn(n)=n^{-5/2}/(\zeta(5/2)-1)$ for $n\ge2$ for both
signs. This law satisfies (SPAN), (LR), (LD2) and (EQ). A finite computation gives
$u(2i)>\max\{u(2i-1),u(2i+1)\}$ for $1\le i\le6$, so each profile has at least seven local
maxima, at $j=0,2,\dots,12$, and $P_N$ has at least $15$ modes for all large $N$. The computed
$u$ decreases strictly on $12\le j\le10^4$. If it decreases strictly on $[12,\infty)$, the
no-tie condition holds with $J_\pm=12$, each profile has exactly seven local maxima, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count} gives exactly $15$ modes for all large $N$. The computed law has
exactly $15$ modes for every $N=2500,2600,\dots,4000$, and $17$, $19$ and $17$ modes at
$N=1000$, $1500$ and $2000$. The number $15$ belongs to this law and this start. For
$f_\sgn(n)=n^{-12/5}/(\zeta(12/5)-1)$, $n\ge2$, of index $7/5$, the computed $u$ has peaks at
$j=2,4,\dots,10$ only, so the proved lower bound is $13$, and the computed law has $13$ modes at
$N=3000$ and $4000$. For the fresh plus start with the law of index $3/2$ above, the minus-end
profile $(0,0,u(2),\dots)$ has a tie at $j=0$ and six local maxima, so the lower bound is $14$.
\end{example}

The main new tool of the proof is a difference form of the local big-jump theorem for sums of
$m$ runs, uniform in $m$. We state it here because it may be of use elsewhere.

\begin{lemma}[difference form of the local big jump]\label{lem:diff-bigjump}
Assume (SPAN), (LR) and (LD2) for the plus sign. Write $f=f_\plus$, $T=T_\plus$,
$\mu=\mu_\plus$, $\alpha=\alpha_\plus$, $a=a_\plus$, $V_m=f^{*m}$, $\nabla V_m(n)=V_m(n-1)-V_m(n)$,
$\delta V_m=V_{m+1}-V_m$ and $q_m=q^\plus_m$, and read $f(x)$ as $f(\lfloor x\rfloor)$ for real
$x\ge1$. There is $C$ such that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $K$ with the following
property. For all $m\ge1$ and all integers $n$ with $L=n-m\mu\ge Ka(m)$,
\begin{align*}
 &\Bigl|\nabla V_m(n)-(\alpha+1)\,m\,\frac{f(L)}{L}\Bigr|\le\varepsilon\,m\,\frac{f(L)}{L},
 \qquad V_m(n)\le C\,m\,f(L),\\
 &\Bigl|\delta V_m(n)-f(L)-\mu(\alpha+1)\,m\,\frac{f(L)}{L}\Bigr|\le\varepsilon\Bigl(f(L)+m\frac{f(L)}{L}\Bigr),\\
 &\Bigl|q_{m+2}(n)-q_{m+1}(n)-2\mu f(L)-\mu^2(\alpha+1)\,m\,\frac{f(L)}{L}\Bigr|\le\varepsilon\Bigl(f(L)+m\frac{f(L)}{L}\Bigr),
\end{align*}
and $q_{m+1}(n)\le C(T(L)+mf(L))$. The bound for $q_{m+2}-q_{m+1}$ also holds for $m=0$ and
$n\ge K$, with $L=n$.
\end{lemma}

The first bound is the derivative of the local big-jump formula $f^{*m}(n)\approx mf(n-m\mu)$.
For a fixed $m$ it follows from the asymptotic form $f(n)-f(n+1)\sim(\alpha+1)f(n)/n$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ld2}. The point is the uniformity in all $m\ge1$ with $L\ge Ka(m)$, with no upper
bound on $m$, since the big-jump zone needs $m$ of order $N$. The local big-jump theorems for
sums, due to Doney~\cite{doney1989} for lattice walks with a regularly varying mass function, to
Denisov, Dieker and Shneer~\cite[Theorem~9.1]{dds2008} and to
Berger~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{berger2019ptrf}, give the values $f^{*m}(n)$ up to a factor $1+o(1)$.
Such a factor says nothing about the difference of two neighbouring values, which is smaller by a
factor of order $1/L$. We take the difference inside the decomposition by the first giant
summand, where it falls on a single summand, and we bound the sums without a giant summand by a
Fourier estimate for truncated sums. We do not know an earlier statement of the difference form.

\begin{remark}[what remains open]\label{rem:mode-open}
Two questions remain. The first is unequal scales. If $\theta_{\sgn,N}\to0$ for one sign, the
kernels of the smaller scale have differences much larger than $B_N^{-1-r}$, and our proof of
(v) fails. The window $KB_N\le k-pN\le K\lambda_NB_N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bigjump} is then open
already for the values of $P_N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}). Parts (i) to (iii) hold without
(EQ). The second is a bound for every $N$. Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-count} is eventual, and its
threshold depends on the laws. For the law of Example~\ref{ex:runs2} and the fair start the computed
count is $17$ or $19$ for $N\le2000$ and is $15$ at every computed $N$ from $2500$ to $4000$, once the antimode at about $x_*\Lambda_N$
has moved past the oscillations of $u_\minus$. We know no bound on the number of modes that
holds for every $N$.
\end{remark}
