\section{Computations}\label{sec:verify}

No proof uses a computed number. The computations check the exact identities,
produce Figure~\ref{fig:atlas} and Table~\ref{tab:antimode}, and test the limit
theorems at moderate $N$. Except for the identities, two examples of
Section~\ref{sec:mode-count} and the queue of Section~\ref{sec:exposure} they use the zeta law
$f_\sgn(n)=n^{-5/2}/\zeta(5/2)$ for both signs, so $\alpha_\pm=3/2$, $p=1/2$ and
$s=3.894745$, with a fresh plus start unless we say otherwise.

For a fresh plus start let $a_t(k)$ and $b_t(k)$ be the probabilities that a plus
run, respectively a minus run, starts at step $t+1$ and the first $t$ steps contain
exactly $k$ plus steps. So $a_t(k)=\rho^{(\plus)}_{t-k}(k)$, $a_0(0)=1$ and $b_0(0)=0$.
Since the runs alternate, with $a_t(k)=b_t(k)=0$ for $k<0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:run-recursion}
 b_t(k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}a_{t-\ell}(k-\ell)\,f_\plus(\ell),\qquad
 a_t(k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}b_{t-\ell}(k)\,f_\minus(\ell)\qquad(t\ge1),
\end{equation}
and by~\eqref{eq:rho-rep} the identity~\eqref{eq:final-explicit} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:run-sum}
 P_N(N-j)=\sum_{t=j}^{N-1}a_t(t-j)\,T_\plus(N-t)+\sum_{m=1}^{j}b_{N-m}(N-j)\,T_\minus(m).
\end{equation}
This gives the whole law in $O(N^3)$ operations, and an initial run enters by adding its
weights to $a_t$ and $b_t$. Every entry is a dot product with nonnegative terms, so in
double precision every computed value has relative error at most
$(1+\gamma_{N+2})^{N+2}-1$ with $\gamma_n=nu/(1-nu)$ and $u=2^{-53}$, which is
$2.8\cdot10^{-9}$ at $N=5000$. At $N=600$ a run in ball arithmetic confirms the
double-precision law.

\emph{The fresh law.} In Figure~\ref{fig:atlas}, with $B_N=74$, the largest relative error
of the bulk on $|k-pN|\le3B_N$ is $0.071$, that of the big-jump kernel where
$|k-pN|\ge4B_N$ and $\min(k,N-k)\ge2B_N$ is $0.38$, attained at the junction with the
bulk, and that of the boundary kernel on $1\le\min(k,N-k)\le B_N$ is $0.094$. At
$N=1200$ and $3000$ the ratio $P_N(k)/G_N(k)$ at $k=\lceil pN+KB_N\rceil$ is $0.354$,
$1.482$, $1.036$ and $0.337$, $1.538$, $1.059$ for $K=1$, $4$, $16$, against the limits
$0.31$, $1.68$, $1.09$ of Remark~\ref{rem:no-single-formula}. The minimizers in
Table~\ref{tab:antimode} are certified against the rounding bound. For the stationary
initial run the ratio of $P_N(N-j)/T_\plus(N)$ to $\kappa^{\mathrm{st}}_\plus(j)$ is
$1.0007$, $1.0031$ and $1.0098$ at $j=1$, $2$ and $5$ for $N=2400$, about half its
deviation at $N=1200$. For Section~\ref{sec:extrema}, the ratio $d_j\mu_\minus^2/T_\minus(j)$
of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff-renewal} is $-0.963$, $-0.996$ and $-0.9996$ at $j=10$, $100$ and
$1000$. The error term of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-diff} for a fresh plus start at $j=20$ is
$0.73$ at $N=1000$ and $0.23$ at $N=3000$, so at this $j$ it decays like $1/N$. For the
fair start the law has exactly three modes, at $k=1$, $N/2$ and $N-1$, for $N=400$ and
$1000$. For the start of Corollary~\ref{cor:four-modes} with $d_0=5$ it has exactly four
modes at $N=1000$, at $k=1$, $499$, $995$ and $1000$. These modes are certified, since every
relative difference of neighbouring values exceeds twice the rounding bound. For
Section~\ref{sec:mode-count}, the zeta law has exactly three modes for the fresh plus and
the fair start at $N=2500$, $3000$, $3500$ and $4000$. For the law $f(n)\propto n^{-5/2}$,
$n\ge2$, of Example~\ref{ex:runs2}, $u(2i)>\max\{u(2i-1),u(2i+1)\}$ for $1\le i\le6$, $u$
decreases strictly on $12\le j\le10^4$, and the fair start has exactly $15$ modes, at $j$
and $N-j$ for $j=0,2,\dots,12$ and at $N/2$, for every $N=2500,2600,\dots,4000$, and $17$,
$19$ and $17$ modes at $N=1000$, $1500$ and $2000$. For the law $f(n)\propto n^{-12/5}$,
$n\ge2$, of index $7/5$ in the same example, the fair start has exactly $13$ modes at
$N=3000$ and $4000$. For the law with $f(1)=0.6$ and $f(2)=0.3$ after
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-max}, $u$ decreases strictly on $2\le j\le10^4$, and the fair start has
exactly three modes, at $k=2$, $N/2$ and $N-2$, while the fresh plus start has four, at
$N=1000$, $1500$ and $2000$. The renewal mass functions are sums of nonnegative terms with
the same rounding bound, and all these signs are certified in the same way.

\emph{The aged law.} For Theorem~\ref{thm:aging} with $t_a=N$, so $\theta=1$, the aged law
at $N=500$, $1000$ and $2000$ is computed from the first-run decomposition with the
initial run~\eqref{eq:aged-nu}, after a check of the method against a sum over all run
sequences for $t_a+N\le12$. The largest relative difference between the aged and the
fresh law on $|k-N/2|\le B_N$ is $0.054$, $0.043$ and $0.035$. At $N=2000$ the atom is
$1.020$ times the limit in part (b), the ratios $P_N(N-j)/(T_\plus(N)\kappa^{(1)}_\plus(j))$
of part (c)(i) are $1.015$ to $1.028$ for $j=1,\dots,5$, and the aging increment
$P^{(t_a)}_N(k)-P^{(0)}_N(k)$ at $L_k=\ell N$ is $0.945$, $0.998$ and $1.002$ times the two
terms that the kernel of part (c) adds to the fresh kernel, for $\ell=0.3$, $0.5$ and
$0.7$. The aged kernel inherits the slow convergence of the fresh kernel near the bulk,
where $P_N/G_N$ is $1.30$ at $\ell=0.3$ for the fresh start and $1.24$ for the aged one.
For Corollary~\ref{cor:aged-mode} the ratio of the larger endpoint atom to the largest
interior value at $\theta=1$ is $0.258$, $0.292$ and $0.330$ for $\alpha=3/2$, growing by
the factor $1.13$ per doubling of $N$ against the asymptotic factor
$2^{1-\alpha+1/\alpha}=1.122$, while the stationary start has already crossed, with the
ratio $1.117$ at $N=2000$. For $\alpha=9/5$ the aged ratio is $0.024$, $0.022$ and $0.019$,
against the factor $0.844$. For the zeta law the factor $1-2^{1-\alpha}=0.29$ in the aged
atom moves the crossover at $\alpha=3/2$ beyond $N=10^6$.

\emph{The queue.} For Section~\ref{sec:exposure} the cycle law is computed
from~\eqref{eq:cyclic} by FFT convolution up to $n=2^{14}$, with the zeta service law of
index $\alpha=3/2$ and the loads $\rho_\plus=0.6$ and $\rho_\minus=0.4$. It agrees with a
dynamic program on the workload itself to a relative $10^{-15}$ for $n\le300$, and with a
simulation of $4\cdot10^5$ cycles. At $n=2^{14}$ the ratio
$f_C(n)/(\lambda(1-\rho)^{-\alpha-1}f_Y(n))$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle}(a) is $0.9996$
for $\rho=0.4$ and $0.9972$ for $\rho=0.6$, and at $n=2^{13}$ the quantities
$n\Delta f_C(n)/f_C(n)$ and $n^2\Delta^2f_C(n)/f_C(n)$ are $-2.499$ and $8.745$ for
$\rho=0.4$, against the limits $-5/2$ and $35/4$ of part (b). The computed $f_C$ is
strictly log-convex on $2\le n<2^{14}$ for both loads. The exposure law is computed by the
recursion~\eqref{eq:run-recursion} with the initial run injected, and a simulation of
$3\cdot10^5$ switching queues at $N=200$ agrees with it ($\chi^2=10.8$ on $11$ degrees of
freedom). At $N=1000$, $2000$ and $4000$, $B_NP_N(\lfloor pN\rfloor)$ is $0.341$, $0.328$ and
$0.318$ against $h_\theta(0)=0.282$. For Corollary~\ref{cor:exposure-tail}(a) the ratio of
$\Prob(N-A_N<\varepsilon N)$ to its prediction at $N=4000$ is $1.076$, $1.063$ and $1.066$ for
the fresh start and $1.009$, $1.012$ and $1.022$ for the random-time start, for
$\varepsilon=0.05$, $0.1$ and $0.2$, down from $1.15$, $1.12$ and $1.09$ at $N=1000$ for the
fresh start. For part (b) the ratio of the two no-switch probabilities is $607$, $1204$ and
$2396$ against the asymptotic values $599$, $1195$ and $2387$. For
Corollary~\ref{cor:exposure-mode} the ratio of the larger atom to the largest interior
value for the random-time start is $3.28$, $3.81$ and $4.39$ at $\alpha=3/2$ and $0.291$,
$0.253$ and $0.220$ at $\alpha=9/5$, and the fresh law has exactly 3 modes at the three
values of $N$.
