\TITLE{Bulk, big jumps and boundary layers in the occupation law of a lattice L\'evy walk}
\SHORTTITLE{The occupation law of a lattice L\'evy walk}

\ABSTRACT{A lattice L\'evy walk moves in runs of alternating direction
whose lengths are independent with regularly varying tails of index between
one and two. We describe the law of the number of right steps after $N$
steps for every value of that number, except for one window that we describe
only in part when the runs of right steps have the smaller fluctuation scale. The law has
four pieces. They are a stable bulk at the scale of the larger run fluctuation,
a big-jump zone on each side in which one run is macroscopic and the bin is
the sum of the contributions of a complete run and of a run cut by the
horizon, a renewal boundary layer at each end given by the tail of one run
times the renewal mass function of the other, and an atom at each endpoint.
The boundary layer joins the big-jump zone continuously, the big-jump zone
joins the bulk in a double limit, and neither the bulk profile nor the
big-jump kernel nor their sum is accurate across that junction. The first run
enters each piece through its own criterion, which is sharp in the bulk and
at the ends, and we give its exact contribution, including for the
equilibrated start of the physics literature. The global mode is an endpoint
or a bulk bin according to a comparison of the endpoint atom with the profile
height, which for the equilibrated start with equal indices is the golden
ratio threshold. For a
fresh start, when the renewal mass function of the opposite runs stays above
its limit, the interior minimum near an end sits at the stable scale of those
runs, with an exact second-order coefficient. The boundary layers identify
both run laws. The bulk theorem uses only regular variation and
aperiodicity. The big-jump theorem uses the local form of regular variation,
and we show that regular variation of the tail alone does not suffice.}
