\TITLE{Bulk, big jumps and boundary layers in the occupation law of a lattice L\'evy walk}
\SHORTTITLE{The occupation law of a lattice L\'evy walk}
\AUTHORS{Arjun Pemmasani\footnote{Department of Mathematics, Harvey Mudd College,
Claremont, CA 91711, USA. \BEMAIL{apemmasani@hmc.edu}}}
\KEYWORDS{L\'evy walk; alternating renewal process; occupation time; local limit theorem;
stable law; big-jump principle; infinite density; renewal theorem; global mode; antimode;
number of modes; bell-shaped function}

\ABSTRACT{A lattice L\'evy walk moves in runs of alternating direction
whose independent lengths have regularly varying tails of index between
$1$ and $2$. We describe the law of the number of right steps at
every point. It has a stable bulk, a big-jump zone on each side, a
renewal boundary layer at each end and an atom at each endpoint, which give a
lattice version of the infinite density. The bulk local limit theorem holds
uniformly under regular variation and aperiodicity alone. One explicit kernel, a
complete giant run plus the run in progress, gives the big-jump zone and the
boundary layer under local regular variation. When one run scale is of smaller
order, we locate the start of its big-jump zone up to a factor $1\pm\delta$. We find
the exact effect of an arbitrary initial run on each piece. This answers a question
of Wang, Schulz, Deng and Barkai. We locate the global mode, with its golden ratio
threshold, and the
interior minimum near an end. For fresh and fair starts, comparable scales, a
second-order smoothness condition and no ties in the boundary profiles, the law has, for
all large times, one more mode than the two profiles have local maxima. So it has three
modes exactly when each profile has one local maximum. This holds for strictly log-convex
run laws. Without runs of length one the fair start has at least five modes, and at
least fifteen for a pure power law of index $3/2$.}
