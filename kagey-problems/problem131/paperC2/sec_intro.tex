\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Fix a generator $Q$ on $d$ states and a start law, and run the chain for $N$
steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$. With $T=\tau\log N$ the chain makes
about $\tau\log N$ switches. Its composition is the vector of occupation counts,
and the support of a composition is a face of the simplex. The companion
paper~\cite{paperC1} describes the law of the composition on every face at this
scale. On a face $F$ the most likely composition has probability
$N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)+o(1)}$ with the score $\Phi_\tau(F)=|F|-1+\tau\lambda_F$, where
$\lambda_F$ is the principal exit rate of $F$. So one power of $N$ per dimension
competes with the cost of staying in the face, and the mode moves to larger faces
as $\tau$ grows. Inside a face the logarithm of the law is $T$ times the Donsker
and Varadhan functional $J_V$ of the chain, read on that face.

This paper turns the description around. We ask which coarse functionals of the
law of the counts determine $Q$, and what each of them loses. We compare three,
from fine to coarse. The first is the law itself, at every $N$ and $T$. The second
is its logarithmic scale on single compositions, the functional $J_V$. The third
is a finite label, the support of the most likely composition when the chain is
killed at known rates. The answer is the same for the second and the third. Both
determine exactly the principal minors of $-Q$. We then describe the generators
with given minors, read a reversible generator from finitely many boundaries of
a design fixed in advance, and read the Freidlin and Wentzell cycle tree of an
Arrhenius family from the same labels.

We continue the lettering of the main theorems of~\cite{paperC1}, which states
Theorems~A to~C.

\subsection{The Donsker and Varadhan functional}\label{sec:intro-dv}

Write $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^\top D_\pi$ for the time reversal of an irreducible
generator with stationary law $\pi$. Call a partition $[d]=X\cup Y$ with
$|X|,|Y|\ge2$ a cut of $Q$ if both blocks $Q[X,Y]$ and $Q[Y,X]$ have rank at most
$1$. Across a cut a cut-transpose replaces the two rank-one blocks by the products
of their factors taken crosswise and transposes the block on $Y$ (Section~\ref{sec:fiber}).

\begin{introtheorem}[what the Donsker and Varadhan functional determines]\label{thm:D}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators on $[d]$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $J_V=J'_V$ on the simplex if and only if $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same
principal minors.
\item The irreducible generators with the principal minors of $-Q$ form a finite
set. It contains $Q$ and $Q^*$. It is $\{Q,Q^*\}$ when $Q$ has no cut, in
particular when $d\le3$, and $\{Q\}$ when $Q$ is reversible. In general it is the
set of generator points of the cut-transpose orbit of $-Q$, and it can contain
generators other than $Q$ and $Q^*$.
\item From the stationary start the law of the composition is the same under $Q$
and under $Q^*$, for every $N$ and every $T$ with $Tq_{\max}\le N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:dv}, part (ii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible}
with the example after it, and part (iii) is Proposition~\ref{prop:stationary}.
Part (i) needs three inputs. The first is the variational formula
$\lambda_V(v)=\min_\alpha(J_V(\alpha)-v\cdot\alpha)$ for
$\lambda_V(v)=-\varrho(Q+\operatorname{diag}v)$. The second is the irreducibility
of the multiaffine polynomial $\det(-Q_F-\operatorname{diag}w)$ for every
irreducible face, which is Lemma~5.1 of Al Ahmadieh~\cite{alahmadieh2023}. The third
is the Perron-branch lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:perron-branch}). It says that the
Perron root on an open set of fields determines that polynomial, hence every
principal minor. Kim and Landim~\cite[Section~5]{kl2024} show that $J_V$
determines the minors of orders one and two, which with the stationary law fix a
reversible generator, and they note that the uniform three-cycle and its reversal
share $J_V$. Theorem~\ref{thm:D} answers the question that their example leaves,
which generators share $J_V$. By their Corollary~5.8 the joint functional of the
empirical measure and the empirical flow determines the generator, so adding the
flow removes the whole fiber. Part (iii) shows that the fiber is not an artefact
of the logarithmic scale. From the stationary start even the exact law of the
counts cannot separate $Q$ from $Q^*$. For a start outside a finite union of
proper subspaces the second-order amplitudes of the local law do separate every
member of the fiber (Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitudes}).

\subsection{Modal supports}\label{sec:intro-modal}

Weight each composition by $e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$ for a field $v\le0$. This is the
chance that the chain survives when it is killed at state $i$ at the known rate
$-v_i$. We record only which face carries the most likely surviving composition,
as a function of $(\tau,v)$, and call this the modal-support map. The data are the
boundaries between the open regions where one face wins strictly
(Definition~\ref{def:controlled}).

\begin{introtheorem}[what the modal supports determine]\label{thm:E}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators and let the start have full support.
They have the same modal-support map, outside a closed nowhere dense set of
$(\tau,v)$, if and only if $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same principal minors. If the
minors differ, there is a nonempty open set of $(\tau,v)$ on which both maps have
a strict winner and the two winners differ.
\end{introtheorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:modal}, with Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} for
the last sentence. The proof is a descent through the faces. For every irreducible
face $F$ and a generic field on $F$, the exposure lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure})
finds a proper subface $G$ that shares a boundary of strict regions with $F$, and
along that boundary $\lambda_F=\lambda_G-(|F|-|G|)/\tau$. Induction on $|F|$ then
traces $\lambda_F$ on an open set of fields, and the Perron-branch lemma extends
it to all fields.

We should say plainly what Theorem~\ref{thm:E} is for. The survival probability
under calibrated killing is a finer observation. It equals $e^{-T\lambda_V(v)}$ up
to a bounded factor, so it gives every principal minor at once, with no descent
(Remark~\ref{rem:survival}). The modal support is not easier to sample. The most
likely composition carries probability $N^{-\Phi+o(1)}$, so estimating which face
carries it from independent runs needs far more runs than estimating the survival
probability. We know of no experiment in which the modal support is observed and
the survival probability is not. We therefore read Theorem~\ref{thm:E} as a
statement about retention. The forward theory of~\cite{paperC1} outputs the
support of the most likely composition, and so does any exact optimizer that
reports only a maximizer of the weighted law. Theorem~\ref{thm:E} says that this
output loses nothing that $J_V$ keeps.

\emph{What is borrowed and what is new.} The data come first. The principal
minors are not observed. Theorem~\ref{thm:dv} reads all of them off $J_V$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:modal} reads all of them off the modal-support map through the
exposure lemma and the Perron-branch lemma. This is the main contribution of the
first half of the paper. The fiber of the principal-minor map of an irreducible
matrix is known. Loewy~\cite{loewy1986}, and Hartfiel and
Loewy~\cite{hartfielloewy1984} for order at most three, show that an irreducible
matrix without a cut is determined by its principal minors up to diagonal
similarity and transposition. Al Ahmadieh~\cite[Theorem~4.1 and Lemma~4.5]{alahmadieh2023} shows that for
order at least four this happens exactly when there is no cut or the matrix is
diagonally similar to a symmetric one, and Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and
Raj~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{cggr2024} describe the whole fiber, up to diagonal
similarity and transposition, by sequences of cut-transposes. The second
contribution is the translation of this fiber to generators, which is the shortest
step. A Perron and Frobenius normalization picks exactly one generator in each
class of diagonal similarity, so the generator fiber is finite, every member is
irreducible with the spectrum of $Q$, and a cut-transpose acts as $Q$ on one side
of the cut and as $Q^*$ on the other (Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitudes}). The third is the stationary obstruction of
part (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.

\subsection{A reversible design and finite horizons}\label{sec:intro-reversible}

For a reversible chain on a known connected graph with $m$ edges the fields can be
fixed before anything is observed.

\begin{introtheorem}[a reversible design]\label{thm:F}
Let $Q$ be reversible and irreducible on a known connected graph with $m$ edges,
and suppose that known constants $R$ and $b$ bound the norm of the symmetrized
generator above and every edge product $\sqrt{q_{ij}q_{ji}}$ below.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item A design whose fields and time scales depend only on $R$, $b$ and the
labels reads $d-1$ singleton switches and $m$ pair-entry times off the
modal-support map, and these $m+d-1$ numbers determine $Q$.
\item No family of fewer than $m+d-1$ scalar $C^1$ observations, with finitely many
locally constant labels, identifies $Q$ on any open set of such generators.
\item The reconstruction is Lipschitz in the observed coordinates, with constants
that depend only on $d$ and $b/R$.
\item At the finite horizon $N$, with $L=\log N$, the observed pair-entry times are
$t_{ij}(1-\log L/(2L))+c_{ij}/L+O(\log L/L^2)$, uniformly over the generators of
the class and the starts with $\min_i\mu_i\ge\mu_*$, and the start bound cannot be
dropped.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Parts (i) to (iii) are Theorems~\ref{thm:reversible-reconstruction},
\ref{thm:minimality} and~\ref{thm:stability}, and part (iv) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-horizon} with Remark~\ref{rem:finite-start}. The coordinates
are those of Wolfer and Watanabe~\cite{wolferwatanabe2021}, and Kim and
Landim~\cite[Lemma~5.1 and Corollary~5.3]{kl2024} recover the same quantities from
$J_V$. What Theorem~\ref{thm:F} adds is a design fixed in advance, its minimality, its
stability with constants from $R$ and $b$ alone, and its finite-horizon behaviour.

\subsection{Low temperature and the cycle tree}\label{sec:intro-lowtemp}

Let the rates be $q_{ij}=e^{-V_{ij}/\eta}$ with fixed costs $V_{ij}\ge0$ and a
temperature $\eta\downarrow0$, and let the time scale be $\tau=e^{h/\eta}$ for a
height $h$. Then the competition between dimension and exit rate takes place on the
Freidlin and Wentzell cycles of the chain. The depth $D(F)$ of a face is the
exponential order of its exit rate, and it is the largest depth of a cycle inside
$F$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:depth}). Away from finitely many heights the winner is a cycle
of least size among those deeper than $h$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:noncritical}). At a
critical height explicit prefactors decide, and a noncycle can win on an open window
of the rescaled time scale (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} and
Example~\ref{ex:four-state}).

The noncycle window raises a question that we settle here. Modal winners need not be
cycles, so do the modal supports determine the cycles at all? The two obvious
experiments do not. The unperturbed modal path shows the depth of each face it
selects and nothing about the cycles that are never the smallest eligible face.
Killing the complement of a face at a large rate does not show its depth either,
because the whole space then has an exit rate of the same order, and the winner leaves
the face only at a larger height. The cure is to reward the face instead. For a
proper face $G$, a constant $c>0$ and a real $\gamma$ we put the field
$v^{G,\gamma}=c\,e^{-\gamma/\eta}\ones_G$, and we let the threshold $\vartheta(G)$ be the
supremum of the $\gamma$ for which $G$ wins under $v^{G,\gamma}$ at some height above
$\gamma$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the cycle tree from calibrated rewards]\label{thm:G}
Let $(Q_\eta)$ be an Arrhenius family with all rates positive, and let the start have
full support.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $\vartheta(G)\le D(G)$ for every proper face $G$, with equality when $G$ is a
cycle.
\item $D(F)=\max_{G\subseteq F}\vartheta(G)$ for every proper face $F$.
\item A nonempty proper set $C$ is a cycle if and only if $D(C')<D(C)$ for every
nonempty proper subset $C'$ of $C$.
\end{enumerate}
So the modal-support map, restricted to the fields $v^{G,\gamma}$, determines the
depth of every face, the cycles, their depths and the cycle tree. No step uses
reversibility, and no nondegeneracy of the depths is assumed.
\end{introtheorem}

Parts (i) and (ii) are Theorem~\ref{thm:attraction} and part (iii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:cycles-from-depths}. The design needs three calibrated quantities,
the time unit, the amplitude of the reward and the labels of the states, and no rate
of $Q$. The reward keeps a face whose rate is known, namely the whole space with
$\lambda_V(v)\le0$, and this is what the killing design destroys. Criterion (iii)
holds with ties between the depths of different faces, because Catoni's cycles are
strict and depths increase strictly along nested cycles (Lemma~\ref{lem:nested}). Its
weak form, with $D(C')\le D(C)$, fails already on three states. Finally the endpoints
of the critical windows determine the prefactors of the faces on the lower envelope,
and only bounds for the others (Theorem~\ref{thm:window-prefactors}).

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The rate inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the killed
chain~\cite{dv1975}, and the exact law of the counts goes back to
Good~\cite{good1961}. Bertini, Gabrielli and Landim~\cite{bgl2024} and
Landim~\cite{landim2023} expand the level-two rate functions of finite chains in a
parameter, and Kim and Landim~\cite{kl2024} treat the joint rate of the empirical
measure and the flow~\cite{bfg2014,bfg2015}. Their Section~5 is the starting point of
Theorem~\ref{thm:D}, and the matrix theory behind the fiber is credited above and in
Section~\ref{sec:fiber}, together with Engel and Schneider~\cite{engelschneider1980}.
Cohen~\cite{cohen1981} and Friedland~\cite{friedland1981} study the convexity of
$v\mapsto\varrho(A+\operatorname{diag}v)$, and Delmas, Dronnier and Zitt~\cite{ddz2024}
prove a multiplicative analogue of the Perron-branch lemma. The cycle framework is that of
Freidlin and Wentzell~\cite{freidlinwentzell2012}, Catoni~\cite{catoni1999}, Olivieri and
Vares~\cite{olivierivares2005}, Trouv\'e~\cite{trouve1996} and Cirillo, Nardi and
Sohier~\cite{cirillonardisohier2015}, and the eigenvalue asymptotics behind the prefactors go
back to Wentzell~\cite{wentzell1972}. What is new at low temperature is the score of dimension
against exit rate, the competition of envelopes at a critical depth with its noncycle window,
and the reading of the cycle tree from calibrated rewards. The question grew out of
Problem~131 of Kagey's collection~\cite{kagey131}.

\subsection{Plan of the paper}\label{sec:plan}

Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes the notation and states the results of~\cite{paperC1}
that we use. They are the first-order results, the face maxima with a field, their
uniform version on a compact class and the constant of the local law, and nothing else of
the forward theory enters.
Section~\ref{sec:minors} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:D}. Section~\ref{sec:modal} sets up
the modal-support map and proves Theorem~\ref{thm:E}. Section~\ref{sec:reversible}
treats the reversible design and the finite horizon, and Section~\ref{sec:lowtemp} the
low temperature. Section~\ref{sec:verify} lists the computations that check the paper
and the open questions.
