\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Suppose that we record how long a continuous-time Markov chain on $[d]=\{1,\dots,d\}$ spends in
each state, and not the order of its visits or its jumps. What does this record determine about
the generator $Q$? We compare 3 resolutions of the law of the occupation measure. The first is the
law itself. The second is its logarithmic scale, the Donsker and Varadhan
functional $J_V$~\cite{dv1975}, which is the rate function of the occupation measure. The third
is a finite label, the support of the most likely vector of counts of a discretized chain killed
at known rates. This paper answers the question at each of the 3 resolutions, and at low
temperature for the second and the third.

Kim and Landim~\cite[Section~5]{kl2024} began this inverse problem for $J_V$. Their Lemma~5.1
reads the exit rates $q_i$ and the edge products $q_{ij}q_{ji}$ off $J_V$, that is, the principal
minors of orders 1 and 2 of $-Q$. Together with the stationary law, which $J_V$ gives through its
zero set (their Lemma~5.2), this fixes $Q$ when $Q$ is reversible (their Corollary~5.3). Their
Example~5.4 shows that the uniform 3-cycle and its reversal have the same $J_V$, so $J_V$ does not
determine a nonreversible generator, and they call their conditions optimal. By their
Corollary~5.8 the joint functional of the empirical measure and the empirical flow determines the
rates when every state is recurrent. What $J_V$ determines for a nonreversible chain was left open.

\subsection{The Donsker and Varadhan functional}\label{sec:intro-dv}

\begin{introtheorem}[what the Donsker and Varadhan functional determines]\label{thm:A}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators on $[d]$. Then $J_V=J'_V$ on the simplex if and only if
$-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same principal minors. In that case the exit rate $\lambda_F(v)$ of every
face $F$ under every field $v$, and the functional $J_F$ of the chain killed on leaving $F$, are
the same for $Q$ and $Q'$.
\end{introtheorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:dv}. It has 3 inputs. The first is the variational formula
$\lambda_V(v)=\min_\alpha(J_V(\alpha)-v\cdot\alpha)$ for $\lambda_V(v)=-\varrho(Q+\operatorname{diag}v)$.
The second is the irreducibility of the multiaffine polynomial $\det(-Q_F-\operatorname{diag}w)$
for every irreducible face, which is Lemma~5.1 of Al Ahmadieh~\cite{alahmadieh2023}. The third is
the Perron-branch lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:perron-branch}). It says that the Perron root on an open
set of fields determines that polynomial, hence every principal minor. The 3-cycle of Kim and
Landim is the case $d=3$ of the next statement.

The set of generators with given principal minors comes from the matrix literature. Write
$Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^\top D_\pi$ for the time reversal, with $\pi$ the stationary law. A partition
$[d]=X\cup Y$ with $|X|,|Y|\ge2$ is a cut of $Q$ if the blocks $Q[X,Y]$ and $Q[Y,X]$ have rank at
most 1. Across a cut, a cut-transpose of $-Q$ replaces the 2 rank-one blocks by the products of
their factors taken crosswise and transposes the block on $Y$ (Section~\ref{sec:fiber}). The
cut-transpose orbit of $-Q$ is the set of matrices reached from $-Q$ or from $-Q^\top$ by
finitely many cut-transposes, each across a cut of the current matrix, up to positive diagonal
similarity. Each of them has exactly one generator point, the generator $-D^{-1}CD$ obtained
from a member $C$ by a positive diagonal similarity (Definition~\ref{def:orbit}).

\begin{corollary}[the generator fiber]\label{cor:A-fiber}
Let $Q$ be an irreducible generator. The generators with the principal minors of $-Q$ are the
generator points of the cut-transpose orbit of $-Q$. They form a finite set of irreducible
generators with the spectrum of $Q$, which contains $Q$ and $Q^*$. It is $\{Q,Q^*\}$ when $Q$ has
no cut, in particular when $d\le3$, and $\{Q\}$ when $Q$ is reversible. In general it can contain
generators other than $Q$ and $Q^*$.
\end{corollary}

This is Corollary~\ref{thm:nonreversible} with the example after it. The fiber of the
principal-minor map of an irreducible matrix, up to diagonal similarity and transposition, is due
to Loewy~\cite{loewy1986}, Hartfiel and Loewy~\cite{hartfielloewy1984}, Al
Ahmadieh~\cite{alahmadieh2023} and Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and Raj~\cite{cggr2024}. Our part is
a short translation to generators. A Perron and Frobenius normalization picks exactly one
generator in each class of diagonal similarity, and a cut-transpose acts as $Q$ on one side of
the cut and as $Q^*$ on the other (Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitudes}).

\subsection{The exact law}\label{sec:intro-law}

At the first resolution we ask what the law of the occupation times determines. Let $O(T)$ be the
vector of the times that the chain in continuous time spends in each state up to time $T$. A
memoryless block of $Q$ is a set $B$ of at least 2 and at most $d-1$ states that every state of
$B$ leaves at the same rate and with the same landing law, and that every jump into $B$ enters
with the law $\pi_B/\pi(B)$. Reversing $Q$ inside $B$ means replacing the block $Q_B$ by
$D_{\pi_B}^{-1}Q_B^\top D_{\pi_B}$.

\begin{introtheorem}[what the exact occupation law determines]\label{thm:law}
Let $Q$ be an irreducible generator on $[d]$ and $Q'$ a generator.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item If $O(T_0)$ has the same law under $Q$ from a start $\mu$ and under $Q'$ from a start
$\mu'$, for one $T_0>0$, then it has the same law for every $T$, and $-Q'$ has the principal
minors of $-Q$.
\item For every start outside a finite union of proper linear subspaces, which depend on $Q$, the
law of $O(T_0)$ at one $T_0>0$ determines $Q$.
\item Let $Q'$ be irreducible and start each chain from its stationary law. If the laws of
$O(T_0)$ agree for one $T_0>0$, then $Q'$ is $Q$ or $Q^*$ when $d\le4$, when $Q$ has no cut, and
when $Q$ has positive rates outside a closed nowhere dense set of measure zero. In the first case
the moments of $O(T)$ of order 1 and 2 up to order $T^4$ suffice, and in the last case those up to
order $T^5$. Conversely $Q^*$ always gives the stationary law of $Q$.
\item This fails in general. Reversing $Q$ inside a memoryless block keeps the stationary law of
$O(T)$ for every $T$, and for $d=5$ it gives a generator other than $Q$ and $Q^*$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

This is Theorems~\ref{thm:one-horizon}, \ref{thm:generic-start}, \ref{thm:stationary-law}
and~\ref{thm:memoryless} with Example~\ref{ex:five-state}, which gives the rates. The proof of (i) reads the exponential of the leading eigenvalue of $Q-W$, for a large
diagonal $W$, off the Laplace transform at one time. The exponent is algebraic in $W$, and the
exponential of a nonconstant algebraic function is not algebraic. So the law at one time gives the
principal minors, and with them everything that $J_V$ determines. Part (ii) then compares the
finitely many members of the fiber of Corollary~\ref{cor:A-fiber}. The second moments in (iii)
give the symmetric part of the symmetrized generator and the square of its antisymmetric part,
and for $d\le4$ the antisymmetric part has a single invariant plane. The same results hold for the
counts of $I+hQ$ for all $N$ at one fixed $h$, while one fixed $N$ is not enough
(Remark~\ref{rem:one-N}). Across a cut an exact gluing formula
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gluing}) shows that a cut-transpose keeps the stationary law if and only if
one side of the cut is balanced (Corollary~\ref{cor:balance}). A memoryless block is a balanced
side, and we conjecture that reversals of memoryless blocks are the only obstruction
(Conjectures~\ref{conj:fiber} and~\ref{conj:balanced}).

So the first level of the comparison is now answered. From a generic start the law determines $Q$,
from the stationary start it determines $Q$ up to time reversal in the cases of (iii), and $J_V$
sees only the principal minors. Earlier we had only that the stationary law of the counts is the
same under $Q$ and $Q^*$, by path reversal (Remark~\ref{rem:stationary}), and that the constants of
the local law separate the fiber from a generic start (Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitudes}).

\subsection{Modal supports}\label{sec:intro-modal}

The label enters through a chain that rarely switches. Fix a start law and run the chain for $N$
steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$, so that it makes about $T$ switches. Its composition is
the vector of occupation counts, and the support of a composition is a face of the simplex. For
$T=\tau\log N$ the most likely composition on a face $F$ has probability
$N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)+o(1)}$, with the score $\Phi_\tau(F)=|F|-1+\tau\lambda_F$ and $\lambda_F$ the
exit rate of $F$~\cite{paperC1}. One power of $N$ per dimension competes with the cost of staying
in the face. So the face of the mode is a nontrivial function of $\tau$, and it becomes a function
of $(\tau,v)$ when each composition is weighted by $e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$ for a field $v$. For $v\le0$
this weight is the chance that the chain survives when it is killed at state $i$ at the known rate
$-v_i$. The first-order modal-support map $W_Q$ records which face minimizes the score with the
field, on the open dense set of $(\tau,v)$ where exactly one face does
(Section~\ref{sec:observation}).

\begin{introtheorem}[what the first-order modal supports determine]\label{thm:B}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators and let the start have full support. Then $W_Q=W_{Q'}$
outside a closed nowhere dense set of $(\tau,v)$ if and only if $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same
principal minors. If the minors differ, there is a nonempty open set of $(\tau,v)$ on which both
maps have a strict winner and the 2 winners differ.
\end{introtheorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:modal}. The proof is a descent through the faces. For every irreducible
face $F$ and a generic field on $F$, the exposure lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}) finds a proper
subface $G$ that shares a boundary of strict regions with $F$. Along that boundary
$\lambda_F=\lambda_G-(|F|-|G|)/\tau$. Induction on $|F|$ traces $\lambda_F$ on an open set of
fields, and the Perron-branch lemma extends it to all fields.

The modal support is an artificial observable. The survival probability under calibrated killing
is finer, it is easier to sample, and it gives every principal minor at once
(Remark~\ref{rem:survival}). We know of no experiment in which the modal support is observed and
the survival probability is not. We study the label because the forward theory
of~\cite{paperC1} describes the law of the counts through it. The word \emph{exactly} applies to
$W_Q$ only. The finite-horizon label, the support of the most likely composition at a given $N$,
determines $W_Q$ and so at least the minors, and whether it determines more is open
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}).

\subsection{Low temperature and the cycle tree}\label{sec:intro-lowtemp}

Let the rates be $q_{ij}=e^{-V_{ij}/\eta}$ with fixed costs $V_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i\ne j$, so that
every rate is positive, and let the time scale be $\tau=e^{h/\eta}$ for a height $h$. We take
$N\to\infty$ first at fixed $\eta$ and then $\eta\downarrow0$, and the observer knows $\eta$. The
depth $D(F)$ of a proper face is the exponential order of its exit rate, and it is the largest
depth of a Freidlin and Wentzell cycle inside $F$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:depth}). Away from finitely
many heights the winner is a cycle of least size among those deeper than $h$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:noncritical}). At a critical height a noncycle can win on an open window of the
rescaled time scale (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} and Example~\ref{ex:four-state}).

So winners need not be cycles. By Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B} at each $\eta$, the
functionals $J^\eta_V$, or the maps $W_{Q_\eta}$, determine every exit rate, hence every depth.
But they do so through the whole functional, or through fields chosen after $Q_\eta$ is known. We
also want a design fixed in advance. Readings at one fixed field fail. The unperturbed path shows
the depth only of the faces it selects. Killing the complement of a face at one large rate keeps
the face winning above its depth, and by the shift rule rewarding the face at the same rate is
the same experiment. What works is to scan the size of the field on the calibrated scale and read
a threshold. For a proper face $G$, a constant $c>0$ and a real $\gamma$ put
$v^{G,\gamma}=c\,e^{-\gamma/\eta}\ones_G$, equivalently a killing at rate $c\,e^{-\gamma/\eta}$ off
$G$. A face $G$ wins at $(h,v)$ if for all small $\eta$ it is the unique minimizer of the score
$|F|-1+e^{h/\eta}\lambda_F(v)$, so that it carries the mode for all large $N$ at each such $\eta$.
The threshold $\vartheta(G)$ is the supremum of the $\gamma$ for which $G$ wins under
$v^{G,\gamma}$ at some height $h>\gamma$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the cycle tree]\label{thm:D}
Let $(Q_\eta)$ be an Arrhenius family with all rates positive, and let the start have full support.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item The functionals $J^\eta_V$, along any sequence $\eta\to0$, determine the depth of every face,
the cycles, their depths and the cycle tree. So do the maps $W_{Q_\eta}$.
\item For every proper face $G$, $\vartheta(G)\le D(G)$, with equality when $G$ is a cycle, and
$D(F)=\max_{G\subseteq F}\vartheta(G)$ for every proper face $F$.
\item A nonempty proper set $C$ is a cycle if and only if $D(C')<D(C)$ for every nonempty proper
subset $C'$ of $C$.
\end{enumerate}
No step uses reversibility, and no nondegeneracy of the depths is assumed.
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Corollary~\ref{cor:tree-from-dv}, part (ii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:attraction} and
part (iii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:cycles-from-depths}. Part (ii) is a design fixed before $Q$ is
known. It needs the temperature, the time unit, the amplitude $c$ and the labels of the states,
and no rate of $Q$, and with (iii) it reads the cycle tree from finitely many thresholds. Criterion
(iii) holds with ties between the depths of different faces, because cycles are strict and depths
increase strictly along nested cycles (Lemma~\ref{lem:nested}). Its weak form, with
$D(C')\le D(C)$, fails already on 3 states. Finally the endpoints of the critical windows
determine the prefactors of the faces on the lower envelope and only bound the others
(Theorem~\ref{thm:window-prefactors}).

\emph{Comparison with $\Gamma$-expansions.} Landim~\cite{landim2023} for nonreversible chains and
Bertini, Gabrielli and Landim~\cite{bgl2024} for reversible ones expand the level-two rate
function of a family of finite chains along the metastable time scales. Kim and
Landim~\cite{kl2024} do the same for the joint functional of the measure and the flow. Their
families have rates with a limit that is positive on some edge, and every ratio of products of
rates is comparable~\cite[(2.5)]{kl2024}. An Arrhenius family satisfies this after a rescaling of
time, since every such ratio is $e^{-b/\eta}$ for some real $b$. So we do not claim a weaker
hypothesis. Their expansions give the metastable states and wells on each time scale, and the reduced
chains between them. Theorem~\ref{thm:D} adds the
depth of every face, not only of the metastable wells, the criterion (iii) that recognizes a cycle
from depths alone, and a readout from finitely many calibrated observations. Criterion (iii) is
in neither Section~4 of Catoni~\cite{catoni1999} nor Sections~2 and~3 of Cirillo, Nardi and
Sohier~\cite{cirillonardisohier2015}, which characterize cycles by communication heights and state
the weak monotonicity of depth. We did not check Trouv\'e~\cite{trouve1996}, and we claim no more.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The rate inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the killed
chain~\cite{dv1975}, and the exact law of the counts goes back to Good~\cite{good1961}. The joint
rate of the empirical measure and the flow is that of Bertini, Faggionato and
Gabrielli~\cite{bfg2014,bfg2015}. The matrix theory behind the fiber is credited above and in
Section~\ref{sec:fiber}, together with Engel and Schneider~\cite{engelschneider1980}.
Cohen~\cite{cohen1981} and Friedland~\cite{friedland1981} study the convexity of
$v\mapsto\varrho(A+\operatorname{diag}v)$, and Delmas, Dronnier and Zitt~\cite{ddz2024} prove a
multiplicative analogue of the Perron-branch lemma. The cycle framework is that of Freidlin and
Wentzell~\cite{freidlinwentzell2012}, Catoni~\cite{catoni1999}, Olivieri and
Vares~\cite{olivierivares2005}, Trouv\'e~\cite{trouve1996} and Cirillo, Nardi and
Sohier~\cite{cirillonardisohier2015}, and the eigenvalue asymptotics behind the prefactors go back
to Wentzell~\cite{wentzell1972}.

Identifiability from coarse observations has a long history for aggregated Markov chains, where
one observes the lumped path, that is, which class of states the chain occupies at each time, as
in models of ion channels. Fredkin and Rice~\cite{fredkinrice1986}, Ryd\'en~\cite{ryden1996},
Larget~\cite{larget1998} and Bruno, Yang and Pearson~\cite{brunoyangpearson2005} study when two
generators give the same law of the lumped path, and give canonical forms for the equivalence
classes. Xiang, Zhou, Deng and Yang~\cite{xzdy2024} identify the generator of a stationary chain,
reversible or in a class of irreversible ones, from hitting-time distributions observed at some
states. Our data are of another kind. The law of a lumped path keeps the order of the visits and
merges states, while the occupation law keeps every state and forgets the order. So neither
observable refines the other, and the obstructions differ. Ours are time reversal and the
reversal of memoryless blocks (Theorem~\ref{thm:law}). In their setting Ryd\'en also bounds the
number of states needed to represent the lumped process, and Larget gives necessary and
sufficient conditions for 2 aggregated processes to be equivalent. We know of no
identifiability statement for the exact occupation law.
