\section{Modal supports}\label{sec:modal}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. We set up the modal-support map in
Section~\ref{sec:observation}, make the notion of equal boundaries precise in
Section~\ref{sec:controlled}, and prove the descent in Section~\ref{sec:exposure}.

\subsection{The killed chain and its modal support}\label{sec:observation}

Fix a field $v\in\R^d$ and a time scale $\tau>0$, put $T=\tau L$, and weight each
composition by $e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$. Along a path the weight is $\prod_te^{Tv_{X_t}/N}$. If
$v\le0$, this is the probability that the chain survives when it is killed at each visit to
$i$ with probability $1-e^{Tv_i/N}$. So $P_N(n)e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$ is the probability of
surviving with composition $n$, and its maximizers are the modes of the composition of the
surviving chain, since a normalization does not move a mode. On a face the extra killing
shifts the diagonal of the killed generator, $Q_F+\operatorname{diag}v_F$ in place of $Q_F$,
and $\lambda_F(v)$ is the exit rate of $F$ for the killed chain.

The shift $v\mapsto v+c\ones$ multiplies the weighted law by the constant $e^{Tc}$, and since
$\lambda_F(v+c\ones)=\lambda_F(v)-c$ it moves every score $\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)$ by the same
$-\tau c$. So it changes no winner, and every field may be taken negative. Throughout this
section the field and the time scale are calibrated, that is, $v$ and $\tau$ are known in the
units of $Q$.

\begin{proposition}[the controlled score law]\label{prop:controlled-score}
Fix $Q$, a start $\mu$ with full support, a field $v$ and $\tau>0$, and let $T=\tau L$. For every
admissible face $F$,
\[
 \log M_F(T,v)\le-\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)\,L+O(\log L),
\]
with equality up to $O(\log L)$ if $F$ is irreducible, and for a singleton $M_{\{i\}}(T,v)$ is
given exactly by~\eqref{eq:vertexmax}. A reducible admissible face $F$ has
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)\ge\Phi_{\tau,v}(C)+1$ for one of its strongly connected components $C$.
Hence if $F$ is the unique minimizer of $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ over the admissible faces, then $F$ is
irreducible and the support of every maximizer of the weighted law is $F$ for all large $N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For large $N$ we have $T_0\le\tau L\le N^{1/3}$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(a) gives the upper
bound. An irreducible face satisfies (H) (Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(a)), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax} gives the equality. Let $F$ be reducible and
admissible, with $k=|F|$. Then $\lambda_F(v)$ is the least $\lambda_C(v)$ over the components
$C$ of $F$, and a component $C$ where it is attained has $|C|\le k-1$. It is irreducible, hence
admissible, and $\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)=\Phi_{\tau,v}(C)+k-|C|\ge\Phi_{\tau,v}(C)+1$. So a unique
minimizer $F$ of $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ is irreducible. Let $\varepsilon>0$ be the least gap
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(G)-\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)$ over the other admissible faces $G$. Their weighted maxima
are at most $N^{-\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)-\varepsilon+o(1)}$, that of $F$ is at least
$N^{-\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)-o(1)}$, and a face that is not admissible carries no mass. There are
finitely many faces, so for large $N$ every maximizer has support $F$.
\end{proof}

The upper bound holds up to the boundary of the face, and this is what lets a fixed field act
on all compositions at once.

\subsection{Strict regions and controlled boundaries}\label{sec:controlled}

Let $\Omega=(0,\infty)\times\R^d$ be the space of pairs $\omega=(\tau,v)$, and fix a start
of full support. For a generator $Q$ let $\mathcal S_Q$ be the set of
$\omega\in\Omega$ at which $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ has a unique minimizer over the admissible faces,
and let $W_Q(\omega)$ be that minimizer. We call $\mathcal S_Q$ the strict set and $W_Q$ the
modal-support map.

\begin{lemma}[strict regions]\label{lem:strict}
Let $Q$ be a generator and $\mu$ a start of full support.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The strict set $\mathcal S_Q$ is open and dense in $\Omega$, its complement is closed
and nowhere dense, and $W_Q$ is locally constant on $\mathcal S_Q$.
\item At every $\omega\in\mathcal S_Q$ the face $W_Q(\omega)$ is irreducible, and it is the
support of every maximizer of the weighted law for all $N\ge N_0(\omega)$.
\item Both $\mathcal S_Q$ and $W_Q$ are invariant under $(\tau,v)\mapsto(\tau,v+c\ones)$ for
every real $c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(b) is Proposition~\ref{prop:controlled-score} and (c) is the shift rule. For (a), each score
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)$ is continuous in $(\tau,v)$, since a Perron root is continuous in the
entries, and there are finitely many faces. So a strict inequality between the least score and
all others persists near a point of $\mathcal S_Q$, which shows that $\mathcal S_Q$ is open and
$W_Q$ locally constant. By Proposition~\ref{prop:controlled-score} a reducible admissible face
never attains the least score. So the complement of $\mathcal S_Q$ lies in the union, over pairs
of distinct irreducible faces $F\ne H$, of the closed sets $Z_{FH}$ where
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)=\Phi_{\tau,v}(H)$. Each $Z_{FH}$ has empty interior. If $|F|\ne|H|$, the
difference $|F|-|H|+\tau(\lambda_F(v)-\lambda_H(v))$ vanishes for at most one $\tau$ at fixed
$v$. If $|F|=|H|$, pick $j\in F\setminus H$. Then $\lambda_H(v)$ does not depend on $v_j$, while
$\partial\lambda_F/\partial v_j=-\alpha^*_F(v)_j<0$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:variational}(b), so the difference vanishes for at most one $v_j$ when the
other coordinates are fixed. A finite union of closed sets with empty interior has empty
interior, which gives (a).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[controlled boundaries]\label{def:controlled}
Fix a start $\mu$ of full support. Two generators $Q$ and $Q'$ have the same controlled
boundaries if $W_Q=W_{Q'}$ on $\mathcal S_Q\cap\mathcal S_{Q'}$.
\end{definition}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:strict}(a) the set $\mathcal S_Q\cap\mathcal S_{Q'}$ is open and dense, and
its complement is closed and nowhere dense. So $Q$ and $Q'$ have the same controlled boundaries
exactly when their modal-support maps agree outside a closed nowhere dense subset of $\Omega$.
Since $W_Q$ and $W_{Q'}$ are locally constant there, the set where they differ is open. So
$Q$ and $Q'$ fail to have the same controlled boundaries exactly when there is a nonempty open
set of $\omega$ on which both maps have a strict winner and the winners differ. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:strict}(c) it is the same to ask for agreement only on the open quadrant
$(0,\infty)\times(-\infty,0)^d$, where every field is a killing rate, since every point of
$\Omega$ is moved into the quadrant by a shift. Ties are never observed and never used. The
definition involves no threshold. Thresholds enter in the next lemmas, each with what it
depends on.

\subsection{Suppression and exposure}\label{sec:exposure}

The next lemma uses the bound of Collatz and Wielandt. For a Metzler matrix $B$ and a positive
vector $u$, the Perron root of $B$ is at most $\max_i(Bu)_i/u_i$~\cite{bermanplemmons1994}.

\begin{lemma}[suppression]\label{lem:suppression-nr}
Let $Q$ be a generator, $F$ a nonempty face, $\mathcal K\subset\R^F$ compact and $\eta>0$.
There is $\bar v_0$, depending only on $Q$, $F$, $\mathcal K$ and $\eta$, such that for every
field $v$ with $v_F\in\mathcal K$ and $v_j\le-\bar v_0$ for all $j\notin F$, and every face
$G\not\subseteq F$, we have $\lambda_G(v)\ge\lambda_{G\cap F}(v)-\eta$ if $G\cap F\ne\emptyset$,
and $\lambda_G(v)\ge\bar v_0$ if $G\cap F=\emptyset$. Every larger value of $\bar v_0$ also
serves.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $P=G\cap F$, $S=G\setminus F$ and $B=Q_G+\operatorname{diag}v_G$, whose Perron root is
$-\lambda_G(v)$. If $P=\emptyset$, every row of $B$ has off-diagonal sum at most $q_s$ and
diagonal $-q_s+v_s\le-q_s-\bar v_0$, so its row sums are at most $-\bar v_0$, and the bound of
Collatz and Wielandt with $u=\ones$ gives $\lambda_G(v)\ge\bar v_0$.

Let $P\ne\emptyset$ and $\varrho_P(v)=-\lambda_P(v)$, and let $\mathbf J_P$ have ones off the
diagonal of $P$. For $\zeta>0$ the Perron root of the irreducible Metzler matrix
$Q_P+\operatorname{diag}v_P+\zeta\mathbf J_P$ is continuous in $(\zeta,v_P)$ and decreases to
$\varrho_P(v)$ as $\zeta\downarrow0$. By Dini's theorem some $\zeta$ makes it at most
$\varrho_P(v)+\eta/2$ for all $v_F\in\mathcal K$. Let $x$ be its Perron vector with $\max x=1$,
and $x_*>0$ the least entry of $x$ over $\mathcal K$. Test $B$ with $u=x$ on $P$ and
$u=\epsilon$ on $S$, where $\epsilon=\eta x_*/(2q_{\max})$. For $p\in P$, since
$\mathbf J_Px\ge0$,
\[
 (Bu)_p\le\bigl((Q_P+\operatorname{diag}v_P)x\bigr)_p+\epsilon q_{\max}
 \le(\varrho_P+\tfrac\eta2)x_p+\epsilon q_{\max}\le(\varrho_P+\eta)x_p .
\]
For $s\in S$,
\[
 (Bu)_s\le\sum_{p\in P}q_{sp}x_p+\epsilon\Bigl(\sum_{s'\in S\setminus\{s\}}q_{ss'}-q_s+v_s\Bigr)
 \le q_{\max}-\epsilon\bar v_0\le(\varrho_P+\eta)\epsilon
\]
once $\bar v_0\ge q_{\max}/\epsilon+\max_{\mathcal K}|\varrho_P|+\eta$. There are finitely many
$P$ and $S$, so one $\bar v_0$ serves for all of them, and so does every larger value, as in the
case $P=\emptyset$. The Perron root of $B$ is then at most
$\varrho_P(v)+\eta$, that is $\lambda_G(v)\ge\lambda_P(v)-\eta$.
\end{proof}

For an irreducible face $F$ with $k=|F|\ge2$ and an irreducible face $H\subsetneq F$,
singletons included, put
\[
 \rho_H(v)=\frac{k-|H|}{\lambda_H(v)-\lambda_F(v)},\qquad v\in\R^F .
\]
The denominator is positive by Lemma~\ref{lem:face-monotone}, and $\rho_H$ is real-analytic,
since the Perron roots of irreducible Metzler matrices are simple. Also
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)<\Phi_{\tau,v}(H)$ exactly when $\tau>\rho_H(v)$. So as $\tau$ decreases, the
first proper subface to tie with $F$ is a maximizer of $\rho_H(v)$.

\begin{lemma}[exposure by descent]\label{lem:exposure}
Let $Q$ be irreducible with a start of full support, and let $F$ be an irreducible face with
$|F|\ge2$. There is a closed nowhere dense set $\mathcal E\subset\R^F$ such that every
$v^0\in\R^F\setminus\mathcal E$ has a unique partner $G$, the irreducible face $G\subsetneq F$
that maximizes $\rho_H(v^0)$. Put $\tau_1=\rho_G$. There are an open neighbourhood
$\mathcal U$ of $v^0$ with compact closure and numbers $\delta>0$ and $\bar v_0$, depending on
$Q$, $F$ and $v^0$, such that on the open set
\[
 O=\bigl\{(\tau,v)\in\Omega:\ v_F\in\mathcal U,\ v_j<-\bar v_0\ \text{for } j\notin F,\
 |\tau-\tau_1(v_F)|<\delta\bigr\}
\]
the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item The points of $O$ with $\tau>\tau_1(v_F)$ lie in $\mathcal S_Q$ with $W_Q=F$, and those
with $\tau<\tau_1(v_F)$ lie in $\mathcal S_Q$ with $W_Q=G$.
\item On the hypersurface $\tau=\tau_1(v_F)$ we have
$\lambda_F(v)=\lambda_G(v)-(|F|-|G|)/\tau_1(v_F)$.
\end{enumerate}
For $F=V$ the condition on $v_j$ is empty.
\end{lemma}

So every irreducible face shares a boundary of strict regions with a proper subface, on an open
set of $(\tau,v)$, and the position of that boundary is a relation between the two exit rates.
Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} turns this into identifiability.

\begin{proof}
Let $k=|F|$.

\emph{Generic partners.} Let $H_1\ne H_2$ be irreducible proper subfaces of $F$ with some
$j\in H_1\setminus H_2$, and take $v=te_j$ with $t\to\infty$. Then $\lambda_{H_2}$ stays fixed,
$\lambda_F\le\lambda_{H_1}\le q_j-t$ since a Perron root is at least every diagonal entry, and
$\lambda_F\ge-t$ by the bound of Collatz and Wielandt with $u=\ones$. Hence $\rho_{H_2}\to0$,
while $\rho_{H_1}\ge(k-|H_1|)/q_j>0$, as $q_j>0$ on the irreducible face $F$. So
$\rho_{H_1}-\rho_{H_2}$ is a nonzero real-analytic function, with a closed nowhere dense zero
set. The set $\mathcal E$ where the maximum of $\rho_H$ over $H$ is attained twice is closed and
lies in the finite union of these zero sets.

\emph{The boundary.} Let $v^0\notin\mathcal E$ and let $G$ be the unique maximizer of
$\rho_H(v^0)$. Choose $\delta>0$ with $3\delta<\tau_1(v^0)$ and
$\rho_H(v^0)<\tau_1(v^0)-3\delta$ for every irreducible $H\subsetneq F$ other than $G$. By
continuity these hold with $2\delta$ in place of $3\delta$ on an open set around $v^0$, and we
take for $\mathcal U$ a ball around $v^0$ whose closure lies in that open set. Let $\tau_\pm$ be
the largest and the least value of $\tau$ on the closure of $O$. As $\tau_1>2\delta$ on the
compact closure of $\mathcal U$, we get $\tau_->\delta$. On the closure of $O$ we have
$\tau-\rho_H\ge\delta$ and $\lambda_H-\lambda_F$ is bounded below by a positive constant, so
$\Phi(H)-\Phi(F)=(\lambda_H-\lambda_F)(\tau-\rho_H)\ge\kappa$ for some $\kappa>0$. Also
$\Phi(F)-\Phi(G)=(\lambda_G-\lambda_F)(\tau_1(v_F)-\tau)$ is negative above the hypersurface and
positive below it, and a reducible face loses to one of its components
(Proposition~\ref{prop:controlled-score}). So among the faces inside $F$ the least score is
attained only by $F$ above and only by $G$ below.

It remains to remove the faces $G'\not\subseteq F$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:suppression-nr} with
the closure of $\mathcal U$ as $\mathcal K$ and $\eta=1/(2\tau_+)$, which gives a threshold
$\bar v_1$. If $P=G'\cap F$ is nonempty and $C$ is a component of $P$ with
$\lambda_C(v)=\lambda_P(v)$, then
$\Phi(G')\ge|P|+\tau\lambda_P(v)-\frac12\ge\Phi(C)+\frac12$, and $C$ is an irreducible face
inside $F$. Let $\bar v_0\ge\bar v_1$ be so large that $\tau_-\bar v_0$ exceeds by $\frac12$ the
largest score of $F$ on the closure of $O$, a bound that depends only on $Q$, $F$ and
$\mathcal U$. As $\bar v_0$ also serves in Lemma~\ref{lem:suppression-nr}, an empty $P$ gives
$\Phi(G')\ge\tau_-\bar v_0$. Then every $G'\not\subseteq F$ scores at
least $\frac12$ above the least score, which proves (i). The tie $\Phi(F)=\Phi(G)$ on the
hypersurface is (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[identifiability]\label{prop:identifiability}
Let $Q$ be irreducible, let the start have full support, and let $Q'$ be a generator with the
same controlled boundaries. Then $Q'$ is irreducible and $-Q'$ has the principal minors of
$-Q$. Equivalently, if $Q$ and $Q'$ are irreducible and $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have different principal
minors, then there is a nonempty open set of $(\tau,v)$ on which both have a strict winner and
the two winners differ.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two forms are equivalent by the remark after Definition~\ref{def:controlled}. We prove the
first.

\emph{Irreducibility.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:face-monotone}, at a fixed field $v$ the face $V$ is
the strict winner for $Q$ once $\tau$ is large, and by continuity on an open set of $\Omega$.
That open set meets the dense set $\mathcal S_{Q'}$, and at a common point $W_{Q'}=V$. A
reducible face is never a unique minimizer (Proposition~\ref{prop:controlled-score}), so $Q'$ is
irreducible.

\emph{Singletons.} Fix $i\ne1$, let $\bar\tau=1/(2\max(q_{\max},q'_{\max}))$, and let $I$ be a
compact interval in $(0,\bar\tau]$ and $J$ a compact interval that contains $q_i-q_1$ and
$q'_i-q'_1$ in its interior. Let $\bar v_0$ be the larger of the thresholds of
Lemma~\ref{lem:suppression-nr} for $Q$ and $Q'$, with $F=\{1,i\}$, the compact set
$[-1,1]\times([-1,1]+J)$ of $(v_1,v_i)$ and $\eta=1/(4\bar\tau)$, enlarged as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}. Consider the open set of points $(\tau,v)$ with $\tau$ in the
interior of $I$, $|v_1|<1$, $v_i-v_1$ in the interior of $J$ and $v_j<-\bar v_0$ for
$j\notin\{1,i\}$. The pair $\{1,i\}$, if it is irreducible, has an exit rate at least
$\min(q_1-v_1,q_i-v_i)-\sqrt{q_{1i}q_{i1}}$, the smaller eigenvalue of a $2\times2$ matrix with
real spectrum, and $\sqrt{q_{1i}q_{i1}}\le q_{\max}$. So it scores at least $\frac12$ above the
better singleton, for both generators, and Lemma~\ref{lem:suppression-nr} removes the faces
that leave $\{1,i\}$. The singleton $\{i\}$ beats $\{1\}$ exactly when $v_i-v_1>q_i-q_1$. So the
strict winners of $Q$ are $\{1\}$ and $\{i\}$ on the two sides of the hyperplane
$v_i-v_1=q_i-q_1$, and those of $Q'$ on the two sides of $v_i-v_1=q'_i-q'_1$. If the two
hyperplanes differed, the open slab between them would carry different strict winners. So
$q'_i-q'_1=q_i-q_1$. Put $c=q'_1-q_1$. Then $\lambda'_{\{i\}}=\lambda_{\{i\}}+c$ for every $i$.

\emph{The induction.} We show by induction on $|F|$ that every irreducible face $F$ of $Q$ is
irreducible for $Q'$ and that $\lambda'_F=\lambda_F+c$ on $\R^F$. Singletons are the base. Let
$|F|\ge2$, choose $v^0\notin\mathcal E$ with partner $G$, and take $O$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}. The set $O\cap\mathcal S_{Q'}$ is dense in $O$, and there $W_{Q'}=W_Q$.
So $F$ is the unique minimizer of the scores $\Phi'$ of $Q'$ at points of a dense subset of the
part of $O$ above the hypersurface, and $G$ at points of a dense subset of the part below. In
particular $F$ and $G$ are irreducible for $Q'$. By continuity of the scores,
$\Phi'(F)\le\Phi'(G)$ on the closure of the upper part and $\Phi'(F)\ge\Phi'(G)$ on the closure
of the lower part, so $\Phi'(F)=\Phi'(G)$ on the hypersurface. With (ii) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure} and the induction hypothesis for $G$,
\[
 \lambda'_F(v)=\lambda'_G(v)-\frac{|F|-|G|}{\tau_1(v_F)}=\lambda_G(v)+c-\frac{|F|-|G|}{\tau_1(v_F)}
 =\lambda_F(v)+c\qquad(v_F\in\mathcal U).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:perron-branch} extends this to all of $\R^F$. For $F=V$ both generators have
$\lambda_V(0)=\lambda'_V(0)=0$, since $\ones$ is a positive null vector of $Q$ and of $Q'$. So
$c=0$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:perron-branch} with $F=V$ and $c=0$ gives equal principal minors.
\end{proof}

The induction is an algorithm. For each face choose a field at random, decrease $\tau$ from a
large value, and read the first change of label and its position. The proof uses the strict
regions only on an open set where $V$ wins, on the open sets of the singleton step and on the
sets $O$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}. Their thresholds depend on $Q$ and $Q'$. So no experiment
is fixed in advance, unlike the reversible design of Section~\ref{sec:reversible}, and the
statement is one of identifiability.

\begin{theorem}[the modal supports and the minors]\label{thm:modal}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators and let the start have full support. They have the
same controlled boundaries if and only if $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same principal minors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One direction is Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}. For the other, suppose the minors
agree. By Theorem~\ref{thm:dv}, $Q$ and $Q'$ have the same irreducible faces and the same
$\lambda_F$ on each, so the scores $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ of the irreducible faces agree. With a start of
full support every irreducible face is admissible, and every reducible admissible face scores
above one of its components (Proposition~\ref{prop:controlled-score}). So for both generators a
point lies in the strict set exactly when the least score over the irreducible faces is attained
once, and the strict winner is that face. Hence $\mathcal S_Q=\mathcal S_{Q'}$ and
$W_Q=W_{Q'}$.
\end{proof}
