Dear Editors,

Please consider "What the occupation law of a finite Markov chain determines: principal minors, modal supports and cycle trees" for Stochastic Processes and their Applications. The preprint is on arXiv as [arXiv number to be added at submission].

The main result is that the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the occupation measure of an irreducible finite Markov chain determines exactly the principal minors of the negative generator (Theorem A). This answers the question raised by the 3-cycle example of Kim and Landim (Stochastic Process. Appl. 189, 2025). The generators with given minors form a finite set. It consists of the generator and its time reversal when there is no rank-one cut, and from the stationary start no statistic of the counts separates the two.

We then turn to a chain that makes about τ log N switches in N steps. The face of the simplex that carries its most likely vector of occupation counts is described in my paper "Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains" (arXiv:2610.09258). The support of this most likely vector under killing at known rates retains the same information as the functional (Theorem B). The proof is a descent over the faces that exposes the Perron root of every face. For a reversible chain a design fixed in advance reads the generator from a minimal number of boundaries, stably, and uniformly over a compact class at a finite horizon (Theorem C). At low temperature calibrated rewards read the whole Freidlin and Wentzell cycle tree from the modal supports, with no reversibility and no nondegeneracy assumption (Theorem D). This settles whether modal data determine the cycles at all, a question raised by the open windows on which a noncycle is the modal winner.

The paper fits the journal's scope on characterization, structural properties and limit theorems of Markov processes. It uses large deviations of the occupation measure, Perron and Frobenius theory and the cycle theory of Catoni. It connects them with recent work on the principal-minor map by Al Ahmadieh and by Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and Raj, which it credits in detail.

The manuscript has 40 pages in the journal's preprint format, within the usual 30 to 40.

Every stated result is proved in the article. The results of arXiv:2610.09258 that are used are stated in Section 2 with pointers. No proof rests on a computation, and Section 7 lists what the scripts in the public repository check.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
