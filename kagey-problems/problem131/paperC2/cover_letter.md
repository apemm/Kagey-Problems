Dear Editors,

Please consider "What the occupation law of a finite Markov chain determines: principal minors, modal supports and cycle trees" for Stochastic Processes and their Applications. The preprint is on arXiv as [arXiv number to be added at submission].

A finite Markov chain whose switching probability is of order log N / N makes about τ log N switches in N steps. A companion paper, "Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains" (in preparation, to be posted on arXiv before this submission), describes which face of the simplex carries the most likely vector of occupation counts. This paper asks the inverse question. Which coarse functionals of the occupation law determine the generator?

We prove that the Donsker and Varadhan functional determines exactly the principal minors of the negative generator. This answers the question raised by the three-cycle example of Kim and Landim (Stochastic Process. Appl. 189, 2025). The generators with given minors form a finite set, which is the generator and its time reversal when there is no rank-one cut, and from the stationary start no statistic of the counts separates the two. The support of the most likely composition under killing at known rates retains the same information, through a descent over the faces that exposes the Perron root of every face. For a reversible chain a design fixed in advance reads the generator from a minimal number of boundaries, stably, and uniformly over a compact class at a finite horizon. At low temperature calibrated rewards read the whole Freidlin and Wentzell cycle tree from the modal supports, with no reversibility and no nondegeneracy assumption. This settles whether modal data determine the cycles at all, a question raised by the open windows on which a noncycle is the modal winner.

The paper fits the journal's scope on characterization, structural properties and limit theorems of Markov processes. It uses large deviations of the occupation measure, Perron and Frobenius theory and the cycle theory of Catoni, and it connects them with recent work on the principal-minor map by Al Ahmadieh and by Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and Raj, which it credits in detail.

The manuscript has 40 pages in the journal's preprint format, within the usual 30 to 40.

Every stated result is proved in the article. The results of the companion paper that are used are stated in Section 2 with pointers. No proof rests on a computation, and Section 7 lists what the scripts in the public repository check.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
