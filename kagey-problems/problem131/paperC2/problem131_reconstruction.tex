\begin{abstract}
A Markov chain on $d$ states that switches about $\tau\log N$ times in $N$
steps has a most likely vector of occupation counts. We ask what such
data determine about the generator $Q$. The Donsker and Varadhan functional
of the occupation measure determines exactly the principal minors of $-Q$,
answering the question raised by the three-cycle example of Kim and
Landim (Stochastic Process.\ Appl.\ 189 (2025) 104733). The generators with these minors form a finite set, which is
$Q$ and its time reversal when $-Q$ has no rank-one cut and an orbit of
cut-transposes in general. From the stationary start no statistic of the
counts separates $Q$ from its reversal. The support of the most likely
composition under killing at known rates retains the same information. For a
reversible chain on a known graph with $m$ edges a design fixed in advance
reads $m+d-1$ boundary coordinates, which is minimal, and recovers $Q$
stably, with a finite-horizon correction uniform over a compact class. At low
temperature, with Arrhenius rates, the unperturbed modal path misses cycles
and killing the complement of a face masks its depth. A reward of known size
on the face does neither. The largest reward exponent at which a cycle still
wins is its depth, the depth of every face is the largest such threshold over
its subfaces, and a set is a cycle exactly when every proper subset is
strictly shallower. So the modal supports determine the Freidlin and Wentzell
cycle tree, without reversibility or nondegeneracy.
\end{abstract}
