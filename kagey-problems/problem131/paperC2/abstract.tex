What does the occupation measure of a finite Markov chain determine about its generator? For an
irreducible generator $Q$ on $d$ states, the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the occupation
measure determines exactly the principal minors of $-Q$. Kim and Landim (Stochastic Process.\
Appl.\ 189 (2025) 104733) showed that it determines a reversible generator, and their 3-cycle shows
that it does not determine a nonreversible one. By known matrix theory the generators with these
minors form a finite set. It is $Q$ and its time reversal when $-Q$ has no rank-one cut, and in
general it comes from an orbit of cut-transposes. For a chain that switches about $\tau\log N$
times in $N$ steps, the first-order support of the most likely vector of counts under killing at
known rates determines exactly the same minors. For an Arrhenius family with all rates positive,
with long runs first and low temperature second, the functionals determine the depth of every
face and the Freidlin and Wentzell cycle tree, without reversibility or nondegeneracy. A set is a
cycle exactly when every proper subset is strictly shallower. A design fixed in advance reads the
same tree. Under a field of size $c\,e^{-\gamma/\eta}$ on a face, the largest exponent $\gamma$
at which the face still carries the mode is at most its depth, with equality for cycles, and the
depth of a face is the largest such threshold over its subfaces.\ifarxivbuild\ A final section
reads a reversible generator on a known graph from a minimal number of boundaries of a design
fixed in advance, stably and at a finite horizon.\fi
