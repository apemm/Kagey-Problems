\begin{abstract}
We study a persistent random walk on the square lattice, which is a lattice
model of a run-and-tumble particle with four orientations. At each step the
walk keeps its direction, turns left or right, or reverses, and turns and
reversals are rare, with fixed ratios between their probabilities. The far corner $(N,0)$, the lattice version of the ballistic front, is
reached only by the path that starts east and never switches. The origin is
reached by many paths that switch. For every even $N\ge4$ the corner and the
origin are equally likely at exactly one switching rate. At this crossing the
expected number of switches is asymptotically a constant multiple of $\log N$,
and we find the constant. When the walk reverses more often than it turns, the crossing agrees
with that of the persistent walk on the line with $N/2$ steps, up to a negative
power of $N$. When it turns more often, the crossing is set by a local limit
theorem in two dimensions, and the expected number of switches at the
crossing is about twice the logarithm of $N$ instead of the logarithm itself.
In every regime the relative correction to the leading term is the same as on
the line. The sequence of directions is a one-dimensional Ashkin--Teller
chain, in which the corner is full order and the origin is zero
magnetization. On one curve of switching probabilities this chain is two
decoupled Ising chains, the planar walk is exactly two independent copies of
the walk on the line, and the two crossings agree at every $N$. With turns only they agree
up to a relative error of at most order $(\log N)/N$.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability statement}
\sloppy
The code, data and Lean files that support this paper are openly available
at \url{https://github.com/apemm/Kagey-Problems}, in the folder
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131} at the release
\nolinkurl{paperE-arxiv-v1}. The scripts are in the subfolder
\nolinkurl{paperE}. They need numpy, scipy, mpmath and sympy, and they import
the line walk from the subfolder \nolinkurl{paperC}, so both subfolders are
needed. To rerun the
checks, run \texttt{python -B verify\_all.py -{}-paper e} in the folder
\nolinkurl{problem131}. The largest cases, such as the planar crossings for
$N\ge1280$, run only with the option \texttt{-{}-full}.
Section~\ref{sec:verify} summarizes the checks. The Lean~4 proofs are
in the subfolder \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC}, whose file \nolinkurl{MAP.md} matches
each statement to its Lean theorems. The file \nolinkurl{SUPPLEMENT.md} lists
each check, the two methods behind it and the predictions that we wrote down
before most of the computations.\par
