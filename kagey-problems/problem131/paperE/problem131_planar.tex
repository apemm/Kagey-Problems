\begin{abstract}
We study a persistent random walk on the square lattice that, at each step,
keeps its direction, turns left or right, or reverses, with given
probabilities. After $N$ steps the far corner $(N,0)$ is reached only by the
path that starts east and never switches. For every even $N\ge4$ this corner and the origin are equally
likely at exactly one switching rate, which we find to first order. When the
walk reverses more often than it turns, this crossing agrees with the crossing
of the persistent walk on the line with $N/2$ steps, up to a negative power of
$N$. When it turns more often, the crossing comes from a local limit theorem in
two dimensions. On one curve of rates the planar walk is exactly two
independent copies of the walk on the line, and with turns only its crossing is
that of the walk on the line up to a small error.
\end{abstract}
