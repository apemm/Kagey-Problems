\begin{abstract}
We study a persistent random walk on the square lattice, a lattice model of a
run-and-tumble particle with four orientations. At each step it keeps its
direction, turns left or right, or reverses. Turns and reversals are rare,
with fixed ratios between their probabilities. The far corner is reached
only by the path that starts east and never switches, while the origin is
reached by many paths that switch and return. For every even length of at
least four, these two sites are equally likely at exactly one switching
rate. We find its first two orders as the length grows. When reversals
dominate, the crossing agrees with that of the line walk with half as many
steps, up to a negative power of the length. When turns dominate, a
two-dimensional local limit theorem sets the crossing. The expected number
of switches there is about twice the logarithm of the length, against one
logarithm on the line, with the same relative logarithmic correction.
The direction sequence is also a one-dimensional Ashkin--Teller chain.
The corner is full order and the origin is zero magnetization. On the curve
where this chain splits into two independent Ising chains, the planar and
line crossings agree exactly at every length. With turns only they agree
up to a relative error of order the logarithm of the length divided by the
length.
\end{abstract}

$N\ge1280$, run only with the option \texttt{-{}-full}.
Section~\ref{sec:verify} summarizes the checks. The Lean~4 proofs are
in the subfolder \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC}, whose file \nolinkurl{MAP.md} matches
each statement to its Lean theorems. The file \nolinkurl{SUPPLEMENT.md} lists
each check, the two methods behind it and the predictions that we wrote down
before most of the computations.\par
