\section{The two-state walk and the complete graph}\label{sec:complete}

This section applies the second-order theory to the two-state walk and to the complete graph,
where every face ties at first order and a few exact facts resolve the tie.

\subsection{The two-state walk}\label{sec:papersAB}

We use the polynomials $S_{a,b}$ and $S_N$, the root $u_N$, the crossing $p_N$,
$z_N=N(1-p_N)$ and $c_0=\frac12\log(\pi/8)$ of Section~\ref{sec:notation}. The
first result shows that, at every $N$, the comparison of a symmetric pair with
one of its states is exactly the two-state walk.

\begin{proposition}[an exact pair identity]\label{prop:pair}
Let $i\ne j$ with $q_{ij}=q_{ji}=a$, $q_i=q_j=q$ and $\mu_i=\mu_j>0$, and let
$F=\{i,j\}$. For every $N\ge2$, $1\le m\le N-1$ and $h>0$ such that $I+hQ$ is
stochastic and $qh<1$,
\[
\frac{P_N(me_i+(N-m)e_j)}{P_N(Ne_i)}=S_{m,N-m}(u),\qquad u=\frac{ah}{1-qh}.
\]
So the balanced compositions, with $m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ or
$m=\lceil N/2\rceil$, are the most likely ones with support $F$,
$M_F=M_{\{i\}}S_N(u)$, and $M_F>M_{\{i\}}$ exactly when $u>u_N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We group the paths by their number of switches.
A path with this composition is a word in $i,j$ with $m$ letters $i$. Each stay has probability
$1-qh$ and each switch $ah$, so a word with $s$ switches has probability
$\mu_i(1-qh)^{N-1-s}(ah)^s=\mu_i(1-qh)^{N-1}u^s$, while $P_N(Ne_i)=\mu_i(1-qh)^{N-1}$. Sum over
these words and divide. This gives $\sum_wu^{s(w)}=S_{m,N-m}(u)$, which is the identity.
By~\cite[Proposition~4.1]{paperA} the polynomials $S_{m,N-m}$ grow coefficientwise as $m$ moves toward the
middle, and $S_{m,N-m}=S_{N-m,m}$. So the balanced $m$ give the largest $S_{m,N-m}(u)$. Finally
$S_N$ increases strictly and equals $1$ only at $u_N$ (Section~\ref{sec:notation}).
\end{proof}

Lean checks the identity for a chain with two states. For $d\ge3$ the paths with
this composition never leave $F$, so the same computation applies, but this case
is not in Lean.

\begin{corollary}[the two-state walk]\label{cor:paperA}
Let $d=2$, $q_{12}=q_{21}=1$ and $\mu=(\frac12,\frac12)$, the walk of~\cite{paperA} with
$T=N(1-p)$. Then $K_{\{1\}}=K_{\{2\}}=\frac12$ and $K_{\{1,2\}}=\sqrt{2/\pi}$, so
$b_{FG}=c_0$ for $F=\{1,2\}$ and $G=\{1\}$. For every $N\ge2$, $M_{\{1,2\}}=M_{\{1\}}$ holds
exactly at $T=z_N$, and $z_N+\frac12\log z_N=L+c_0+O(1/L)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:symface} with $k=2$, $\tree(q_F)=1$ and $\mu(F)=1$ gives $K_{\{1,2\}}$, and
then $b_{FG}=-\log(K_{\{1,2\}}/K_{\{1\}})=c_0$. Proposition~\ref{prop:pair} with $a=q=1$ gives
$M_{\{1,2\}}=M_{\{1\}}S_N(h/(1-h))$, and $h/(1-h)=u_N$ exactly when $p=p_N$. So for every $N$
the only solution of $M_{\{1,2\}}=M_{\{1\}}$ is $T=z_N$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}(i) it lies in
$[L/2,2L]\subset[1,N^{1/3}]$ for large $N$. Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}(ii) with
$\Delta=\delta=1$ gives the equation.
\end{proof}

The persistent-walk paper~\cite{paperA} proves the next term $1/(8z_N)$ of this equation and
bounds its remainder at every $N$.

\subsection{The complete graph}\label{sec:completegraph}

From now on in this section $Q=J-dI$. The chain switches to each other state with
probability $h$ (the $r$ of~\cite{paperB}) and keeps its state with probability
$p=1-(d-1)h$. The
following symbols of~\cite{paperB} are local to this section and to
Appendix~\ref{app:complete}. Put
\[
\sigma=p-h,\qquad u=\frac hp,\qquad v=\frac h\sigma=\frac u{1-u},\qquad
V_N=\frac{p^{N-1}}d .
\]
So $u$ is the odds of a switch to one given state against no switch, and $V_N$ is the
probability of a vertex $Ne_i$ for the uniform start, that is, $M_{\{i\}}$. For a composition
$n$ we write $S_n(u)$ for the sum of $u^j$ over the words with the counts $n$, where $j$ is the
number of switches of the word, and $W_n(j)$ for the number of these words with $j$ switches. For a
composition with some zero coordinates, $S_n$ is the polynomial of its positive coordinates,
which does not depend on $d$. By~\eqref{eq:runform}, the uniform start has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SnVN}
P_N(n)=V_NS_n(u)\qquad(0<p\le1),
\end{equation}
and so does every start that is constant on $\supp n$, with $V_N$ replaced by
$\mu_ap^{N-1}$ for $a\in\supp n$. A composition is \emph{balanced} if its positive coordinates
differ by at most $1$. For the uniform start all faces of $k$ states have the same face maximum
$M_F$, and all balanced compositions with $k$ positive coordinates have the same probability. For
$p\ge1/d$ this probability is $M_F$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} below. (\BVersion~\cite{paperB}
writes $C_{N,k}$ for it.) Put
\[
a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-\frac{k+\frac12}{k-1}\log k,\qquad
D_k=e^{-(k-1)a_k}=\frac{k^{k+1/2}}{(4\pi)^{(k-1)/2}}\qquad(k\ge2),
\]
and $D_1=1$. Numerically $a_2=-0.4674$, $a_3=-0.6571$ and $a_4=-0.8139$.

\begin{corollary}[the complete graph]\label{cor:paperB}
A face $F$ of $k$ states has $\lambda_F=d-k$ and $\alpha^*_F$ uniform, and if $\mu(F)>0$ then
$K_F=\frac{\mu(F)}kD_k$. So $\Phi_1(F)=d-1$ for every face, and all faces tie at $\tau=1$. With
the uniform start, the crossing of a face of $k\ge2$ states with a vertex has
$\Delta=\delta=k-1$ and $b_{FG}=a_k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each $Q_F$ is symmetric with row sums $-(d-k)$, and the complete graph on $F$ has $k^{k-2}$
spanning trees by Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-cayley}. So Corollary~\ref{cor:symface} gives
$\lambda_F$, $\alpha^*_F$ and
\[
K_F=\mu(F)k^{(k+1)/2}k^{(k-2)/2}(4\pi)^{-(k-1)/2}=\frac{\mu(F)}kD_k.
\]
A vertex has $K=\frac1d$ and $\lambda=d-1$ for the uniform start, so
$b_{FG}=-\frac1{k-1}\log(dK_F)=-\frac1{k-1}\log D_k=a_k$. The arithmetic that gives $b_{FG}$
from $K_F$ here and in Corollary~\ref{cor:paperA} is checked in Lean.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[first-order winners on other graphs]\label{rem:outcomes}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull} reads the first-order winners of any chain off the exit rates of its
admissible faces. Two cases have short proofs. On the complete graph a face of $k$ states has
$\Phi_\tau(F)=\tau(d-k)+k-1=(d-1)\tau+(k-1)(1-\tau)$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB}. So the
vertices win for $\tau<1$, the whole set $[d]$ wins for $\tau>1$, and all faces tie at $\tau=1$.
Now let $Q$ be symmetric and fix a state $x$. Rayleigh--Ritz with the unit vector $f$ that equals
$(d-1)^{-1/2}$ off $x$ and $0$ at $x$ gives $\lambda_{[d]\setminus\{x\}}\le q_x/(d-1)$, with equality
exactly when $f$ is an eigenvector of $-Q$ restricted to $[d]\setminus\{x\}$. That matrix maps
$f$ to the vector with entries $q_{yx}(d-1)^{-1/2}$, so equality holds exactly when
$q_{yx}=q_x/(d-1)$ for all $y\ne x$. Take unit rates on a connected graph and a start of full
support, and let $x$ have the least degree $\delta$. The vertices of
degree $\delta$ and the face $[d]$ tie at $\tau_0=(d-1)/\delta$. The restricted matrix is block
diagonal, so a component $C$ of $[d]\setminus\{x\}$ of least exit rate has
$\lambda_C=\lambda_{[d]\setminus\{x\}}$ and
$\Phi_{\tau_0}(C)\le\tau_0\lambda_{[d]\setminus\{x\}}+d-2\le d-1$. Equality needs
$q_{yx}=\delta/(d-1)$ for every $y\ne x$, which for unit rates means that the graph is complete.
So the winners go from the vertices straight to $[d]$ exactly when the graph is complete. On
every other connected graph a face that avoids $x$ beats both at $\tau_0$, and the mode can
move to a community that does not contain its first vertex.
\end{remark}

By Example~\ref{ex:complete-face} at the center, $D_k=dK_F=k/\sqrt{\det(2\pi\Sigma_k)}$ with
$\Sigma_k=\Sigma_{\ones/k}=(2/k^2)(I-J/k)$ in the coordinates $\alpha'$. So $D_k$ is the mass $k$
of a face, in units of a vertex, over the Gaussian volume of the fluctuations of its
fractions. \BVersion~\cite[Proposition~3.7]{paperB} also obtains it by summing the exact law over
the whole face. Example~\ref{ex:complete-face},
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} and Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete} give every composition whose fractions are
bounded away from the boundary of its face. Let $\alpha=n/N$ have $k$ positive coordinates,
all at least $\varepsilon$, and put $A=\sum_i\sqrt{\alpha_i}$. Uniformly for such compositions and for
$1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior}
\begin{gathered}
\frac{P_N(n)}{V_N}=D(\alpha)\,\frac{T^{(k-1)/2}e^{(A^2-1)T}}{N^{k-1}}
\Bigl(1+O_\varepsilon\Bigl(\frac1T+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr)\Bigr),\\
D(\alpha)=\frac{A^{k/2+1}}{(4\pi)^{(k-1)/2}(\prod_i\alpha_i)^{3/4}},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
for the uniform start. The relation between $D(\alpha)$ and $C_F(\alpha)$ is given in
Example~\ref{ex:complete-face}, and $V_N=\frac1de^{-(d-1)T}(1+O(T^2/N))$.
This is~\cite[Theorem~3.3]{paperB}, and at the center
$D(\ones/k)=D_k$. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $A^2\le k$, with equality only at the
center.

\begin{theorem}[balanced crossings]\label{thm:simplex-crossing}
Let $\mu$ be uniform and let $F$ be a face of $2\le k\le d$ states.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For every $N\ge k$ there is exactly one $p=p_{N,k}\in(0,1)$ with $M_F=V_N$. The face
maximum $M_F$ is larger than $V_N$ below $p_{N,k}$ and smaller above it, and so is the
probability of a balanced composition of $F$. The crossing $p_{N,k}$ is strictly increasing in $N$,
$p_{k,k}=1/(1+(d-1)(k!)^{-1/(k-1)})$, and every crossing has $u\le(k!)^{-1/(k-1)}<1$, so
$p_{N,k}>1/d$.
\item For large $N$, uniformly for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
\log\frac{M_F}{V_N}=(k-1)\Bigl[T-L+\frac12\log T-a_k\Bigr]
+O\Bigl(\frac1T+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr).
\]
With $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$, the ratio $M_F/V_N$ tends to $e^{(k-1)(t-a_k)}$, uniformly for
$t$ in compact sets.
\item The value $T_{N,k}=N(1-p_{N,k})/(d-1)$ of $T$ at the crossing satisfies
\[
T_{N,k}+\frac12\log T_{N,k}=L+a_k+O(1/L),
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex-root-expansion}
T_{N,k}=L-\frac12\log L+a_k+\frac{\log L}{4L}+O\Bigl(\frac1L\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\item $a_2>a_3>a_4>\cdots$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

For $d=2$, $T_{N,2}$ is the crossing $z_N$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:paperA}, and $a_2=c_0$.
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:complete}. The exact part uses that $S_n$ has nonnegative
coefficients and the first nonzero coefficient $W_n(k-1)=k!$, from the words that use each
state in a single run. The next coefficient $W_n(k)=k!(k-1)(N-k)/2$ grows with $N$, which moves
the root. The asymptotic part is Theorems~\ref{thm:facemax} and~\ref{thm:crossing}. The
equation in (iii) has the next term $1/(8T_{N,k})$ on its right side. The persistent-walk
paper~\cite{paperA} proves it for $k=2$, and \Bversion~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{paperB} proves it
for every $k\ge2$ through a Bessel majorant. We do not reprove that term here.

A count of run lengths alone does not explain the next theorem. For fixed numbers of runs the
choices of run lengths are most numerous at the balanced split when all states have the same
number of runs, but not in general. If one state has a single run and another has several, the
count grows when visits move to the second state. What works is to sum over the numbers of runs
first. The law then becomes an integral of a product of one polynomial per state, and this
polynomial is log-concave in the count of its state.

\begin{theorem}[exact balancing]\label{thm:simplex-balance}
Let $1/d\le p<1$, and let $\mu$ be constant and positive on a face $F$. Moving one unit from a
coordinate $b$ of a composition with support $F$ to another coordinate $a$, where $n_b\ge n_a+2$ and
$n_a\ge1$, strictly increases the probability of the composition. So the most likely compositions with support
$F$ are exactly the balanced ones, and they all have the same probability.
\end{theorem}

For an integer $n\ge1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refresh-polynomial}
B_n(w)=\sum_{m=1}^n\binom{n-1}{m-1}\frac{w^m}{m!}
=\frac wn L^{(1)}_{n-1}(-w),
\end{equation}
where $L^{(1)}_{n-1}$ is the Laguerre polynomial~\cite[Eq.~18.5.12]{dlmf}.
The proof in Appendix~\ref{app:complete} rests on the log-concavity of $B_n(w)$ in $n$ for
$w>0$. The condition $p\ge1/d$ holds for $T\le N/d$, and so at every crossing
by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(i).

\begin{corollary}[exactly one mode change]\label{cor:simplex-modes}
Let $\mu$ be uniform, and fix $d\ge2$ and $C>1$. There is $N_0$ such that for $N\ge N_0$ and
every $p$ with $0<T\le CL$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item If $p>p_{N,d}$, the global modes are exactly the $d$ vertices.
\item If $p<p_{N,d}$, they are exactly the balanced compositions with $d$ positive
coordinates.
\item If $p=p_{N,d}$, they are these two classes and nothing else.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover $p_{N,2}<p_{N,3}<\cdots<p_{N,d}$ and
$p_{N,d}-p_{N,k}=(d-1)(a_k-a_d+o(1))/N$. So all the crossings of balanced compositions with the
vertices lie in a $p$-interval of length $(d-1)(a_2-a_d+o(1))/N$, and as $p$ decreases the
balanced compositions with $d$ positive coordinates cross first.
\end{corollary}

On the triangle the middle composition
$(\frac N3,\frac N3,\frac N3)$ overtakes the vertices while the middles of the edges are still
below them by the factor $e^{-(a_2-a_3)}=0.827$. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:complete}.
In the tie window at $\tau^*=1$ this is Theorem~\ref{thm:window}, whose envelope $E$ has a
single kink, at $t=a_d$.

\begin{remark}[an intermediate face can be a mode at finite $N$]\label{rem:simplex-edge-mode}
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} does not hold for every $N$. Take $d=3$, $N=4$
and $p=10/17$, so $u=7/20$. For an edge center and a full center, counting the words gives
\[
\frac{P_4(2,2,0)}{V_4}=2u+2u^2+2u^3=\frac{4123}{4000}>1,
\qquad
\frac{P_4(2,1,1)}{V_4}=6u^2+6u^3=\frac{3969}{4000}<1.
\]
Here $p>1/3$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} every global mode is a vertex or a
balanced composition, and the three edge centers are the only global modes. Also
$0<T=14/17<\log4$, so this $p$ lies in the range of Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} for
every $C>1$. Hence $N_0>4$ when $d=3$. These values are checked in Lean.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A start}\label{sec:start}

By Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB} the start enters $K_F$ only through $\mu(F)$. It changes the
constants by an amount of order $1$, the order at which the faces are separated, so a start can
decide which face wins.

\begin{corollary}[crossings with a start]\label{cor:start-crossings}
Let $F$ be a face of $k\ge2$ states with $\mu(F)>0$, and let $i$ be a state with $\mu_i>0$. Put
$a_k(\mu)=a_k+\frac1{k-1}\log\frac{k\mu_i}{\mu(F)}$. For large $N$ the equation
$M_F=M_{\{i\}}$ has a root in $[1,N^{1/3}]$, and every such root satisfies
$T+\frac12\log T=L+a_k(\mu)+O(1/L)$. With $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$,
$\log(M_F/M_{\{i\}})=(k-1)(t-a_k(\mu))+o(1)$, uniformly for $t$ in compact sets.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB}, $\log(K_F/K_{\{i\}})=\log D_k+\log\frac{\mu(F)}{k\mu_i}=-(k-1)a_k(\mu)$,
and $\Delta=\delta=k-1$. So Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} gives the root and its equation. For the
last claim, Theorem~\ref{thm:window}(i) at $\tau^*=1$ gives
$\log(M_F/M_{\{i\}})=\ell_F(t)-\ell_{\{i\}}(t)+O(\log L/L)$, and
$\ell_F(t)-\ell_{\{i\}}(t)=\log(K_F/\mu_i)+(k-1)t$.
\end{proof}

So a start changes only the constant of each crossing, as in~\cite[Theorem~4.4]{paperB}.

\begin{example}[the start decides which face wins]\label{ex:K3-start}
Let $d=3$ and $\mu=(\frac12,\frac12,0)$, and put
$t_{\rm ef}=\log\frac32+2a_3-a_2=\frac12\log\frac{32\pi}{243}$. Fix $t$, put
$T=L-\frac12\log L+t$ and let $N$ be large. If $t<a_2$, the global modes are $(N,0,0)$ and
$(0,N,0)$. If $a_2<t<t_{\rm ef}$, they are exactly the balanced compositions of the edge
$\{1,2\}$. If $t>t_{\rm ef}$, every global mode has three positive coordinates. The window
$(a_2,t_{\rm ef})=(-0.4673558,-0.4412978)$ has width
$\log\frac{16}{9\sqrt3}=0.0260580$, while with the uniform start no proper face
wins (Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}).
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(b) and (c) the admissible faces are the faces that meet
$\{1,2\}$, and they satisfy (H). By Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB} they all tie at $\tau^*=1$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} applies with $m^*=2$. For a face $F$ of $k$ states its line is
$\ell_F(t)=\log K_F-(3-k)t$, and $\ell_F(t)+2t$ equals $\log\mu_i$ for $F=\{i\}$ and
$\log(\mu(F)/k)+(k-1)(t-a_k)$ for $k\ge2$. So $\ell_F+2t$ is $g_1=\log\frac12$ for the
vertices $1$ and $2$, $g_2=\log\frac12+t-a_2$ for the edge $\{1,2\}$, $g_2-\log2$ for the
other two edges and $g_3=\log\frac13+2(t-a_3)$ for the full face. Hence $E+2t$ is the upper
envelope of $g_1$, $g_2$ and $g_3$. Here $g_2>g_1$ exactly when $t>a_2$,
$g_2>g_3$ exactly when $t<t_{\rm ef}$, and $g_3>g_1$ exactly when
$t>t^*=a_3+\frac12\log\frac32$. By the definition of $a_k$,
$t_{\rm ef}-a_2=\log\frac32+2(a_3-a_2)=4\log2-\frac52\log3=\log\frac{16}{9\sqrt3}$, which is
positive since $16^2=256>243=(9\sqrt3)^2$. Also $t^*-a_2=\frac12(t_{\rm ef}-a_2)$, so
$a_2<t^*<t_{\rm ef}$. Hence the kinks of $E$ are $a_2$ and $t_{\rm ef}$, and $E$ is the line of
the two vertices for $t<a_2$, of the edge $\{1,2\}$ for $a_2<t<t_{\rm ef}$, and of the full face
for $t>t_{\rm ef}$. For fixed $t$ that is not a kink, the consequence in
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} puts every global mode on a face with that line. The vertices $Ne_1$
and $Ne_2$ have the same probability $\frac12p^{N-1}$. On the edge, $\mu$ is constant and
$p>1/3$ for large $N$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} the most likely compositions
with support $\{1,2\}$ are exactly the balanced ones, and they all have the same probability.
\end{proof}

The comparison of the lines and the value of the width are checked in Lean.

\subsection{A weak field}\label{sec:complete-field}

Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield} applies to the complete graph with the uniform start. There
$\alpha^*_F$ is uniform, so the field shifts the line of a face $S$ by the average
$\bar\xi_S=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\xi_i$ of the field over $S$. In the Potts chain the
field $\xi_i/N$ acts at each site in state $i$. The scores $\ell_S(t,\xi)$ below are the lines
$\ell^\xi_S$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}(c) at $\tau^*=1$, measured from the line
$\ell_{\{1\}}(t)=-\log d-(d-1)t$ of a vertex without field, that is
$\ell_S(t,\xi)=\ell^\xi_S(t)-\ell_{\{1\}}(t)$. The orientation and the slope convention are those
of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}, with $\lambda_S=d-|S|$ and $\log K_S=\log D_{|S|}-\log d$.

\begin{corollary}[weak-field modal phase diagram]\label{cor:weakfield-phases}
Let $\mu$ be uniform, put $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$ and define the face scores
\[
 \ell_S(t,\xi)=
 \begin{cases}
 \xi_i,& S=\{i\},\\
 (|S|-1)(t-a_{|S|})+\bar\xi_S,& |S|\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Uniformly for $(t,\xi)$ in compact sets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakfield-maximum}
 \log\max_n\frac{e^{\langle\xi,n\rangle/N}P_N(n)}{V_N}
 \longrightarrow \max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[d]}\ell_S(t,\xi).
\end{equation}
For fixed $(t,\xi)$ and all large $N$, every global mode of $\widetilde P_{N,\xi}$ has a support that
maximizes the limiting score. If the maximizing support is unique, all global modes have exactly
that support, and if it has $|S|\ge2$ states they satisfy $n/N=\ones_S/|S|+O(1/L)$. Equal
limiting scores do not imply that both supports hold modes at finite $N$.

If the fields are sorted as $\xi_1\ge\cdots\ge\xi_d$, the maximum of the scores is
the upper envelope of the $d$ lines $\ell_k(t)=\ell_{\{1,\dots,k\}}(t,\xi)$, namely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakfield-lines}
 \ell_1(t)=\xi_1,\qquad
 \ell_k(t)=(k-1)(t-a_k)+\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\xi_i,
 \quad 2\le k\le d.
\end{equation}
Away from ties, the winning support size is nondecreasing in $t$. If the fields are strictly
ordered, the winning supports are the nested sets $\{1,\ldots,k\}$. Every support size
$k\in\{2,\ldots,d-1\}$ is the unique winning size on an open interval of $t$ for suitable fixed
fields. So proper faces can hold the mode under a field of order $1/N$ per step.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1. The best composition on a support.} Let $S$ have $k\ge2$ states. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(ii), $\log(M_S/V_N)=(k-1)(t-a_k)+o(1)$, uniformly for $t$ in
a compact set. Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}(b), with $\alpha^*_S$ uniform, gives
$\log(M^\xi_S/V_N)=\ell_S(t,\xi)+o(1)$, uniformly for $(t,\xi)$ in compact sets. A vertex has
$M^\xi_{\{i\}}=e^{\xi_i}V_N$ exactly. There are finitely many supports, which
proves~\eqref{eq:weakfield-maximum}.

\emph{Step 2. The modes.} A support whose score is below the maximum by a fixed amount has
$M^\xi_S$ smaller than the largest face maximum for large $N$, so it holds no global mode. A
global mode with support $S$ maximizes $w_\xi$ on $S$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}(b)
gives its location.

\emph{Step 3. The lines.} Among the supports of size $k$ the largest $\bar\xi_S$ is
the average of the $k$ largest fields, which gives~\eqref{eq:weakfield-lines}. The line
$\ell_k$ has slope $k-1$. If size $k_1$ wins at $t_1$ and size $k_2$ at $t_2>t_1$, adding
$\ell_{k_1}(t_1)\ge\ell_{k_2}(t_1)$ and $\ell_{k_2}(t_2)\ge\ell_{k_1}(t_2)$ gives
$(k_2-k_1)(t_2-t_1)\ge0$, so $k_2\ge k_1$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:hull}(b). If the fields are
strictly ordered,
the unique best support of size $k$ is $\{1,\dots,k\}$.

\emph{Step 4. Every intermediate size.} To realize a given $2\le k<d$, set
$\xi_1=\cdots=\xi_k=0$ and $\xi_{k+1}=\cdots=\xi_d=-H$, and take $t>a_k$ so close to
$a_k$ that $t<a_{k-1}$ if $k>2$. Then the score of $\{1,\dots,k\}$ is positive, the
largest vertex score is $0$, and every face with $2\le j<k$ states scores at
most $(j-1)(t-a_j)<0$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(iv). A face with $j>k$ states
scores at most $(j-1)(t-a_j)-H(j-k)/j$, and another face with $k$ states at most
$(k-1)(t-a_k)-H/k$, so both lose once $H$ is large. The comparisons are strict,
so they hold on an open interval of $t$, and small changes make the fields
strictly ordered without removing the phase.
\end{proof}

One field can make every support size win in turn, from one state to all $d$. This is the
sequence of phases in Figure~\ref{fig:complete-envelope}.

\begin{corollary}[one field realizes the full face hierarchy]
\label{cor:weakfield-full-hierarchy}
Let $\mu$ be uniform, fix $d\ge2$ and $H>3/4$, and let $\xi_i=-H(i-1)^2$ for $1\le i\le d$. In
particular $\xi_i=-(i-1)^2$ works in every fixed dimension. Put
\[
 t_k=\log D_k-\log D_{k+1}+\frac{H(4k-1)}6\quad(1\le k<d),
\]
and $t_0=-\infty$, $t_d=+\infty$. Then $t_1<\cdots<t_{d-1}$, and for every
$k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$ the support $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ is the unique winning support
on the nonempty interval $t_{k-1}<t<t_k$. So for each fixed $t$ in this
interval and all large $N$, every global mode of $\widetilde P_{N,\xi}$ has exactly
those $k$ positive coordinates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k(i-1)^2=(k-1)(2k-1)/6$ and $-(k-1)a_k=\log D_k$, the lines
\eqref{eq:weakfield-lines} are $\ell_k(t)=(k-1)t+\log D_k-H(k-1)(2k-1)/6$, also for
$k=1$, and $\ell_{k+1}(t)-\ell_k(t)=t-t_k$. Extend $\log D_k$ to the smooth function
$c(x)=(x+\frac12)\log x-\frac{x-1}2\log(4\pi)$ for $x\ge1$. Then
$0<c''(x)=(x-\frac12)/x^2\le\frac12$, and integrating twice over unit intervals
gives $\log D_{k+2}-2\log D_{k+1}+\log D_k\le\frac12$. Hence
\[
 t_{k+1}-t_k=\frac{2H}{3}-(\log D_{k+2}-2\log D_{k+1}+\log D_k)>0.
\]
For $t_{k-1}<t<t_k$, summing the differences $\ell_{j+1}(t)-\ell_j(t)=t-t_j$
shows that line $k$ strictly exceeds every other line. The fields are strictly
decreasing, so the unique best support of size $k$ is $\{1,\ldots,k\}$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:weakfield-phases} gives the conclusion for finite $N$. Most of this
arithmetic is checked in Lean.
\end{proof}
