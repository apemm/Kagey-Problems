\section{First-order face selection}\label{sec:firstorder}

\subsection{An example with four states on a cycle}\label{sec:worked}

Take $d=4$ states on a cycle, with rate $1$ between neighbors and the uniform
start. Up to symmetry the admissible faces are a vertex, an adjacent pair, an
arc of three states and the whole cycle, with exit rates $2$, $1$, $2-\sqrt2$
and $0$. For the arc, $-Q_F=2I-A$ with $A$ the adjacency matrix of the path
$1$--$2$--$3$, whose largest eigenvalue is $\sqrt2$. The chain stays in a face $F$
up to time $T$ with probability about $e^{-T\lambda_F}$, and this mass is shared
by about $(N/\sqrt T)^{|F|-1}$ compositions. Hence
$M_F\approx N^{-(|F|-1)}T^{(|F|-1)/2}e^{-T\lambda_F}$, and with $T=\tau\log N$
this is $N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)}$ up to powers of $\log N$.
Figure~\ref{fig:C4worked}(a) plots the four exponents. The vertices win for
$\tau<1$, the pairs for $1<\tau<2$ and the whole cycle for $\tau>2$. The arcs
never win, since they beat the pairs only for $\tau>1+\sqrt2$, where the
cycle beats them. The whole cycle carries almost all the mass for every
$\tau>0$, and it carries the mode only for $\tau>2$, because it spreads its
mass over about $N^3$ compositions. Theorems~\ref{thm:first}
and~\ref{thm:facemax} make this rule exact, and for a pair
Corollary~\ref{cor:symface} gives the constant $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ in front.

\subsection{Face selection}

We also need an upper bound on a whole face. This includes the region near its boundary, where the local law does not apply. For an irreducible face $F$ put
$V_F=\{\theta\in\R^F:\varrho(A_F-\operatorname{diag}\theta)=0\}$. Each
$\theta\in V_F$ gives an exponential bound with the linear rate
$\alpha\mapsto\langle q-\theta,\alpha\rangle$, and $J_F$ is the supremum of these
linear rates. Indeed, for $u\in(0,\infty)^F$ the entrywise quotient $\theta=A_Fu/u$
lies in $V_F$, since $u$ is a positive null vector of the irreducible
Metzler matrix $A_F-\operatorname{diag}\theta$ and only its Perron root has a
positive eigenvector. Then $\langle q-\theta,\alpha\rangle$ is the function of $u$
inside the supremum that defines $J_F(\alpha)$. Conversely every $\theta\in V_F$
arises in this way from a positive Perron vector of
$A_F-\operatorname{diag}\theta$. For a field $v\in\R^F$ the vector
$\theta=q-v-\lambda_F(v)\ones$ lies in $V_F$, since
$A_F-\operatorname{diag}\theta=Q_F+\operatorname{diag}v+\lambda_F(v)I$.

\begin{lemma}[a uniform upper bound]\label{lem:upper}
Let $F$ be irreducible with $k=\lvert F\rvert\ge2$, and let $\theta\in V_F$.
There is $C_\theta$, depending on $\theta$, $Q$ and $\mu$ and bounded for $\theta$
in compact subsets of $V_F$, such that for every $n\in\Z_{\ge0}^F$ with
$\supp n=F$ and every $h$ with $hq_{\max}\le\frac12$, the probability that
$X_1,\dots,X_{\lvert n\rvert}$ have composition $n$ is at most
$C_\theta h^{k-1}\exp\bigl(-h\langle n,q-\theta\rangle\bigr)$.
\end{lemma}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:torus}. It writes the probability as an integral
over a torus and bounds the integrand by its value at the real point. The lemma
holds for a path of any length $\lvert n\rvert$, so it also applies to the blocks of
a path. With $\theta=q-v-\lambda_F(v)\ones$ and $\lvert n\rvert=N$ it gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper-field}
 P_N(n)e^{Tv\cdot n/N}\le C_\theta h^{k-1}e^{-T\lambda_F(v)}
\end{equation}
for every $n$ with support $F$. The next theorem uses the local law and the
face maxima of Section~\ref{sec:secondorder}, which are proved there without
it. Its part (i) is a weak form of Theorem~\ref{thm:stratified}(b), and its part
(ii) is the only part that bounds the face maxima of reducible faces.

\begin{theorem}[first-order face selection]\label{thm:first}
Fix $Q$ and $\mu$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, let $k=\lvert F\rvert$, and let
$\mathcal K\subset\Delta_F^\circ$ be compact. There is $N_0$ such that,
uniformly for $N\ge N_0$, $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$ and $\supp n=F$ with
$n/N\in\mathcal K$,
\[
 \log P_N(n)=-(k-1)L-T\,J_F(n/N)+O_{\mathcal K}(1+\log T).
\]
\item Let $F$ be admissible with components $C_1,\dots,C_r$, and let $k^*$ be
the least size of a component $C_j$ with $\lambda_{C_j}=\lambda_F$. There are
$\kappa$, $T_0$ and $N_0$ such that for $N\ge N_0$ and $T_0\le T\le N^{1/3}$
\begin{align*}
 \log M_F&\ge-(k_F-1)L+(k_F-k^*)\log T-T\lambda_F-\kappa,\\
 \log M_F&\le-(k_F-1)L+(k_F-1)\log T-T\lambda_F+\kappa.
\end{align*}
If $F$ is not admissible, $M_F=0$.
\item Let $T=\tau L$ with $\tau>0$ fixed, and let $\mathcal F_\tau$ be the set
of admissible faces that minimize $\Phi_\tau$. Then
$\log\max_nP_N(n)=-L\min_F\Phi_\tau(F)+O(\log L)$, with the minimum over
admissible faces, and for large $N$ every global mode $\hat n$ has
$\supp\hat n\in\mathcal F_\tau$. This holds for every start $\mu$.
\item If in (iii) every face in $\mathcal F_\tau$ is irreducible, then every
global mode $\hat n$ satisfies $\hat n/N=\alpha^*_F+O(1/L)$ with
$F=\supp\hat n$. This is the case when $Q$ has symmetric support or $\mu$ has
full support (Theorem~\ref{thm:hull}(c)).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) This is Theorem~\ref{thm:stratified}(b), since $J_V=J_F$ on $\Delta_F$, $\log C_F$
is bounded on $\mathcal K$ and $J_{\{i\}}=q_i$.

(ii) This follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:reducible} below, whose proof in
Appendix~\ref{app:counts} does not use the present theorem. After $T_1$ and $N_1$ are
enlarged, that theorem gives $\log M_F=-(k_F-1)L+p_F\log T-T\lambda_F+O(1)$ for $N\ge N_1$ and
$T_1\le T\le N^{1/3}$. Since $T\ge1$ and $k_F-k^*\le p_F\le k_F-1$, both bounds hold with
$T_0=T_1$ and $N_0=N_1$. If $F$ is not admissible, no path has support $F$, so $M_F=0$.
Appendix~\ref{app:first} gives a direct proof by explicit paths, which does not
use the local law.

(iii) For large $N$ we have $T_0\le T=\tau L\le N^{1/3}$, and both bounds in (ii)
read $\log M_F=-L\,\Phi_\tau(F)+O(\log L)$ for every admissible face, while
$M_F=0$ for the others. Since $\max_nP_N(n)=\max_FM_F$ over finitely many faces,
the first claim follows. Let $m_\tau=\min_F\Phi_\tau(F)$. An admissible
$F\notin\mathcal F_\tau$ has $\Phi_\tau(F)\ge m_\tau+\varepsilon_0$ for a fixed
$\varepsilon_0>0$, so $M_F\le N^{-m_\tau-\varepsilon_0+o(1)}$, which is smaller
than $\max_nP_N(n)=N^{-m_\tau+o(1)}$ for large $N$. A global mode has positive
probability, so its support is admissible, and hence it lies in $\mathcal F_\tau$.

(iv) By (iii), for large $N$ a global mode $\hat n$ maximizes $P_N$ on its face
$F\in\mathcal F_\tau$, which satisfies (H) by Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(b). So
Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax}(b) with $T=\tau L\le N^{1/3}$ gives
$\hat n/N=\alpha^*_F+O(1/L)$.
\end{proof}

On a reducible face the power of $T$ can differ from $(k_F-1)/2$, as in
Example~\ref{ex:threecycle}. Theorem~\ref{thm:reducible} below gives the power and the constant
for every admissible face.

\begin{remark}[a field]\label{rem:first-field}
Let $v\in\R^d$, put $M_F(T,v)=\max\{P_N(n)e^{Tv\cdot n/N}:\supp n=F\}$ and
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)=|F|-1+\tau\lambda_F(v_F)$. On an admissible face,
$Q_F+\operatorname{diag}v_F$ is block triangular in the order of the
components, so $\lambda_F(v_F)=\min_j\lambda_{C_j}(v_{C_j})$. The upper bound in
(ii) holds for $M_F(T,v)$ with $\lambda_F(v_F)$ in place of $\lambda_F$. The proof
in Appendix~\ref{app:first} applies with~\eqref{eq:upper-field} on each block and
the exact weight $e^{hv_iN_j}$ on a block $\{i\}$ of length $N_j$. For $F$ satisfying (H),
Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax} gives the matching lower bound. If $\mu$ has full
support, a reducible admissible face $F$ has a component $C$ with
$\lambda_C(v_C)=\lambda_F(v_F)$, which is admissible with fewer states, so
$\Phi_{\tau,v}(C)\le\Phi_{\tau,v}(F)-1$. Hence for
fixed $\tau$ and $v$, (iii)
and (iv) hold for the weighted law with $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ and $\alpha^*_F(v)$, every
face that minimizes $\Phi_{\tau,v}$ is irreducible, and a strict minimizer carries
every maximizer of the weighted law for large $N$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reading the winners off a convex hull}

For an admissible face $F$ put $P_F=(\lambda_F,\lvert F\rvert-1)$, and let $\mathcal S$
be the set of these points. Then $\Phi_\tau(F)=\tau x+y$ at $(x,y)=P_F$. Picture
a line of slope $-\tau$ moving up from far below. The first points of $\mathcal S$ that
it touches are the winners at $\tau$. For each $\tau>0$ the points of
$\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S$ where $\tau x+y$ is least form a vertex or an edge of
$\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S$, and we call the union of these over all $\tau>0$ the
lower-left boundary of $\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S$. Figure~\ref{fig:C4worked}(b) shows
it for the 4-cycle.

\begin{theorem}[geometry of the winners]\label{thm:hull}
Let $\mathcal F_\tau$ be the set of admissible faces that minimize
$\Phi_\tau$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The lower-left boundary of $\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S$ is a path whose
vertices lie in $\mathcal S$. Read from right to left, they are
$v_i=(\lambda^{(i)},y^{(i)})$, $0\le i\le m$, with
$\lambda^{(0)}>\dots>\lambda^{(m)}$ and $y^{(0)}<\dots<y^{(m)}$. Put
$\tau_i=(y^{(i)}-y^{(i-1)})/(\lambda^{(i-1)}-\lambda^{(i)})$ for $1\le i\le m$,
$\tau_0=0$ and $\tau_{m+1}=\infty$. Then $\tau_1<\dots<\tau_m$. For
$\tau_i<\tau<\tau_{i+1}$, $\mathcal F_\tau$ is the set of faces $F$ with
$P_F=v_i$. At $\tau=\tau_i$ it is the set of faces whose point lies on the
closed edge $[v_{i-1},v_i]$.
\item If $0<\tau<\tau'$, $F\in\mathcal F_\tau$ and $G\in\mathcal F_{\tau'}$,
then $\lambda_F\ge\lambda_G$ and $\lvert F\rvert\le\lvert G\rvert$. Both
inequalities are strict when $\mathcal F_\tau\cap\mathcal F_{\tau'}=\emptyset$.
\item If $Q$ has symmetric support, or $\mu$ has full support, then every face
in $\mathcal F_\tau$ is irreducible, for every $\tau>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) We prove this through the least value $g(\tau)=\min_{(x,y)\in\mathcal S}(\tau x+y)$ for $\tau>0$. Each point
$(x,y)$ of $\mathcal S$ gives the affine function $\tau\mapsto\tau x+y$, whose slope is $x$, and different
points give different functions. Hence $g$ is a minimum of finitely many affine functions, so it is
concave and piecewise affine. Let $\tau_1<\dots<\tau_m$ be the points of $(0,\infty)$ where its
slope changes, and put $\tau_0=0$ and $\tau_{m+1}=\infty$.

\emph{Between two kinks.} On $(\tau_i,\tau_{i+1})$ the function $g$ is affine. The affine
function of a point of $\mathcal S$ is nowhere below $g$. So if it equals $g$ at one $\tau$ inside this
interval, it equals $g$ on the whole interval. Hence exactly one point of $\mathcal S$ attains $g$ there.
We call it $v_i=(\lambda^{(i)},y^{(i)})$, and for $\tau_i<\tau<\tau_{i+1}$ the set
$\mathcal F_\tau$ consists of the faces $F$ with $P_F=v_i$. The slope of $g$ on this interval is
$\lambda^{(i)}$. The slope of a concave function decreases, and it changes at each $\tau_i$, so
$\lambda^{(0)}>\dots>\lambda^{(m)}$. By continuity both $v_{i-1}$ and $v_i$ attain $g(\tau_i)$,
namely
\[
\tau_i\lambda^{(i-1)}+y^{(i-1)}=\tau_i\lambda^{(i)}+y^{(i)} .
\]
This is the formula for $\tau_i$ in the statement, and since $\tau_i>0$ it gives
$y^{(i)}>y^{(i-1)}$.

\emph{At a kink.} The points $(x,y)$ of $\mathcal S$ that attain $g(\tau_i)$ are those on the line
$\tau_ix+y=g(\tau_i)$, which passes through $v_{i-1}$ and $v_i$. Let $(x,y)$ be one of them. For
$\tau$ slightly below $\tau_i$ we have $\tau x+y\ge g(\tau)=\tau\lambda^{(i-1)}+y^{(i-1)}$, with
equality at $\tau=\tau_i$. Subtracting gives $(\tau-\tau_i)(x-\lambda^{(i-1)})\ge0$, so
$x\le\lambda^{(i-1)}$. In the same way, $\tau$ slightly above $\tau_i$ gives
$x\ge\lambda^{(i)}$. So $(x,y)$ lies on the closed edge $[v_{i-1},v_i]$. Conversely every point of
$\mathcal S$ on this edge lies on the line and so attains $g(\tau_i)$.

\emph{The boundary.} On $\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S$ the function $\tau x+y$ is least exactly on the
convex hull of the points of $\mathcal S$ where it is least. By the two steps above this hull is the
vertex $v_i$ for $\tau_i<\tau<\tau_{i+1}$ and the edge $[v_{i-1},v_i]$ for $\tau=\tau_i$. So the
lower-left boundary is the path $v_0\cdots v_m$, and its vertices lie in $\mathcal S$.

(b) We prove this by adding two inequalities. Since $F$ wins at $\tau$ and $G$ wins at $\tau'$, we have
$\Phi_\tau(F)\le\Phi_\tau(G)$ and $\Phi_{\tau'}(G)\le\Phi_{\tau'}(F)$. Adding them gives
$(\tau'-\tau)(\lambda_F-\lambda_G)\ge0$, so $\lambda_F\ge\lambda_G$. Then the first inequality
gives $\lvert F\rvert-\lvert G\rvert\le\tau(\lambda_G-\lambda_F)\le0$. If
$\lambda_F=\lambda_G$, the two inequalities give $\lvert F\rvert=\lvert G\rvert$, so
$\Phi_{\tau'}(F)=\Phi_{\tau'}(G)$ and $F\in\mathcal F_{\tau'}$. So if $\mathcal F_\tau$ and
$\mathcal F_{\tau'}$ are disjoint, then $\lambda_F>\lambda_G$, and the first inequality gives
$\lvert F\rvert<\lvert G\rvert$.

(c) With symmetric support every admissible face is irreducible by
Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(c). Now let $\mu$ have full support, and let $F$ be admissible and
reducible. Take a component $C$ of $F$ with $\lambda_C=\lambda_F$. It is irreducible with
$\mu(C)>0$, so it is admissible by Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(b). It has the same exit rate as
$F$ and fewer states, so $\Phi_\tau(C)\le\Phi_\tau(F)-1$. Hence $F\notin\mathcal F_\tau$.
\end{proof}

Note that a face with small $\lambda_F$ for its size is a set of states that the chain
rarely leaves. As $\tau$ grows the exit rate counts for more, so the mode moves
to larger such sets. It need not grow around its first vertex and it can move
to a disjoint set. Faces of one size share the term $\lvert F\rvert-1$. Hence only
the faces with the least exit rate of each size can win.

\subsection{Reducible faces}\label{sec:reducible-max}

Let $F$ be admissible, with components $C_1,\dots,C_r$ numbered as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:admissible}(a). A path of positive probability with support $F$ visits them in
this order, each in one block of consecutive times. The order is unique, since consecutive
blocks are joined by an edge and the components form an acyclic graph, in which at most one path
passes through every vertex. Put $k_j=|C_j|$ and $\lambda_j=\lambda_{C_j}$, so
$\lambda_F=\min_j\lambda_j$. Let $k^*$ be the least $k_j$ with $\lambda_j=\lambda_F$, let
$S^*$ be the set of $j$ with $\lambda_j=\lambda_F$ and $k_j=k^*$, and put
\[
a_F=\frac{k^*-1}2,\qquad p_F=a_F+k_F-k^*,\qquad R_j=(q_{xy})_{x\in C_j,\,y\in C_{j+1}} .
\]
For an irreducible $C$ with $|C|\ge2$, states $y,x\in C$ and $t\in(0,\infty)^C$ let
$\Phi_C(t)_{yx}=e^{-\langle q,t\rangle}H_{A_C,e_y,e_x}(t)$, in the notation before
Lemma~\ref{lem:torus-complex}. As in Lemma~\ref{lem:continuum}, this is the density of the
occupation times of the chain started at $y$ on the event that it stays in $C$ up to time
$|t|$, visits every state of $C$ and ends at $x$. For $C=\{i\}$ put $\Phi_C(t)=e^{-q_it}$. For
$j\in S^*$ let $r$ and $l$ be the vectors $r_{C_j}$ and $l_{C_j}$ of Definition~\ref{def:faces},
with $l\cdot r=1$, and let
\begin{align*}
A_j&=\sup\,e^{\lambda_F|t^<|}\,\mu_{C_1}^\top\Phi_{C_1}(t^{(1)})R_1\cdots\Phi_{C_{j-1}}(t^{(j-1)})R_{j-1}\,r,\\
B_j&=\sup\,e^{\lambda_F|t^>|}\,l^\top R_j\Phi_{C_{j+1}}(t^{(j+1)})R_{j+1}\cdots\Phi_{C_r}(t^{(r)})\,\ones,
\end{align*}
over all $t^{(m)}\in(0,\infty)^{C_m}$ for $m<j$, respectively $m>j$, where $|t^<|$ and $|t^>|$
are their total times, $A_1=\mu_{C_1}\cdot r$ and $B_r=l\cdot\ones$. Finally let
\[
K^{\rm red}_F=\max_{j\in S^*}\frac{A_jB_j}{(2\pi)^{a_F}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{C_j}}},
\]
where $\Sigma_{C_j}$ is $\Sigma_\alpha$ of Definition~\ref{def:tilt} for the face $C_j$ at
$\alpha=\alpha^*_{C_j}$, and the denominator is $1$ if $k^*=1$. If $F$ is irreducible, then
$p_F=(k_F-1)/2$ and $K^{\rm red}_F=K_F$.

\begin{theorem}[face maxima on reducible faces]\label{thm:reducible}
Let $F$ be admissible. Then $0<K^{\rm red}_F<\infty$, and there are $T_1$, $N_1$ and $C$ such that
for $N\ge N_1$ and $T_1\le T\le N^{1/3}$
\[
\Bigl|\frac{M_F}{N^{-(k_F-1)}T^{p_F}e^{-T\lambda_F}}-K^{\rm red}_F\Bigr|\le C\Bigl(\frac1T+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr).
\]
In particular the ratio lies between $\frac12K^{\rm red}_F$ and $2K^{\rm red}_F$ after $T_1$ and
$N_1$ are enlarged.
\end{theorem}

Hence the power of $T$ is $p_F$, between the two powers of Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii). The time
goes to the components of least exit rate and least size $k^*$, and to one of them when
$k^*\ge2$. Such a component contributes $h^{k^*-1}T^{-(k^*-1)/2}$ through its local law, and each
other component $C$ gets time of order one and contributes $h^{|C|-1}$. Each switch from one component to the next contributes
one more factor $h$. The constants $A_j$ and $B_j$ are maxima over these short blocks.
For irreducible $F$ the theorem is Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax}(b) at $v=0$. The proof is in
Appendix~\ref{app:counts}.

\begin{corollary}[crossings of admissible faces]\label{cor:reducible-crossing}
Let $F$ and $G$ be admissible, with $\Delta=k_F-k_G\ge1$ and $\delta=\lambda_G-\lambda_F>0$. Put
$\pi=p_F-p_G$, $\kappa=\log(K^{\rm red}_F/K^{\rm red}_G)$ and $D_N=\log M_F-\log M_G$, and let
$T_1$ serve for both faces in Theorem~\ref{thm:reducible}.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For large $N$, $D_N$ has a zero in $[\frac\Delta{2\delta}L,\frac{2\Delta}\delta L]$.
\item For large $N$, every zero $T_N\in[T_1,N^{1/3}]$ of $D_N$ satisfies
$\delta T_N+\pi\log T_N=\Delta L-\kappa+O(1/L)$.
\item All such zeros lie in one interval of length $O(1/L)$, and
\[
T_N=\frac\Delta\delta\Bigl[L-\frac\pi\Delta\log L+b_{FG}\Bigr]+O\Bigl(\frac{\log L}L\Bigr),\qquad
b_{FG}=-\frac\pi\Delta\log\frac\Delta\delta-\frac\kappa\Delta .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

The coefficient of $\log\log N$ is $\pi/\Delta$. It is $\frac12$ when $F$ and $G$ are
irreducible, which gives Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}(i) to (iii) for zeros in $[T_1,N^{1/3}]$. In
Example~\ref{ex:threecycle}, where $q_{12}=b$, we have $p_{\{1\}}=0$, $p_{\{1,2\}}=1$ and $p_{\{1,2,3\}}=1$, and
$K^{\rm red}_{\{1,2\}}=b$ from $A_2=\sup_tbe^{(1-b)t}=b$ and $B_2=1$. So the corollary gives the
coefficients $1$ and $0$ of parts (ii) and (iii) there, with their constants. We do not claim
that the zero is unique.
