\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

The theorems, propositions, lemmas and corollaries of this paper are proved in
the text or in the appendices, with two kinds of outside input. We quote
results of Papers~A and~B where we say so (for example the uniform Bessel
bound~\eqref{eq:planar-bessel}, and the expansion behind the value for the
complete graph in Proposition~\ref{prop:nextterm}(iv)). And the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} marked Lean are proved in Lean and not in the text.
No proof rests on a floating-point computation. Exact computer algebra enters
in two places. First,
Proposition~\ref{prop:triangles}, which proves the row $\Theta_\varepsilon$ of
Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}, rests on two exact resultants of characteristic
polynomials, computed in sympy by \texttt{verify\_examples.py}
(Section~\ref{app:examples-checks}). Second, the values of $c_F$ for the 3-arc
and for $C_4$ after Corollary~\ref{cor:C4} are exact evaluations, by
\texttt{verify\_second\_order.py}, of the formula for $c_F$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:nextterm}(ii). The proposition is proved, and only these
two values are computed.

The other numbers in the paper are of three kinds. Some are closed forms. Some
are computations of the law at a finite $N$, namely the finite-$N$ row
of $P_4$ in Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}, Figure~\ref{fig:cycles}(b), the crossing
of the 3-arc at $N=10^{14}$ in Section~\ref{sec:examples}, the planar crossings
and zero-turn shares in Section~\ref{sec:planar} and Figure~\ref{fig:planar},
and Table~\ref{tab:sticky}. These are done in two independent ways where
possible (for example by the run expansion and by a torus integral, or by a
dynamic program and an exact formula). They illustrate the theorems, and no
statement is deduced from them. Finally, the value $0.115$ in
Section~\ref{sec:sticky} is a Monte Carlo estimate.

For the planar walk, \texttt{verify\_planar.py} computes $o_N$ in four ways and
finds the crossings. The script \texttt{verify\_planar\_product.py} checks the
product formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:product} in exact rational arithmetic
for $N=4,6,8$. In floating point it also checks the inequality
$F(y/2)\le2F(y)$ of Appendix~\ref{app:planar}, the bound on the number of
switches in Lemma~\ref{lem:oneswitch}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:pureturns}(a)
and~(b) for $N$ up to $96$. These checks guard against slips. The proofs do
not use them.

Many finite and algebraic steps, and the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} marked Lean, are proved in Lean~4 in the folder
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperC}, and its file
\nolinkurl{MAP.md} matches each statement of
the paper to its Lean theorems. For the planar walk Lean covers the identity
and the unique crossing for $s>0$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp}. The
asymptotic results, the product curve and the theorem on turns only are not in
Lean.

\paragraph{Open questions.} We list them in the order of the paper.

\emph{The local law at the boundary.} Theorem~\ref{thm:local} holds on compact
subsets of the open face, and a local law up to the boundary is open. The
same formula cannot hold there. Take a star with center $d$ and
$\ell=d-1\ge3$ leaves, and a start of full support. Then $P_N(n)=0$ whenever
$\supp n=[d]$ and $n_d<\ell-1$, since a path visits the center between any two
runs at the leaves, although $n/N$ lies in the open face. This zero set is
checked in Lean.

\emph{Reducible faces.} For a reducible face, Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii) places
the power of $T$ in $M_F$ between $k_F-k^*$ and $k_F-1$, and heuristically it
is $(k^*-1)/2+k_F-k^*$. We do not prove this.

\emph{Uniqueness and constants.} Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} puts all crossings
of two faces in an interval of length $O(1/L)$, but uniqueness is proved only in
special cases, such as Papers~A and~B, the pairs of Proposition~\ref{prop:pair}
and the planar walk. We have no tree formula
for $K_F$ on non-reversible faces like Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}(b),
and no closed form for $c_F$. At finite $N$ the order of the phases can
differ from first order when a face lies close to the hull, as on the path
$P_4$ (Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}).

\emph{The planar walk.} The first-order heuristic at the start of
Section~\ref{sec:planar} applies face selection to a linear function of the
composition. We prove its conclusion for the planar walk, but not such a rule
in general. The errors in Theorem~\ref{thm:fourdir} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:planar-turn} are only $o(1)$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:fourdir} needs $T\le N^{1/8}$. The next term of the crossing
equation when turns dominate is open, and so is the crossover when $s-2r$ tends
to $0$ as $N$ grows. A heuristic suggests that the next term comes from
replacing $T$ by $T+\frac1{4r}-\frac1{2(r+s)}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:fourdir}.
For $s=0$ this agrees with the term $1/(8z_N)$ of Paper~A, through
Theorem~\ref{thm:pureturns}(c). Off the product curve of
Proposition~\ref{prop:product} we have no closed form for $o_N$ or for the
crossing, and Corollary~\ref{cor:productcompare} only brackets the crossing.
With turns only, the computations give $N(v^*_N-u_N)=0.37\,L^2/N$ at
$N=96$ and $0.38\,L^2/N$ at $N=10240$. So the order $L^2/N$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:pureturns}(c) seems to be right, but we do not prove a lower
bound of this order.

\emph{Sticky priors.} We do not
treat random exit probabilities as in~\cite{fox2011}, or the probability of
a direct jump for $d\ge4$.
