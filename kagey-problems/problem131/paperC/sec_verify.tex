\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

Every theorem, proposition, lemma and corollary of this paper is proved in the
text or in the appendices. The only statement we do not prove is
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:L} for facets with three or more states, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:layer} uses it only for faces with four or more states. We quote
results of~\cite{paperA} where we say so. No proof rests on a computation. The
formal proofs and the computations below check parts of the paper independently.

\subsection*{Formal proofs}

Many finite and algebraic steps are proved in Lean~4 (v4.33.1, with Mathlib) in
the folders \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC} and \nolinkurl{lean/PaperB} of the repository
named under Code availability, and the file \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC/MAP.md}
matches each statement of the paper to its Lean theorems. No proof there uses
\texttt{sorry} or \texttt{admit}. The files cover the run expansion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:runs}), the lower bound and the reversible form of the rate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rate}), the restriction identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:restriction}), the finite form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull}, the tree formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}(b)
for two and three states, the exact parts of Example~\ref{ex:threecycle}, the
exact statements on the complete graph and near a vertex in
Sections~\ref{sec:complete} and~\ref{sec:vertex}, the multiaffine expansion of
$R_F$, the normalization (N) and the fact that a diagonal similarity keeping a
Metzler matrix Metzler has entries of one sign, part~(e) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible} for positive rates, and the determinants,
adjugates and prefactors of Example~\ref{ex:four-state}. Mathlib has no Perron
and Frobenius theorem for irreducible Metzler matrices, so the positive null
vector in (N) is a hypothesis of the Lean statements, and the asymptotic results
are not in Lean.

\subsection*{Computations}

The scripts are in the folder named under Code availability. Each check is an
assertion, and the checks in floating point use a tolerance. None of them is
interval-certified. The script \texttt{verify\_all.py} runs all eleven with
\texttt{py -I} and writes each output, headed by the sha256 of its script, to the
folder \nolinkurl{data}, in about fifteen minutes on a laptop. The older scripts
\texttt{verify\_first\_order.py}, \texttt{verify\_second\_order.py},
\texttt{verify\_forest.py}, \texttt{verify\_rate\_flows.py},
\texttt{verify\_weak\_field.py} and \texttt{verify\_examples.py} compute the law
in two independent ways and check Sections~\ref{sec:model}
to~\ref{sec:secondorder} and~\ref{sec:complete}. The script
\texttt{verify\_layer.py} checks the constants and kernels of
Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer}, including the inequality
$I_0/I_1\le1+2/\zeta$. The script \texttt{verify\_reconstruction.py} checks
Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction}. It recovers $Q$ from the strict winners on $40$
random reversible chains, checks the constant $\frac{20}3+\frac{28}9<10$ in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} and the bounds $\phi_*$ and $g_*$ on $2000$
chains, checks on $64$ random generators with
a rank-one cut that the generator point of the cut-transpose has the principal
minors of $-Q$ (in $49$ of them it is neither $Q$ nor $Q^*$), and checks on $200$
generators with three states that every generator with the same minors is $Q$ or
$Q^*$. The script \texttt{verify\_finite\_horizon.py} computes the entry times of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-horizon} on the path $1-2-3$ for $N=10^3$ to $10^6$ from
the run expansion. The script \texttt{verify\_cycles.py} redoes
Examples~\ref{ex:four-state} and~\ref{ex:branch-switch} in exact arithmetic, and
\texttt{verify\_attraction.py} tests Theorem~\ref{thm:attraction} on $11$ cost
matrices and $138$ proper faces at $t=10^{-32}$ and $10^{-48}$. Every cycle has
the grid threshold $D(G)-\frac14$, no noncycle exceeds it, and
$\max_{G\subseteq F}\vartheta(G)$ gives $D(F)$ on every face. The figures
illustrate the theorems, and no statement is deduced from them.

\subsection*{Open questions}

A local law up to the lower-dimensional corners of a face is open, and so is the
facet layer for faces with four or more states without Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:L}.
For a reducible face we expect the power of $T$ in $M_F$ to be
$(k^*-1)/2+k_F-k^*$, between the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii). The
crossings of Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} are known to be unique only in special
cases. The crossing equation for the weighted law, which would read the
amplitudes $K_F(v)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible}(f) off the boundaries, is
not proved. At low temperature it is open whether some calibrated field separates
$a_{124}$ from $a_{12}$ in Example~\ref{ex:four-state}.
