\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

Every theorem, proposition, lemma and corollary of this paper is proved in the
text or in the appendices, apart from the results of~\cite{paperA}
and~\cite{paperB} that we quote where we say so. No proof rests on a
computation. Hence the formal proofs and the scripts below are independent checks of parts of the paper. All of them are in the repository named under Code availability.

\subsection*{Formal proofs}

The folders \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC} and \nolinkurl{lean/PaperB} prove many finite
and algebraic steps in Lean~4 (v4.33.1, with Mathlib), with no \texttt{sorry} or
\texttt{admit}. The file \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC/MAP.md} gives for each statement the Lean theorems
that prove it or its finite core, and lists what is not in Lean. Mathlib has no Perron and Frobenius theorem for irreducible Metzler
matrices, so a positive Perron vector is a hypothesis where one is
needed, and the asymptotic results are not in Lean.

\subsection*{Computations}

The command \texttt{py -I verify\_all.py} in the folder \nolinkurl{paperC} runs the
9 scripts that check this paper, in about four minutes, and writes each output,
headed by the sha256 of its script, to \nolinkurl{paperC/data}. Each check is an
assertion, and the checks in floating point use a tolerance without interval
certification. The scripts \texttt{verify\_first\_order.py} and
\texttt{verify\_second\_order.py} compute the law in two independent ways, by the run
expansion and by a dynamic program or a discrete torus integral, and check
Sections~\ref{sec:model} to~\ref{sec:secondorder}, including the gaps quoted after
Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. The script \texttt{verify\_full\_range.py} checks
Lemma~\ref{lem:torus}, Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete-hessian} and Theorems~\ref{thm:local}(b)
and~\ref{thm:facemax}(a). For $q_{12}=1$, $q_{21}=2$ and $N=4000$ the ratio $R$ of the exact
law to Theorem~\ref{thm:local}(b) has $T|R-1|\le0.25$ at points of $[0.45,0.85]$ for $T=16$, $63$, $200$, $400$
and $1000=N/4$, while the continuum form has $T|R-1|$ of order $T$ from $T=200$ on. The script \texttt{verify\_examples.py} finds the
first-order winners on complete graphs, cycles and small graphs in two ways, and the
finite-$N$ modes from two exact codes. The script \texttt{verify\_rate\_flows.py}
computes no law and checks the rate of Proposition~\ref{prop:rate} against a form
through flows. The script \texttt{verify\_complete.py} checks
\ifarxivbuild Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance}, Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(i) and
Remark~\ref{rem:simplex-edge-mode} in exact arithmetic for small $N$, and the failure of
balancing for a start in one state quoted after Theorem~\ref{thm:C}. No script checks
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(ii) and (iii) or Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}
at large $N$.\else the exact results on the complete graph in Section~7 of the arXiv version of
this paper~\cite{arxivC1}, in exact arithmetic for small $N$.\fi{} The script \texttt{verify\_layer.py} checks the constants and kernels of
Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} on three chains with three states. One of them
is the cycle of Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic}(e), with $\omega_\beta=5.1820$
and $\gamma_\beta=3.4179$ against $q_3=3$, and the script recomputes the
derivatives of $J_G(\beta,z)$ and $\varkappa_\beta$ for it exactly. The
generalized kernel matches $f_F/f_G$ to $O(1/T)$ for $x$ from $0.01/T$ to $T/4$. The
script also checks the example after Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic}, where
$J_G(\beta,\cdot)$ is affine at the uniform $\beta$ to $10^{-15}$.
The script \texttt{verify\_layer\_general.py} checks Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}
on two facets with four states, namely a facet of a non-reversible face with five
states and a path whose jump chain is periodic. It computes the word sum exactly
and prints $S$ times the relative error for $S$ from $4$ to $32$. These products
settle near $0.438$ and $0.545$ at $z=0$, as~\eqref{eq:L-facet} predicts. The script
\texttt{verify\_counts.py} checks Section~\ref{sec:reducible-max} and
Section~\ref{sec:layer-counts}. It checks Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-counts}(a) against an
enumeration of all paths in exact arithmetic, for every $N\le12$ and two values of $h$, and
the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-counts}(c) on a grid up to $N'=10^4$. For the cycle of
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic}(e) with $i=3$ and $\beta=(\frac12,\frac12)$ it computes
the exact law by a dynamic program and the lattice kernel by a contour integral, and the error
$E$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:layer-counts}(a) satisfies $0<E\le0.62\,T^2/N$ and falls by a factor
of about $2.5$ each time $N'$ doubles, at $T=8$, $n_3=10$ and $200\le N'\le1600$. On the face $\{1\}\to\{2,3\}$ with
$q_{12}=q_{23}=q_{32}=1$ and the start in state $1$, where $p_F=\frac32$ and
$K^{\rm red}_F=\sqrt{2/\pi}$, it computes $M_F$ exactly at $N=40000$, and the slope of
$\log M_F$ in $\log T$ is within $10^{-3}$ of $\frac32$ for $8\le T\le20$, far from the powers $1$
and $2$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii), while $M_F$ divided by $N^{-2}T^{3/2}$ is within
$0.05\%$ of $\sqrt{2/\pi}$. The
figures only illustrate the theorems. Hence no statement is deduced from them or from
these numbers.

\subsection*{Open questions}

A local law up to the lower-dimensional corners of a face is open. The results on reducible
faces (Theorems~\ref{thm:first}(ii) and~\ref{thm:reducible} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:reducible-crossing})\ifarxivbuild{} and Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(ii)\fi{}
are proved for $T\le N^{1/3}$, and we have not extended them to $T\le h_*N$. We do not know whether the
zero in Corollary~\ref{cor:reducible-crossing} is unique, or where the most likely counts of a
reducible face lie. For a facet of three or more states, the law of the counts near the facet
when $T^2/N$ is of order one is open. Theorem~\ref{thm:layer-counts} has an error of order one
there, and a statement would need a discrete form of Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}. We do not know
whether $\mathfrak r_\beta=0$ forces $\pi^{\rm in}_\beta=\pi^{\rm out}_\beta$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic}.
