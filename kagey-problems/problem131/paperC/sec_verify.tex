\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

The theorems, propositions, lemmas and corollaries of this paper are proved in
the text, in the appendices or in the supplement, with two kinds of outside input. We quote
results of~\cite{paperA} where we say so (the uniform Bessel bound~\eqref{eq:planar-bessel},
the clipped products of~\cite[Lemma~3.2]{paperA}, the growth of the polynomials $S_{a,b}$ toward the
middle and the expansion of $z_N$). And the rows of Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} marked ``Lean
only'' are proved in Lean and not in the text. The results of~\cite{paperB} quoted in
Remark~\ref{rem:B-boundary} are not used. No proof rests on a floating-point computation.
Exact computer algebra enters in two places. First,
Proposition~\sref{prop:triangles} of the supplement, which proves the row $\Theta_\varepsilon$ of
Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}, rests on two exact resultants of characteristic
polynomials. The script \texttt{verify\_examples.py} computes them, as described in
Section~\sref{app:examples-checks} of the supplement. Second, the values of $c_F$ for the 3-arc
and for $C_4$ after Corollary~\ref{cor:C4} are exact evaluations, by
\texttt{verify\_second\_order.py}, of the formula for $c_F$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:nextterm}(ii). The proposition is proved, and only these
two values are computed. The value $c_F=-(k-1)/8$ for the complete graph is computed by
hand in Section~\sref{supp:nextterm} of the supplement, and \texttt{verify\_second\_order.py} also evaluates it
exactly for $k=3$ and $4$. The script \texttt{verify\_forest.py} lists the
spanning trees one by one. It checks Lemma~\ref{lem:forest} in exact rational
arithmetic for $119$ generators with two to six states. It checks
Proposition~\ref{prop:forest}(a) to~(c) in floating point against
Definition~\ref{def:tilt}, and part~(d) for the directed $3$-cycle. The script
\texttt{verify\_rate\_flows.py} checks the statements of Remark~\ref{rem:flows} in floating point
on $480$ random faces with two to five states. The script \texttt{verify\_weak\_field.py} checks
Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}. It checks (a) by listing all words of a chain with three states,
and (b) on four faces with the continuum density of Lemma~\ref{lem:continuum}, computed by the
torus integral of Lemma~\ref{lem:torus}. There the tilted face maxima are compared with
$\langle\xi,\alpha^*_F\rangle$, with the term
$(\gamma_F\cdot\Sigma_F\hat\xi+\frac12\hat\xi^\top\Sigma_F\hat\xi)/T$ and with the shift
$\Sigma_F(\gamma_F+\hat\xi)/T$ of the maximizer that completing the square suggests (the
proposition states only their order $1/T$), and on a pair at $N=20000$ with an exact dynamic
program of the chain. It checks (d) with the transfer matrix at $N=10^6$. Each check is an
assertion in these scripts, and the floating-point checks use a tolerance.

The statements of Sections~\ref{sec:complete} and~\ref{sec:vertex} that come from~\cite{paperB} are
also checked by its scripts in \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/archive/paperB}, which use
only the Python standard library. The script
\texttt{verify\_simplex\_threshold.py} checks the refresh form, the balancing transfers, the
example at $N=4$ and the balanced crossings at high precision. The script
\texttt{verify\_geometry.py} checks the exact law against a dynamic program and against all words,
\texttt{verify\_frontier\_modes.py} checks the weak field, and
\texttt{verify\_vertex\_completion.py} checks the identities near a vertex and the root brackets
of Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root}. The script \texttt{verify\_general\_start.py} checks the
law with a start against all words, and it computes the phases of Example~\ref{ex:K3-start} at
$N=90$ and the ends of the window of Remark~\ref{rem:K3-finite} at $N\ge3000$, by bisection. The
script \texttt{verify\_B\_tie\_repair.py} runs dynamic programs over all compositions at $N=300$ and
$1002$. None of these checks is interval-certified.

The other numbers in the paper are of three kinds. Some are closed forms. Some
are computations of the law at a finite $N$, namely Figure~\ref{fig:simplex},
the finite-$N$ row of $P_4$ in Table~\ref{tab:outcomes},
Figure~\ref{fig:cycles}(b), the crossing
of the 3-arc at $N=10^{14}$ in Section~\ref{sec:examples}, the phases after
Example~\ref{ex:K3-start} and the widths in Remark~\ref{rem:K3-finite}. These are done in two
independent ways where possible (for example by the run expansion and by a torus integral, or
by a dynamic program and an exact formula). They illustrate the theorems, and no
statement is deduced from them.

\paragraph{Open questions.} We list them in the order of the paper.

\emph{The local law at the boundary.} Theorem~\ref{thm:local} holds on compact
subsets of the open face, and a local law up to the boundary is open. The
same formula cannot hold there. Take a star with center $d$ and
$\ell=d-1\ge3$ leaves, and a start of full support. Then $P_N(n)=0$ whenever
$\supp n=[d]$ and $n_d<\ell-1$, since a path visits the center between any two
runs at the leaves, although $n/N$ lies in the open face. This zero set is
checked in Lean. Near a vertex of the complete graph the leading crossing is known uniformly
for rare counts of total $o(b)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root}), but neither the next term
uniformly in that range nor the case of a nonuniform start is known.

\emph{Reducible faces.} For a reducible face, Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii) places
the power of $T$ in $M_F$ between $k_F-k^*$ and $k_F-1$, and heuristically it
is $(k^*-1)/2+k_F-k^*$. We do not prove this.

\emph{Uniqueness and constants.} Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} puts all crossings
of two faces in an interval of length $O(1/L)$, but uniqueness is proved only in
special cases, such as the two-state walk~\cite{paperA}, the balanced compositions of the complete graph
(Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}), the pairs of Proposition~\ref{prop:pair} and the planar
walk~\cite{paperE}. Proposition~\ref{prop:forest} gives a tree formula
for $K_F$ on every irreducible face, but we have a closed form for $c_F$ only for symmetric
pairs and for the complete graph. At finite $N$ the order of the phases can
differ from first order when a face lies close to the hull, as on the path
$P_4$ (Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}).

\emph{The complete graph at finite $N$.} Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes} has no effective
$N_0$, and by Remark~\ref{ex:simplex-edge-mode} it does not hold for every $N$. The discrete
correction term $-\frac{(k-1)^2}2z^2/N$, with $z=Nu=T/p$ the normalized switch coordinate,
and the second-order ends of the window in
Remark~\ref{rem:K3-finite} are not proved, and neither is the failure of balancing at every large
$N$ for a start that is not constant on the face. A number of states that grows with $N$ is also
open. The two-state crossing value is $z_N=N(1-p_N)$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:paperA}.

\emph{Linear functions of the composition.} For the planar walk the position after $N$ steps is
a linear function of the composition, and the origin is a line of compositions. The heuristic of
Section~\ref{sec:intro-faces} applies face selection to it. For the planar walk its
conclusion is the subject of the companion paper~\cite{paperE}, in
preparation, but we have no such rule in general.
