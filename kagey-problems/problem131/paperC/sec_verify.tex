\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

Every theorem, proposition, lemma and corollary of this paper is proved in the
text or in the appendices, apart from the results of~\cite{paperA}
and~\cite{paperB} that we quote where we say so. No proof rests on a
computation. The formal proofs and the scripts below check parts of the paper
independently. All of them are in the repository named under Code availability.

\subsection*{Formal proofs}

The folders \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC} and \nolinkurl{lean/PaperB} prove many finite
and algebraic steps in Lean~4 (v4.33.1, with Mathlib), with no \texttt{sorry} or
\texttt{admit}. The file \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC/MAP.md} matches each statement
to its Lean theorems. A positive Perron vector is a hypothesis where one is
needed, since Mathlib has no Perron and Frobenius theorem for irreducible Metzler
matrices, and the asymptotic results are not in Lean.

\subsection*{Computations}

The command \texttt{py -I verify\_all.py} in the folder \nolinkurl{paperC} runs the
6 scripts that check this paper, in about four minutes, and writes each output,
headed by the sha256 of its script, to \nolinkurl{paperC/data}. Each check is an
assertion, and the checks in floating point use a tolerance without interval
certification. The scripts \texttt{verify\_first\_order.py},
\texttt{verify\_second\_order.py}, \texttt{verify\_rate\_flows.py} and
\texttt{verify\_examples.py} compute the law in two independent ways and check
Sections~\ref{sec:model} to~\ref{sec:secondorder} and~\ref{sec:complete},
including the gaps quoted after Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. The script
\texttt{verify\_layer.py} checks the constants and kernels of
Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} on three chains with three states. One of them
is the cycle of Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic}, with $\omega_\beta=5.1820$
and $\gamma_\beta=3.4179$ against $q_3=3$, and the script recomputes the
derivatives of $J_G(\beta,z)$ and $\varkappa_\beta$ for it exactly. The
generalized kernel matches $f_F/f_G$ to $O(1/T)$ for $x$ from $0.01/T$ to $T/4$.
The script \texttt{verify\_layer\_general.py} checks Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}
on two facets with four states, a facet of a non-reversible face with five
states and a path whose jump chain is periodic. It computes the word sum exactly
and prints $S$ times the relative error for $S$ from $4$ to $32$. These products
settle near $0.438$ and $0.545$ at $z=0$, as~\eqref{eq:L-facet} predicts. The
figures illustrate the theorems, and no statement is deduced from them or from
these numbers.

\subsection*{Open questions}

A local law up to the lower-dimensional corners of a face is open. For a reducible face we
expect the power of $T$ in $M_F$ to be $(k^*-1)/2+k_F-k^*$, between the bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii). Example~\ref{ex:threecycle} agrees, since there
$M_{\{1,2\}}$ has the power $T^1$ with $k_F=2$ and $k^*=1$. We do not know whether a zero of
$D_N$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} can fail to be unique. We know no example where it
fails, and we prove uniqueness only in special cases, such as the crossings with a vertex
on the complete graph (Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}). A discrete form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:layer}, with the Laguerre polynomials of Section~\ref{sec:vertex} in
place of the Bessel kernel, would need a discrete form of Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}.
