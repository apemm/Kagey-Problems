\section{Verification and open questions}\label{sec:verify}

Every theorem, proposition, lemma and corollary of this paper is proved in the
text or in the appendices. We quote results of~\cite{paperA} and~\cite{paperB}
where we say so. No proof rests on a computation. The formal proofs and the
computations below check parts of the paper independently.

\subsection*{Formal proofs}

Many finite and algebraic steps are proved in Lean~4 (v4.33.1, with Mathlib) in
the folders \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC} and \nolinkurl{lean/PaperB} of the repository
named under Code availability, and the file \nolinkurl{lean/PaperC/MAP.md}
matches each statement of the paper to its Lean theorems. No proof there uses
\texttt{sorry} or \texttt{admit}. The files cover the run expansion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:runs}), the lower bound and the reversible form of the rate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rate}), the restriction identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:restriction}), the finite form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull}, the tree formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}(b)
for two and three states, the exact parts of Example~\ref{ex:threecycle}, and the
exact statements on the complete graph and near a vertex in
Sections~\ref{sec:complete} and~\ref{sec:vertex}. Mathlib has no Perron and
Frobenius theorem for irreducible Metzler matrices, so a positive Perron vector is
a hypothesis of the Lean statements where one is needed, and the asymptotic
results are not in Lean.

\subsection*{Computations}

The scripts are in the folder named under Code availability. Each check is an
assertion, and the checks in floating point use a tolerance. None of them is
interval-certified. The script \texttt{verify\_all.py} runs all eight with
\texttt{py -I} and writes each output, headed by the sha256 of its script, to the
folder \nolinkurl{data}. The scripts
\texttt{verify\_first\_order.py}, \texttt{verify\_second\_order.py},
\texttt{verify\_forest.py}, \texttt{verify\_rate\_flows.py},
\texttt{verify\_weak\_field.py} and \texttt{verify\_examples.py} compute the law
in two independent ways and check Sections~\ref{sec:model}
to~\ref{sec:secondorder} and~\ref{sec:complete}. They were written for an earlier
version of the paper, and a few of their checks concern statements that this
version no longer makes. The script \texttt{verify\_layer.py} checks the constants
and kernels of Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer}, including the inequality
$I_0/I_1\le1+2/\zeta$ and the ratios for the non-reversible cycle after
Theorem~\ref{thm:layer}. The script \texttt{verify\_layer\_general.py} checks
Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet} on two facets with four states, a facet of a
non-reversible face with five states and a path with a periodic jump chain. It
computes the word sum $f^{(z)}_G(S\beta)$ exactly by a recursion over the words,
compares it with $S^{-3/2}C_G(\beta,z)e^{-SJ_G(\beta,z)}$ computed from the complex
critical point of Lemma~\ref{lem:critical}, and prints $S$ times the relative
error for $S$ doubling from $4$ to $32$ at real and complex $z$. The products
settle to constants, as~\eqref{eq:L-facet} predicts. The figures illustrate the
theorems, and no statement is deduced from them.

\subsection*{Open questions}

A local law up to the lower-dimensional corners of a face is open. For a reducible face we
expect the power of $T$ in $M_F$ to be $(k^*-1)/2+k_F-k^*$, between the bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii). The crossings of Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} are known
to be unique only in special cases. A discrete form of Theorem~\ref{thm:layer},
with the Laguerre polynomials of Section~\ref{sec:vertex} in place of the Bessel
kernel, is not stated.
