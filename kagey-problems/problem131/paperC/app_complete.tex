\section{Proofs for the complete graph}\label{app:complete}

This appendix proves Theorems~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing} and~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}, with the notation of Section~\ref{sec:completegraph}. The
first lemma writes the law with blocks between refreshes in place of runs.

\begin{lemma}[the refresh form]\label{lem:refresh}
Let $N\ge1$ and let $n$ be a composition with support $F$. Sums over $m$ run over the integer vectors
with $1\le m_i\le n_i$ for $i\in F$, and $M=\sum_im_i$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item With $0^0=1$,
\[
P_N(n)=\sum_m\Bigl(\sum_{i\in F}\mu_im_i\Bigr)\frac{(M-1)!}{\prod_im_i!}\,
h^{M-1}\sigma^{N-M}\prod_{i\in F}\binom{n_i-1}{m_i-1}.
\]
\item Let $1/d<p<1$, so that $0<u<1$ and $v>0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex-positive-sum}
S_n(u)=(1-u)^{N-1}\sum_mM!\,v^{M-1}\prod_{i\in F}\frac1{m_i!}\binom{n_i-1}{m_i-1},
\end{equation}
which is a sum of positive terms, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex-balance-integral}
S_n(u)=\frac{(1-u)^{N-1}}{v}\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\prod_{i\in F}B_{n_i}(vt)\,dt,
\end{equation}
where $B_n$ is defined in~\eqref{eq:refresh-polynomial}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) The transition matrix is $I+hQ=\sigma I+hJ$, so a word $x_1\cdots x_N$ has probability
$\mu_{x_1}\prod_{s<N}(\sigma\mathbf 1\{x_s=x_{s+1}\}+h)$. We expand each factor into its two
terms and call the steps where the term $h$ is chosen the refreshes. If there are $M-1$
refreshes, they cut the word into $M$ blocks, and a term of the expansion is not zero only if the
word is constant on each block. So $P_N(n)$ is the sum, over the sequences of $M$ block labels
and over the block lengths, of $\mu_{\ell_1}h^{M-1}\sigma^{N-M}$, where $\ell_1$ is the label of
the first block. If $m_i$ blocks carry the label $i$, there are $m_i(M-1)!/\prod_jm_j!$ sequences
of labels that start with $i$. Consecutive labels may be equal. Given the labels, the lengths of
the $m_i$ blocks of the label $i$ form an ordered sum of $n_i$ with $m_i$ positive parts, in
$\binom{n_i-1}{m_i-1}$ ways. Multiplying and summing gives (a).

(b) For the uniform start $\sum_i\mu_im_i=M/d$. By~\eqref{eq:SnVN} we divide (a) by
$V_N=p^{N-1}/d$, and $h^{M-1}\sigma^{N-M}/p^{N-1}=u^{M-1}(1-u)^{N-M}=(1-u)^{N-1}v^{M-1}$, since
$\sigma/p=1-u$. This gives~\eqref{eq:simplex-positive-sum}. We write
$M!=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^M\,dt$ and sum over each $m_i$ separately. All terms are nonnegative, so
the sum and the integral can be exchanged, and this gives~\eqref{eq:simplex-balance-integral}.
\end{proof}

When $\sigma\ge0$, part (a) says that the chain keeps its state with probability $\sigma$ and
otherwise draws a fresh state uniformly from all $d$ states, possibly the same one. The proof
does not need $\sigma\ge0$.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}}

Let $F$ be a face of $k$ states and $n$ a balanced composition of $F$, so that $P_N(n)/V_N=S_n(u)$
by~\eqref{eq:SnVN}, with $u=(1-p)/((d-1)p)$.

\emph{Part (i), one crossing.} A word with $j$ switches is a sequence of $j+1$ run colors in
which successive colors differ, together with run lengths. If color $i$ appears $m_i$ times,
its run lengths can be chosen in $\binom{n_i-1}{m_i-1}$ ways. So every coefficient of $S_n$ is
nonnegative, and the first nonzero one is $W_n(k-1)=k!$, from the $k!$ orders of $k$ single
runs. Hence $S_n$ increases strictly from $0$ to $\infty$ on $u>0$ and equals $1$ exactly once.
As $p$ increases over $(0,1)$, $u$ decreases over $(0,\infty)$. This gives $p_{N,k}$ and the
two inequalities for the balanced composition.

\emph{Part (i), monotonicity in $N$.} Choose the balanced compositions for increasing
$N$ by adding $1$ to one of the smallest coordinates. Each coefficient $W_n(j)$
is then nondecreasing. Indeed, inserting an extra copy of the incremented color just before
its first occurrence keeps the number of switches, and deleting the first letter of the first
run of that color inverts it. The coefficient of $u^k$ is
\[
W_n(k)=\frac{k!(k-1)}2\sum_i(n_i-1)=\frac{k!(k-1)}2(N-k),
\]
since exactly one run color appears twice. For each choice of it there are
$(k+1)!/2-k!=k!(k-1)/2$ admissible sequences of run colors, all orders minus those with the
two copies adjacent, and $n_i-1$ ways to split its $n_i$ visits into two runs. This coefficient
increases strictly with $N$. So at every $u>0$ the value $S_n(u)$ increases strictly with $N$,
the root of $S_n(u)=1$ decreases strictly, and $p=1/(1+(d-1)u)$ increases.

\emph{Part (i), the first crossing.} If $N=k$, every coordinate is $1$ and
$S_n(u)=k!u^{k-1}$, which gives $p_{k,k}$. Since the root decreases with $N$,
every root has $u\le(k!)^{-1/(k-1)}<1$, and $u<1$ means $h<p$, that is,
$p>1/d$.

\emph{Part (i), the face maximum.} For $p\ge1/d$, Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} gives
$M_F=P_N(n)$. For $p<1/d$ we have $u>1$, which exceeds the root of $S_n(u)=1$, so
$M_F\ge P_N(n)>V_N$. Hence $M_F=V_N$ exactly at $p_{N,k}$, with the same two inequalities.

\emph{Part (ii).} By~\eqref{eq:logmax} with $K_F=D_k/d$ and $\lambda_F=d-k$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB}), uniformly for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
\log M_F=-(k-1)L+\tfrac{k-1}2\log T+\log(D_k/d)-(d-k)T+O(1/T+T^2/N).
\]
By~\eqref{eq:vertexmax}, $\log V_N=-\log d-(d-1)T+O(T^2/N)$. Subtracting, and using
$\log D_k=-(k-1)a_k$, gives the display. With $T=L-\frac12\log L+t$ and $t$ in a compact set,
$T-L+\frac12\log T=t+\frac12\log(T/L)=t+O(\log L/L)$, and the error tends to $0$.

\emph{Part (iii).} Let $G$ be a vertex, so that $M_G=V_N$, $\Delta=\delta=k-1$ and
$\log(K_F/K_G)=\log D_k=-(k-1)a_k$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}(i) the function
$\log(M_F/M_G)$ has a zero in $[L/2,2L]$, and by (i) this zero is $T_{N,k}$.
Dividing~\eqref{eq:crossing} by $k-1$ gives the implicit equation. For the explicit form put
$z_*=L-\frac12\log L+a_k+e_L/L$ with $e_L=\frac14\log L-\frac12a_k$. Since
$\log z_*=\log L-\frac{\log L}{2L}+\frac{a_k}L+O(\frac{(\log L)^2}{L^2})$, we get
$z_*+\frac12\log z_*=L+a_k+O((\log L)^2/L^2)$. The derivative of $z+\frac12\log z$ is at
least $1$, so $T_{N,k}-z_*=O(1/L)$, which is~\eqref{eq:simplex-root-expansion}.

\emph{Part (iv).} We have $a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-f(k)$ with
$f(x)=(x+\frac12)\log x/(x-1)$. For $x\ge2$ the sign of $f'(x)$ is the sign of
$g(x)=2x^2-x-1-3x\log x$. Here $g(2)=5-6\log2>0$, and
$g'(x)=4x-4-3\log x>0$ for $x\ge2$, since $g'(2)>0$ and
$g''(x)=4-3/x>0$. So $f$ increases strictly and $a_k$ decreases strictly. This order is
checked in Lean. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance}}

Let $\mu_a=\mu_0$ for $a\in F$. By~\eqref{eq:runform}, $P_N(n)=\mu_0p^{N-1}S_n(u)$ for every
composition with support $F$, so it is enough to show that the move strictly increases $S_n(u)$.
The rest follows, since $S_n$ is symmetric in the coordinates of $n$ and repeated moves reach a
balanced composition. At $p=1/d$ we have $u=1$, $S_n(1)=N!/\prod_in_i!$, and the move multiplies
it by $n_b/(n_a+1)>1$. Let $1/d<p<1$, so that $v>0$.

\emph{Log-concavity.} We show that for each $w>0$ the sequence $B_n(w)$,
$n\ge1$, is strictly log-concave. Write
\[
\frac{B_n(w)}w=b_{n-1},\qquad
b_j=\sum_{i=0}^j\binom ji\rho_i,\quad
\rho_i=\frac{w^i}{(i+1)!},\qquad
c_j=\sum_{i=0}^j\binom ji\rho_{i+1}.
\]
Pascal's identity gives $b_{j+1}=b_j+c_j$. The ratio $c_j/b_j$ is the mean of the
strictly decreasing sequence $f_i=\rho_{i+1}/\rho_i=w/(i+2)$ under the weights
$\omega_i=\binom ji\rho_i$, $0\le i\le j$, normalized. Let $\omega'_i=\binom{j+1}i\rho_i$,
$0\le i\le j+1$, be the weights for $j+1$, and put $\omega_{j+1}=0$. On
$0\le i\le j$ the ratio $\omega'_i/\omega_i=(j+1)/(j+1-i)$ increases in $i$. So for
$i<l$ we have $\omega'_i\omega_l-\omega'_l\omega_i\le0$, with strict inequality when
$l=j+1$. Hence
\[
\sum_i\omega'_if_i\sum_l\omega_l-\sum_i\omega'_i\sum_l\omega_lf_l
=\sum_{i<l}(\omega'_i\omega_l-\omega'_l\omega_i)(f_i-f_l)<0,
\]
since $f_i>f_l$ for $i<l$. So $c_{j+1}/b_{j+1}<c_j/b_j$, and $b_{j+1}/b_j=1+c_j/b_j$
decreases strictly with $j$. By the Laguerre form in~\eqref{eq:refresh-polynomial}, this is a
Tur\'an-type inequality for the Laguerre polynomials.

\emph{The transfer.} Let $n_b\ge n_a+2$. By log-concavity,
$B_{n_a+1}(w)B_{n_b-1}(w)>B_{n_a}(w)B_{n_b}(w)$. We apply this
for $w=vt>0$ in~\eqref{eq:simplex-balance-integral} and integrate. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}}

For large $N$, $T\le CL$ forces $h\to0$ and so $p>1/d$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} every global mode is a vertex or a balanced
composition. So we compare the ratios $M_F/V_N$, which equal $S_n(u)$ at the balanced
compositions $n$ of $F$ and depend on $F$ only through $k=\lvert F\rvert$, with $1$ and with each
other. Fix $0<c<1$.

\emph{Case $T\le cL$.} The ratio $S_n(u)$ increases with $u$, and so with $T$. So it is at most
its value at $T=cL$, which is $\exp(-(k-1)(1-c)L+O(\log L))\to0$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(ii). So the vertices are the only modes, and $p>p_{N,d}$.

\emph{Case $cL\le T\le CL$.} Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(ii) gives
$\log(M_F/V_N)=(k-1)(y-a_k)+o(1)$ uniformly, with $y=T-L+\frac12\log T$ the same for every $k$.
Fix $0<\delta<(a_{d-1}-a_d)/2$ when $d\ge3$, and any $\delta>0$ when $d=2$.
If $y\le a_d-\delta$, every face ratio is at most
$e^{-(k-1)\delta+o(1)}<1$, so only the vertices are modes. If
$y\ge a_d+\delta$, the full center beats the vertices, and it beats a
balanced composition with $2\le k<d$ positive coordinates by
\[
(d-k)(y-a_d)+(k-1)(a_k-a_d)+o(1)>0 .
\]
If $|y-a_d|<\delta$, a proper face with $k\ge2$ states has
$(k-1)(y-a_k)+o(1)\le-(k-1)\delta+o(1)<0$, since $a_k-a_d>2\delta$. So it
loses to the vertices, and the comparison of the vertices with the full centers
is decided by the unique crossing $p_{N,d}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}(i).
In each subcase the conclusion agrees with (i) to (iii).

The last claim follows from~\eqref{eq:simplex-root-expansion} and
$p_{N,k}=1-(d-1)T_{N,k}/N$, since $a_2>\cdots>a_d$. \qed
