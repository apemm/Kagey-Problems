\begin{abstract}
A Markov chain with exit rates $q_i$ runs for $N$ steps with transition matrix
$I+(T/N)Q$. On each irreducible face that carries start
mass, we prove a local limit theorem for the occupation counts with an
explicit constant and relative error $O(1/T)$, uniformly for $1\le
T\le\varepsilon N$ with $\varepsilon\max_iq_i<1$ and on compact subsets of the
open face. Up to $O(1)$, minus the log-probability of counts $n$ with support $F$ is
$|F|-1$ times $\log(N/\sqrt T)$ plus $N$ times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the chain
$I+(T/N)Q$ at $n/N$. Every admissible face has an explicit power of $T$ in its largest
probability for large $T\le N^{1/3}$, and a constant given by a
finite-dimensional variational formula. For
$T=\tau\log N$ the face of the mode minimizes $\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$ at first
order, with $\lambda_F$ the exit rate. If an admissible face has $\Delta\ge1$ more states than
another and an exit rate smaller by $\delta>0$, we locate every crossing of their largest
probabilities for large $T\le N^{1/3}$ up to $O(1/\log N)$. For irreducible faces the crossing is
unique for $T\le\varepsilon N$, with coefficient $1/2$ of $\log\log N$. Near a facet, the
continuous-time occupation density is the facet density times an explicit kernel, with relative
error $O(1/T)$ at times $x\le cT$ off the facet, $c$ small. The counts obey a lattice form with
extra error $T^2/N$. The kernel is Bessel for $x=o(T)$ at facet profiles where the removed state has
equal entry and exit laws, and at a profile where the rate has a first correction, of order
$m\ge3$, if and only if $x=o(T^{1-2/m})$.
On the complete graph with the
uniform start, each face crosses a vertex exactly once.
\end{abstract}

\section*{A note on the use of AI}
I wrote the original paper, the preprint~\cite{paperB}, and its mathematics. In the revision of
October 2026 I used Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) for revision, and for developing and checking the
proofs of the results added in that revision, namely the local law over the whole switching range,
the counts near a facet, the face maxima of reducible faces, the uniqueness of the crossing and the
exact range of the Bessel form. I also used it for literature search, and it generated the
verification code and the Lean files, which I reviewed. Section~\ref{sec:verify} separates the
formal proofs from the numerical checks. I take responsibility for all claims, proofs and
computations.
