Dear Editors,

Please consider “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, crossings and the boundary layer” for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

A finite Markov chain with a switching probability of order log N / N makes about τ log N switches in N steps. The paper asks which states its most likely vector of occupation counts uses, and when the answer changes.

We prove a local limit theorem in each face of the simplex, uniform in the time scale and with an explicit constant. On a face the law weighs one power of N per dimension against the Donsker and Varadhan cost of the chain killed on leaving the face, which is the restriction of the cost of the whole chain. First-order selection is read off a convex hull. The constants locate every crossing between irreducible faces up to o(1), with the coefficient 1/2 of log log N for every such pair, and they describe the windows where several faces tie. Near a facet of any face the law is the facet law times an explicit kernel, with relative error O(1/T). The proof rests on a local law for a weighted chain on the facet with a small complex parameter, which we prove on a torus through a complex critical point. For reversible chains the kernel is a Bessel kernel, the facet form of Pedler's formula, while the time spent in the rare state is small compared with T. Without reversibility we prove the Bessel form in logarithmic form and in relative form up to order T^(1/3), and a numerical example shows that this limit is real. On the complete graph all faces tie at first order. There we prove that each face crosses a vertex exactly once at every N, that the most likely compositions on each face are the balanced ones whenever T ≤ N/d, and that for large N the mode changes exactly once.

The exact law of the counts is Good's. What is new is the saddle-point analysis that Good proposed, with its uniform constant, the comparison of faces of different dimension through one functional, the crossings with their log log N term, and the boundary layer. The introduction says this in detail.

The results of the preprint arXiv:2610.09258, “Vertex crossings in a symmetric Markov multinomial model,” on the crossings of the complete graph with a vertex and near a vertex are reproved here as cases of the general theory, and the paper cites that preprint for the results it does not reprove. The related preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats the two-state walk at every N, and the paper uses two of its results where it says so. A companion paper in preparation asks what the occupation law determines about the generator. It cites this paper and is not needed to read it. A separate paper on the elephant random walk with rare reversals is a different study and shares no results with this one.

Every stated theorem is proved in the article and its appendices, and no statement is conditional. No proof rests on a computation. Section 9 lists what the Lean files and the scripts in the public repository check independently.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
