Dear Editors,

Please consider “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, support selection and the boundary layer” for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

A finite Markov chain with transition matrix I + (T/N)Q makes about T switches in N steps. The paper describes the law of its vector of occupation counts when switches are rare, and reads off from it which face of the simplex carries the most likely counts.

On each face we prove a local limit theorem with an explicit constant, uniformly for 1 ≤ T ≤ N^(1/3) and with relative error O(1/T + T^2/N). Its exponent is one power of N per dimension of the face plus T times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the whole chain, whose restriction to a face is the functional of the chain killed on leaving it. For T = τ log N the face of the mode minimizes τ λ_F + |F| − 1 at first order, and the winners are read off a convex hull. The constants locate each crossing of two irreducible faces up to O(1/log N), with the coefficient 1/2 of log log N for every such pair. When only two sizes of face compete at each switch, this locates every change of the mode, and otherwise the paper describes the windows where several faces tie. Near a facet that is irreducible and has start mass, the law on an irreducible face is the facet law times an explicit kernel, with relative error O(1/T) while the time off the facet is below a small multiple of T. The proof rests on a local law for a weighted chain on the facet with a small complex parameter, which we prove on a torus through a complex critical point. For reversible chains the kernel is a Bessel kernel, the facet form of Pedler's formula, while the time spent in the rare state is small compared with T. Without reversibility we prove the Bessel form in logarithmic form and in relative form for times o(T^(1/3)). For a cycle on three states an exact third derivative shows that the relative form fails beyond that order. On the complete graph all faces tie at first order. There each face crosses a vertex exactly once at every N, the most likely compositions on each face are the balanced ones whenever T ≤ N/d, and for large N and T up to a multiple of log N the mode changes exactly once.

The exact law of the counts is Good's, and for two states the regime is that of Dobrushin's classical limit theorem and of the Markov binomial literature that followed it. What is new is the count vector of d states, the saddle-point analysis that Good proposed, with its uniform constant, the comparison of faces of different dimension through one functional, the location of the mode changes with their log log N term, and the boundary layer. The introduction says this in detail.

This paper replaces the preprint arXiv:2610.09258, “Vertex crossings in a symmetric Markov multinomial model,” as version 2 of the same arXiv entry. The general theory of Sections 2 to 6 is not in the preprint. Sections 7 and 8 and Appendices C and D reprove, as cases of the general theory, the results of the preprint on the crossings of the complete graph with a vertex, exact balancing, the single mode change and the crossings near a vertex. Three groups of results of the preprint are not reproved, and the paper cites the preprint for them. They are the next term of the crossing equation, the Bessel majorant with its crossover, and the results on a start and a field. One paragraph of the introduction lists this correspondence. The preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats the two-state walk at every N, and the paper uses one of its lemmas where it says so. A companion paper in preparation asks what the occupation law determines about the generator. It is not needed to read this one. It uses Proposition 5.12, a forward statement on the face maxima uniformly over a compact class of chains, which is proved here in full. A separate paper on the elephant random walk with rare reversals is a different study and shares no results with this one.

Every stated theorem is proved in the article and its appendices, and no statement is conditional. No proof rests on a computation. Section 9 lists what the Lean files and the scripts in the public repository check independently.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
