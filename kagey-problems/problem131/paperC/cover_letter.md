Dear Editors,

Please consider “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, support selection and the boundary layer” for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

A finite Markov chain with transition matrix I + (T/N)Q follows the continuous-time chain with generator Q up to time T, while its transition matrix tends to the identity. The paper describes the law of its vector of occupation counts in this degenerating regime, for 1 ≤ T ≤ εN with any fixed ε < 1/max q_i.

The main result is a local limit theorem on each face whose states communicate within it and carry start mass. It holds uniformly on compact parts of the open face and for 1 ≤ T ≤ εN, with an explicit constant and relative error O(1/T). Up to O(1), minus the logarithm of the probability is (|F| − 1) log(N/√T) plus N times the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the chain I + (T/N)Q, whose restriction to a face is the functional of the chain killed on leaving it. Good's discrete formula sits on a torus, and in the torus variable it is analytic in T/N up to T/N = 0, where it is the formula of the continuous-time chain. So one Laplace argument with T/N as a parameter gives the law uniformly as the chain degenerates, and the comparison with the continuum is needed only for bounded T. For T ≤ N^(1/3) the functional of the continuous-time chain gives the law with error O(1/T + T^2/N), and the term T^2/N is a real second-order term of that exponent. Two consequences follow. First, for T = τ log N the face of the mode minimizes τ λ_F + |F| − 1 at first order, and the winners are read off a convex hull. If one such face has Δ ≥ 1 more states than another and an exit rate smaller by δ > 0, their largest probabilities cross exactly once in 1 ≤ T ≤ εN for large N, and the constants locate the crossing up to O(1/log N), with the coefficient 1/2 of log log N for every such pair. On a face whose states do not all communicate, the largest probability has the power T^((k*−1)/2 + |F| − k*), where k* is the least size of a component of least exit rate, with a constant given by a finite-dimensional variational formula, and the coefficient of log log N becomes (p_F − p_G)/Δ for these powers. When Q has symmetric support or the start full support, and only two sizes of face compete at each switch, this locates every change of the mode. Second, near a facet, the occupation density of the continuous-time chain on a face is the facet density times an explicit kernel, with relative error O(1/T) while the time off the facet is below a small multiple of T. The proof rests on a local law for a weighted chain on the facet with a small complex parameter, which we prove on a torus through a complex critical point. The kernel is Bessel, the facet form of Pedler's formula, while the time off the facet is o(T) at the facet profiles where the laws of entry to and exit from the removed state agree, which are all profiles for reversible chains and some profiles for some others. At a profile where the rate has a first correction, of order m ≥ 3, the relative Bessel form holds if and only if that time is o(T^(1−2/m)), and for a non-reversible cycle on three states m = 3. The counts near a facet follow the same law with a lattice kernel, built from Laguerre polynomials, with the extra relative error T^2/N, and for a pair face the discrete form of Pedler's formula is exact. Sections 7 and 8 of the arXiv version treat the complete graph with the uniform start as a worked example. There each face crosses a vertex exactly once at every N, and the most likely compositions on each face are the balanced ones whenever T ≤ N/d. The journal version states these results in one paragraph of the introduction.

The exact law of the counts is Good's, and for two states the regime is that of Dobrushin's classical limit theorem and of the Markov binomial literature that followed it. What is new is the count vector of d states, the saddle-point analysis that Good proposed, with its constant and uniformly as the transition matrix tends to the identity, where the exact large deviation results for a fixed chain give no uniform bound, the comparison of faces of different dimension through one functional, the location of the mode changes with their log log N term, and the boundary layer. The introduction says this in detail.

The arXiv version of this paper is version 2 of the entry arXiv:2610.09258, whose version 1 is our preprint “Vertex crossings in a symmetric Markov multinomial model.” The two overlap only on the complete graph. The general theory of Sections 2 to 6 is not in the preprint, and the preprint's complete-graph results are reproved here as cases of that theory or, in Sections 7 and 8 of the arXiv version, by its exact arguments. A table in the introduction lists the results of the preprint that this version reproves, which part is covered, and whether the proof is a case of the general theory. The paragraph before it names the results of the preprint that Sections 7 and 8 of the arXiv version reprove, and those that are not reproved. The preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats the two-state walk at every N, and the paper uses one of its lemmas where it says so. A companion paper, “What the occupation law of a finite Markov chain determines,” is under consideration at Stochastic Processes and their Applications. It asks what the occupation law determines about the generator, and it is not needed to read this one. It uses Proposition 5.12, a forward statement on the face maxima uniformly over a compact class of chains, which is stated and proved here. A separate paper on the elephant random walk with rare reversals is a different study and shares no results with this one.

The length comes from the paper being self-contained. Every proof of every stated result is in the article or its appendices. To keep it under 80 pages the journal version leaves out Sections 7 and 8 of the arXiv version of the paper (arXiv:2610.09258v2), on the complete graph and near a vertex, with their Appendices C and D, and it states their main results in one paragraph of the introduction with a citation. It keeps the numbers of the other sections and statements, so its sections run from 1 to 6 and then 9, and its appendices are A, B and E. No statement is conditional. No proof rests on a computation. Section 9 lists what the Lean files and the scripts in the public repository check independently.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
