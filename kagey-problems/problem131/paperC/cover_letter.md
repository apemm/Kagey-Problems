Dear Editors,

Please consider “Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, modal supports and reconstruction” for the Journal of Theoretical Probability.

A finite Markov chain with a switching probability of order log N / N makes about τ log N switches in N steps. The paper asks which states its most likely vector of occupation counts uses, and then turns the question around and asks what the law of the counts determines about the generator.

For the forward question we prove a local limit theorem in each face of the simplex, uniform in the time scale and with an explicit constant. On a face the law weighs one power of N per dimension against the Donsker and Varadhan cost of the chain killed on leaving the face, which is the restriction of the cost of the whole chain. First-order selection is read off a convex hull. The constants locate every crossing between irreducible faces up to o(1), with the coefficient 1/2 of log log N for every such pair, and they describe the windows where several faces tie. Near a facet the law follows a Bessel kernel, the facet form of Pedler's formula, which we prove for faces with at most three states. On the complete graph all faces tie at first order, and we prove exact balancing and exactly one mode change.

For the inverse question we show that two irreducible generators have the same Donsker and Varadhan functional exactly when they have the same principal minors, and that the support of the most likely composition under killing at known rates carries the same information. The generators with given principal minors form a finite set, which is the generator and its time reversal when there is no cut. For a reversible chain a design fixed in advance reads the generator from finitely many boundaries, with a minimality theorem, Lipschitz stability and an explicit finite-horizon correction. At low temperature the winners are Freidlin and Wentzell cycles of least size away from finitely many heights, explicit prefactors decide at a critical height, a noncycle can win on an open window, and rewards of known size read off the cycle tree.

The exact law is Good's, and the fiber of the principal-minor map is the matrix theory of Hartfiel and Loewy, Loewy, Al Ahmadieh and Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and Raj. The depth of a face rests on Catoni's exit theorem, and the eigenvalue asymptotics behind the prefactors go back to Wentzell. What is new is the saddle-point analysis that Good proposed, with its uniform constant, the boundary layer, the translation of the principal-minor fiber to generators, the reading of every minor from the modal supports, the obstruction from the stationary start, and the competition of dimension against exit rate at low temperature. The introduction says this in detail.

The manuscript incorporates the preprint arXiv:2610.09258, “Vertex crossings in a symmetric Markov multinomial model.” Its symmetric model is now the complete-graph case of the general theory, and this paper will replace it. The related preprint arXiv:2610.06935 treats the two-state walk at every N, and the paper quotes a few of its results where it says so. A separate paper on the elephant random walk with rare reversals is a different study and shares no results with this one.

Every stated theorem is proved in the article and its appendices, except the one hypothesis named in the facet layer theorem, which that theorem uses only for faces with four or more states. No proof rests on a computation. Section 11 lists what the Lean files and the scripts in the public repository check independently.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering the paper.

Arjun Pemmasani
