\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $Q$ be a generator on $d$ states, and run the chain for $N$ steps with
transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$. It then makes about $T$ switches, and we let $T$
range from $1$ to $N^{1/3}$. The occupation counts $n_i=\#\{t\le N:X_t=i\}$
have support $F=\{i:n_i>0\}$, which is a face of the simplex. We describe the
law of the count vector on every face. Inside a face it is a local limit law
with an explicit constant. Its exponent is the lattice entropy $(|F|-1)\log N$
of the face plus $T$ times the Donsker and Varadhan cost of the point, and this
cost is one functional of the whole chain. Near a facet the law on the face is
the law on the facet times an explicit kernel. The most likely count vector,
the mode, lies on the face where this law is largest. A crossing of two faces is
a value of $T$ at which their largest probabilities are equal. When
$T=\tau\log N$ the law locates every crossing of two irreducible faces up to
$O(1/\log N)$, and so every change of the mode when only two sizes of face
compete.

To state the law, let $J_V$ be the Donsker and Varadhan rate function of the
empirical measure of the continuous-time chain with generator $Q$, given
in~\eqref{eq:JV}. Say that a face $F$ satisfies (H) if the chain can move
between any two states of $F$ without leaving $F$ and the start gives $F$
positive mass. Write $\lambda_F$ for the exit rate of $F$ and $M_F$ for the
largest probability of a count vector with support $F$
(Definition~\ref{def:faces}).

\begin{introtheorem}[the law on a face]\label{thm:A}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$. Let $F$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, with
$k=\lvert F\rvert\ge2$ states, and let $\mathcal K$ be a compact subset of the
open face. Uniformly for $\supp n=F$, $n/N\in\mathcal K$ and $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
 -\log P_N(n)=(k-1)\log N+T\,J_V(n/N)-\frac{k-1}2\log T-\log C_F(n/N)
 +O\Bigl(\frac1T+\frac{T^2}N\Bigr),
\]
where $C_F>0$ is the explicit function of Definition~\ref{def:tilt}. Uniformly
for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
 M_F=N^{-(k-1)}\,T^{(k-1)/2}K_F\,e^{-T\lambda_F}\bigl(1+O(1/T+T^2/N)\bigr),
\]
where $K_F=C_F(\alpha^*_F)$ and $\alpha^*_F$ is the quasi-ergodic law of $F$.
For $k=1$ the second display holds with $K_F$ the start mass of the state.
\end{introtheorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is Theorems~\ref{thm:stratified} and~\ref{thm:facemax}.
It rests on a local limit theorem for the occupation fractions of the chain
killed on leaving $F$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:local}). The rate of that chain is the
restriction of $J_V$ to the face (Proposition~\ref{prop:restriction}). Its least
value is $\lambda_F$, attained only at $\alpha^*_F$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:variational}). The factor $T^{(k-1)/2}$ appears because
the visit fractions fluctuate on the scale $T^{-1/2}$ in each of the $k-1$ free
directions. For reversible faces $K_F$ involves the square root of a weighted
count of spanning trees (Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}). The face maxima hold
with a field $v$ that weights a composition by $e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$, uniformly for
$v$ in a compact set, and uniformly over a compact class of reversible chains
(Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-facemax}).

\subsection{The mode and its crossings}\label{sec:intro-faces}

When $T=\tau\log N$ the cost $e^{-T\lambda_F}$ of staying in $F$ and the cost
$N^{-(|F|-1)}$ of spreading over the count vectors of $F$ are both powers of $N$.
Put $\Phi_\tau(F)=\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$. A face is admissible if a path of
positive probability has exactly that support.

\begin{introtheorem}[the support of the mode]\label{thm:B}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $T=\tau\log N$ with $\tau>0$ fixed. Every admissible face $F$ has
$M_F=N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)+o(1)}$. For large $N$ the support of every most likely
composition is an admissible face that minimizes $\Phi_\tau$ among the
admissible faces. If each such minimizing face satisfies \textup{(H)}, then the
visit fractions of every most likely composition are within $O(1/\log N)$ of
the quasi-ergodic law of its support.
\item Let $F$ and $G$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, and suppose that $F$ has
$\Delta\ge1$ more states than $G$ and an exit rate smaller by $\delta>0$. For
large $N$ the equation $M_F=M_G$ has a root $T$ in $[1,N^{1/3}]$, and every
such root satisfies
\[
 \delta T+\frac\Delta2\log T=\Delta\log N-\log\frac{K_F}{K_G}
 +O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii)--(iv) and part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. Theorem~\ref{thm:hull} reads the winners in (i) off
the lower-left boundary of the convex hull of the points $(\lambda_F,|F|-1)$. So
as $\tau$ grows the winning size can only grow. Solving the equation in (ii)
gives $T=\frac\Delta\delta[\log N-\frac12\log\log N+O(1)]$. The coefficient
$\frac12$ of $\log\log N$ holds for every such pair, whatever the graph, the
rates, the sizes or the start, and without reversibility. On a reducible face
it can be $1$ or $0$ (Example~\ref{ex:threecycle}). If $Q$ has symmetric support
or $\mu$ full support, and at each switch of (i) only two values of
$(\lambda_F,|F|)$ compete, Corollary~\ref{cor:mode-changes}
locates every change of the mode for $T$ of order $\log N$ up to $O(1/\log N)$.
When more compete, Theorem~\ref{thm:window} describes the window of $T$ in which
they do.

\subsection{The boundary layer}\label{sec:intro-layer}

Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} follows the law up to a facet
$G=F\setminus\{i\}$. Let $x$ be the time spent in $i$ and $S=T-x$. We pull the
visits to $i$ out of the run expansion. This writes the density on $F$ as a
contour integral of the density of a weighted chain on $G$ with a complex
parameter. For a pair face the integral is the Bessel kernel
$e^{-\gamma x}\sqrt{abS/x}\,I_1(2\sqrt{abSx})$ times the density on the facet.
This is the facet form of Pedler's formula for two-state chains, and we prove it
with an explicit error for all $x$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-face}). The
constants $a$, $b$ and $\gamma$ are explicit for every irreducible chain, and
$\gamma-q_i$ is $ab/2$ times an effective resistance
(Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-constants}). For every irreducible face whose facet
satisfies (H), Theorem~\ref{thm:layer} gives the law for $x$ up to a small
multiple of $T$. A generalized kernel holds there with relative error $O(1/T)$.
For a reversible chain the Bessel kernel holds with relative error
$O(1/T+\sqrt{x/T})$. Without reversibility it holds up to a factor $e^E$ with
$E=O(1/T+\sqrt{x/T}(1+x))$. Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-cubic} finds the term
of order $x^{3/2}T^{-1/2}$ in $E$, and for a cycle on three states this term is
$\frac{49}{64\sqrt{ab}}\,x^{3/2}S^{-1/2}$. So there, for $x$ up to a small
multiple of $T$, the relative form holds if and only if $x=o(T^{1/3})$. The
proof rests on a local law for
the weighted facet chain (Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}). We prove it on a torus
through a complex critical point, with a Laplace lemma for a complex Hessian.

\subsection{The complete graph}\label{sec:intro-complete}

On the complete graph with the uniform start, $Q=J-dI$ with $J$ the all-ones
matrix, every face ties at first order and the constants decide. A face of $k$
states has $K_F=\frac1dk^{k+1/2}(4\pi)^{-(k-1)/2}$, and the $k^{k-2}$ spanning
trees of $K_k$ enter through it. Put
$a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-\frac{k+1/2}{k-1}\log k$, write $V_N$ for the
probability of a vertex, and call a composition balanced if its positive
coordinates differ by at most $1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the complete graph]\label{thm:C}
Let $Q=J-dI$ and let the start be uniform.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ be a face of $2\le k\le d$ states. For every $N\ge k$ the equation $M_F=V_N$ has
exactly one root $T_{N,k}$ in $(0,N/(d-1))$, and the switch probability $T_{N,k}/N$ at the
root decreases strictly as $N$ grows. As $N\to\infty$,
\[
 T_{N,k}+\tfrac12\log T_{N,k}=\log N+a_k+O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\item For every $N$ and every $0<T\le N/d$, the most likely compositions with a given support
are exactly the balanced ones.
\item Since $a_2>a_3>\dots>a_d$, for large $N$ and $T$ up to any fixed multiple of $\log N$ the
mode changes exactly once, from the $d$ vertices straight to the balanced compositions with
$d$ positive coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}, part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} and part (iii) is
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}. Parts (i) and (ii) hold at every $N$.
Figure~\ref{fig:complete-envelope} shows the window.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The exact law of the count vector goes back to Whittle~\cite{whittle1955},
Dawson and Good~\cite{dawsongood1957}, Goodman~\cite{goodman1958} and
Good~\cite{good1961}. For two states it is the Markov binomial law of
Gabriel~\cite{gabriel1959}. Dobrushin~\cite{dobrushin1953} classified the limit
laws of the number of visits of a two-state chain whose switch probabilities
scale with the length, and for switch probabilities of order $1/N$ the limit is
not Gaussian. This is the case $d=2$ of our regime. \v{C}ekanavi\v{c}ius and
Vellaisamy~\cite{cv2010} give compound Poisson approximations to the Markov
binomial law and survey the work since Dobrushin. What is new here is the count
vector of $d$ states, the uniformity in $T$, the explicit constants and the
competition of faces. Good proposed a saddle-point analysis of his formula (10)
and did not carry it out~\cite[pp.~42--43]{good1961}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is that analysis, uniform in $T$ and with its constant.
Polettini~\cite{polettini2015} and Carugno, Vivo and Coghi~\cite{cvc2022} derive
the exact law from the BEST and matrix-tree theorems, and
Stadje~\cite{stadje1999} gives limit laws when one state becomes nearly
absorbing. In continuous time, Brydges, van der Hofstad and
K\"onig~\cite{bhk2007} give the joint density of the local times on the event
that the range is a given set, and Huang, Kious, Sidoravicius and
Tarr\`es~\cite{hkst2018} write it with Bessel functions and spanning trees.
Pedler's occupation-time density for two-state chains~\cite{pedler1971},
continued by Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir~\cite{kmn2010}, is the Bessel kernel
of the boundary layer. The large deviations of Markov additive processes of Ney
and Nummelin~\cite{nn1987a,nn1987b}, the eigenvalue method of
Miller~\cite{miller1961} for sums on a finite chain, the local limit theorem of
Herv\'e and Ledoux~\cite{herveledoux2013} and the exact large deviation
asymptotics of Kontoyiannis and Meyn~\cite[Theorem~6.3]{km2003} concern a fixed
chain. Our
local law is for the whole count vector of a killed chain whose switch
probabilities shrink with $N$.

The rate inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the killed
chain~\cite{dv1975,dupuisliu2015,chendeng2013}. Guillin, Nectoux and
Wu~\cite{gnw2024} prove a large deviation principle for the occupation measure
of a process conditioned to stay in a set. The quasi-ergodic law goes back to
Darroch and Seneta~\cite{darrochseneta1965,darrochseneta1967}, and Collet,
Mart\'inez and San Mart\'in~\cite{cmsm2013} is the standard reference. Dietz and
Sethuraman~\cite{dietz2007,dietz2005}, Bouguet and
Cloez~\cite{bouguetcloez2018} and Bena\"im, Bravo and Faure~\cite{bbf2026}
study chains whose switching decays along the trajectory, while here the rate is fixed during each path and changes with $N$.
Bertini, Gabrielli and Landim~\cite{bgl2024}, Landim~\cite{landim2023} and Kim
and Landim~\cite{kl2024} expand the rate functions of a fixed chain in a
parameter, the last for the joint rate of the empirical measure and the
flow~\cite{bfg2014,bfg2015}. Here the functional enters through its
restriction to each face, and faces of different dimension compete.

The two-state walk is treated at every $N$ in~\cite{paperA}. This paper
\ifarxivbuild replaces our preprint~\cite{paperB} on the complete graph, which
is the first version of the same arXiv entry\else supersedes our
preprint~\cite{paperB} on the complete graph\fi. We reprove
\cite[Theorems~3.1 to 3.3, 3.8 and 6.4, Proposition~3.6, Corollary~3.9,
Example~3.10 and Lemma~6.1]{paperB} as cases of the general theory, namely as
Corollary~\ref{cor:paperB}, the paragraph after it, Theorems~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing},
\ref{thm:simplex-balance} and~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root},
Example~\ref{ex:complete-face}, Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes},
Remark~\ref{rem:simplex-edge-mode} and Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel}. We do not reprove the next term of the
crossing equation in~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{paperB}, the Bessel majorant and the
crossover of~\cite[Theorems~6.2 and C.1]{paperB}, or the results
of~\cite[Sections~4 and 5]{paperB} on a start and a field. A companion paper~\cite{paperC2} asks what
the Donsker and Varadhan functional and the modal supports determine about the
generator.
