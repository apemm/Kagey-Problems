\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Which states does the most likely occupation-count vector of a slowly
switching Markov chain use? Fix a generator $Q$ on $d$ states and a start
law, and take $N$ steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$, so that the
chain makes about $T$ switches. The occupation counts
$n_i=\#\{t\le N:X_t=i\}$ have support $F=\{i:n_i>0\}$, which we call a
face. Write $M_F$ for the largest probability on that exact support and
$\lambda_F$ for its principal exit rate (Definition~\ref{def:faces}). A face
is admissible if a path of positive probability has exactly that support.
When $T=\tau\log N$ the cost $e^{-T\lambda_F}$ of staying in $F$ and the
cost $N^{-(|F|-1)}$ of sharing the mass of $F$ among its count vectors are
both powers of $N$, and they compete. The paper describes the law of the
count vector on every face at this scale, up to the boundary of the face,
and reads off the face that carries the mode and the times at which it
changes.

\subsection{The law on a face}\label{sec:intro-faces}

Put $\Phi_\tau(F)=\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[first-order face selection]\label{thm:A}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$, and let $T=\tau\log N$ with
$\tau>0$ fixed. Every admissible face $F$ has $M_F=N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)+o(1)}$.
For large $N$ the support of every most likely composition is an admissible
face that minimizes $\Phi_\tau$ among the admissible faces. If the chain can
pass between any two states of each such minimizing face without leaving it,
then the visit fractions of every most likely composition are within
$O(1/\log N)$ of the quasi-ergodic law of its support
(Definition~\ref{def:faces}).
\end{introtheorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii)--(iv).
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull} reads the winners off the lower-left boundary of the
convex hull of the points $(\lambda_F,|F|-1)$, so as $\tau$ grows the
winning size can only grow. The exponent is one functional. The rate
$J_F$ inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the chain
killed on leaving $F$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:restriction} shows that
$J_F$ is the restriction to the face of the functional $J_V$ of the whole
chain, where $V=[d]$ is the face of all states. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:stratified} states the law of the count vector on every
face in one display, as the lattice entropy $(|F|-1)\log N$ of the face
plus $T$ times the Donsker and Varadhan cost of the point.

First order fixes a crossing only up to a factor $1+o(1)$. To locate it up
to $o(1)$ we need the constant in front. Say that a face $F$ satisfies (H)
if the chain can move between any two states of $F$ without leaving $F$, and
the start gives $F$ positive mass. For such faces we prove a local limit
theorem inside the face (Theorem~\ref{thm:local}), and it gives the
following result.

\begin{introtheorem}[face maxima and crossings]\label{thm:B}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, with $k=\lvert F\rvert$ states. Uniformly
for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
 M_F=N^{-(k-1)}\,T^{(k-1)/2}K_F\,e^{-T\lambda_F}\bigl(1+O(1/T+T^2/N)\bigr),
\]
where $K_F>0$ is the explicit constant of Definition~\ref{def:tilt} (for
$k=1$ it is the start mass of the state).
\item Let $F$ and $G$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, and suppose that $F$ has
$\Delta\ge1$ more states than $G$ and an exit rate smaller by $\delta>0$. For
large $N$ the equation $M_F=M_G$ has a root $T$ in $[1,N^{1/3}]$, and every
such root satisfies
\[
 \delta T+\frac\Delta2\log T=\Delta\log N-\log\frac{K_F}{K_G}
 +O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax} and part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. The factor $T^{(k-1)/2}$ appears because the
visit fractions fluctuate on the scale $T^{-1/2}$ in each of the $k-1$ free
directions. Solving the equation in (ii) gives
$T=\frac\Delta\delta[\log N-\frac12\log\log N+O(1)]$, so the coefficient
$\frac12$ of $\log\log N$ holds for every such pair, whatever the graph, the
rates, the sizes or the start, and without reversibility. For reversible
faces $K_F$ involves the square root of a weighted count of spanning trees
(Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}), and on a general face two counts of trees
(Proposition~\ref{prop:forest}). When several faces tie at first order,
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} describes the window of $T$ in which they compete,
and a weak field shifts the line of each face in that window by the field
averaged against its quasi-ergodic law (Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}).
The face maxima hold with a field $v$ that weights a composition by
$e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$, uniformly for $v$ in a compact set, and over a compact
class of reversible chains (Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-facemax}).

The local law stays away from the boundary of the face. At the boundary a
new scale appears. Let $x=Tn_i/N$ be the time spent in a state $i$ whose
count is small, and $S=T-x$. Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} pulls the
visits to $i$ out of the exact run expansion and writes the density on $F$
as a contour integral against the density on the facet $F\setminus\{i\}$
with a complex weight. For a pair face the integral is a Bessel kernel,
$e^{-\gamma x}\sqrt{abS/x}\,I_1(2\sqrt{abSx})$ times the density on the
facet, which is the facet form of Pedler's formula for two-state chains,
and we prove it with an explicit error for all $x$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-face}). The constants $a$, $b$ and $\gamma$ are
explicit for every irreducible chain through the left and right tilt
vectors at the facet profile, and $\gamma-q_i$ is $ab/2$ times an effective
resistance (Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-constants}). For every face,
Theorem~\ref{thm:layer} describes the layer for $x$ up to
a small multiple of $T$. A generalized kernel holds there with relative
error $O(1/T)$. The Bessel kernel holds up to a factor $e^{E}$ with $E$ of
order $1/T+\sqrt{x/T}(1+x)$. For a reversible chain this improves to a
relative error $O(1/T+\sqrt{x/T})$ on the whole range. Without
reversibility we get the relative Bessel form only for $x$ up to a constant
times $T^{1/3}$, and a non-reversible cycle on three states shows that the
restriction is real. The proof rests on a local law of the facet chain
with a small complex weight (Theorem~\ref{thm:L-facet}), which we prove on a
torus through a complex critical point with a Laplace lemma for a complex
Hessian. The near-vertex
law of Section~\ref{sec:vertex}, where one count is large and a few counts
are of order one and the crossing with the vertex moves to $T$ of order
$\sqrt N$, is the discrete pair-face case of this layer, in the regime where
the Laguerre kernel cannot yet be replaced by the Bessel kernel
(Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root}).

\subsection{The complete graph}\label{sec:intro-complete}

On the complete graph with the uniform start, $Q=J-dI$ with $J$ the all-ones
matrix, every face ties at first order and the constants decide. A face of
$k$ states has $K_F=\frac1dk^{k+1/2}(4\pi)^{-(k-1)/2}$, and the $k^{k-2}$
spanning trees of $K_k$ enter through it. Put
$a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-\frac{k+1/2}{k-1}\log k$, write $V_N$ for the
probability of a vertex, and call a composition balanced if its positive
coordinates differ by at most $1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the complete graph]\label{thm:C}
Let $Q=J-dI$ and let the start be uniform.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ be a face of $2\le k\le d$ states. For every $N\ge k$ the equation $M_F=V_N$ has
exactly one root $T_{N,k}$ in $(0,N/(d-1))$, and the switch probability $T_{N,k}/N$ at the
root decreases strictly as $N$ grows. As $N\to\infty$,
\[
 T_{N,k}+\tfrac12\log T_{N,k}=\log N+a_k+O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\item For every $N$ and every $0<T\le N/d$, the most likely compositions with a given support
are exactly the balanced ones.
\item Since $a_2>a_3>\dots>a_d$, for large $N$ and $T$ up to any fixed multiple of $\log N$ the
mode changes exactly once, from the $d$ vertices straight to the balanced compositions with
$d$ positive coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}, part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} and part (iii) is
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}. Parts (i) and (ii) hold at every $N$, so on
the complete graph we know the face maxima and their crossings with a vertex
exactly, and part (iii) gives the mode for large $N$.
Figure~\ref{fig:complete-envelope} shows the window. A start law changes the
constant of a face through its start mass, and a weak field changes it
through the field averaged over the face. Either can let a face of
intermediate size win (Example~\ref{ex:K3-start} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:weakfield-phases}), and one field makes every size win in
turn.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The exact law of the count vector goes back to Whittle~\cite{whittle1955},
Dawson and Good~\cite{dawsongood1957}, Goodman~\cite{goodman1958} and
Good~\cite{good1961}. Good's formula (3) is the run expansion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:runs}, and his formula (10) is the continuum density of
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuum}. What our setting adds to (10) is the killing on
leaving the face and the exponents $m_i-1$ of the times spent in the
states, where $m_i$ is the number of runs in state $i$, and the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuum} checks them. He suggested the saddle-point
analysis of the density and did not carry it out, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is that analysis, uniform in $T$ and with its
constant. Polettini~\cite{polettini2015} and Carugno, Vivo and
Coghi~\cite{cvc2022} derive the exact law from the BEST theorem and the
matrix-tree theorem. Stadje~\cite{stadje1999} gives the generating functions
of the joint numbers of visits and limit laws when one state becomes nearly
absorbing. In continuous time, Brydges, van der Hofstad and
K\"onig~\cite{bhk2007} give the joint density of the local times on the
event that the range is a given set, and Huang, Kious, Sidoravicius and
Tarr\`es~\cite{hkst2018} write it with Bessel functions and spanning trees
for symmetric rates. The Bessel kernel of the boundary layer is Pedler's
occupation-time density for two-state chains~\cite{pedler1971}, continued
by Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir~\cite{kmn2010}, and
Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} is its facet version. Herv\'e and
Ledoux~\cite{herveledoux2013} prove a quantitative local limit theorem for
densities of finite-state Markov additive processes, and Kontoyiannis and
Meyn~\cite[Theorem~6.3]{km2003} give exact large deviation asymptotics for a
bounded functional of a fixed chain. Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is a local form
for the whole vector of counts, for a chain killed on leaving $F$ whose
switch probabilities shrink with $N$.

The rate inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the killed
chain~\cite{dv1975}. Dupuis and Liu~\cite[Section~3.1]{dupuisliu2015} give
it for reversible jump processes, Chen and Deng~\cite{chendeng2013} use it
for the principal Dirichlet eigenvalue, and Guillin, Nectoux and
Wu~\cite{gnw2024} prove a large deviation principle for the occupation
measure of a process conditioned to stay in a set. The quasi-ergodic law
goes back to Darroch and Seneta~\cite{darrochseneta1965,darrochseneta1967},
with the book of Collet, Mart\'inez and San Mart\'in~\cite{cmsm2013} as the
standard reference. Dietz and Sethuraman~\cite{dietz2007,dietz2005} and
Bouguet and Cloez~\cite{bouguetcloez2018} study chains whose switching
decays along the trajectory. Here the rate is fixed during each path and
changes with $N$. Bertini, Gabrielli and Landim~\cite{bgl2024} and
Landim~\cite{landim2023} expand the level-two rate functions of finite
chains in a parameter, and Kim and Landim~\cite{kl2024} extend this to the
joint rate of the empirical measure and the flow~\cite{bfg2014,bfg2015}.
These expansions concern the rate functional of a fixed chain. Here the
functional enters through its restriction to each face, and the competition
is between faces of different dimension.

The question grew out of Problem~131 of Kagey's Open Problem
Collection~\cite{kagey131}, a two-direction crossing problem, whose
two-state case is treated in~\cite{paperA}. This paper \ifarxivbuild replaces our preprint~\cite{paperB} on the complete graph, which is the first version of the same arXiv entry\else supersedes our preprint~\cite{paperB} on the complete graph\fi. Its results on the crossings with a vertex and near a
vertex appear here as cases of the general theory, in
Sections~\ref{sec:complete} and~\ref{sec:vertex}. We cite \Bversion{} for the results that we do not reprove.

\subsection{Plan of the paper}\label{sec:plan}

Section~\ref{sec:model} fixes the notation and gives the exact law of the
composition as a sum over runs. Section~\ref{sec:dv} introduces the killed
functional and the restriction identity. Section~\ref{sec:firstorder}
proves first-order face selection, Section~\ref{sec:secondorder} the local
law with a field, the face maxima, the crossing equation and the tie
windows, and Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} the boundary layer.
Section~\ref{sec:complete} treats the two-state walk and the complete
graph, and Section~\ref{sec:vertex} the compositions near a vertex.
Section~\ref{sec:verify} describes the computer checks and the formal
proofs. The appendices hold the torus and Laplace proofs of the local law,
the proofs for the complete graph and the proofs near a vertex. From the
persistent-walk paper~\cite{paperA} we use two results, the coefficientwise
growth of the polynomials $S_{a,b}$ toward the middle in
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair} and its bound on clipped products in
Appendix~\ref{app:vertex}. We also recall its expansion of the crossing
$z_N$ and its fixed-edge expansion, for comparison only.

A companion paper~\cite{paperC2} asks what the Donsker and Varadhan
functional and the modal supports determine about the generator. It
recovers a reversible generator on a known graph from modal supports, with
a finite-horizon correction, and at low temperature it reads the Freidlin
and Wentzell cycle tree off the modal supports. That paper rests on the killed functional and the first-order
results of Sections~\ref{sec:dv} and~\ref{sec:firstorder}, on
Definition~\ref{def:tilt} with the face maxima of Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax},
and on Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-facemax}. Nothing here depends on it.
