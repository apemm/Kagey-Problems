\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Which states does the most likely occupation-count vector of a slowly
switching Markov chain use? Fix a generator $Q$ on $d$ states and a start
law, and take $N$ steps with transition matrix $I+(T/N)Q$, so that the
chain makes about $T$ switches. The occupation counts
$n_i=\#\{t\le N:X_t=i\}$ have support $F=\{i:n_i>0\}$, which we call a
face. Write $M_F$ for the largest probability on that exact support and
$\lambda_F$ for its principal exit rate (Definition~\ref{def:faces}). A face
is admissible if a path of positive probability has exactly that support.
When $T=\tau\log N$ the cost $e^{-T\lambda_F}$ of staying in $F$ and the
cost $N^{-(|F|-1)}$ of sharing the mass of $F$ among its count vectors are
both powers of $N$, and they compete. The paper describes the law of the
count vector on every face at this scale, reads off the face that carries
the mode and the times at which it changes, and then turns the description
around and asks which generators are consistent with it.

\subsection{The law on a face}\label{sec:intro-faces}

Put $\Phi_\tau(F)=\tau\lambda_F+|F|-1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[first-order face selection]\label{thm:A}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$, and let $T=\tau\log N$ with
$\tau>0$ fixed. Every admissible face $F$ has $M_F=N^{-\Phi_\tau(F)+o(1)}$.
For large $N$ the support of every most likely composition is an admissible
face that minimizes $\Phi_\tau$ among the admissible faces. If the chain can
pass between any two states of each such minimizing face without leaving it,
then the visit fractions of every most likely composition are within
$O(1/\log N)$ of the quasi-ergodic law of its support
(Definition~\ref{def:faces}).
\end{introtheorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is Theorem~\ref{thm:first}(ii)--(iv).
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull} reads the winners off the lower-left boundary of the
convex hull of the points $(\lambda_F,|F|-1)$, so as $\tau$ grows the
winning size can only grow. The exponent is one functional. The rate
$J_F$ inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the chain
killed on leaving $F$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:restriction} shows that
$J_F$ is the restriction to the face of the functional $J_V$ of the whole
chain, where $V=[d]$ is the face of all states. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:stratified} states the law of the count vector on every
face in one display, as the lattice entropy $(|F|-1)\log N$ of the face
plus $T$ times the Donsker and Varadhan cost of the point.

First order fixes a crossing only up to a factor $1+o(1)$. To locate it up
to $o(1)$ we need the constant in front. Say that a face $F$ satisfies (H)
if the chain can move between any two states of $F$ without leaving $F$, and
the start gives $F$ positive mass. For such faces we prove a local limit
theorem inside the face (Theorem~\ref{thm:local}), and it gives the
following result.

\begin{introtheorem}[face maxima and crossings]\label{thm:B}
Fix the rates $q_{ij}$ and the law of $X_1$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, with $k=\lvert F\rvert$ states. Uniformly
for $1\le T\le N^{1/3}$,
\[
 M_F=N^{-(k-1)}\,T^{(k-1)/2}K_F\,e^{-T\lambda_F}\bigl(1+O(1/T+T^2/N)\bigr),
\]
where $K_F>0$ is the explicit constant of Definition~\ref{def:tilt} (for
$k=1$ it is the start mass of the state).
\item Let $F$ and $G$ satisfy \textup{(H)}, and suppose that $F$ has
$\Delta\ge1$ more states than $G$ and an exit rate smaller by $\delta>0$. For
large $N$ the equation $M_F=M_G$ has a root $T$ in $[1,N^{1/3}]$, and every
such root satisfies
\[
 \delta T+\frac\Delta2\log T=\Delta\log N-\log\frac{K_F}{K_G}
 +O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:facemax} and part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. The factor $T^{(k-1)/2}$ appears because the
visit fractions fluctuate on the scale $T^{-1/2}$ in each of the $k-1$ free
directions. Solving the equation in (ii) gives
$T=\frac\Delta\delta[\log N-\frac12\log\log N+O(1)]$, so the coefficient
$\frac12$ of $\log\log N$ holds for every such pair, whatever the graph, the
rates, the sizes or the start, and without reversibility. For reversible
faces $K_F$ involves the square root of a weighted count of spanning trees
(Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}), and on a general face two counts of trees
(Proposition~\ref{prop:forest}). When several faces tie at first order,
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} describes the window of $T$ in which they compete,
and a weak field shifts the line of each face in that window by the field
averaged against its quasi-ergodic law (Proposition~\ref{prop:weakfield}).

The local law stays away from the boundary of the face. At the boundary a
new scale appears. Let $x=Tn_i/N$ be the time spent in a state $i$ whose
count is small, and $S=T-x$. Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} pulls the
visits to $i$ out of the exact run expansion and writes the density on $F$
as a contour integral against the density on the facet $F\setminus\{i\}$
with a complex weight. For a pair face the integral is a Bessel kernel,
$e^{-\gamma x}\sqrt{abS/x}\,I_1(2\sqrt{abSx})$ times the density on the
facet, which is the facet form of Pedler's formula for two-state chains,
and we prove it with an explicit error for all $x$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-face}). The constants $a$, $b$ and $\gamma$ are
explicit for every irreducible chain through the left and right tilt
vectors at the facet profile, and $\gamma-q_i$ is $ab/2$ times an effective
resistance (Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-constants}). For every face with at
most three states the layer theorem holds without further hypothesis. Its
Bessel form is exact up to a factor $e^{E}$ with $E$ of order
$1/T+\sqrt{x/T}(1+x)$ up to $x\le\delta T$, so the relative form holds for
$x\ll T^{1/3}$ when the chain is not reversible, and a generalized kernel
holds with relative error $O(1/T)$ on the whole range. For faces with four
or more states the same statements are conditional on one hypothesis, a
local law with complex weights, which we state, prove for two-state facets,
and leave open in general (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:L} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:layer}). The near-vertex law of Section~\ref{sec:vertex},
where one count is large and a few counts are of order one and the crossing
with the vertex moves to $T$ of order $\sqrt N$, is the discrete pair-face
case of this layer, in the regime where the Laguerre kernel cannot yet be
replaced by the Bessel kernel (Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex-uniform-root}).

\subsection{The complete graph}\label{sec:intro-complete}

On the complete graph with the uniform start, $Q=J-dI$ with $J$ the all-ones
matrix, every face ties at first order and the constants decide. A face of
$k$ states has $K_F=\frac1dk^{k+1/2}(4\pi)^{-(k-1)/2}$, and the $k^{k-2}$
spanning trees of $K_k$ enter through it. Put
$a_k=\frac12\log(4\pi)-\frac{k+1/2}{k-1}\log k$, write $V_N$ for the
probability of a vertex, and call a composition balanced if its positive
coordinates differ by at most $1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[the complete graph]\label{thm:C}
Let $Q=J-dI$ and let the start be uniform.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Let $F$ be a face of $2\le k\le d$ states. For every $N\ge k$ the equation $M_F=V_N$ has
exactly one root $T_{N,k}$ in $(0,N/(d-1))$, and the switch probability $T_{N,k}/N$ at the
root decreases strictly as $N$ grows. As $N\to\infty$,
\[
 T_{N,k}+\tfrac12\log T_{N,k}=\log N+a_k+O\Bigl(\frac1{\log N}\Bigr).
\]
\item For every $N$ and every $0<T\le N/d$, the most likely compositions with a given support
are exactly the balanced ones.
\item Since $a_2>a_3>\dots>a_d$, for large $N$ and $T$ up to any fixed multiple of $\log N$ the
mode changes exactly once, from the $d$ vertices straight to the balanced compositions with
$d$ positive coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-crossing}, part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:simplex-balance} and part (iii) is
Corollary~\ref{cor:simplex-modes}. Figure~\ref{fig:complete-envelope} shows
the window. A start law changes the constant of a face through its start
mass, and a weak field changes it through the field averaged over the
face. Either can let a face of intermediate size win
(Example~\ref{ex:K3-start} and Corollary~\ref{cor:weakfield-phases}), and
one field makes every size win in turn.

\subsection{What the occupation law determines}\label{sec:intro-reconstruction}

Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} turns the description around and asks
which coarse functionals of the law of the counts determine the generator.
The finest is the law itself. Its logarithmic scale on single compositions
is, by Theorem~\ref{thm:stratified}, the Donsker and Varadhan functional
$J_V$. Write $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^\top D_\pi$ for the time reversal of an
irreducible generator with stationary law $\pi$, and call a partition
$[d]=X\cup Y$ with $|X|,|Y|\ge2$ a cut of $Q$ if both blocks $Q[X,Y]$ and
$Q[Y,X]$ have rank at most $1$.

\begin{introtheorem}[what the Donsker and Varadhan functional determines]\label{thm:D}
Let $Q$ and $Q'$ be irreducible generators on $[d]$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $J_V=J'_V$ on the simplex if and only if $-Q$ and $-Q'$ have the same
principal minors.
\item The generators with the principal minors of $-Q$ form a finite set. It
contains $Q$ and $Q^*$. It is $\{Q,Q^*\}$ when $Q$ has no cut, in particular
when $d\le3$, and it is $\{Q\}$ when $Q$ is reversible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{introtheorem}

Part (i) is the equivalence of (i) and (iii) in Corollary~\ref{cor:dv-fiber},
and part (ii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible}(b)--(e). The proof of (i)
needs three inputs. They are the variational formula
$\lambda_V(v)=\min_\alpha(J_V(\alpha)-v\cdot\alpha)$ for
$\lambda_V(v)=-\varrho(Q+\operatorname{diag}v)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:variational}), the irreducibility of the multiaffine
polynomial $\det(-Q_F-\operatorname{diag}w)$ for every irreducible face
(Lemma~\ref{lem:RF-irreducible}), and the Perron-branch lemma, by which the
Perron root on an open set of fields determines that polynomial
(Lemma~\ref{lem:perron-branch}). Part (ii) rests on the matrix theory of
Loewy, Hartfiel and Loewy, Al Ahmadieh and Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and
Raj, who describe the matrices with given principal minors up to diagonal
similarity, through transposition and a cut-transpose move across each cut.
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} answers the question left open by Example~5.4 of Kim
and Landim~\cite{kl2024}, a three-cycle and its reversal with the same
$J_V$. By their Corollary~5.8 the joint functional of the empirical measure
and the empirical flow determines the generator, so adding the flow removes
the whole fiber.

The modal supports give a strengthening. Weight each composition by
$e^{Tv\cdot n/N}$ for a field $v\le0$, which is the chance that the chain
survives when it is killed at known rates, and observe only which face
carries the mode of the surviving composition, as a function of $\tau$ and
$v$. Throughout Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} the field and the time
scale are calibrated, that is, known in the units of $Q$. The data are the
boundaries between the regions of $(\tau,v)$ where one face wins strictly.
Corollary~\ref{cor:dv-fiber} shows that these boundaries determine the
principal minors too, so a single label at each point loses nothing that
$J_V$ keeps. The survival probability itself is a finer observation. It
equals $e^{-T\lambda_V(v)}$ up to a bounded factor, so it gives $\lambda_V$
on all fields and, through the Perron-branch lemma, every principal minor
at once, with no descent through the faces. The coarser label does not
require measuring a probability, only the support of the most likely
composition, and it is the datum that the forward theory describes.
Reading the minors from it needs a descent through the faces, which
exposes the Perron root of every face on an open set of fields
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}). The fields used there depend on the generators
being compared, so this part is a statement of identifiability, with no
design fixed in advance.

For a reversible chain on a known connected graph with $m$ edges a design
is fixed in advance. Fields with constants built from a norm bound and an
edge bound alone expose $d-1$ singleton switches and $m$ pair-entry times,
and these $m+d-1$ numbers determine $Q$ by a Perron reconstruction
(Theorem~\ref{thm:reversible-reconstruction}). No family of fewer than
$m+d-1$ smooth observations can do this (Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality}),
and the reconstruction is Lipschitz in the observed coordinates
(Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}). At a finite horizon the pair-entry times
carry a correction of relative size $\log\log N/(2\log N)$ with an explicit
next term, uniformly over a compact class of chains and starts
(Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-horizon}), and the start bound cannot be dropped
(Remark~\ref{rem:finite-start}).

\emph{What is borrowed and what is new.} The fiber of the principal-minor map of
an irreducible matrix is known up to diagonal similarity and transposition.
The paper adds three things. The first, and the shortest, is the
translation to generators. A Perron and Frobenius normalization picks
exactly one generator in each diagonal class, so the fiber is finite,
every member is irreducible with the spectrum of $Q$, and a cut-transpose
acts as $Q$ on one side of the cut and as $Q^*$ on the other
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible}). The second is the data. The minors are
not observed, and the exposure lemma with the Perron-branch lemma reads all
of them off the modal-support map. The third is the amplitude obstruction.
From the stationary start the law of the count vector is the same under
$Q$ and $Q^*$ for every $N$ and $T$ with $Tq_{\max}\le N$, so from that
start no count statistic separates them. For every start outside a finite
union of proper subspaces the second-order amplitudes of the local law
separate every member of the fiber from $Q$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nonreversible}(f)).

\subsection{Low temperature}\label{sec:intro-lowtemp}

When the rates are $q_{ij}=e^{-V_{ij}/\eta}$ and the time scale is
$\tau=e^{h/\eta}$, the competition between dimension and exit rate takes
place on the Freidlin and Wentzell cycles of the chain. The depth of a face
is the largest depth of a cycle it contains (Theorem~\ref{thm:depth}). Away
from the finite set of cycle depths the winner is a cycle of least size
among those deeper than $h$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:noncritical}). At a critical
depth a finite problem with explicit prefactors decides, and its winners
need not be cycles. A four-state reversible example has a three-state
noncycle winner on an open window of the rescaled time
$x=Te^{-\gamma/\eta}/\log N$ at a critical height $\gamma$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} and
Example~\ref{ex:four-state}). The controls read the cycle tree. A field of
Arrhenius size on a face rewards it. The largest field exponent at which a
cycle still wins is its depth, and the depth of every face is the largest
such threshold over its subfaces (Theorem~\ref{thm:attraction}). The
depths determine the cycles, since a set is a cycle exactly when every
proper subset is strictly shallower, for every Arrhenius family with all
rates positive and with no nondegeneracy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cycles-from-depths}), and the window endpoints determine
the prefactors of the faces on the envelope
(Theorem~\ref{thm:window-prefactors}).

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The exact law of the count vector goes back to Whittle~\cite{whittle1955},
Dawson and Good~\cite{dawsongood1957}, Goodman~\cite{goodman1958} and
Good~\cite{good1961}. Good's formula (3) is the run expansion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:runs}, and his formula (10) is the continuum density of
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuum}, up to the killing and the exponent $n_i-1$ that
our setting needs. He suggested the saddle-point analysis of the density
and did not carry it out, and Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is that analysis,
uniform in $T$ and with its constant. Polettini~\cite{polettini2015} and
Carugno, Vivo and Coghi~\cite{cvc2022} derive the exact law from the BEST
theorem and the matrix-tree theorem. Stadje~\cite{stadje1999} gives the
generating functions of the joint numbers of visits and limit laws when one
state becomes nearly absorbing. In continuous time, Brydges, van der Hofstad
and K\"onig~\cite{bhk2007} give the joint density of the local times on the
event that the range is a given set, and Huang, Kious, Sidoravicius and
Tarr\`es~\cite{hkst2018} write it with Bessel functions and spanning trees
for symmetric rates. The Bessel kernel of the boundary layer is Pedler's
occupation-time density for two-state chains~\cite{pedler1971}, continued
by Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir~\cite{kmn2010}, and Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer}
is its facet version. Herv\'e and Ledoux~\cite{herveledoux2013} prove a
quantitative local limit theorem for densities of finite-state Markov
additive processes, and Kontoyiannis and Meyn~\cite[Theorem~6.3]{km2003}
give exact large deviation asymptotics for a bounded functional of a fixed
chain. Theorem~\ref{thm:local} is a local form for the whole vector of
counts, for a chain killed on leaving $F$ whose switch probabilities shrink
with $N$.

The rate inside a face is the Donsker and Varadhan functional of the killed
chain~\cite{dv1975}. Dupuis and Liu~\cite[Section~3.1]{dupuisliu2015} give
it for reversible jump processes, Chen and Deng~\cite{chendeng2013} use it
for the principal Dirichlet eigenvalue, and Guillin, Nectoux and
Wu~\cite{gnw2024} prove a large deviation principle for the occupation
measure of a process conditioned to stay in a set. The quasi-ergodic law
goes back to Darroch and Seneta~\cite{darrochseneta1965,darrochseneta1967},
with the book of Collet, Mart\'inez and San Mart\'in~\cite{cmsm2013} as the
standard reference. Dietz and Sethuraman~\cite{dietz2007,dietz2005} and
Bouguet and Cloez~\cite{bouguetcloez2018} study chains whose switching
decays along the trajectory. Here the rate is fixed during each path and
changes with $N$. Bertini, Gabrielli and Landim~\cite{bgl2024} and
Landim~\cite{landim2023} expand the level-two rate functions of finite
chains in a parameter, and Kim and Landim~\cite{kl2024} extend this to the
joint rate of the empirical measure and the flow~\cite{bfg2014,bfg2015}.
Their Section~5 shows that the Donsker and Varadhan functional determines
the exit rates and the products $q_{ij}q_{ji}$, which with the stationary
law determine a reversible generator, and gives a three-cycle and its
reversal as an example of non-identifiability. The matrix theory behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} is Hartfiel and Loewy~\cite{hartfielloewy1984} and
Loewy~\cite{loewy1986} for the case without a cut, Engel and
Schneider~\cite{engelschneider1980} for diagonal similarity to a symmetric
matrix, and Al Ahmadieh~\cite{alahmadieh2023} and Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar
and Raj~\cite{cggr2024} for the general fiber. Delmas, Dronnier and
Zitt~\cite{ddz2024} prove a multiplicative analogue of the Perron-branch
lemma. The reversible coordinates and the dimension count of
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality} are those of Wolfer and
Watanabe~\cite{wolferwatanabe2021}.

The cycle framework of Section~\ref{sec:lowtemp} is that of Freidlin and
Wentzell~\cite{freidlinwentzell2012}, Catoni~\cite{catoni1999}, Olivieri
and Vares~\cite{olivierivares2005}, Trouv\'e~\cite{trouve1996} and Cirillo,
Nardi and Sohier~\cite{cirillonardisohier2015}. The depth identity rests on
Catoni's mean exit theorem. The logarithmic asymptotics of the principal
eigenvalue of a killed generator with Arrhenius rates, and its graph and
determinant expressions, go back to Wentzell~\cite{wentzell1972} and to
Freidlin and Wentzell~\cite[Chapter~6]{freidlinwentzell2012}, so the
prefactor proposition is classical in substance. What is new there is the
score of dimension against exit rate, the finite competition of envelopes
at a critical depth with its noncycle window, and the attraction design.

The question grew out of Problem~131 of Kagey's Open Problem
Collection~\cite{kagey131}, a two-direction crossing problem, whose
two-state case is treated in~\cite{paperA}. The present paper incorporates
the symmetric-model preprint~\cite{paperB}.

\subsection{Plan of the paper}\label{sec:plan}

Section~\ref{sec:model} fixes the notation and gives the exact law of the
composition as a sum over runs. Section~\ref{sec:dv} introduces the killed
functional and the restriction identity. Section~\ref{sec:firstorder}
proves first-order face selection, Section~\ref{sec:secondorder} the local
law with a field, the face maxima, the crossing equation and the tie
windows, and Section~\ref{sec:boundary-layer} the boundary layer.
Section~\ref{sec:complete} treats the two-state walk and the complete
graph, and Section~\ref{sec:vertex} the compositions near a vertex.
Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} proves the reconstruction theorems and
Section~\ref{sec:lowtemp} the low-temperature results.
Section~\ref{sec:verify} describes the computer checks and the formal
proofs. The appendices hold the torus and Laplace proofs of the local law,
the proofs for the complete graph and the proofs near a vertex. We quote a
few results of~\cite{paperA}, namely the run count, the growth of the
polynomials $S_{a,b}$ toward the middle, the uniform Bessel bound
recalled in~\eqref{eq:planar-bessel} and the expansion of $z_N$.
