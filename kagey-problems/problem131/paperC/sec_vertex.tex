\section{Near a vertex}\label{sec:vertex}

So far the positive coordinates of a composition were of order $N$, since
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} holds on compact parts of the open face. Near a vertex of the complete
graph the crossing has another scale. We keep the setting and the notation of
Section~\ref{sec:completegraph}, namely $Q=J-dI$, the uniform start, $p$, $u$, $v$, and
$S_n(u)=P_N(n)/V_N$ by~\eqref{eq:SnVN}. Let one count $b$ be large and let the other
positive counts $a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ be small compared with $b$, with $\ell\ge1$ fixed. We put
$|a|=a_1+\cdots+a_\ell$ and write $S_{(b,a)}$ for $S_n$ with $n=(b,a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)$. These
rare counts are not the constants $a_k$ of Section~\ref{sec:completegraph}, which do not
appear here. As $S_n$ does not depend on the start, $P_N(n)\le(\max_i\mu_i)p^{N-1}S_n(u)$ for
every start, so upper bounds on $S_n$ hold for every start.

A rare run away from the ends needs two switches, which gives a factor $u^2$, and it can go in
about $b$ places. So the rare counts enter through $bu^2$, and the crossing moves to odds $u$ of
order $b^{-1/2}$, that is, to $T$ of order $\sqrt N$ instead of $\log N$. For an integer $a\ge1$
put
\[
B_a(w)=\sum_{m=1}^{a}\binom{a-1}{m-1}\frac{w^m}{m!},\qquad
\mathcal I(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{x^j}{j!\,(j+1)!}=\frac{I_1(2\sqrt x)}{\sqrt x},
\]
where $I_1$ is the modified Bessel function~\cite[Eq.~10.25.2]{dlmf}, and $\mathcal I(0)=1$.
By~\cite[Eq.~18.5.12]{dlmf}, $B_a(w)=(w/a)L^{(1)}_{a-1}(-w)$ with the Laguerre polynomial
$L^{(1)}_{a-1}$, so the next lemma is a quantitative form of the limit of Laguerre polynomials
to Bessel functions.

\begin{lemma}[a uniform Laguerre--Bessel inequality]\label{lem:uniform-laguerre-bessel}
For every integer $a\ge1$ and real $w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-laguerre-bessel}
0\le1-\frac{B_a(w)}{w\,\mathcal I(aw)}\le\frac w2 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:vertex}. Put
\[
\mathcal B(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}B_{a_i}(y),\qquad
\kappa(y)=\frac{y\,\mathcal B'(y)}{\mathcal B(y)} .
\]
The polynomial $\mathcal B$ has positive coefficients, $\mathcal B(0)=0$ and
$\mathcal B(1)\ge1$, so $\mathcal B(y_a)=1$ has exactly one positive root $y_a$, and $y_a\le1$.
The polynomial $S_{(b,a)}$ has nonnegative coefficients, zero constant term and a positive
coefficient at $u^\ell$, so $S_{(b,a)}(u)=1$ has exactly one positive root $u_{b,a}$, the
crossing odds of the composition $(b,a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)$ with a vertex.

\begin{theorem}[the vertex law]\label{thm:vertex-uniform-root}
Fix $\ell\ge1$ and put
\[
 c_\ell=\ell+\sqrt\ell+1,\qquad
 \varepsilon=\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}},\qquad \delta=16c_\ell\varepsilon.
\]
For all positive counts $b,a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ with $\delta\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-uniform-bracket}
 (1-\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}\ \le u_{b,a}\le
 (1+\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b},\qquad \mathcal B(y_a)=1.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-uniform-root}
 \biggl|\frac{u_{b,a}}{\sqrt{y_a/b}}-1\biggr|\le16c_\ell\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}}
 \qquad\text{whenever }\sqrt{\frac{|a|+1}{b}}\le\frac1{32c_\ell},
\end{equation}
so $u_{b,a}=\sqrt{y_a/b}\,(1+o(1))$ as $(|a|+1)/b\to0$. The constants are not
optimized.
\end{theorem}

For fixed rare counts the bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-randomization} give
$S_{(b,a)}(\sqrt{y/b})\to\mathcal B(y)$ as $b\to\infty$, so the crossing is near
$\sqrt{y_a/b}$, and the theorem shows that this stays correct uniformly as long as $|a|=o(b)$. For $d=2$ and $\ell=1$ its leading term
$\sqrt{y_a/b}$ is that of the fixed-edge expansion of~\cite[Theorem~4.10]{paperA}, where
$a$ is fixed. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:vertex}. It rests on two facts. First,
$\log\mathcal B$ is concave, and for $0<y\le1$ its elasticity $\kappa(y)$ lies between
$\max\{\ell,\frac12\sqrt{|a|y}\}$ and $\ell+\sqrt{\ell|a|y}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-elasticity}). Second, $S_{(b,a)}(u)=(1-u)^{|a|}\E[\mathcal B(v\mathcal X)]$
exactly, where $v=u/(1-u)$ and $\mathcal X$ is a gamma variable plus a compound binomial sum
with mean $2+(b-1)u$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-randomization}). Jensen's inequality then gives
$S_{(b,a)}(u)\ge(1-u)^{|a|}\mathcal B(bu^2)$, which exceeds $1$ at $u=(1+\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}$. The
tangent line of $\log\mathcal B$ at $bu^2$ and the moment generating function of $\mathcal X$
give an upper bound that is below $1$ at $u=(1-\delta)\sqrt{y_a/b}$.

\begin{remark}[the boundary of the simplex]\label{rem:B-boundary}
The preprint~\cite{paperB} also treats compositions whose small coordinates are not near a vertex, and
we quote these results without using them. It gives a Bessel majorant for every positive count
vector, with relative error $O(L^2/N)$ when $T=O(L)$~\cite[Theorem~6.2]{paperB}. When at least
two coordinates are of order $N$, a small coordinate starts to matter once it has size of order
$N/(\log N)^2$~\cite[Remark~6.3 and Theorem~C.1]{paperB}, and smaller rare counts change the
expansion of the crossing only by $o(1)$~\cite[Corollary~C.3]{paperB}.
\end{remark}
