\title[Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains]{Occupation counts of rarely switching Markov chains: stratified local laws, crossings and the boundary layer}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Arjun} \sur{Pemmasani}}\email{apemmasani@hmc.edu}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Mathematics}, \orgname{Harvey Mudd College}, \orgaddress{\city{Claremont}, \state{CA}, \postcode{91711}, \country{USA}}}

\abstract{A Markov chain on finitely many states makes about $\tau\log N$ switches
in $N$ steps. We ask which states its most likely vector of occupation
counts uses. On each face of the simplex the law weighs a lattice entropy
of one power of $N$ per dimension against the Donsker and Varadhan cost of
the chain killed on leaving the face, which is the restriction of the cost
of the whole chain. A local limit theorem in each face gives an explicit
constant. It locates every crossing between irreducible faces up to
$o(1)$, with a coefficient $1/2$ of $\log\log N$ independent of the
chain, and describes the window of tied faces. Near a facet of a face
the law is the facet law times an explicit kernel, up
to a relative error $O(1/T)$ for $T$ switches. For a reversible chain the
kernel is a Bessel kernel up to a relative error that is small when the
time $x$ in the rare state is $o(T)$. Without reversibility we prove the
Bessel form in logarithmic form, and in relative form only for $x$ up to
order $T^{1/3}$, and numerics on a cycle of three states show that this
restriction is real. On the complete graph all faces tie at first order.
There each face crosses a vertex exactly once at every $N$, the most likely
compositions on each face are balanced whenever $T\le N/d$ on $d$ states,
and for large $N$ the mode changes exactly once.}

\keywords{occupation times of Markov chains, mode, local limit theorem, principal Dirichlet eigenvalue, Donsker and Varadhan functional, Bessel kernel}
