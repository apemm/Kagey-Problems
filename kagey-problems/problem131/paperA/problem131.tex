\author{Arjun Pemmasani\\[2pt]
\small Department of Mathematics, Harvey Mudd College\\
\small Claremont, CA 91711, USA\\
\small \texttt{apemmasani@hmc.edu}}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In a finite zero-field Ising chain, the weight of the configurations with a
given magnetization is a classical sum over domain walls, and for long chains
it is close to a modified Bessel function. We bound the relative error of
this Bessel form explicitly, for every length, every coupling and every
magnetization, also when the number of domain walls grows with the length,
and the bound fixes the sign of the first correction. We apply it to the
coupling at which the all-up configuration and zero magnetization
(magnetization one for an odd number of spins) become equally likely, which
has recently been described as a thermal transition of the finite chain and
estimated by an approximate formula and a numerical fit. We prove its
asymptotics, with explicit constants, for free and for periodic ends. The
coupling grows like half the logarithm of the length minus half the logarithm
of that logarithm, the term that the earlier approximations lack and that
explains why a fit on chains of moderate length finds a slope below one half.
At the crossing the central magnetization carries about as many domain walls
as the logarithm of the length, so the crossing sharpens only like the inverse
logarithm of the length, not like the inverse length as at a first-order
transition. For free ends we also trap the crossing in an explicit interval at
every length.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} exact results; finite-size scaling; rigorous results in statistical mechanics.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The crossing}

The weight $Z_N(M)$ of the configurations of a finite Ising chain with
magnetization $M$, which is the partition function of the
fixed-magnetization ensemble, is a classical finite sum over domain
walls~\cite{ising1925,antal2004,dantchev2022}. When the mean number of domain
walls stays bounded as the chain grows, it is close to a modified Bessel
function~\cite{antal2004,dantchev2023,dantchev2024}. We bound the relative
error of this Bessel form explicitly at every length, every coupling and every
magnetization (Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-M}). The error has one sign, and it stays small when
the mean number of domain walls grows like the logarithm of the length. Our
main application is a crossing that lives in exactly this regime.

Take $N\ge2$ spins $\sigma_i=\pm1$ with free ends and zero-field Ising
weight $\exp(J\sum_{i<N}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})$, where $J>0$. Here $J$ is
dimensionless, and it is the product $\beta J$ of the inverse temperature and
the exchange coupling in the notation of~\cite{stepanyan2024}. The all-up
configuration is the only one with magnetization $\sigma_1+\dots+\sigma_N=N$.
Zero magnetization (or magnetization $1$ for odd $N$, and the same holds for
$-1$) comes from many configurations. For small $J$ domain walls cost
little and a central magnetization is the more likely of the two, and for large $J$
the all-up configuration is. They are equally likely at a single coupling,
the \emph{central crossing}. Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024} study
this crossing in the periodic chain as a thermal transition of a finite
system, the analogue of the temperature of a first-order transition, and
approximate it by $\beta J\approx(2\log N-1)/5$. Like them, we compare one
central bin with the single all-up configuration (Remark~\ref{rem:convention}
says what changes for other comparisons). We find its asymptotics with
explicit constants for free and for periodic ends, and for free ends we bound
it at every $N$.

Read from left to right, the spins are the steps of a persistent random
walk~\cite{goldstein1951,gillis1955}, a correspondence that
Garc\'ia-Pelayo~\cite{garciapelayo2009} uses to derive the exact
magnetization laws of the open and the closed chain, also in a field. The first step is fair, and
each later step repeats the previous one with probability $p$, where
$e^{-2J}=(1-p)/p$. The magnetization is the position after $N$ steps, so the
walk that never turns, which reaches an end, competes with the many walks
that end in the middle.

With two steps the walk ends in the middle exactly when it turns once, so the
three bins have probabilities $\frac p2$, $1-p$ and $\frac p2$, and they are
level at $p=\frac23$. With three steps the bins have probabilities
$\frac{p^2}2$, $\frac{1-p^2}2$, $\frac{1-p^2}2$ and $\frac{p^2}2$, and they are
level at $p=1/\sqrt2$. In terms of the coupling these two crossings are at
$J=\frac12\log2$ and $J=\frac12\log(1+\sqrt2)$. In general exactly one word reaches a given endpoint,
namely the word that never turns, and very many words reach the middle by
turning a few times (each turn brings a factor of about $1-p$).

The middle bin and the endpoints are level when the walk turns about $\log N$
times on average. More precisely, the crossing value $p_N$ of $p$ satisfies
$N(1-p_N)=\log N-\frac12\log\log N-0.467\ldots+o(1)$, and for every $N\ge2$
we trap $N(1-p_N)$ between two explicit functions of $N$ whose distance tends
to $0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n}). For $p<p_N$ the middle is more likely, and
for $p>p_N$ the ends are. In terms of the coupling, the crossing is at
$J=\frac12(\log N-\log\log N)+O(\log\log N/\log N)$ for free and for periodic
ends, and the correlation length there is about $N/(2\log N)$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:couplings}). The approximations of~\cite{stepanyan2024}
have no $\log\log N$ term, and this term explains why the slope of their
numerical fit is below $\frac12$ (Table~\ref{tab:couplings}). At the
crossing the central bin carries about $\log N$ domain walls, so its energy
exceeds that of the all-up configuration only by $2\log N-\log\log N+O(1)$,
and the
crossing sharpens like $1/\log N$ in the coupling, not like $1/N$ as at a
first-order transition (Proposition~\ref{prop:walls}).

The crossing lies between two regimes in which the law is well understood.
When $p=1-\tau/N$ with $\tau>0$ fixed, the rescaled walk tends to the integrated
telegraph process and the magnetization per spin has a scaling
limit~\cite[Theorem~2.1 and Remark~3.4]{herrmann2010}. When $0<p<1$ is fixed,
the bin has a normal limit (Theorem~\ref{thm:clt}). At the crossing $\tau$ grows
like $\log N$, so neither limit decides it, and we need bounds that hold
uniformly in $\tau$. Our main tool is a comparison of the middle bin with a
modified Bessel function, with a relative error that is explicit for every $N$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel}).

A walk with $d$ directions, which
is the $d$-state Potts chain, is treated in~\cite{paperB}, and general slowly
switching chains in~\cite{paperC}.

\subsection{Setting and notation}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{The walk.} The walk makes $N\ge1$ steps. We record a run as a word
$w=w_1\cdots w_N$ in the letters $\sR$ (right) and $\sL$ (left). The letter $w_1$
is $\sR$ or $\sL$ with probability $\frac12$, and given $w_1,\dots,w_i$,
\[
\Prob(w_{i+1}=w_i)=p,\qquad \Prob(w_{i+1}\ne w_i)=q=1-p .
\]
An index $i<N$ with $w_i\ne w_{i+1}$ is a \emph{switch}. Let $\chg(w)$ be the
number of switches and $\rep(w)=N-1-\chg(w)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wordprob}
\Prob(w)=\tfrac12\,p^{\rep(w)}q^{\chg(w)} .
\end{equation}
For $p>0$ we put $u=q/p$, the odds of a switch.

\paragraph{Bins.} The walk ends in bin $k$ if $w$ has $k$ letters $\sR$, for
$0\le k\le N$. We write $P_N(k)$ for the probability of bin $k$, and $P_0(0)=1$.
Bins $0$ and $N$ are the \emph{endpoints}. Only the constant word
$\sL\cdots\sL$ reaches bin $0$, and only $\sR\cdots\sR$ reaches bin $N$, so
\[
P_N(0)=P_N(N)=\tfrac12p^{N-1}\qquad(N\ge1).
\]
A middle bin is $m_N=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$. For odd $N$ the two middle bins
have equal probability by symmetry.

\paragraph{Spins.} Put $\sigma_i=+1$ if $w_i=\sR$ and $\sigma_i=-1$ if
$w_i=\sL$, and $X_N=\sigma_1+\dots+\sigma_N$. The bin is $K_N=(N+X_N)/2$. Since
$\chg(w)=\sum_{i<N}(1-\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})/2$, equation~\eqref{eq:wordprob}
shows that $\Prob(w)$ is proportional to
$\exp(J\sum_{i<N}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})$ with $e^{-2J}=u$. So the
spins form the Ising chain in zero field with free boundaries, and $J>0$
exactly when $p>\frac12$.

\paragraph{Scales.} Let $L=\log N$ and $\tau=Nq$. Each of the $N-1$ steps after
the first switches with probability $q$, so the expected number of switches is
$(N-1)q=\tau-q$. We call $\tau$ the \emph{switching scale}. In the Ising chain a switch is a domain wall, and
$q=1/(1+e^{2J})$, so $\tau$ is the expected number of domain walls plus $q$. The central crossing has
$\tau$ of order $L$. Near the edges the crossings have larger $\tau$, up to order
$\sqrt N$ for the bins next to an edge (Section~\ref{sec:allbin}).
We use several equivalent parameters, namely the persistence $p$, the odds
$u=q/p=e^{-2J}$ of a switch, the coupling $J$, the switching scale $\tau=Nq$,
and in the Bessel estimates $w=Nu$ and $x=w/2$. Near the central crossing
$u$ is of order $L/N$, and then $\tau$, $Nu$ and $2x$ differ by $O(L^2/N)$.

\paragraph{Ratios and crossings.} For a bin $k$ with $1\le k\le N-1$ we write
$a=k$ and $b=N-k$ for its numbers of letters $\sR$ and $\sL$. For $p>0$ the
ratio of bin $k$ to an endpoint depends on $p$ only through $u$, and it is a
polynomial in $u$ with nonnegative integer coefficients
(\eqref{eq:allbin-polynomial} below). We write
\[
S_{a,b}(u)=\frac{P_N(a)}{P_N(0)},\qquad S_N(u)=S_{m_N,\,N-m_N}(u)
\]
for this ratio and for its value at the middle. \emph{The crossing} of bin $k$
is the value of $p$ at which bin $k$ is exactly as likely as an endpoint, that
is, $S_{a,b}(u)=1$. It is unique (Section~\ref{sec:allbin}), and we write
$p_{a,b}$ for it and $u_{a,b}=(1-p_{a,b})/p_{a,b}$ for its odds. The
\emph{central crossing} is $p_N=p_{m_N,N-m_N}$, with odds $u_N$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zN}
z_N=N(1-p_N)=\frac{Nu_N}{1+u_N}
\end{equation}
is the value of $\tau$ there.

\paragraph{Special functions and asymptotic notation.} The functions $I_0$ and
$I_1$ are the modified Bessel functions of the first kind, of orders zero and
one. We use them through their power
series and their behavior at infinity~\cite[Eqs.~10.25.2, 10.40.1]{dlmf},
\[
I_0(2x)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{x^{2r}}{(r!)^2},\quad
I_1(2x)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{x^{2r+1}}{r!\,(r+1)!},\quad
I_\nu(z)=\frac{e^z}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\Bigl(1-\frac{4\nu^2-1}{8z}+O(z^{-2})\Bigr)
\]
as $z\to+\infty$ along the positive real axis, for $\nu=0,1$.
We write $f\sim g$ if $f/g\to1$. Each $O(\cdot)$ comes with the parameters it
is uniform in, and $O_a(\cdot)$ means that the constant may depend on $a$.

\paragraph{Ising dictionary.} Sections~\ref{sec:exact} to~\ref{sec:allbin}
are written in the variables of the walk, and they translate as follows.
Bin $k$ is magnetization $X_N=2k-N$, so the endpoints are full magnetization
$\pm N$. A middle bin has magnetization zero for even $N$ and either $-1$
or $1$ for odd $N$. A switch is a domain wall, and its odds $u=e^{-2J}$ are the
Boltzmann factor of one wall. If $Z_N(M)$ is the sum of the weights
$\exp(J\sum_{i<N}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})$ of the configurations with
magnetization $M$, then $Z_N(N)=e^{(N-1)J}$ and
$S_{a,b}(u)=Z_N(a-b)/Z_N(N)$. The crossing of bin $k$ is the coupling
$J_{a,b}=-\frac12\log u_{a,b}$ at which magnetization $a-b$ and
magnetization $N$ are equally likely, and at the center it is
$J_N^{\mathrm F}=-\frac12\log u_N$. The correlation length of the infinite
chain in zero field, with no constraint on the magnetization, is
$\xi(J)=-1/\log\tanh J$, and $\tanh J=(1-u)/(1+u)$, so
$N/\xi=N\log\frac{1+u}{1-u}=2Nu(1+O(u^2))$. Since $\tau=Nu/(1+u)$, this gives
$\tau=\frac N{2\xi}(1+O(u))$ as $u\to0$. To leading order, the switching scale is half the
length of the chain measured in correlation lengths. It is exactly the
scaling variable $\zeta=N(1-\tanh J)/2$ of Antal, Droz and
R\'acz~\cite{antal2004} (we keep the letter $\zeta$ for $\zeta_N$ below).

\subsection{Main results}

\paragraph{The coupling at the crossing.} In Ising terms the free central
crossing is the coupling $J_N^{\mathrm F}=-\frac12\log u_N$, and
$J_N^{\mathrm P}$ is the coupling at the periodic one. Put
$c_0=\frac12\log(\pi/8)=-0.467\ldots$ and $c_{\mathrm P}=\frac12\log(\pi/2)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing} and Corollary~\ref{cor:couplings} give
\[
J_N^X=\tfrac12(\log N-\log\log N)+\frac{\frac14\log\log N-\frac12c_X}{\log N}
+O\Bigl(\frac{(\log\log N)^2}{(\log N)^2}\Bigr)
\]
for free ends ($X=\mathrm F$, with $c_{\mathrm F}=c_0$) and for periodic ends
($X=\mathrm P$). Since
$c_{\mathrm P}=c_0+\log2$, periodic ends lower the coupling at the crossing by
$(\log2)/(2\log N)$ to leading order. At either crossing the correlation
length is about $N/(2\log N)$, a vanishing fraction of the chain. The
approximation $\beta J\approx(2\log N-1)/5$ of~\cite[Eq.~(16)]{stepanyan2024}
grows too slowly. At $N=10^5$ the exact periodic coupling is $4.576$, the
periodic case of the display, without its error term, gives $4.578$, and the approximation gives
$4.405$ (Table~\ref{tab:couplings}).

\paragraph{A Bessel bound at every $N$.} Put $h=N/2$ and
$F(x)=2x(I_0(2x)+I_1(2x))$. Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel} says that for every
$N\ge2$ and every $x>0$,
\[
0\le1-\frac{hS_N(x/h)}{F(x)}\le\frac{x(x+1)}h .
\]
Near the crossing $x=Nu/2$ is about $L/2$, so the relative error is
$O(L^2/N)$ (tiny for large $N$). Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel} gives the
same kind of bound for every bin, with relative error at most $Nu^2/2+u$.

\paragraph{The central crossing at every $N$.} Let $\zeta_N$ be the positive
root of $8\zeta e^{2\zeta}=\pi N^2$, that is, $\zeta_N=\frac12W_0(\pi N^2/4)$
with $W_0$ the principal branch of Lambert's function, and let
$\varepsilon_N=\zeta_N(\zeta_N+1)/N+1/(16\zeta_N^2)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n}
says that
\[
\zeta_N+\frac1{8\zeta_N}-\varepsilon_N<z_N<\zeta_N+\frac1{8\zeta_N}
\qquad\text{for every }N\ge2.
\]
Note that $\varepsilon_N\to0$. In terms of the coupling this traps
$J_N^{\mathrm F}$ between $\frac12\log(N/y-1)$ at
$y=\zeta_N+\frac1{8\zeta_N}$ and at $y=\zeta_N+\frac1{8\zeta_N}-\varepsilon_N$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(a)). For $N\ge175$ the proof is analytic. For
$2\le N\le174$ we decide the sign of $S_N-1$ at two rationals, one on each side
of the root, in exact integer arithmetic, and the Lean kernel repeats this
check. The theorem has two simpler consequences. With $c_0$ as above, the
crossing satisfies
\[
z_N+\tfrac12\log z_N=L+c_0+\frac1{8z_N}+R_N,\qquad
|R_N|\le\frac1{4L^2}+\frac{L^2+L}N\quad(N\ge2)
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-eq}), and as $N\to\infty$
\[
z_N=L-\tfrac12\log L+c_0+\frac{\frac14\log L-\frac12c_0+\frac18}{L}
+O\Bigl(\frac{(\log L)^2}{L^2}\Bigr)
\]
(Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}). The convergence is slow, and $z_N/L$ is still
only $0.84$ at $N=10^4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:crossing}).

\paragraph{Every bin.} Let bin $a$ be at distance $a$ from the nearer edge,
with $b=N-a\ge a$. Next to the edge the crossing is at
$p_N^{\mathrm{adj}}=(1+\sqrt{N-1})/(2+\sqrt{N-1})$. For $N\ge4$ the two bins
next to the edges are the least likely of all at the central crossing
(Proposition~\ref{prop:adjacent}). As $a\to\infty$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-expansion} expands $2\sqrt{ab}\,u_{a,b}$ to the same
order as $z_N$, uniformly in $b\ge a$. For fixed $a$ the crossing comes
instead from a Laguerre polynomial. Then $u_{a,b}=c_a/\sqrt b-1/b+O_a(b^{-3/2})$,
where $c_a^2$ is the positive root of an explicit polynomial of degree $a$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:edge-laguerre}). The two regimes match
(Theorem~\ref{thm:edge-matching}).

\paragraph{Periodic boundaries.} If the steps are closed into a loop, so that
the last step also interacts with the first, the central crossing moves by
$(\log2)/N$ in the odds to leading order (Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing}).
Under either boundary, at odds $u_N^X+t/N$, where $u_N^X$ is the central
crossing of that boundary, the ratio of bin $N/2+yN/\sqrt{\log N}$ to an
endpoint tends to $e^{t-2y^2}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window}).

\paragraph{Domain walls and the energy gap.} At the free crossing the central
bin carries $z_N+\frac12+O(1/z_N)$ domain walls on average
(Proposition~\ref{prop:walls}), so its mean energy exceeds that of the
all-up configuration by $2\log N-\log\log N+O(1)$ in units of the exchange
constant. At
a first-order transition the two coexisting phases differ in energy by an
amount proportional to $N$. Accordingly the ratio of the central bin to an
endpoint changes by a factor $e$ over a range of couplings of width about
$1/(2\log N)$. The $\log\log N$ term is the cost of comparing full order
with one bin instead of the whole disordered band. The probability that the
chain is fully ordered is $\frac12$ at a coupling larger than
$J_N^{\mathrm F}$ by $\frac12\log\log N+O(1)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:ordered}).

We prove these results in Sections~\ref{sec:exact} to~\ref{sec:periodicwindow},
in this order. Section~\ref{sec:verify} lists what was computed and what is in
Lean, and the appendix has the longer proofs of Section~\ref{sec:middle}.

\subsection{Related work}

Early treatments of the correlated random walk include Goldstein~\cite{goldstein1951}
and Gillis~\cite{gillis1955}. Renshaw and Henderson~\cite{renshaw1981} study the
symmetric correlated walk, derive exact transition probabilities and
investigate limiting distributions. Konno~\cite[Lemma~2 and Theorem~7]{konno2009}
writes the exact law with the hypergeometric function ${}_2F_1$ and proves the
integrated telegraph limit when $p=1-\theta/N$ with $\theta\in(0,1)$ fixed.
Herrmann and Vallois~\cite[Theorem~2.1 and Remark~3.4]{herrmann2010}
give the process limit and its law, including the Bessel density and endpoint
atoms. With $\tau$ replaced by $Nu$, their law gives the Bessel form of every
bin that we use in Section~\ref{sec:allbin-bessel}. These fixed-scale limits
do not by themselves give the uniform finite-$N$ bounds needed when $\tau$
grows like $\log N$. Dekking and Kong~\cite{dekkingkong2011} study the Markov
binomial law, give its variance and find when it has one, two or three modes.
Their results already give the crossing of the bins next to the edges and the
ordering of the bins in a row (Section~\ref{sec:allbin-order}).

For the Ising chain, Antal, Droz and R\'acz~\cite{antal2004} give the
magnetization law at a fixed scale $\tau$ under several boundary conditions, and
Dantchev and Rudnick~\cite{dantchev2022} give exact free and periodic
coefficients. Dantchev, Tonchev and Rudnick replace these coefficients by
Bessel functions at a fixed value of their scaling variable $x_t=2Nu$ (which is
about $2\tau$), for periodic
ends~\cite[Section~4.3.1 and Appendix~D]{dantchev2023} and with a relative
error $O(1/N)$ for free ends~\cite[Section~3, Eq.~(3.10)]{dantchev2024}. These
errors are stated for $x_t$ of order one, while at the crossing $x_t$ grows
like $2\log N$. The scaling variable of Antal, Droz and R\'acz is our $\tau$,
and their fixed-$\tau$ scaling law already predicts the central crossing if
one evaluates it at $\tau$ of order $\log N$ (a regime in which it is not
proved). In that law the magnetization per spin has a density, and
neighboring bins are $2/N$ apart. So the middle bin and an endpoint balance
when the endpoint atom $e^{-\tau}/2$ equals $2/N$ times the density at zero
magnetization, which is $\frac12\tau e^{-\tau}(I_0(\tau)+I_1(\tau))$
by~\cite[Eq.~(47)]{antal2004}. This gives $\tau(I_0(\tau)+I_1(\tau))=N/2$,
which is the equation $F(x_B)=h$ of the Bessel root of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel}(ii) with $\tau=2x_B$. Its root $z$ satisfies
$z+\frac12\log z=L+c_0+1/(8z)+O(z^{-2})$, the crossing equation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-eq} without its remainder. So the scaling law
gives the Bessel root. What we add is a proof that the Bessel root is the
crossing up to a relative error $O(L/N)$ in the odds, with explicit bounds
at every $N$, and the bracket of Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n}. In the same way
the large-argument form of the known Bessel laws suggests the $\log\log N$
term, the periodic shift by $\log2$ and the Gaussian profile of
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window}, and for these our contribution is the
proof.
Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite[Section~III.B]{stepanyan2024} study two crossings of
the periodic chain as thermal transitions. In the first, the all-up
configuration and magnetization $N-2$ (one flipped spin) are equally likely
exactly at $J=\frac14\log N$, as they show. In the second,
the all-up configuration and zero magnetization are equally likely, and they
estimate the coupling. We give the second with its constants under both boundary
conditions, and for free ends we bound it at every $N$ and find the crossing
of every bin. The Markov binomial estimates of
\v{C}ekanavi\v{c}ius and Mikalauskas assume ranges of the transition
probabilities that exclude $p\to1$ in this symmetric model (see
\cite[Theorems~1.1--1.3, pp.~220--221]{cekanavicius2001} and
\cite[Theorems~1.1--1.3, pp.~308--309]{cekanavicius2001ld}).

In short, the exact laws, the Bessel forms and their fixed-scale limits are
known, and we use them as inputs. What is new here, as far as we know, is
quantitative. First, we prove relative Bessel bounds that are explicit in
$N$, in $u$ and in the bin (Theorems~\ref{thm:bessel},
\ref{thm:allbin-bessel} and~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel}), and that stay small
when the mean number of domain walls grows like $\log N$. Second, we bracket
the free crossing at every $N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n}). Third, we expand
the actual crossings, uniformly over $b\ge a$ near a growing edge
(Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-expansion}), and describe the crossing window about
the exact root (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The crossing that Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024} read as a thermal
transition of the finite chain compares one microstate, the all-up
configuration, with one magnetization of a broad band. At the crossing the
band has width of order $N/\sqrt{\log N}$ in the magnetization, its free
energy relative to full order is the parabola of
Remark~\ref{rem:free-energy}, and it carries about $\log N$ domain walls
(Proposition~\ref{prop:walls}). So the energy gap between the two compared
states grows like $\log N$, not like $N$, and the crossing sharpens only like
$1/\log N$ in the coupling. In this sense it is a finite-size crossover between
order and disorder rather than a sharpening transition, in line with the
absence of transition signals in the microcanonical entropy of finite
chains~\cite{sitarachu2020}. Its location is set
by the balance of one wall-free state against the entropy of about $\log N$
walls, which is where the $\log\log N$ term comes from. The boundary shift by
$\log2$ comes from the parity of the number of walls on a ring.

Three couplings organize the row. At the adjacent crossing
(Proposition~\ref{prop:adjacent}) the odds are about $N^{-1/2}$, so
$\xi\approx\sqrt N/2$, and for periodic ends this is the first transition
$J=\frac14\log N$ of~\cite{stepanyan2024}. At the central crossing
$\xi\approx N/(2\log N)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:couplings}). The chain is fully
ordered with probability $\frac12$ when $N/\xi\approx2\log2$
(Remark~\ref{rem:ordered}), which is the usual finite-size crossover, where
the correlation length is comparable with the chain. The central crossing
lies $\frac12\log\log N+O(1)$ below it in the coupling, because it compares one
microstate with one bin. At a first-order transition the finite-size rounding
has width of order the inverse of the energy gap between the coexisting
phases, which grows like the volume~\cite{privman1983,borgs1990,lee1991}.
Here the gap is $2\mathcal W_N(u_N)=2\log N-\log\log N+O(1)$, and the
width $1/(2\mathcal W_N(u_N))$ shrinks only like $1/\log N$.

The bounds behind these statements hold at every $N$ and every coupling.
Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel} bounds the ratio of the partition function at
magnetization $a-b$ to the one at full magnetization, so it gives the
fixed-magnetization partition function of the free chain, as used
in~\cite{dantchev2022,dantchev2024}, with an explicit relative error, also
when the mean number of domain walls grows with $N$. The approach extends to
the $d$-state Potts chain~\cite{paperB}. Three questions are open. The
bracket of Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n} is proved only for free ends and for the
level $1$ of Remark~\ref{rem:convention}, and the same method should give it
for periodic ends and for other levels. In a magnetic field the two letters
of the walk get different weights, and the run polynomial becomes a
polynomial in two variables. Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024} find
that their two transitions merge above a field of about
$2J\log2/\log(N/2)$, and the biased walk is the natural route to a proof. We
do not treat the field here.

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

Aside from Remark~\ref{rem:sign}, which is computer-assisted throughout, the
proofs use two computations, namely the integer certificates for $N\le174$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n} (Step~4) and Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-eq}, and
the evaluation at $N=2774$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(d). Each one is
described where it is used. The scripts are Python, and all but one use only
the standard library. They check the exact law, the orderings, the Bessel
bounds, the asymptotic formulas and the couplings of Table~\ref{tab:couplings},
and where possible each reported number is computed in two independent ways.
For instance, the couplings of Table~\ref{tab:couplings} come from the run
polynomials and again from the transfer matrix of the chain (with NumPy), and
the two agree to $10^{-12}$.

Many of the results are formalized in Lean~4, in the folder
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem131/lean/PaperA}. This includes
Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n} and Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-eq} for all
$N\ge2$, and all four parts of Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}, with explicit
constants in place of the error terms of parts (b) and (c). For $N\le174$ the
Lean kernel checks
the same certificates as Step~4 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n},
with rational bounds on $\zeta_N$ that Lean proves itself. For part (d) of the
corollary, Lean proves the rational bounds at
$N=2774$ and the extension to every larger $N$. For
Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-eq} and $N\le174$ it checks rational
certificates of its own, built on the same brackets. Lean also proves
Corollary~\ref{cor:boundary-layer} for every sequence $v_N$ that satisfies its
hypotheses, Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion}, and the large-argument forms of $I_0$
and $I_1$ quoted in Section~\ref{sec:setting}, with explicit remainders. It
also proves the finite Ising dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:setting},
Theorems~\ref{thm:allbin-expansion}, \ref{thm:edge-laguerre},
\ref{thm:edge-matching} and~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:couplings}, with each error term replaced by a bound that
holds from some point on and is at least as strong as the paper's. Only
the standard axioms
are used. The following are not formalized. In
Section~\ref{sec:allbin}, Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-M} as stated (Lean has
Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel}, of which it is a restatement). In
Section~\ref{sec:periodicwindow},
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:walls}. Also not
formalized are the remarks and the numbers in the tables. The file
\nolinkurl{MAP.md} in that folder gives the Lean theorem for each result, or
says that there is none.
