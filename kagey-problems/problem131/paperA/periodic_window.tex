\section{Periodic boundaries and the crossing window}\label{sec:periodicwindow}

\subsection{Periodic boundaries}\label{sec:periodic}

We now close the sequence of steps into a loop, so that the last step also
interacts with the first. With this closing bond the spins form the periodic
Ising chain, with weight proportional to
$\exp(J\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})$, $\sigma_{N+1}=\sigma_1$ and again
$u=e^{-2J}$. Antal, Droz and R\'acz~\cite{antal2004} give its magnetization law
at a fixed scale, and Dantchev and Rudnick~\cite{dantchev2022} give exact
coefficients. We find how much the closing bond moves the central crossing,
and then describe the row near the crossing under both boundaries.

Write $S^{\mathrm F}_{N,k}=S_{k,N-k}$ and $S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}$ for the
bin-to-endpoint ratios under free and periodic boundaries. Put $a=k$, $b=N-k$
and $s=\sqrt{ab}$. Counting cyclic words by their runs gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periodicratio}
S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}(u)=\sum_{r=1}^{\min(a,b)}\frac Nr
 \binom{a-1}{r-1}\binom{b-1}{r-1}u^{2r}.
\end{equation}
To see this, note that a cyclic word with $a$ letters $\sR$ and $b$ letters
$\sL$ switches an even number $2r$ of times and has $r$ runs of each letter.
Choose compositions of $a$ and of $b$ into $r$ positive runs and a position for
the start of a distinguished run of letters $\sR$, and divide by the $r$ choices of
that run. This is the count of~\cite[Eq.~(30)]{antal2004}
and~\cite[Eq.~(2.9)]{dantchev2022}. The normalization of the periodic
measure cancels in the ratio. In
particular $S^{\mathrm P}_{N,1}=Nu^2$, which gives the adjacent crossing
$J=(\log N)/4$ of Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024}. Every interior
ratio increases strictly from $0$ to $\infty$ in $u>0$, and the ratios
increase strictly toward the middle, apart from the symmetric middle tie, by
the coefficient comparison of Proposition~\ref{prop:allbin-order}.

\begin{theorem}[periodic relative error]\label{thm:periodic-bessel}
For $N\ge2$, $1\le k\le N-1$, and $u>0$, define
\[
\widehat S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}(u)=\frac{Nu}{\sqrt{k(N-k)}}
 I_1\!\left(2u\sqrt{k(N-k)}\right).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periodicbound}
0\le1-\frac{S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}(u)}{\widehat S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}(u)}
\le\frac{Nu^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

So under periodic boundaries the ratio of every bin to an endpoint is close to
a single Bessel function $I_1$, with the same kind of error as in Theorem~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel}.

\begin{proof}
Put $x=su$, $A=ab/N$, $U_r=U_r(a)$ and $V_r=U_r(b)$. Shifting the index in
\eqref{eq:periodicratio} gives
$S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}(u)=(Nu/s)\sum_{r\ge0}U_rV_rx^{2r+1}/(r!(r+1)!)$, so the
ratio to $\widehat S^{\mathrm P}_{N,k}$ is an average of the $U_rV_r$ with
weights $x^{2r+1}/(r!(r+1)!)$, whose sum is $I_1(2x)$. By
\eqref{eq:allbin-odd-deficit}, $0\le1-U_rV_r\le r(r+1)/(2A)$, and the second
identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:clipped}(c) bounds the deficit by
$x^2/(2A)=Nu^2/2$.
\end{proof}

A cyclic word switches an
even number of times (it must come back to its first letter),
so only $I_1$ appears in the periodic approximation, while the free words that
start and end on different letters give the extra $I_0$ term of the free one.
As $I_0\approx I_1$ for large arguments, the free ratio is about twice the
periodic one near the crossing. Both grow like $e^{Nu}$, so the periodic
crossing needs $Nu$ about $\log2$ larger. Antal, Droz and
R\'acz~\cite[Eqs.~(37) and~(47)]{antal2004} work at a fixed value of $\tau=Nq$
(their scaling variable $\zeta=Nt/2$ with $t=1-\tanh J$, which equals $\tau$). There the endpoint atoms are
$1/(2\cosh \tau)$ (periodic) and $e^{-\tau}/2$ (free), which again differ by a
factor of about $2$ for large $\tau$, while their densities at zero magnetization
agree to leading order as $\tau\to\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[periodic crossing and boundary shift]\label{thm:periodic-crossing}
Let $u_N^{\mathrm P}$ be the positive root of $S^{\mathrm P}_{N,m_N}(u)=1$,
let $p_N^{\mathrm P}=1/(1+u_N^{\mathrm P})$, and let $u_N^{\mathrm F}=u_N$ be
the free central root. Put $c_{\mathrm P}=\tfrac12\log(\pi/2)$. As
$N\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
Nu_N^{\mathrm P}
 &=L-\tfrac12\log L+c_{\mathrm P}
 +\frac{\tfrac14\log L-\tfrac12c_{\mathrm P}+\tfrac38}{L}
 +O\!\left(\frac{(\log L)^2}{L^2}\right),\label{eq:periodicasym}\\
N(u_N^{\mathrm P}-u_N^{\mathrm F})
 &=\log2+\frac{\tfrac14-\tfrac12\log2}{\log N}
 +o(1/\log N).\label{eq:boundaryshift}
\end{align}
Replacing $Nu_N^{\mathrm P}$ by $N(1-p_N^{\mathrm P})$ preserves
\eqref{eq:periodicasym}. At the periodic crossing the coupling
$J_N^{\mathrm P}=-\frac12\log u_N^{\mathrm P}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isingtemperature}
J_N^{\mathrm P}
=\tfrac12(\log N-\log\log N)
+\frac{\tfrac14\log\log N-\tfrac12c_{\mathrm P}}{\log N}
+O\!\left(\frac{(\log\log N)^2}{(\log N)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Since $c_{\mathrm P}=c_0+\log2$, the periodic expansion is the free one
shifted by $\log2$, with $\frac38$ in place of $\frac18$ in the $1/L$ term.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1. The first order.} At the middle $2s=N+O(1/N)$. At
$u=(1\pm\theta)L/N$ with fixed $\theta\in(0,1)$ the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel} tends to $0$, and the large-argument form of
$I_1$ makes $\widehat S^{\mathrm P}$ tend to $\infty$ with the plus sign and
to $0$ with the minus sign. Since $S^{\mathrm P}_{N,m_N}$ increases in $u$, we
get $Nu_N^{\mathrm P}\sim L$.

\emph{Step 2. The crossing equation.} Write $z=Nu_N^{\mathrm P}$. At the root
$S^{\mathrm P}=1$, so $\log\widehat S^{\mathrm P}=O(L^2/N)$ by
\eqref{eq:periodicbound}. Replacing the argument $2su$ by $z$, and the factor
$Nu/s$ by $2z/N$, changes $\log\widehat S^{\mathrm P}$ by $O(L/N^2)$.
Here $(\log I_1)'=I_0/I_1-1/z=O(1)$ for large positive arguments
by~\cite[Eqs.~10.29.2, 10.40.1]{dlmf}. Also
$I_1(z)=e^z(2\pi z)^{-1/2}(1-3/(8z)+O(z^{-2}))$~\cite[Eq.~10.40.1]{dlmf}. So
\[
z+\tfrac12\log z=L+c_{\mathrm P}+\frac3{8z}+O(z^{-2}+L^2/N),
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:inversion} with $\ell=L$, $C=c_{\mathrm P}$ and
$B=\frac38$ gives \eqref{eq:periodicasym}. As $N(u-u/(1+u))=O(L^2/N)$ for
$Nu=O(L)$, it also holds for $N(1-p_N^{\mathrm P})$.

\emph{Step 3. The shift and the coupling.} By \eqref{eq:zN},
$Nu_N=z_N+O(L^2/N)$. Subtracting \eqref{eq:thirdorder}, where
$c_0=c_{\mathrm P}-\log2$ and the correction is $\frac18$, gives
\eqref{eq:boundaryshift}, even with error $O((\log L)^2/L^2)$. Finally
$J=-\frac12\log u=\frac12(L-\log z)$, and
$\log z=\log L-(\frac12\log L-c_{\mathrm P})/L+O((\log L)^2/L^2)$ gives
\eqref{eq:isingtemperature}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The crossing window}\label{sec:window}

Before we return to the coupling~\eqref{eq:isingtemperature} in
Section~\ref{sec:coupling}, we describe the whole row near the crossing. The factor $e^{-2y^2}$ in
the next theorem has a simple heuristic. Moving $yN/\sqrt{\log N}$ away from
the middle multiplies the Bessel argument
by $2\sqrt{k(N-k)}/N\approx1-2y^2/\log N$. The argument is about $\log N$, so
it drops by about $2y^2$, and the ratio, which grows like $e$ to the argument,
drops by about the factor $e^{-2y^2}$.

\begin{theorem}[spatial crossing window]\label{thm:critical-window}
Let $X\in\{\mathrm F,\mathrm P\}$ and let $u_N^X$ be its exact central
crossing. For fixed real $t,y$ set
\[
u=u_N^X+\frac tN,\qquad
k_N(y)=\operatorname{nint}\!\left(\frac N2+\frac{yN}{\sqrt{\log N}}\right),
\]
where either nearest-integer convention may be used. Then, as $N\to\infty$,
uniformly on compact sets of $(t,y)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criticalprofile}
S^X_{N,k_N(y)}(u_N^X+t/N)\longrightarrow e^{t-2y^2}.
\end{equation}
If $M_N^X(t)$ is the number of interior bins with probability strictly above
the endpoint probability at that parameter, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criticalcount}
\frac{\sqrt{\log N}}N M_N^X(t)\longrightarrow\sqrt{2\max(t,0)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

So the crossing window has width about $1/N$ in $u$ and about
$N/\sqrt{\log N}$ in space, and inside it the ratio of each bin to an endpoint is a
Gaussian bump times $e^t$. A weak limit theorem (a limit for the overall shape of the law) cannot
give this, since it says nothing about a single bin such as the endpoint. The
Gaussian itself is expected. At $m=2y/\sqrt{\log N}$ the factor $e^{-2y^2}$ is
$e^{-m^2\log N/2}$, the large-$\tau$ Gaussian $e^{-m^2\tau/2}$ of Antal, Droz
and R\'acz~\cite[Section~IV.E, Eq.~(51)]{antal2004} at $\tau\approx\log N$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} adds the comparison with the endpoint,
centered at the exact crossing and uniform on compact sets.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1. The profile.} Put $\lambda=k/N-\frac12$ and
$\eta=2\sqrt{k(N-k)}/N=\sqrt{1-4\lambda^2}$. For the stated rounding and
bounded $y$,
\[
\eta=1-\frac{2y^2}{L}+O\Bigl(L^{-2}+\frac1{N\sqrt L}\Bigr),
\qquad Nu_N^X=L+O(\log L).
\]
So the Bessel argument $Nu\eta$ at $(u,k)$ is $Nu_N^X+t-2y^2+o(1)$, uniformly
on the compact set. By Theorems~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel}
and~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel} both ratios are within relative error
$O(L^2/N)$ of their Bessel forms, and $I_0(z)$ and $I_1(z)$ equal
$e^z(2\pi z)^{-1/2}(1+O(1/z))$~\cite[Eq.~10.40.1]{dlmf}, so the prefactors
relative to their central values tend to $1$ uniformly. The change of argument
gives $e^{t-2y^2}$, and the exact central ratio is $1$, which proves
\eqref{eq:criticalprofile}.

\emph{Step 2. The count.} The interior ratios increase toward the middle, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:allbin-order} for $X=\mathrm F$ and by the coefficient
comparison after \eqref{eq:periodicratio} for $X=\mathrm P$. If
$t\le0$, the central ratio is at most $1$, so no interior bin is above the
endpoint. Let $t>0$ and $0<\theta<\sqrt{t/2}$. By Step~1, for large $N$ the
bins at scaled distance less than $\sqrt{t/2}-\theta$ are above the endpoint
and those at distance $\sqrt{t/2}+\theta$ are below, and by monotonicity so
are all farther bins. Count the bins in between, divide by $N/\sqrt L$ and let
$\theta\downarrow0$. Rounding changes the count by $O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[constrained free energies]\label{rem:free-energy}
Let $Z^X_N(\mu)$ be the sum of the Ising weights of the configurations with
magnetization $\mu$ under boundary $X$, and $\mathcal F^X_N(\mu)=-\log Z^X_N(\mu)$ its
dimensionless free energy, as in the fixed-magnetization ensemble
of~\cite{dantchev2022,dantchev2024}. The normalization cancels in the
ratio of a bin to an endpoint, so $-\log S^X_{N,k}=\mathcal F^X_N(2k-N)-\mathcal F^X_N(N)$. On
a compact set of $(t,y)$ the limit $e^{t-2y^2}$ in
\eqref{eq:criticalprofile} is bounded below by a positive constant, so the
convergence there also holds for logarithms, uniformly. Hence
\[
\mathcal F^X_N(2k_N(y)-N)-\mathcal F^X_N(N)\longrightarrow2y^2-t
\]
at the odds $u_N^X+t/N$. In words, near the crossing the free energy of a
fixed magnetization, measured from full magnetization, is a parabola in the
scaled magnetization, lowered by $t$. This compares single bins. Each of them
has probability of order $\sqrt{\log N}/N$ there, so it says nothing about
the total weight of the nearly ordered configurations.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The coupling at the crossing}\label{sec:coupling}

We now turn to the coupling at the two central crossings, since this is how
Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024} state the crossing. Write
$J_N^{\mathrm F}=-\frac12\log u_N$ for the coupling at the free central
crossing. For $J>0$ let $\xi(J)=-1/\log\tanh J$ be the correlation length of
the infinite chain, so that $\langle\sigma_0\sigma_r\rangle=e^{-r/\xi(J)}$
there. This is the zero-field correlation length with no constraint on the
magnetization, evaluated at the coupling of the crossing. The spin
correlations in a chain of fixed magnetization behave differently, and can
even become negative~\cite[Sections~II and~IV]{balog2025}.

\begin{corollary}[couplings and correlation lengths at the crossings]\label{cor:couplings}
As $N\to\infty$,
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The free coupling satisfies $J_N^{\mathrm F}=\frac12(\log N-\log\log N)
+\dfrac{\frac14\log\log N-\frac12c_0}{\log N}
+O\Bigl(\dfrac{(\log\log N)^2}{(\log N)^2}\Bigr)$.
\item The two couplings differ by $J_N^{\mathrm F}-J_N^{\mathrm P}=\dfrac{\log2}{2\log N}
+O\Bigl(\dfrac{\log\log N}{(\log N)^2}\Bigr)$.
\item The correlation lengths satisfy $N/\xi(J_N^{\mathrm F})=2\log N-\log\log N+\log(\pi/8)
+O(\log\log N/\log N)$, and
$N/\xi(J_N^{\mathrm P})=2\log N-\log\log N+\log(\pi/2)
+O(\log\log N/\log N)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

All three parts are facts about $Nu$ at the crossings, read on other scales.
The coupling is $-\frac12\log u$, so it sees $Nu$ only through $\log(Nu)$. The
shift of $\log2$ in $Nu$ between the two boundary conditions therefore becomes
a shift of order $1/\log N$ in the coupling. The correlation length is
$1/(2u)$ to leading order, so $N/\xi$ is about $2Nu$.

\begin{proof}
(a) By \eqref{eq:zN}, $u_N=z_N/(N-z_N)$, so
$J_N^{\mathrm F}=\frac12(L-\log z_N)+O(L/N)$. Taking logarithms in
\eqref{eq:thirdorder} gives
$\log z_N=\log L-(\frac12\log L-c_0)/L+O((\log L)^2/L^2)$, and (a) follows in
the same way as \eqref{eq:isingtemperature} in Step~3 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing}.

(b) The difference is $J_N^{\mathrm F}-J_N^{\mathrm P}
=\frac12\log(u_N^{\mathrm P}/u_N^{\mathrm F})=\frac12\log(1+\delta)$, where
$\delta=N(u_N^{\mathrm P}-u_N^{\mathrm F})/(Nu_N^{\mathrm F})$. By
\eqref{eq:boundaryshift} the numerator is $\log2+O(1/L)$. By \eqref{eq:zN} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(b) the denominator is $z_N+O(L^2/N)=L+O(\log L)$.
So $\delta=(\log2)/L+O(\log L/L^2)$, and
$\frac12\log(1+\delta)=\delta/2+O(\delta^2)$.

(c) With $u=e^{-2J}$ we have $\tanh J=(1-u)/(1+u)$, so
$N/\xi(J)=N\log\frac{1+u}{1-u}=2Nu+O(Nu^3)$ for $0<u\le\frac12$. At either
crossing $Nu^3=O(L^3/N^2)$. It remains to insert
$Nu_N=z_N+O(L^2/N)=L-\frac12\log L+c_0+O(\log L/L)$, from
Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(b), and the expansion \eqref{eq:periodicasym} of
$Nu_N^{\mathrm P}$, and to recall that $2c_0=\log(\pi/8)$ and
$2c_{\mathrm P}=\log(\pi/2)$.
\end{proof}

So at either crossing the correlation length is about $N/(2\log N)$. The chain
is ordered over long stretches, but each stretch is a vanishing fraction of the
chain, and $N/\xi$ grows like $2\log N$. Antal, Droz and
R\'acz~\cite{antal2004} give the magnetization law when $\tau$ is fixed, and
there $N/\xi$ stays bounded (it is about $2\tau$). The crossing lies outside that
regime, which is why we need bounds that are uniform in $\tau$. Part~(b) is the
shift by $\log2$ in $Nu$ of \eqref{eq:boundaryshift}, seen on the logarithmic
scale of the coupling. So the boundary condition moves the coupling at the
crossing by an amount that decays only like $1/\log N$.

Stepanyan et~al.\@~\cite[Section~III.B]{stepanyan2024} call the periodic
central crossing the second thermal transition of the finite chain. They
give the approximate formula $\beta J\approx(2\log N-1)/5$~\cite[Eq.~(16)]{stepanyan2024},
and their Fig.~1(b) shows the fitted line $\beta J\approx0.3864\log N-0.21055$.
Table~\ref{tab:couplings} and Figure~\ref{fig:coupling} compare both with the
exact crossing and with \eqref{eq:isingtemperature} without its error term. The line is accurate for
$10\le N\le100$, but neither approximation has a $\log\log N$ term. The main
terms of \eqref{eq:isingtemperature} have slope $\frac12-1/(2\log N)$ in
$\log N$, up to $O(\log\log N/(\log N)^2)$, so the exact curve bends slowly
toward slope $\frac12$. Its secant slope is $0.385$ on $10\le N\le10^2$, close
to the fitted $0.3864$, then $0.417$ on $10^2\le N\le10^3$, and $0.450$ on
$10^4\le N\le10^5$. At the tabulated sizes $N=10^3,10^4,10^5,10^6$, the main terms of
\eqref{eq:isingtemperature} are closer to the exact value than either
approximation, and at $N=10^5$ they are off by $0.002$, against $0.171$ and
$0.338$.

\begin{remark}[other comparisons]\label{rem:convention}
We compare one central bin with one endpoint, as Stepanyan
et~al.\@~\cite{stepanyan2024} do. Comparing instead with both endpoints, or
more generally solving $S^X_{N,m_N}(u)=\Theta$ for a fixed $\Theta>0$, only adds $\log\Theta$
to the constant. For periodic ends, Step~2 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing} goes through with $\log\Theta$ added to the
right side of its crossing equation. For free ends the same argument works
with Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel}(i) and \eqref{eq:bracket-log} in place of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel}, and with $\frac18$ in place of $\frac38$.
So \eqref{eq:periodicasym} and \eqref{eq:thirdorder} hold for these roots with
$c_X+\log\Theta$ in place of $c_X$, and the coupling moves by $-(\log\Theta)/(2\log N)$
to leading order. For $\Theta=2$ the free constant becomes $c_0+\log2=c_{\mathrm P}$,
so this $\log2$, which comes from the convention, should not be confused
with the shift by $\log2$ between the boundary conditions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-crossing}. For odd $N$, adding the two central bins
as well as the two endpoints gives $\Theta=1$ again. The bracket of
Theorem~\ref{thm:every-n} is proved only for $\Theta=1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Domain walls and the energy gap}\label{sec:walls}

We now describe the crossing in terms of domain walls. Given that
the free chain is in bin $m_N$, a word has probability proportional to
$u^{\chg(w)}$, so the number of domain walls has the law of the coefficients
of $S_N$. Let $\mathcal W_N(u)=uS_N'(u)/S_N(u)$ be its mean, and put
$\chi(w)=I_0(w)/(I_0(w)+I_1(w))$.

\begin{proposition}[domain walls at the crossing]\label{prop:walls}
Let $N\ge2$ and $u>0$, put $w=Nu$, and suppose that $w(w+2)<2N$. Let
$\Delta=-\log(1-w(w+2)/(2N))$. Then for every $\delta>0$,
\[
\bigl|\mathcal W_N(u)-w-\chi(w)\bigr|\le w(e^\delta-1)+\frac\Delta\delta .
\]
At the crossing,
$\mathcal W_N(u_N)=z_N+\frac12+\frac1{8z_N}+O\bigl((\log N)^{-2}\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}

In words, the central bin at the crossing carries one half more domain wall
than the switching scale. Numerically, $\mathcal W_N(u_N)-z_N$ is $0.5191$,
$0.5166$ and $0.5132$ at $N=10^3,10^4,10^5$, against $0.5224$, $0.5162$ and
$0.5126$ for $\frac12+\frac1{8z_N}$.

\begin{proof}
Put $\vartheta=\log u$, $x=hu=w/2$ with $h=N/2$, $g(\vartheta)=\log S_N(e^\vartheta)$ and
$G(\vartheta)=\log F(he^\vartheta)$. Both are logarithms of power series in $e^\vartheta$ with
nonnegative coefficients, so both are convex in $\vartheta$, and $g'(\vartheta)=\mathcal W_N(u)$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel}(i), $G(\vartheta)-\log h-\Delta\le g(\vartheta)\le G(\vartheta)-\log h$,
and the upper bound holds at every $\vartheta$. From $F'=2F+2I_0(2x)$ we get
$G'(\vartheta)=xF'(x)/F(x)=w+\chi(w)$. The function $\chi$ decreases, since
$I_1/I_0$ is the mean of $\cos t$ under the density proportional to
$e^{w\cos t}$ on $[0,\pi]$~\cite[Eq.~10.32.3]{dlmf}, and its derivative in $w$
is the variance of $\cos t$, which is positive. By convexity,
\[
\mathcal W_N(u)\le\frac{g(\vartheta+\delta)-g(\vartheta)}\delta
\le\frac{G(\vartheta+\delta)-G(\vartheta)+\Delta}\delta\le G'(\vartheta+\delta)+\frac\Delta\delta
\le we^\delta+\chi(w)+\frac\Delta\delta,
\]
and in the same way $\mathcal W_N(u)\ge we^{-\delta}+\chi(w)-\Delta/\delta$.
Since $1-e^{-\delta}\le e^\delta-1$, this proves the bound.

At the crossing $w=Nu_N=z_N+O(L^2/N)$ and $\Delta=O(L^2/N)$, and
$\delta=(L/N)^{1/2}$ makes the bound $O(L^{3/2}N^{-1/2})$. The large-argument
forms of $I_0$ and $I_1$ give $\chi(w)=\frac12+\frac1{8w}+O(w^{-2})$, which
proves the second claim.
\end{proof}

Since $u=e^{-2J}$, the definition of $\mathcal W_N$ gives
\[
\frac d{dJ}\log\frac{P_N(m_N)}{P_N(0)}=-2\mathcal W_N(u).
\]
The energy
$-\sum_{i<N}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ of a configuration exceeds that of the all-up
configuration by twice its number of domain walls. So at the crossing the mean
energy gap between the central bin and the all-up configuration is
$2\mathcal W_N(u_N)=2\log N-\log\log N+O(1)$, by Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(b),
and over a range of couplings of width
$1/(2\mathcal W_N(u_N))$, which is $0.061$ at $N=10^4$ and $0.048$ at $N=10^5$,
the ratio of the central bin to an endpoint changes by about a factor $e$. The
same proof with Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel} in place of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bessel}, where $G$ becomes $\log(xI_1(2x))$ and
$G'=wI_0(w)/I_1(w)$, gives the mean number of domain walls
$Nu+\frac12+O(1/(Nu))$ at the periodic crossing for even $N$.

This also accounts for a second fit of Stepanyan et~al. They measure the mean
domain length $l_d=\langle N/(n_d+\delta_{0,n_d})\rangle$, with $n_d$ the
number of domain walls of the periodic chain, at their second transition and
fit $l_d\propto N^{0.757}$ on $20\le N\le100$~\cite[Eq.~(18)]{stepanyan2024}.
At the exact periodic crossing, where $n_d=2r$ has weight proportional to
$\binom N{2r}u^{2r}$, we get $l_d=9.27$ at $N=20$ and $l_d=31.21$ at $N=100$,
and a least-squares exponent $0.755$ on that range. With about $\log N$ walls
one expects $l_d$ of order $N/\log N$, whose local exponent on that range is
$0.74$ and tends to $1$ only slowly. So the fitted power is a local slope, by
the same mechanism as the slope $0.3864$ of their fitted coupling.

\begin{remark}[full order against one bin]\label{rem:ordered}
The chain is fully ordered, all up or all down, with probability
$P_N(0)+P_N(N)=p^{N-1}$. This is $\frac12$ at $u_{\mathrm{ord}}=2^{1/(N-1)}-1$, that
is, at the coupling
$J_{\mathrm{ord}}=\frac12\log\frac{N-1}{\log2}+O(1/N)$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:every-n}(a), $J_N^{\mathrm F}=\frac12\log(N/z_N-1)$, so
\[
J_{\mathrm{ord}}-J_N^{\mathrm F}=\tfrac12\log\frac{z_N}{\log2}+O\Bigl(\frac{\log N}N\Bigr)
=\tfrac12\log\log N-\tfrac12\log\log2+o(1).
\]
The difference is $1.046$ at $N=10^3$ and $1.330$ at $N=10^5$. So full order
wins against the rest of the chain only at a coupling about $\frac12\log\log N$
above the central crossing. The $\log\log N$ in $J_N^{\mathrm F}$ measures how
much less likely one central bin is than the whole disordered band.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A magnetic field}\label{sec:field}

A dimensionless field $\mathcal B=\beta h>0$, in the notation
of~\cite{stepanyan2024}, multiplies the weight of a
configuration by $e^{\mathcal B(\sigma_1+\dots+\sigma_N)}$. Magnetization
$N-2a$, with $a$ spins down, then has weight $e^{-2\mathcal Ba}$ relative to
full magnetization, beyond the zero-field ratio. By the symmetry of the row, its
ratio to the all-up configuration is
\[
R^X_{N,a}(u)=S^X_{N,a}(u)\,e^{-2\mathcal Ba}\qquad(1\le a\le N-1),
\]
for either boundary $X\in\{\mathrm F,\mathrm P\}$. We keep $\mathcal B$ fixed
as $N$ grows and as the coupling varies (this is different from varying the
temperature at fixed physical field $h$). The ratio increases strictly from $0$ to $\infty$ in $u$, so
it equals $1$ at a single $u=u^{(a)}_N$, and the all-up configuration is more
likely than magnetization $N-2a$ exactly when $u<u^{(a)}_N$. The roots depend
on $X$ and $\mathcal B$, which we suppress in the notation.

There are two comparisons behind the transitions of Stepanyan
et~al.\@~\cite[Section~III.C]{stepanyan2024}. The one-spin crossing is
$u^{(1)}_N$. For $N\ge3$, define
\[
u^{(\ge2)}_N=\min_{2\le a\le N-1}u^{(a)}_N.
\]
At this value the all-up configuration ties with the most likely bin having
at least two down spins, excluding the all-down configuration (whose ratio
to all up is always $e^{-2\mathcal BN}<1$). The all-up configuration is
strictly more likely than every other magnetization bin exactly when
$u<\min\{u^{(1)}_N,u^{(\ge2)}_N\}$. These two crossing values need not
coincide when there is only one transition in the sense of that paper.

Both comparisons come from the edge of the row. The right variable is
$y=Nu^2$, as in
Section~\ref{sec:allbin-edge}, and with $H_a$ of \eqref{eq:edge-polynomial}
let $y_a(\mathcal B)$ be the positive root of $H_a(y)=e^{2\mathcal Ba}$. So
$y_1(\mathcal B)=e^{2\mathcal B}$, and at $\mathcal B=0$ these are the roots
$y_a$ of Section~\ref{sec:allbin-edge}.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[edge crossings in a fixed field]\label{thm:field}
Fix a dimensionless field $\mathcal B>0$ and either free or periodic
ends. Let $y_a(\mathcal B)>0$ solve $H_a(y)=e^{2\mathcal Ba}$, with $H_a$
as in \eqref{eq:edge-polynomial}.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item For every fixed integer $a\ge1$,
\[
N(u^{(a)}_N)^2\longrightarrow y_a(\mathcal B)
\qquad(N\to\infty).
\]
In particular, $N(u^{(1)}_N)^2\to e^{2\mathcal B}$.
\item As $a\to\infty$, $y_a(\mathcal B)\to\infty$, so the minimum
\[
y_*(\mathcal B)=\min_{a\ge2}y_a(\mathcal B)
\]
is attained, and
\[
N(u^{(\ge2)}_N)^2\longrightarrow y_*(\mathcal B)
\qquad(N\to\infty).
\]
\item With $\mathcal B_c=\frac12\log2$,
\[
\begin{cases}
y_*(\mathcal B)<e^{2\mathcal B},&0<\mathcal B<\mathcal B_c,\\
y_*(\mathcal B)=e^{2\mathcal B}=2,&\mathcal B=\mathcal B_c,\\
y_*(\mathcal B)>e^{2\mathcal B},&\mathcal B>\mathcal B_c.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In terms of the coupling, these two crossings are
\[
J_N^{(1)}=\tfrac14\log N-\tfrac12\mathcal B+o(1),\qquad
J_N^{(\ge2)}=\tfrac14\log N-\tfrac14\log y_*(\mathcal B)+o(1).
\]
The field moves the last crossing with all up to the edge scale
$J=\frac14\log N+O(1)$ (in zero field it comes from the middle, with
leading coupling $\frac12\log N$). For large $N$, if
$\mathcal B<\mathcal B_c$, another bin is still more likely when the
one-spin bin ties with all up. So all up becomes most likely at a later
crossing, separated from the one-spin crossing by a positive limiting
gap in $J$. If $\mathcal B>\mathcal B_c$, the one-spin bin is last to
fall below all up. This gives only one transition to an all-up maximum,
though the two crossing values defined above are still different.
At $\mathcal B=\mathcal B_c$, their coupling gap tends to zero as
$N\to\infty$ (the two crossings need not agree at finite $N$).

This recovers the limiting dimensionless threshold in Stepanyan
et~al.\@~\cite[Eq.~(20)]{stepanyan2024}. Their physical threshold is
$h_c\approx2J_{\rm ex}\log2/\log(N/2)$, where $J_{\rm ex}$ denotes their
exchange coupling. At the merging point
$\beta J_{\rm ex}\sim\frac14\log N$, so $\beta h_c\to\frac12\log2$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:field} gives the same limit for their periodic chain, and
also for free ends. The agreement
comes from the common fixed-edge limit $H_a$ proved below.

\begin{proof}
(a) For free ends this is the leading term of \eqref{eq:edge-exact-series}.
With $b=N-a$ and $u=\sqrt{y/N}$, the edge variable $bu^2=y(1-a/N)$ tends to $y$,
$V_r=U_r(b)\to1$ for each $r\le a$, and the first two sums carry the factors
$2\sqrt{y/N}$ and $y/N$, so $S^{\mathrm F}_{N,a}(u)\to H_a(y)$ uniformly for $y$
in compact sets. For periodic ends, in \eqref{eq:periodicratio} the term with
$r$ runs is $\frac Nr\binom{a-1}{r-1}\binom{b-1}{r-1}u^{2r}$, and
$\binom{b-1}{r-1}u^{2r-2}\to y^{r-1}/(r-1)!$ uniformly in the same sense, so
$S^{\mathrm P}_{N,a}(u)\to\sum_{r=1}^a\binom{a-1}{r-1}y^r/r!=H_a(y)$. The
limit $H_a(y)e^{-2\mathcal Ba}$ increases strictly and continuously in $y$ from
$0$ to $\infty$, and $R^X_{N,a}$ increases in $u$, so the roots converge.

(b) We need a bound uniform in $a$. Let $Y>0$ and $0<y\le Y$. By
Theorems~\ref{thm:allbin-bessel} and~\ref{thm:periodic-bessel} the ratio is at
most its Bessel form, and $I_0,I_1\le e^z$. For free ends with
$s=\sqrt{a(N-a)}$ and $c=N/(2s)$ this gives
$S^{\mathrm F}_{N,a}(u)\le2u(1+c)e^{2su}$, and for periodic ends
$S^{\mathrm P}_{N,a}(u)\le(Nu/s)e^{2su}$. For every interior bin,
$a(N-a)\ge N-1$, so $Nu/s\le\sqrt{2y}$. Also
$2su\le2\sqrt{ya}$ and $2u\le2\sqrt y$. Thus for either boundary and every
$1\le a\le N-1$,
\[
R^X_{N,a}(u)\le4\sqrt Y\,e^{2\sqrt{Ya}-2\mathcal Ba},
\]
which is below $1$ for every $a\ge A(Y)$, uniformly in $N$. In particular,
every such interior bin has $N(u^{(a)}_N)^2>Y$. Letting
$N\to\infty$ in the first bound with $a$ fixed gives
$H_a(y)e^{-2\mathcal Ba}\le4\sqrt Y\,e^{2\sqrt{Ya}-2\mathcal Ba}<1$ for
$a\ge A(Y)$ and $y\le Y$, so $y_a(\mathcal B)>Y$. Hence $y_a\to\infty$ and
the minimum is attained. Take $Y=y_*(\mathcal B)+1$. For large $N$ the minimum
over $a\ge2$ of $N(u^{(a)}_N)^2$ is a minimum over $2\le a<A(Y)$, and (a) gives
its limit.

(c) Put $t=e^{-2\mathcal B}$ and $y=e^{2\mathcal B}=1/t$. Then
\[
H_a(y)e^{-2\mathcal Ba}=\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}\binom{a-1}r\frac{t^{a-1-r}}{(r+1)!}
\le\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}\binom{a-1}rt^{a-1-r}2^{-r}=\Bigl(t+\frac12\Bigr)^{a-1},
\]
since $(r+1)!\ge2^r$. If $\mathcal B>\frac12\log2$, then $t<\frac12$ and the
right side is below $1$ for every $a\ge2$. As $H_a$ increases, this gives
$y_a(\mathcal B)>e^{2\mathcal B}$ for every $a\ge2$, and so
$y_*(\mathcal B)>e^{2\mathcal B}$ because the minimum is attained. If
$\mathcal B<\frac12\log2$, then at $a=2$ the sum is $t+\frac12>1$, so
$y_2(\mathcal B)<e^{2\mathcal B}$. At $\mathcal B=\frac12\log2$, the same
bound gives $H_a(2)e^{-2\mathcal Ba}\le1$ for every $a\ge2$, with equality
at $a=2$. Hence $y_a(\mathcal B)\ge2$ and $y_*(\mathcal B)=y_2(\mathcal B)=2$.
\end{proof}

The minimizing $a$ grows as the field weakens in the following examples.
For $\mathcal B=0.1$, $0.2$, $0.3$ and $0.5$, respectively, it is
$11$, $4$, $2$ and $2$, and $y_*=0.757$, $1.287$, $1.764$ and $2.972$.
At $N=10^4$, in the same order of fields,
$N(u_N^{(\ge2)})^2$ is $0.758$, $1.288$, $1.764$ and $2.973$ for periodic
ends, and $0.741$, $1.266$, $1.738$ and $2.939$ for free ends. We checked
these numbers directly from the full run polynomials. Interval bounds
give the displayed digits and exclude competing bins near the edge,
and the uniform tail bound excludes all the remaining bins. The free
chain converges more slowly because of the extra terms from its ends
(of relative size $N^{-1/2}$). Here the field is fixed as $N$ grows.
If it decreases with $N$, the comparison moves back toward the middle
of the row, and that crossover remains open.
