# Low rectangle counts force low profile complexity

9 November 2026. This note gives a general consequence of a small aggregate three-corner count without identifying the row or column laws. It applies to arbitrary binary row and column words. The complete proof, including both container consequences and the quantitative modulus below, has passed [independent review](profile_complexity_independent_audit.md).

For binary words \(c,t:[1,h]\to\mathbb F_2\), put
\[
 W(c,t)=\{(x,y)\in[h]^2:c(y)=t(x)\}.
\]
Let \(N_3(c,t)\) count tilted lattice rectangles with exactly three corners in this box.

**Profile-complexity theorem.** For every sequence \(\varepsilon_h\downarrow0\),
\[
 \log\#\{(c,t):N_3(c,t)\le
             \varepsilon_hh^4\log h\}=o(h).
\tag{1}
\]
No balance assumption is needed in (1). Equivalently,
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_{h\to\infty}
 \frac1h\log\#\{(c,t):N_3(c,t)\le
             \varepsilon h^4\log h\}=0.
\tag{2}
\]

This is a complexity statement, not a classification into protected laws. It also implies two uniform consequences for the sea model:

1. The number of balanced row partitions whose sea growth rate is \(h/2-o(h)\) is \(\exp(o(h))\).
2. Every family of stationary sea measures, on arbitrary balanced row partitions, with entropy rate \(\log(h/2)-o(1)\) has column-phase entropy rate \(o(1)\).

## 1. Choose a good direction and average over its lattice cosets

Consider dyadic ranges
\[
 h^{1/32}\le R\le h^{1/16}
\]
and primitive positive directions \(a,b\in[R,2R]\). Distinct directions and disjoint dyadic ranges describe disjoint rectangle families. If \(N_3(c,t)\le\varepsilon_hh^4\log h\), one range and one primitive direction satisfy
\[
 N_{a,b}\le C\varepsilon_hh^4/R^2.
\tag{3}
\]
There are \(\Theta(R^2)\) primitive directions in each range.

Fix a small constant \(\eta>0\), and put
\[
 K=\lfloor\eta h/(10R)\rfloor,\qquad
 D=a^2+b^2\asymp R^2.
\]
The lattice generated by \((a,b)\) and \((-b,a)\) has index D in \(\mathbb Z^2\). Representatives of all its cosets can be chosen inside a fundamental parallelogram of diameter \(O(R)\).

Choose a physical tile center at distance at least \(\eta h\) from the boundary. For each of the D lattice cosets, center a \(K\times K\) lifted matrix near that point:
\[
 G_{ij}=
 1_{\{c(y_0+bi+aj)=t(x_0+ai-bj)\}},
 \qquad1\le i,j\le K.
\tag{4}
\]
The shifts of the centers needed to realize every coset are \(O(R)\), which is negligible compared with \(KR\). All these matrices lie inside the board and contain a common central axis-aligned physical square of side \(n\asymp KR\).

A three-one matrix subrectangle is a physical three-corner rectangle of this primitive direction. The D cosets have disjoint physical point sets, so their rectangle families are disjoint. Consequently their total three-one count is at most \(N_{a,b}\). At least one coset matrix has at most
\[
 N_{a,b}/D
 \le C\varepsilon_hh^4/R^4
 =O_\eta(\varepsilon_hK^4)
 =o_\eta(K^4)
\tag{5}
\]
three-one subrectangles.

The coset average is essential. Without it, the direction bound (3) is too large by a factor comparable with \(R^2\).

## 2. A repaired matrix has few descriptions

The elementary pivot argument in [component_permutation_repair.md](component_permutation_repair.md), Section 1, repairs a matrix satisfying (5) to a disjoint union H of complete bipartite blocks with
\[
 |\{G\ne H\}|\le\rho_hK^2,\qquad
 \rho_h=o_\eta(1).
\tag{6}
\]
For example, \(\rho_h=O_\eta(\varepsilon_h^{1/3})\).

There are at most
\[
 (K+1)^{2K}
\tag{7}
\]
such matrices H: give every row and every column a label in \(\{0,\ldots,K\}\), and put a one exactly when their labels agree and are nonzero. This overcounts, which is harmless.

We will show that H determines the restrictions of c and t to the central physical intervals up to \(2^D\) choices and \(O(\rho_hKR)\) errors.

## 3. The physical incidence graph is a union of complete bipartite graphs

Write physical coordinates relative to the lift base as X,Y. The inverse map is
\[
 i=\frac{aX+bY}{D},\qquad
 j=\frac{-bX+aY}{D}.
\tag{8}
\]
Since \((a,b)=1\), both a and b are units modulo D. The two integrality conditions in (8) are equivalent to the single congruence
\[
 aX+bY\equiv0\pmod D.
\tag{9}
\]
For example, multiplying the second numerator by a and using the first congruence makes it divisible by D.

The chosen central axis-aligned square lies wholly inside the lifted index square. Therefore every pair of a row y and a column x in these physical intervals satisfying (9) is represented by a matrix entry. Its row residue modulo D determines exactly one column residue modulo D.

Thus the physical incidence graph has D complete bipartite components. Every side of every component has size
\[
 n/D+O(1)\asymp K/R.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular all components are nonempty for large h.

On an edge of this graph, the true binary value \(1-G_{ij}\) is
\[
 c(y)+t(x).
\]
For each component, fit \(1-H_{ij}\) by a sum \(\alpha_y+\beta_x\) with the smallest possible number of edge errors. Fix any deterministic tie-breaking rule. If the original matrix error in that component is E, the optimal fit has at most E errors, since the true labels c,t are a candidate. Its edge labels therefore differ from the true sums in at most \(2E\) places.

For a complete bipartite graph with part sizes r,s, two binary vertex labelings whose edge sums differ on F edges can, after one simultaneous flip on both parts, differ on at most
\[
 2F/\min(r,s)
\tag{11}
\]
vertices. To see this, let p,q be the fractions of changed labels on the two parts. Their edge disagreement fraction is \(p+q-2pq\), and
\[
 \min(p+q,2-p-q)\le2(p+q-2pq).
\]
Multiplying by the larger part size gives (11).

Apply (11) with \(F\le2E\), and sum over the D components. By (6) and (10), a suitable choice of one orientation bit per component gives at most
\[
 C\rho_hK^2/(K/R)=C\rho_hKR
\tag{12}
\]
errors in the combined row and column words on the central intervals. This proves the reconstruction claim.

## 4. Cover the board-sized words

Choose \(O(1/\eta)\) central tiles along the diagonal so that their physical row intervals cover \([C\eta h,(1-C\eta)h]\), and their column intervals cover the same range. The tiles may choose different good lattice cosets. A single direction satisfying (3) works for all of them, by the coset averaging argument at each tile center.

For each tile, choose its coset, its repaired matrix H, and its D orientation bits. On overlaps, assign each physical position to one predetermined tile. No agreement between different reconstructed candidates is needed. The total number of candidate row/phase pairs on the covered intervals has logarithm at most
\[
 O_\eta(K\log K+D+\log D)
 =O\!\left(\frac{h\log h}{R}+\frac{R^2}{\eta}\right)
 =o_\eta(h).
\tag{13}
\]
The choice of the good direction and dyadic range costs only \(O(\log h)\) additional bits.

The combined reconstruction error is \(o_\eta(h)\). The uncovered boundary intervals contain only \(O(\eta h)\) row and column positions. Consequently all pairs satisfying the hypothesis of (1) lie within
\[
 O(\eta h)+o_\eta(h)
\]
Hamming distance, on their \(2h\) combined coordinates, of a list of size \(\exp(o_\eta(h))\).

A Hamming ball of radius \(O(\eta h)+o_\eta(h)\) in \(\{0,1\}^{2h}\) has logarithmic size at most
\[
 2hH_2(O(\eta))+o_\eta(h).
\]
First let h tend to infinity for fixed \(\eta\), then let \(\eta\downarrow0\). This proves (1), and the usual diagonal argument gives the equivalent formulation (2).

### 4.1 An explicit power-law modulus

There are absolute constants \(C,\varepsilon_0>0\) such that, for \(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\),
\[
 \limsup_{h\to\infty}\frac1h
 \log\#\{(c,t):N_3(c,t)\le\varepsilon h^4\log h\}
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/6}\log(1/\varepsilon).
\tag{13a}
\]

Use tile centers spaced by a fixed fraction of the common inner-square side. Their outer rotated squares then have bounded overlap, with an absolute bound independent of \(\eta\). Every physical rectangle is consequently counted in only \(O(1)\) outer tile families.

Let \(n_j\) be the three-one count in the chosen coset matrix for tile j. The coset averages and this bounded overlap give
\[
 \sum_j n_j\le C N_{a,b}/D
 \le C\varepsilon h^4/R^4.
\]
The pivot repair changes at most \(C K^{2/3}n_j^{1/3}\) entries in that matrix. There are \(T=O(1/\eta)\) tiles. Hölder's inequality and the physical reconstruction bound therefore give total row/phase error at most
\[
 C\frac RK\,K^{2/3}T^{2/3}
       \left(\frac{\varepsilon h^4}{R^4}\right)^{1/3}
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/3}h/\eta.
\]
Including the boundary intervals, the Hamming radius is
\[
 C(\eta+\varepsilon^{1/3}/\eta)h.
\]
For fixed \(\varepsilon,\eta\), the description count in (13) is still \(\exp(o(h))\). Take \(\eta=\varepsilon^{1/6}\), and count the resulting Hamming balls. This proves (13a).

## 5. Only subexponentially many row partitions can be nearly maximal

Let \(\delta_h\downarrow0\), and consider balanced row partitions \(\pi\) whose sea growth rate satisfies
\[
 \rho_\pi(h)\ge(1/2-\delta_h)h.
\tag{14}
\]
The number of these partitions is \(\exp(o(h))\).

Here is the uniform container argument. Fix a small test threshold \(\theta>0\). The [container purification](container_stability_reduction.md) has a quantitative compactness form: there are constants \(\eta,\delta>0\) such that every container C with
\[
 |C|\ge(1/2-\eta)h^2,\qquad
 e(H_\pi[C])\le\delta h^5\log h
\tag{15}
\]
is close to a full row-class box \(W(c,t)\), with the same row coloring c as \(\pi\), and
\[
 N_3(c,t)\le\theta h^4\log h.
\tag{16}
\]
The constants are uniform over balanced \(\pi\).

Apply the edge-count version of the hypergraph container theorem. If all its containers had size below \((1/2-\eta)h^2\), the fingerprint-aware transversal count would give
\[
 \rho_\pi(h)\le(1/2-\eta+o(1))h,
\]
contrary to (14) for large h. Therefore at least one container satisfies (15), and its exact row word c occurs in a pair counted in (16).

By (2), for arbitrarily small fixed \(\theta\) the number of such pairs has arbitrarily small exponential rate in h. The number of their row projections is no larger. Letting \(\theta\downarrow0\) proves the claim. Identifying complementary row colorings only decreases the count.

This does not identify those partitions. A subexponential family may still contain many nonprotected partitions.

## 6. High-entropy sea measures have negligible phase entropy

Let \(\pi_h\) be arbitrary balanced row partitions, with row colorings \(c_h\), and let \(\mu_h\) be stationary measures on their sea shifts satisfying
\[
 \mathsf h(\mu_h)\ge\log(h/2)-o_h(1).
\tag{17}
\]
Their phase processes \(t_x=c_h(w_x)\) have entropy rate \(o_h(1)\).

Fix a small \(\varepsilon>0\). Choose \(\theta>0\) so small that the set
\[
 Q_h=\{t:N_3(c_h,t)\le\theta h^4\log h\}
\]
has
\[
 \limsup_{h\to\infty}\frac{\log|Q_h|}{h}
 \le\varepsilon,
\tag{18}
\]
using (2). Decrease the container edge and size thresholds so that every large small-edge container is within \(\xi h^2\) cells of a full box with phase in \(Q_h\), where \(\xi>0\) is as small as needed below.

The weighted counting argument from [protected_partition_entropy_stability.md](protected_partition_entropy_stability.md), Sections 2--3, applies with \(Q_h\) in place of the four protected phases. A word whose phase is at least \(\varepsilon h\) from every member of \(Q_h\) is at least that far from the particular box associated with its container. Weight off-box choices by two. Large containers give hth-root bound
\[
 2^{-\varepsilon}(1/2+2\xi+o(1))h<h/2
\]
when \(\xi\) is sufficiently small; small containers have their usual fixed gap. No factor \(|Q_h|\) is needed in this word count, because each fingerprint already determines its container and one associated phase.

The uniform total-block bound is \(((1/2+o(1))h)^h\). The block-entropy argument from that note, together with (17), therefore shows that a length-h phase block is within \(\varepsilon h\) of \(Q_h\) with probability \(1-o_h(1)\).

The number of such nearby phase blocks is at most
\[
 |Q_h|\sum_{j\le\varepsilon h}\binom hj.
\]
Equations (18) and the binary-entropy bound give phase entropy rate at most
\[
 \varepsilon+H_2(\varepsilon)+o_h(1).
\]
Let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This proves the claim.

The conclusion is uniform over row partitions. It gives low phase complexity for all asymptotically maximal sea measures, but it does not identify their phase domains or prove fixed-height component dominance.

## 7. The limiting statement does not mean zero entropy at fixed height

For the ordinary canonical component, its Perron measure has strictly positive phase entropy at every fixed height where the adjacent-row full shift is available. The constant-row-1 history has an internal row-1 self-loop and an internal row-2 transition. Both have positive Perron transition probability.

If \(\pi_s\) is the stationary mass of this history and \(q_s\in(0,1)\) is the conditional probability of one next-row parity, then
\[
 \mathsf h(\text{phase})
 \ge H(\text{next phase}\mid\text{current history})
 \ge\pi_s H_2(q_s)>0.
\]
The first inequality follows by conditioning additionally on the full history-state past and using the Markov property. Thus the theorem says that these positive quantities tend to zero as h grows. It does not assert exact zero phase entropy for any fixed h.
