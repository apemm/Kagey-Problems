# Linear slot lists cannot by themselves exclude right angles

9 November 2026. This note concerns the proposed geometric preprocessing
for the global P palette. It distinguishes a fixed list of admissible
slots for each label from a coordinated placement rule.

For the one-prime placement, write a parity label as
\[
 (a,b)=(2i+1,2j+2),\qquad x(i,j)=4i+P c(i,j).
\]
Here c is a block color. Each block has horizontal diameter O(h), and
O(h) blocks give width O(h^2). The value of P does not enter the
within-block argument below.

**Theorem.** Fix positive constants K and delta, and a finite set
\(\mathcal K\) of integer level slopes. Suppose there are at most Kh
block colors, and every parity label has a prescribed list of at least
delta h admissible colors. For every sufficiently large h, four labels
have a common admissible color and satisfy all the following:

1. Their odd rows are distinct.
2. They are independent in the strengthened P graph, including the
   square tests without the in-strip restriction.
3. Every level \(b+ka\), for \(k\in\mathcal K\), is distinct among them.
4. Their even cells in that common color block form a tilted hidden
   rectangle, with an explicit pair of coincident presentations.

Thus a fixed Cartesian restriction retaining a linear number of block
slots per label cannot guarantee right-angle exclusion. Adding P
properness and any fixed finite family of level rules does not make
every locally permitted choice safe. The theorem does not rule out
coordinated choices within those lists.

## 1. A dense color pool in separated row ranges

Restrict to odd rows in \([h/8,h/4]\) and even rows in
\([3h/4,7h/8]\). These labels form a rectangular index grid with
Theta(h^2) points. The total number of incidences between these labels
and their admissible colors is at least delta h times the grid size.
Some color therefore occurs on at least a delta/K fraction of this
grid.

Choose a fixed integer D, depending only on delta, K, and
\(\mathcal K\), as large as required below. Partition the index grid
into D-by-D boxes, discarding incomplete boundary boxes. The discarded
part has O(Dh) points. For sufficiently large h, some complete box
contains a set S of at least
\[
 \eta D^2,\qquad \eta=\delta/(2K),
\]
labels admitting the same color.

All odd-to-even vertical differences between labels in this box are
at least h/2. All horizontal differences in their common block have
absolute value at most 4D. Consequently, for large enough h,
\[
 d^2<X^2+d^2<(d+1)^2
\]
whenever X is a nonzero such horizontal difference and d is an
odd-to-even vertical difference. The upper inequality follows from
\(16D^2<h+1\le2d+1\). Thus every pair with distinct odd rows is P-free.

## 2. Dense sets contain a suitable oblique rectangle

Choose an integer
\[
 r>10+\max(\{0\}\cup\{|k|:k\in\mathcal K\}).
\]
In the label-index plane use the two lattice vectors
\[
 e_1=(1,r),\qquad e_2=(r,-4).
\]
Their physical even-cell vectors are
\[
 U_0=(4,2r),\qquad V_0=(4r,-8),
\]
and \(U_0\cdot V_0=0\).

The sublattice generated by \(e_1,e_2\) has index \(r^2+4\).
One coset contains at least \(|S|/(r^2+4)\) points of S. On that
coset use integer coordinates s,t, so, up to a translation,
\[
 i=s+rt,\qquad j=rs-4t.
\]
The points lie in an integer square of side n=O_r(D). After padding,
their density in that square is at least a positive constant gamma
depending only on eta and r.

Regard them as the edges of a bipartite graph on the s and t
coordinates. An elementary double count gives at least
\[
 \gamma^4 n^4/64
\]
axis rectangles for sufficiently large n. Indeed, if the edge count
is at least \(\gamma n^2\), the sum of common-neighbor counts over
pairs of s vertices is at least \(\gamma^2n^3/4\). Applying
Cauchy--Schwarz again to those common-neighbor counts gives the
displayed bound.

Some of these rectangles could repeat an odd row or a prescribed
linear level. These exceptions have only O_{\mathcal K,r}(n^3)
possibilities. To see this, write a rectangle's side increments as
d>0 and e>0. For a linear form \(\alpha s+\beta t\), with both
coefficients nonzero, two corner values coincide only if
\[
 \alpha d=\beta e\quad\hbox{or}\quad\alpha d=-\beta e.
\]
There are O(n) possible increment pairs and O(n^2) placements.

The odd-row index i has coefficients 1,r. The level \(b+ka\),
after removal of a constant factor and translation, has coefficients
\[
 r+k,\qquad kr-4.
\]
Both are nonzero by the choice of r. Choosing D sufficiently large
leaves a rectangle with four distinct odd rows and all required
levels distinct. The argument also allows adding k=0 to require
distinct even rows.

## 3. The immutable presentation collision

The four even points of this rectangle have the form
\[
 A,\quad B=A+U,\quad C=A+V,\quad D=A+U+V,
\]
where \(U=dU_0\), \(V=eV_0\), and \(U\cdot V=0\).
They lie in four distinct double columns.

Both coordinates of U are even. Put
\[
 M=A+U/2,\qquad N=A+V+U/2.
\]
The two nondegenerate rectangles
\[
 \{A,M,C,N\},\qquad \{M,B,N,D\}
\]
lie inside the strip. Their symmetric difference is exactly
\(\{A,B,C,D\}\), all of which is black. They have the same white
presentation under every subsequent filling, regardless of whether
M or N is black. This proves the theorem without needing a separate
hidden-rectangle theorem.

## 4. Relation to the list-coloring theorem

For this extension assume \(-1\notin\mathcal K\), as required by the
[linear list-palette theorem](global_P_linear_list_coloring.md).
That theorem provides the needed matching flexibility for P and the
fixed level rules. If every original list has at least \(C_{\mathcal K}h+1\)
colors, the four labels found here can be precolored with their common
color. Remove that color from every other vertex adjacent to one of
them. Every remaining list loses at most one color, so the list theorem
extends this partial coloring to a full proper P-and-level coloring.
The resulting placement still contains the displayed collision.

Therefore even a valid P-and-level coloring from the retained lists
does not automatically supply the geometric exclusion.

The remaining route must correlate the choices of different labels,
add non-pairwise geometric constraints during coloring, or use slots
whose coordinate map differs from \(x=4i+Pc\). Adaptive lists depending
on previous assignments are outside this obstruction.

The dense-box argument also survives deletion of o(h^2) labels:
only o(h^2) of the separated low/high bulk grid can disappear.
Thus the usual subquadratic exceptional-label removal does not
avoid this obstruction.

An independent audit checked the dense-box extraction, the lattice
coset coordinates, the rectangle count and exceptional-level count,
and the half-rectangle presentation collision. The list-extension
qualification \(-1\notin\mathcal K\) is separate from the geometric
theorem, which allows any fixed finite set of integer slopes.
