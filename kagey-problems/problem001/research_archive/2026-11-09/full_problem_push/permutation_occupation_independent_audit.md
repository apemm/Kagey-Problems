# Independent audit of complete occupation for controlled pair families

9 November 2026. [permutation_occupation_mixed_pair_lemma.md](permutation_occupation_mixed_pair_lemma.md) passes review. This is a partial occupation theorem, with the remaining geometric and boundary requirements explicitly retained.

Each translated pair event specifies two images of a uniform bijection, so its probability is exactly 1/[n(n−1)]. A fixed label belongs to at most nD events. There are at most nD/2 templates, each with at most two translations through a fixed slot, giving the same nD slot bound. The shared-label/shared-slot graph therefore has degree at most 4nD−1. Theorem 1 of the [primary Lu–Székely manuscript](https://people.math.sc.edu/lu/papers/randinj.pdf), page 4, gives the canonical conflict graph as a negative dependency graph; adding shared resources preserves that property. Thus 4eD<=n−1 is sufficient.

The product-permutation extension is also valid. Successive image swaps force a canonical event uniformly while preserving every compatible canonical event that already holds. A prescribed image cannot belong to a previously forced distinct assignment, so the successive conditioning is exact. This proves the required negative-dependency inequality directly. With at most r assignments per event, at most 2r resources are involved. The resource load L<=1/(8r), together with x_A=2p_A and the elementary product lower bound, gives the stated local-lemma criterion. Unequal phase capacities must use their actual falling-factorial probabilities; the note does so.

For rigid k-label translated templates, both label and slot event counts are at most nD_k. Hence the displayed higher-arity resource criterion is correct. The already audited oblique mixed-pair degree O(h^(7/4+epsilon)) is small enough for every remaining block of size greater than h^(7/4+delta), with epsilon<delta<1/4. Transferring the finitely many smaller blocks remains subquadratic internally, with quadratic collars.

The one-phase geometric simplification is sound. If a horizontal sea side uses equal filler rows, its endpoints have one unoccupied phase j and its length is divisible by 49. Reducing the equal-norm equation after division by 49 forces both coarse label differences to vanish modulo seven; the midpoint then puts both label columns in phase j, contradicting their phase zero. The other horizontal pairings are the previously reviewed parity and nonsquare-norm cases. For an oriented 0/5 block with v7(a−L)=1, the opposite-parity label-root product has valuation one, whereas label centers in 49Z give horizontal-product valuation at least two. This removes that triangle without a two-phase capacity requirement.

The conclusion does not include vertical parity rivals, equal-residue blocks, unresolved higher-arity supports, or interactions outside the occupied interval. In particular it supplies neither a complete quadratic-width board nor a better unconditional threshold.
