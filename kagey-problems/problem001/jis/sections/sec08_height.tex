\section{Every fixed height}\label{sec:height}

Fix $h$. A blackout of the $h\times m$ grid is a \emph{column word} $w=B_1B_2\cdots B_m$ over the subsets of $\{1,\dots,h\}$, with length $|w|$ and blackout $S(w)$. Let $\mathcal L_h$ be the set of words with a valid blackout, the empty word included. A \emph{factor} of $w$ is a word $B_iB_{i+1}\cdots B_j$.

\begin{proposition}[validity is not a window property]\label{prop:nonlocal}
Let $g\ge1$. The blackout $S=\{(1,2),(3,2),(g+3,1),(g+3,3)\}$ of the $3\times(g+3)$ grid is not valid, but every factor of at most $g$ columns is valid.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diamond $D(1,1)$ and the rectangle on columns $2,g+3$ and rows $1,3$ both show $\{(2,1),(2,3)\}$, while a factor of at most $g$ columns has at most two black points and two different rectangles differ in at least four corners (Lemmas~\ref{lem:diff} and~\ref{lem:threecorners}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[regularity and rational series]\label{thm:regular}
For every $h\ge1$ the language $\mathcal L_h$ is regular, and $G_h(z,u)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal L_h}z^{|w|}u^{|S(w)|}$ is rational in $z$ and $u$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \emph{trace} of a rectangle $R$ is the word whose $i$th letter is the set of rows of its corners in column $i$, and it determines $C(R)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, $w\notin\mathcal L_h$ exactly when two different traces $Y,Y'$ of length $|w|$ have $Y_i\triangle Y'_i\subseteq B_i$ for every $i$. The traces form a regular language, $\emptyset^*\{r,s\}\emptyset^*\{r,s\}\emptyset^*$ for axis-aligned rectangles and, by Lemmas~\ref{lem:leftmost} and~\ref{lem:span}, $\emptyset^*Y_0Y_1\cdots Y_n\emptyset^*$ for finitely many templates \eqref{eq:tilt-param} of span $n\le W_h$. So a finite automaton that reads $w$ and two traces in parallel recognizes the complement of $\mathcal L_h$, and $\mathcal L_h$ is regular. With the weight $u^{|B|}$ on a letter $B$, the transfer matrix $M(u)$ of a deterministic automaton for $\mathcal L_h$ gives $G_h=\alpha(I-zM(u))^{-1}\beta$, rational by Cramer's rule \cite[Proposition~V.6]{flajolet2009}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[fixed height]\label{thm:periodic}
Let $h\ge2$. (a) There are integers $m_0\ge3$, $p\ge1$, and $k_0,\dots,k_{p-1}$ such that $f(h,m)=m+k_{m\bmod p}$ for every $m\ge m_0$. (b) The series $\sum_{m\ge0}a_h(m)z^m$ is rational, with the conventions of Section~\ref{sec:conv}, and the words of optimum blackouts form a regular language.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $E(w)=|S(w)|-|w|$. Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound} and $|S(v)|\le h|v|$ bound the excess of every factor of a valid word by $C_h=\max(q_h,2h-2)$. So a counter for the excess of prefixes, kept in $[k-C_h,C_h]$, gives an automaton for the valid words $\mathcal L_{h,k}$ of excess $k$, and their lengths are eventually periodic. For $m\ge3$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} and Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound} make $f(h,m)-m$ the largest $k$ from $h-1$ to $q_h$ with a word of length $m$ in $\mathcal L_{h,k}$, which gives (a). The optimum words are those of $\mathcal L_{h,k_r}$ of lengths $m\ge m_0$ with $m\equiv r\pmod p$, and finitely many shorter ones, so (b) follows as in Theorem~\ref{thm:regular}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[fixed deficit]\label{cor:deficit}
Let $h\ge2$ and $d\ge0$. The words of valid blackouts with exactly $f(h,m)-d$ points form a regular language with a rational counting series, and so do those with at least $f(h,m)-d$ points.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $w=uv$ is valid of excess $k$, then $k-C_h\le E(u)=k-E(v)\le C_h$ for every integer $k$, so the counter of Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic} makes $\mathcal L_{h,k}$ regular. For $m\ge m_0$ with $m\equiv r\pmod p$, the two languages at length $m$ are $\mathcal L_{h,k_r-d}$ and the union of $\mathcal L_{h,k}$ over $k_r-d\le k\le k_r$. We take these at those lengths, unite over $r$, and add finitely many shorter words.
\end{proof}

The corollary is for fixed $h$ and $d$ and says nothing about counts up to symmetry. Guilhot, Little, and Parkinson \cite[Theorem~1(3)]{glp2025} prove that the words of maximal weight in a regular language form a regular language when the additive weight is bounded above. With our bounds this also gives (a), (b), and Corollary~\ref{cor:deficit}. On valid words of length at least $3$ the excess $E$ is an integer at most $q_h$, and an optimum has $E\ge h-1$. So finitely many removals of the words of maximal excess give each $\mathcal L_{h,k}$ with $k\ge h-1-d$, and a word of level $k$ has deficit $d$ exactly when $k+d$ is the highest level that has its length ($d=0$ gives the optima). Our proof builds the automaton from the rectangles.
