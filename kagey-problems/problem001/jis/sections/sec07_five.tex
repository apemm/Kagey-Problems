\section{Five and six rows}\label{sec:five}

\subsection{Boundary padding}

For a blackout $S$ of the $h\times m$ grid and $L,R\ge0$, let $S_{L,R}$ add the columns
$1-L,\dots,0$ and $m+1,\dots,m+R$, each black exactly in the \emph{padding row}, row $1$ or
row $h$.

\begin{theorem}[finite collar]\label{thm:collar}
Let $h\ge3$, fix the padding row and the padded sides, and let $L^*$ and $R^*$ be $W_h$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:span} on padded sides and $0$ otherwise. Then $S_{L,R}$ is valid for all
$L,R\ge0$ vanishing on unpadded sides if and only if $S_{L^*,R^*}$ is valid.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $S^*=S_{L^*,R^*}$. One direction is clear. Let $S^*$ be valid and $U=S_{L,R}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:leftmost} and \eqref{eq:tilt-param}, a tilted rectangle has at most one corner
in row $1$ and at most one in row $h$. Hence a weak tilted rectangle of $U$ has a black corner in an original column $e$,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:span} its columns lie in
$[e-W_h,e+W_h]\cap[1-L,m+R]\subseteq[1-L^*,m+R^*]$. As $U$ and $S^*$ agree on their common
columns, it is a rectangle of $S^*$ with the same presentation. Suppose distinct
rectangles $R_1$ and $R_2$ of $U$ have the same presentation $P$.

\emph{Step 1. Both axis-aligned.} Added columns are vertices of degree one, so the cycles of
$G_U$ lie in $G_{S^*}$. As $S^*$ is valid with at least three columns, $U$ is axis-valid by
Lemma~\ref{lem:girth}.

\emph{Step 2. Both tilted.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners}, $|P|\le2$, so both are weak, hence
rectangles of $S^*$ with the same presentation, a contradiction.

\emph{Step 3. One of each.} Let $R_1$ be tilted, hence a rectangle of $S^*$. A column of
$R_2$ that meets $P$ is a column of $R_1$, and one that does not has two black corners and is
an original column. So $R_2$ is a rectangle of $S^*$ too, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Five rows}

We write a blackout by the black rows of its columns from left to right. Let $S_8$ be the
blackout $14,\allowbreak 45,\allowbreak 25,\allowbreak 5,\allowbreak 5,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 12$ of the $5\times8$ grid, so that $B_1=\{1,4\}$ and $B_4=\{5\}$.

\begin{theorem}[five rows, (a) and (c) computer-assisted, (b) checked in Lean]\label{thm:fiverow}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item We have $f(5,m)=m+5$ for $m=5$, $6$, and $7$.
\item We have $f(5,m)=m+6$ for every $m\ge8$, attained by $S_8$ followed by $m-8$ columns black in
row $1$ only.
\item The $5\times8$ grid has exactly four maximum blackouts, the images of $S_8$ under row
reflection and column reversal.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(b) The upper bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound}. Since $W_5=5$, Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} with
padding row $1$ on the right reduces $S_8$ to the test board of Figure~\ref{fig:boards}(a). Its
$954$ rectangles have distinct presentations, by three enumerators and Lean.

(a) For $m=5$ the bounds are Corollary~\ref{cor:small-m} and $S_0$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fivefive}. For $m=6,7$ the blackouts $15,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 3,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 45$ and
$1,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 3,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 45,\allowbreak 15$, stored in
\nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_5x6.json} and \nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_5x7_b12.json},
are valid and give the lower bound. Checked SAT certificates show that no blackout of $m+6$
points is valid (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).

(c) Two SAT enumerations and a column search over the $10\,(8)_6\,5^2=5{,}040{,}000$
candidates of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} find exactly the four images.
\end{proof}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $f(h,m)\le h+m$ when a side is at most four, and also on the
$5\times5$, $5\times6$, $5\times7$, and $6\times6$ grids. These are the other grids of area
below $40$, so the guess $f(h,m)=h+m$ first fails at $5\times8$.

\subsection{Six rows and small grids}

Let $S_{16}$ be the blackout of the $6\times16$ grid in Figure~\ref{fig:boards}(b). Its doubles,
in the columns $1,2,3,6,9,10,14,15,16$, carry the edges of $K_{3,3}$ between $\{1,3,5\}$ and
$\{2,4,6\}$.

\begin{theorem}[six rows, (a) computer-assisted, (b) checked in Lean]\label{thm:sixrow}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item For $8\le m\le14$ we have $f(6,m)\le m+8$.
\item For every $m\ge15$ we have $f(6,m)=m+9$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) For $m=8$ this is Corollary~\ref{cor:small-m}. For $9\le m\le14$, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} the doubles of a valid blackout of $m+9$ points carry the edges of
$K_{3,3}$ for one of ten row partitions. Checked LRAT certificates for the structured encoding exclude
every case (Lemma~\ref{lem:enc}).

(b) The upper bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound}. For $m=15$ the blackout
$56,\allowbreak 45,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 14,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 36,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 25,\allowbreak 6,\allowbreak 16$, stored in \nolinkurl{jis/data/six_row_sat_w15_A.json},
gives the lower bound with $24$ points. For $m\ge16$, since $W_6=7$, the test board of
Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} adds $(17,1),\dots,(23,1)$ to $S_{16}$ of Figure~\ref{fig:boards}(b). Its $4{,}627$
rectangles have distinct presentations, by three enumerators and Lean.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[small grids, computer-assisted]\label{thm:refute}
We have $f(6,6)=12$, $f(6,7)=13$, $f(7,7)=14$, and $f(6,8)=15$. The blackout
$15,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 123,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 4,\allowbreak 4,\allowbreak 456,\allowbreak 26$ of the $6\times8$ grid attains the last value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The valid blackouts $14,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 2,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 3456$ of the $6\times6$ grid, $16,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 2,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 3456$ of the
$6\times7$ grid, and $17,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 12,\allowbreak 2,\allowbreak 23,\allowbreak 3456,\allowbreak 67$ of the $7\times7$ grid, stored in
\nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_6x6.json}, \nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_6x7.json}, and
\nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_7x7.json}, give the other lower bounds. Checked SAT
certificates exclude one more point in each case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the $5\times5$ grid]\label{thm:fivefive}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item We have $f(5,5)=10$.
\item (Computer-assisted.) The $a_5(5)=8$ maximum blackouts are the images of $S_0$
below under the square's symmetries.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) The upper bound is Corollary~\ref{cor:small-m}. For the lower bound let $S_0$ be $45,\allowbreak 34,\allowbreak 123,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 15$. Its graph is an
$8$-cycle with two pendant edges, so $S_0$ is axis-valid. By Lemma~\ref{lem:leftmost} its $30$
tilted rectangles are nine unit diamonds, sixteen from Lemma~\ref{lem:tilted-narrow}, and five
spanning the grid. The fifteen weak ones are listed by their two shown corners and, after a bar
for an oblique pair, the white corner of its axis-aligned rectangle.
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
$(1,2),(2,1)\mid(1,1)$ & $(1,3),(2,2)\mid(1,2)$ & $(3,4),(4,3)\mid(4,4)$ & $(2,5),(3,4)\mid(3,5)$ \\
$(2,2),(4,2)$ & $(1,2),(4,3)\mid(1,3)$ & $(4,4),(5,2)\mid(4,2)$ & $(3,5),(4,4)\mid(3,4)$ \\
$(4,3),(5,2)\mid(4,2)$ & $(3,4),(5,2)\mid(5,4)$ & $(2,5),(5,2)\mid(2,2)$ & $(4,5),(5,3)\mid(4,3)$ \\
$(1,3),(4,2)\mid(1,2)$ & $(2,5),(4,3)\mid(4,5)$ & & $(4,5),(5,4)\mid(4,4)$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The fifteen pairs are distinct, and Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners} settles the other
tilted rectangles. Each listed white corner lies on the only axis-aligned rectangle
through its pair, and $(2,2),(4,2)$ has no rival since $B_2\cap B_4=\emptyset$. So $S_0$ is valid.

(b) Of the $44{,}640$ sets of $10$ points whose graph $G_S$ has no four- or six-cycle, exactly the
images of $S_0$ are valid, by three programs.
\end{proof}
