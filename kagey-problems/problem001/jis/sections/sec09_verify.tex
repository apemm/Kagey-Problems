\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

Each computed value and count comes from two separately written programs, except the $7\times8$
count below. Code, data, and commands are in the repository~\cite{repo}, and the paths below
are relative to its folder \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem001}. For each computer-assisted
result, \nolinkurl{jis/scripts/README.md} gives the command, the evidence file with its SHA-256
hash, and the stored time. A longer version of this paper in the repository~\cite{longversion}
has fuller proofs of the four-row computations and of the verification, and
\nolinkurl{jis/removed} keeps text cut from earlier drafts.

An ILP program with CBC~\cite{cbc} through PuLP~\cite{pulp} finds a smallest hitting set for the
sets of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, and brute force checks it. The SAT solvers come from
PySAT~\cite{pysat2018}. SAT encoding A forbids each minimal symmetric difference of two
rectangles. Encoding B has a clause for each pair of rectangles with a common corner and an
indicator for each hidden rectangle, at most one of them true. The structured encodings for six
rows add the column structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} for one row partition. Column
searches add columns from left to right, using $f(6,6)\le12$ on the $6\times7$ and $6\times8$
grids and $f(6,7)\le13$ on the $6\times8$ and $7\times7$ grids.

Our rectangle lists agree with \seqnum{A085582} on squares and \seqnum{A289832} on the other
grids.

CaDiCaL~\cite{biere2024} gives LRAT certificates for $f(5,6)<12$, $f(5,7)<13$, $f(6,6)<13$,
$f(6,7)<14$, $f(7,7)<15$, $f(6,8)<16$, $f(7,8)<17$, and the $70$ six-row instances (widths $8$
to $14$, ten partitions each) behind Theorem~\ref{thm:sixrow}(a). The seven grids use encoding A
over all blackouts, and the six-row instances use the structured encoding B. We split the $6\times8$
instance into the $64$ cases of its first column, and the $7\times7$ and $7\times8$ instances
into $128$, and the second generator checks that the cases cover every first column. Two
separate generators give the same clauses, and all $394$ proofs pass \texttt{lrat-check} of
drat-trim~\cite{cruzfilipe2017,wetzler2014} and the Lean LRAT checker. The encodings are argued
faithful in \nolinkurl{jis/data/unsat_certificates.json}, not formally. The models in
\nolinkurl{jis/data/table_counts/sat_7x8_16.json} give $f(7,8)\ge16$, so $f(7,8)=16$. One
enumeration finds $18$ optima on this grid, and a second did not finish.

The Lean~4 files in \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/lean/KageyProblems/Problem001} of the repository
prove Lemmas~\ref{lem:diff}, \ref{lem:threecorners}, and~\ref{lem:span},
Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}, Theorems~\ref{thm:strip}, \ref{thm:rowbound} (both orientations),
and~\ref{thm:collar}, and that a valid blackout hides at most one rectangle. The kernel checks
that $S_0$, $S_8$, the blackout of Theorem~\ref{thm:refute}, the $6\times15$ blackout of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sixrow}, and the $5\times13$ and $6\times23$ test boards are valid, and that
$\{(1,2),\allowbreak(2,1),\allowbreak(3,2),\allowbreak(2,3)\}$ hides $D(1,1)$ on the $4\times3$
grid, so Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden} needs $m\ge4$. Hence Lean proves $f(5,m)=m+6$ for $m\ge8$,
$f(6,m)=m+9$ for $m\ge15$, $f(5,5)\ge10$, and $f(6,8)\ge15$, from the three standard Lean axioms
alone. It found no error. Before it, Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners} and
Theorems~\ref{thm:rowbound} and~\ref{thm:collar} had only hand proofs, and the test boards had
only Python checks. Not formalized are Lemma~\ref{lem:leftmost}, Theorem~\ref{thm:equality},
$f(5,5)\le10$, the faithfulness of the encodings, the rest of Sections~\ref{sec:strip}
to~\ref{sec:height}, and Appendix~\ref{app:square}.
