\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

The results that rest on a computation are labeled computer-assisted. Every other result in
Sections~\ref{sec:conv} to~\ref{sec:height} has a written proof. Every computed value and count comes from at least two programs
written separately, which agree wherever both ran, with the one exception of the $7\times8$ count below.

All programs are in Python~3.14.3. The ILP program \texttt{solver/engine.py} finds a smallest
hitting set for the difference sets of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff} with CBC~2.10.3~\cite{cbc}
through PuLP~3.3.2~\cite{pulp}. The brute-force check, \texttt{solver/brute.py}, uses no lemma
and shares no code with it. The verifier \texttt{verify\_narrow.py} checks the lemmas and constructions of
Sections~\ref{sec:graph} to~\ref{sec:four} and the $5\times5$ grid in $21$ seconds. The
blackouts of $h+m$ points on the grids $5\times6$, $5\times7$, $6\times6$, $6\times7$, and
$7\times7$ are stored in \nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_*.json}. The SAT solvers are
CaDiCaL~1.5.3 and~1.9.5, Glucose~3, 4, and~4.2, MiniSat~2.2~\cite{een2004}, MapleChrono, and Lingeling from
pysat~1.9.dev15~\cite{pysat2018}, and the algebra uses SymPy~1.14~\cite{sympy2017}.

A SAT instance asks for a valid blackout of at least $k$ points, so by the observation after
Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting} an unsatisfiable instance proves $f(h,m)<k$. Encoding A has a
variable for each point and a clause ``not all black'' for each inclusion-minimal symmetric
difference of two rectangles, with a sequential counter~\cite{sinz2005} for the size. Encoding B has a clause
for each pair of rectangles that share a corner and an indicator for each hidden rectangle, at
most one of them true, with a totalizer~\cite{bailleux2003} for the size. The third encoding,
\texttt{cells\_sat\_check.py}, is of type A but written from scratch. The structured encoders of
Section~\ref{sec:five} add the column structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} for one row
partition and keep only the clauses that involve a tilted rectangle. The collar test boards of
Section~\ref{sec:five} are checked by three separately written enumerators, which list every
rectangle of the board and compare the presentations. The column searches call no solver. They place columns from left to right and stop
when two rectangles inside the placed columns show the same points, which is sound since every
interval of columns of a valid blackout is valid. The search \texttt{table\_counts\_search.py}
also stops when the remaining columns cannot reach the target by the values of $f$ on narrower
grids. So its counts on the $6\times7$ and $6\times8$ grids use $f(6,6)\le12$, from the SAT runs
and the ILP program, and those on the $6\times8$ and $7\times7$ grids use the SAT bound $f(6,7)\le13$.
The five-row and six-row searches use no such bound.

We did not produce DRAT or LRAT proofs. So $f(6,7)<14$, $f(7,7)<15$, $f(6,8)<16$,
$f(7,8)<17$, and the ten row partitions that exclude $m+9$ points at $m=14$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sixrow}(a) rest on unsatisfiable SAT instances without a checked certificate,
and on the agreement of independent encodings on different solvers. Every other upper bound
also follows from a proof by hand, the ILP program, or an exhaustive search with no SAT
solver. The ILP program gives no certificate either, so $f(6,6)<13$ rests on solvers alone. Found blackouts need no such trust, since the stored witnesses and every model of the
counting programs are checked against the definition. A count needs in addition that the
enumeration is complete.

The file \nolinkurl{jis/scripts/README.md} gives, for each computer-assisted result, the
command, the evidence file with its SHA-256 hash, and the stored time with what it measures.
The file \nolinkurl{requirements.txt} pins the package versions. The counts of the optima on the grids $5\times6$ to $7\times7$ in Table~\ref{tab:values} come from two SAT encodings that list every optimum, on Glucose~4 and MiniSat~2.2, and from a column search, all three in the scripts \texttt{table\_counts\_*}. On the $7\times8$ grid no valid blackout of $17$ points exists, by two SAT runs split by the first column, so $f(7,8)=16$. One completed enumeration finds $18$ optima there. The second count did not finish, so the table omits this count.
Table~\ref{tab:values} collects the values, and the rectangle counts of the programs
agree with \seqnum{A085582} and \seqnum{A289832}. Babichev and Babichev~\cite{babichev2026}
count the rectangles of the square grid in near-linear time. The Lean~4 file
\nolinkurl{Problem001.lean} in \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/lean/KageyProblems} checks
Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}, the fact that a valid blackout hides at
most one rectangle, the fact that every rectangle on a strip is axis-aligned, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:strip}, with no \texttt{sorry}. Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners} and the
results from Section~\ref{sec:graph} on are not formalized. Code and data are in the
folder \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem001} of the repository~\cite{repo}.
