\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

The results that rest on a computation are labeled computer-assisted. Every other result in
Sections~\ref{sec:conv} to~\ref{sec:height} has a written proof. Every computed value and count comes from at least two programs
written separately, which agree wherever both ran, with the one exception of the $7\times8$ count below.

The search and counting programs use Python~3.14.3. The ILP program \texttt{solver/engine.py} finds a smallest
hitting set for the difference sets of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff} with CBC~2.10.3~\cite{cbc}
through PuLP~3.3.2~\cite{pulp}. The brute-force check, \texttt{solver/brute.py}, uses no lemma
and shares no code with it. The verifier \texttt{verify\_narrow.py} checks the lemmas and constructions of
Sections~\ref{sec:graph} to~\ref{sec:four} and the $5\times5$ grid in $21$ seconds. The
blackouts of $h+m$ points on the grids $5\times6$, $5\times7$, $6\times6$, $6\times7$, and
$7\times7$ are stored in \nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_*.json}. The SAT solvers are
CaDiCaL~1.5.3 and~1.9.5, Glucose~3, 4, and~4.2, MiniSat~2.2~\cite{een2004}, MapleChrono, and Lingeling from
pysat~1.9.dev15~\cite{pysat2018}, and the algebra uses SymPy~1.14~\cite{sympy2017}.

A SAT instance asks for a valid blackout of at least $k$ points, so by the observation after
Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting} an unsatisfiable instance proves $f(h,m)<k$. Encoding A has a
variable for each point and a clause ``not all black'' for each inclusion-minimal symmetric
difference of two rectangles, with a sequential counter~\cite{sinz2005} for the size. Encoding B has a clause
for each pair of rectangles that share a corner and an indicator for each hidden rectangle, at
most one of them true, with a totalizer~\cite{bailleux2003} for the size. The third encoding,
\texttt{cells\_sat\_check.py}, is of type A but written from scratch. The structured encoders of
Section~\ref{sec:five} add the column structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} for one row
partition and keep only the clauses that involve a tilted rectangle. Three separately written
enumerators and the Lean kernel check the collar test boards of Section~\ref{sec:five}. The column searches call no solver. They place columns from left to right and stop
when two rectangles inside the placed columns show the same points, which is sound since every
interval of columns of a valid blackout is valid. The search \texttt{table\_counts\_search.py}
also stops when the remaining columns cannot reach the target by the values of $f$ on narrower
grids. So its counts on the $6\times7$ and $6\times8$ grids use $f(6,6)\le12$,
and those on the $6\times8$ and $7\times7$ grids use $f(6,7)\le13$.
The five-row and six-row searches use no such bound.

The bounds $f(6,6)<13$, $f(6,7)<14$, $f(7,7)<15$, $f(6,8)<16$, $f(7,8)<17$, and the $70$ instances of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sixrow}(a), at widths $8$ to $14$, have LRAT certificates. Two separately
written generators give the same clauses for all $75$ instances, in encoding A for the five
grids and the structured encoding B for six rows. CaDiCaL~2.1.2~\cite{biere2024} writes the proofs, after
splitting the $6\times8$ instance into the $64$ cases of its first column and the $7\times7$
and $7\times8$ instances into $128$. All $392$ proofs ($28.5$~GB of text) pass
\texttt{lrat-check} of drat-trim~\cite{wetzler2014,cruzfilipe2017} and the LRAT checker of the Lean standard library, which is
proved sound but runs compiled, so it trusts the compiler. That a valid blackout of at least
$k$ points satisfies every clause, by Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} for six rows, is argued in
\nolinkurl{jis/data/unsat_certificates.json} and not formalized. The bounds $f(5,6)\le11$ and
$f(5,7)\le12$ rest on SAT runs and a column search. Other upper bounds use no SAT solver. Found blackouts need no such trust, since the
stored witnesses and the models of the counting programs are checked against the definition,
but a count also needs a complete enumeration.

For each computer-assisted result, \nolinkurl{jis/scripts/README.md} gives the command, the
evidence file with its SHA-256 hash, and the stored time, and \nolinkurl{requirements.txt} pins the package versions. The counts of the optima on the grids $5\times6$ to $7\times7$ in Table~\ref{tab:values} come from two SAT encodings that list every optimum, on Glucose~4 and MiniSat~2.2, and from a column search, all three in the scripts \texttt{table\_counts\_*}. On the $7\times8$ grid, where $f(7,8)=16$ by the certificates above, one enumeration finds $18$ optima, and a second did not finish, so the table omits this count.
The rectangle counts of the programs agree with \seqnum{A085582} and \seqnum{A289832}. Babichev and Babichev~\cite{babichev2026}
count the rectangles of the square grid in near-linear time.

The Lean~4 files in \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/lean/KageyProblems} check
Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}, the fact that a valid blackout hides at
most one rectangle, Theorem~\ref{thm:strip}, the second half of Lemma~\ref{lem:girth}, the part
of Lemma~\ref{lem:H} that the bound needs for one choice of trees, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound} and its transpose, with Mantel's theorem (the case of triangles of
the bound of Tur\'an in Mathlib). They prove Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners} as stated, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} as stated, for either padding row and for one or both sides.

With a list of rectangles
proved complete, the kernel decides that $S_0$, $S_8$, the blackout of
Theorem~\ref{thm:refute}(a), the stored $6\times15$ blackout, and the $5\times13$ and
$6\times23$ test boards are valid, and that the blackout
$\{(1,2),\allowbreak(2,1),\allowbreak(3,2),\allowbreak(2,3)\}$ of the $4\times3$ grid hides
$D(1,1)$, which shows that Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden} needs its hypothesis $m\ge4$. So Lean proves $f(5,m)=m+6$ for $m\ge8$ and $f(6,m)=m+9$ for $m\ge15$, the
case $m=15$ by the stored blackout, and $f(5,5)\ge10$ and $f(6,8)\ge15$. No proof uses
\texttt{sorry} or \texttt{native\_decide}, and the only axioms are \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

The formalization changed no statement of the paper, and it found no error or missing
hypothesis. It changes how some results are established. Lemma~\ref{lem:threecorners},
Theorem~\ref{thm:rowbound} and Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} had proofs only by hand, and the
application of Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} to five and six rows rested on Python checks of the
test boards. Lean now proves all of these.

Not formalized are Lemma~\ref{lem:leftmost}
as stated, Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}, $f(5,5)\le10$, the faithfulness of the
encodings, and the rest of Sections~\ref{sec:three} to~\ref{sec:height},
\ref{app:threegf}, and~\ref{app:square}. Code and data are in
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem001} of the repository~\cite{repo}.
