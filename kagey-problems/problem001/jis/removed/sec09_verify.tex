\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

The results that rest on a computation are marked as computer-assisted where they are
stated. They are the exclusion of the $72$ and $1{,}440$ equality candidates behind
$f(4,4)=7$ and $f(4,5)=8$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:fourboundary}), the count of the $8$ optima
of the $5\times5$ grid among $44{,}640$ candidates (Theorem~\ref{thm:fivefive}(c)), every
count of optima beyond two rows in Table~\ref{tab:values} and in
Proposition~\ref{prop:fourvalues}, the transfer inventory and vector certificate of
Remark~\ref{rem:transfer}, the exact polynomial algebra behind the numerator of
Theorem~\ref{thm:threegf} and the remainders of Appendix~\ref{app:threegf}, the machine sizes
of Theorem~\ref{thm:fourgf}, the values $f(5,6)=11$, $f(5,7)=12$ and $f(6,6)=12$ with every
other value of Table~\ref{tab:values} that no theorem covers, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:fivesmall}. [[P2-scripts]] Everything else in Sections~\ref{sec:conv}
to~\ref{sec:height} has a written proof. The finite enumerations of the width-$21$ witness,
of the $26$-column collar board and of the right-padded boards in Section~\ref{sec:five} are
cross-checks of the hand proofs there and in Appendix~\ref{app:fivecases}, and those proofs do
not depend on them.

\paragraph{The programs.} Two programs compute values and counts, and one script checks the
lemmas and constructions of the narrow grids. The ILP program (\texttt{solver/engine.py})
lists the rectangles of a grid, builds the difference sets of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, prunes them
by the containment rule of Section~\ref{sec:conv}, and finds a smallest hitting set as an
integer linear program with one $0$--$1$ variable per point, solved by CBC through
PuLP~\cite{pulp}. The maximum blackout is $hm$ minus the optimum. To count the optima it then
lists every kept set of the minimum size and checks it against the pruned difference sets, so
the count is exact and does not depend on which optimum the solver returns. The brute-force
check (\texttt{solver/brute.py}) uses no lemma at all. It finds the rectangles by testing every
set of four points with the diagonal test, and it tests every blackout straight from the
definition by computing all presentations and looking for a repeat. It shares no code with the
ILP program and it is slow on purpose. It was run on every grid of Table~\ref{tab:values}
with at most $28$ points, apart from the strips of width $6$ and $7$, whose counts the Strip
Theorem gives. The verifier (\texttt{verify\_narrow.py}) uses only the Python
standard library and runs in about $21$ seconds. It generates rectangles from pairs of equal
diagonals with a common midpoint, independently of the template lists of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilted-narrow}, and it checks the following. The axis criterion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:girth} holds on all $74{,}304$ blackouts of the grids $2\times3$, $3\times3$,
$3\times4$, $4\times3$ and $4\times4$. On three and four rows the tilted rectangles are exactly
those of Lemma~\ref{lem:tilted-narrow}, and the constructions of Theorems~\ref{thm:threevalue}
and~\ref{thm:fourvalue} are valid, at every width from $3$ to $40$ and at the widths $64$, $100$
and $128$. The $72$ and $1{,}440$ candidates of Corollary~\ref{cor:fourboundary} all fail. On the
$5\times5$ grid the largest blackout without a short cycle has $10$ points, exactly $8$ of the
$44{,}640$ ten-point blackouts without a short cycle are valid, they form one orbit under the
symmetries of the square, and none of the $1{,}440$ ten-cycle sets is valid. The first row plus
the first column is valid on every grid up to $10\times10$, and the $21$ stored blackouts of
$h+m$ points on the grids from $5\times5$ to $10\times10$ with $h\le m$ are valid. The new
scripts written for this paper, in the folder \texttt{jis/scripts}, are the following.

\paragraph{Two methods and the ledger.} Every value and every count in
Table~\ref{tab:values} was computed by the ILP program, and on the grids named above also by
the brute-force check. The two agree wherever both ran. The ILP program
needed $723$ seconds on the $5\times7$ grid and $1{,}063$ seconds on the $6\times6$ grid, and
it was not run on larger grids. Before most computations a prediction was written in the
ledger (\texttt{data/ledger.md}), so that the computation could prove it wrong, and several
predictions did go wrong, among them the guess $f(5,5)=9$ and the guess $f(4,8)=13$.

Table~\ref{tab:values} collects the values. The rectangle counts agree with
\seqnum{A085582} on the squares and with \seqnum{A289832} on the other grids, where the entry
$T(n,k)$ of the latter counts the rectangles on an $(n+1)\times(k+1)$ grid of points, so that
our $h\times m$ grid is its $T(m-1,h-1)$. Every value on two, three and four rows and the
value on the $5\times5$ grid also follow from the theorems of Sections~\ref{sec:strip}
to~\ref{sec:five}. The values on the grids $5\times6$, $5\times7$ and $6\times6$ rest on the
ILP program alone, with the lower bound $h+m$ confirmed by the stored blackouts. The counts of
optima beyond two rows are computed and not proved, except that from Theorem~\ref{thm:threegf}
the three-row counts at every width follow from the rational generating function, and the
counts through width $9$ in the table were found from the definition before that theorem
existed. [[P2-table]] The values and counts of the table are the entries of \seqnum{A397315}
and \seqnum{A399580}, read as tables by antidiagonals with offset $2$.
