\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

The results that rest on a computation are labeled computer-assisted. Everything else in
Sections~\ref{sec:conv} to~\ref{sec:height} has a written proof, except for the few remarks
that say a proof is only sketched or not written out. Of these, only
Remark~\ref{rem:fourmemory} is used later, as the design of a computer-assisted count. Every computed value and count
was found by at least two programs written separately. They agree wherever both ran. Before
most computations a prediction was written in the ledger
\nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem001/data/ledger.md}. Several were wrong, among them
$f(5,5)=9$, $f(4,8)=13$, $f(5,8)=13$ and $f(6,8)=14$.

All programs are in Python~3.14.3. The ILP program \texttt{solver/engine.py} finds a smallest
hitting set for the difference sets of Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}. It has a binary variable for each
kept point and a constraint for each inclusion-minimal difference set. It uses CBC through
PuLP~\cite{pulp} and lists every optimum. It took $723$ seconds on the $5\times7$ grid and
$1{,}063$ seconds on the $6\times6$ grid. On the larger grids of Section~\ref{sec:five} it did
not finish in $30$ minutes. The brute-force check \texttt{solver/brute.py} uses no lemma and
shares no code with the ILP program. The verifier \texttt{verify\_narrow.py} uses only the
standard library and runs in $21$ seconds. It checks the lemmas and constructions of
Sections~\ref{sec:graph} to~\ref{sec:four} and the $5\times5$ grid. The blackouts of $h+m$
points on the grids $5\times6$, $5\times7$, $6\times6$, $6\times7$ and $7\times7$ come from
\nolinkurl{game/game.js}. They are rechecked and stored in the files
\nolinkurl{jis/data/small_cells_sat_*.json}. The SAT solvers come from pysat~1.9.dev15
(CaDiCaL~1.5.3 and~1.9.5, Glucose~3, 4 and~4.2, MiniSat~2.2, MapleChrono and Lingeling), and
the algebra uses SymPy~1.14.

Every SAT instance asks for a valid blackout of at least $k$ points. By the observation after
Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}, an
 unsatisfiable instance proves $f(h,m)<k$. Encoding A has a
variable for each point and a clause ``not all black'' for each inclusion-minimal symmetric
difference of two rectangles, with a sequential counter for the size. Encoding B finds
the rectangles separately, from a corner and two perpendicular sides. It has a clause for
each pair of rectangles that share a corner, and an indicator for each hidden rectangle, of
which at most one may be true. A totalizer encodes the size. The third encoding,
\texttt{cells\_sat\_check.py}, is of type A but written from scratch. The structured encoders of

Section~\ref{sec:five} add the column structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} for one row
partition and keep only the clauses that involve a tilted rectangle. The column searches use
no solver. They place columns from left to right and stop as soon as two rectangles inside
the placed columns show the same points. This is sound, since every interval of columns of a
valid blackout is valid. We did not produce DRAT proofs. The safeguard is that independent
encodings on different solvers always gave the same answer.

Table~\ref{tab:values} collects the values. The rectangle counts agree with
\seqnum{A085582} on the squares and with \seqnum{A289832} on the other grids. Note that the
entry $T(n,k)$ of \seqnum{A289832} counts the rectangles of an $(n+1)\times(k+1)$ grid of
points. So our $h\times m$ grid is its entry $T(m-1,h-1)$.

\texttt{sorry}. The code and data are in the folder \nolinkurl{kagey-problems/problem001} of
the repository~\cite{repo}, with the scripts in
\nolinkurl{jis/scripts} and their results in \nolinkurl{jis/data}.
