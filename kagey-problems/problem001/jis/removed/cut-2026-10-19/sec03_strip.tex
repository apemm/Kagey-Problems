\begin{proof}
(a) Distinct rectangles share at most one column. If they share one, the difference
set is the four points of the other columns $p$ and $q$. Otherwise it is the eight points
of four columns, which contain such a set. Every pair $\{p,q\}$ occurs, since a
third column $z$ gives rectangles on $\{z,p\}$ and $\{z,q\}$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}, $S$ is valid if and only if $K$ meets every two columns,
that is, at most one column has no kept point.

(b) By (a), $|K|\ge m-1$, so $|S|\le m+1$, with equality exactly when one column is full
and every other column has one black point. These blackouts are valid by (a).

(c) There are $m$ choices for the full column and two in each other column.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
By Burnside's lemma, $4o_m$ is $a_2(m)$ plus the numbers of optima fixed by the
reflection (none, since it moves every singleton column), and by the reversal and the
half turn (each fixes none for even $m$, and $2^{(m-1)/2}$ for odd $m$, with a central full
column).

Put $n=m-1$. An optimum is given by its full column $e$ and the rows $r_1,\ldots,r_n$ of
its other black points, from left to right. Kings attack exactly when their columns are
equal or adjacent, so $n$ nonattacking kings on the $2\times2n$ board are given by
columns $c_1<\cdots<c_n$ with $c_{j+1}\ge c_j+2$ and any rows $r_j$. The $n+1$ gaps $c_1-1$,
$c_{j+1}-c_j-2$ for $1\le j<n$, and $2n-c_n$ are nonnegative and sum to $1$, so exactly one, say the
$e$th, is $1$, and $c_j=2j-1+[j\ge e]$. This is a bijection with the optima. The
reflection swaps the rows in both. The reversal sends $e$ to $n+2-e$, reverses
the rows, and sends $c$ to $2n+1-c$, where $2n+1-c_{n+1-j}=2j-1+[j\ge n+2-e]$. So the
bijection commutes with both actions.
\end{proof}
