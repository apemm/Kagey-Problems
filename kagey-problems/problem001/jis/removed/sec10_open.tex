\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[(1),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Four rows.} Theorem~\ref{thm:fourgf} writes $\sum_{m\ge6}a_4(m)z^m$ as a
rational function through products of matrices, and the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:foursurvival} is $a_4(m)=O(m^4\,212^{m/4})$. We do not know the
growth constant
$\lim a_4(m)^{1/m}$, which of the three row partitions of the four double columns
dominates, or a compact scalar generating function. [[P2-fourrow]]
\item \emph{The smallest five-row width with excess six.} We have $f(5,m)=m+5$ for
$m=5,6,7$ and $f(5,m)=m+6$ for every $m\ge21$ (Theorems~\ref{thm:fivefive}
and~\ref{thm:fiverow}), so the least $m$ with $f(5,m)=m+6$ lies between $8$ and $21$.
The value $f(5,m)$ for $8\le m\le20$ is open. [[P2-fiverow]]
\item \emph{Six and more rows.} Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic} says that $f(h,m)-m$ is
eventually periodic in $m$ with values between $h-1$ and $q_h$. Is it eventually
constant, and does it reach the row bound $q_h$, as it does for $h\le5$? For $h=6$ the
question is whether $f(6,m)=m+9$ for all large $m$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:collar} a
single valid blackout of a $6\times m$ grid with nine double columns, a padding row
and a collar of $W_6=7$ columns would settle it.
\item \emph{Five-row optima.} Nothing is known about $a_5(m)$ beyond $a_5(5)=8$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden} has no five-row analogue, so a finite transfer for five rows
would have to account for hidden rectangles, and we do not know whether the fraction
of valid blackouts among the candidates of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} tends to zero
as it does on four rows.
\item \emph{Squares.} The exact values $f(n,n)$ are known for $n\le6$ only, and
$f(5,5)=10$ and $f(6,6)=12$ are the sums of the two sides. Theorem~\ref{thm:square}
leaves the gap between $n^{6/5}$ and $n^{4/3}$, and its lower bound comes with no
explicit threshold. A proof without a computer that the $5\times5$ optimum is unique
up to symmetry is also missing.
\end{enumerate}
